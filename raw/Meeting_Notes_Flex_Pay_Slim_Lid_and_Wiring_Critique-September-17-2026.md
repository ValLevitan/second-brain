## Summary

Val walked Genadi through his list of complaints about the Flex-Pay-Slim top lid and the space under it. He said plainly he was reporting what he saw, and asked for the whole thing to be made elegant, tidy and industrial, because installations are starting and there will be a lot of criticism.

**Glued ports.** The ports that are glued come away. The one on the touchscreen side is not glued at all. Genadi said this is already fixed.

**Boards and wires lying open.** When the lid is opened, the boards lie flat and exposed, next to a mass of loose wires. Val said they cannot just sit open like that — something can fall in, and it does not look good. He thinks the wires need to be shorter. Genadi's point was that this is a service compartment: the monitor locks when closed and it is only opened for service, so tidying and shortening the wires should be enough without adding another cover.

Val disagreed on the cover. He does not want a box. He wants a flat metal mesh sitting on small legs or standoffs, laid neatly over the area, with the wires and boards underneath. He said it would cost pennies and lift the look enormously. Genadi confirmed he meant a metal mesh. Val said he prefers mesh over a plain metal plate. Genadi said he understood.

**The lid stopper.** The plastic lid is currently held by a taut string. Val does not like it — he called it wrong and ugly. He wants two struts placed closer to the hinge that limit the travel instead. He also wants them sprung or damped, ideally small gas shocks, so the lid does not slam when released. He accepted this adds a few dollars.

Genadi asked which behaviour Val wanted: a lid that does not slam on closing, or one that opens by itself. Val said both are fine. Self-opening and smooth is convenient and elegant; if they prefer that you lift it by force and it simply does not slam, that is at least better. He left the choice to Genadi. Val added that the surface was deliberately made smooth with nothing to grip, so it is better if the lid rises slightly on its own. Genadi noted that with a gas strut no separate stoppers are needed, because the lid will rise to the strut's height. Val agreed.

**Front wiring.** Val repeated a request Andrei had already made: the wiring that currently connects at the front must not connect there. It has to run around and connect at the back. Val thought it was already done; Genadi said it is not done. Val noted it.

Genadi closed by saying the wiring is somewhat difficult for servicing.

## Decisions Made

- **The exposed boards and wires get a flat metal mesh cover on standoffs** — not a box and not a solid plate. (Val's instruction; Genadi acknowledged.)
- **The taut-string lid stopper is replaced by two struts near the hinge, damped so the lid does not slam** — gas struts preferred. (Val)
- **Whether the lid self-opens or only resists slamming is Genadi's choice** — Val explicitly left it open. (Val)
- **With a gas strut, separate travel stoppers are not needed** — the strut sets the open height. (Genadi, agreed by Val)
- **Front-side wiring is routed to the back** — confirmed still outstanding. (Val, repeating Andrei's earlier request)
