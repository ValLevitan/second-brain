## Summary

Second meeting in Felipe's February operator series. Ray is Inamax's data/operations executive (14–15 years Illinois casino business, then lottery with Intralot across 15 state contracts) — an analytics-literate counterpart who runs Tableau over Intralot imports, Pulse data from independent locations, and his own SAS feeds.

**Georgia market read (Ray).** From ~25,000 units in 2017 to ~45–50,000 now; convenience is near saturation, but redemption-driven "clerkless" verticals keep opening — Inamax holds the **Kroger account** and is installing across Georgia grocery. Everyone sells the same approved games, so operators differentiate by individualized development; Inamax integrates machines into its own player-tracking panel and point of sale at every location. July gift-card legislation is the big upcoming change.

**Competitive landscape.** Inamax is close to IGT (long relationship, pre-release player case studies with real players from their locations). Top five games on Inamax's route: Aristocrat legacy themes ("Legends"/Sun and Moon/Midnight Train family), Light & Wonder game package 2 (Huff N' Puff etc.), IGT Curve (new), Light & Wonder set 1 — then **JVL's Peach 3 at number six**. Ray's thesis: the majors win on 25–30-year-old legacy theme recognition. Aristocrat runs high RTP, Light & Wonder low; Georgia players baffle him analytically — they sit at a 50%-redemption Light & Wonder over a 90–95% Banilla for theme comfort. Progressives are not chased in Georgia and nobody has "keyed in" on them; redemption ceiling preference ~$10,000 and below. Novomatic: overpriced, unproven numbers, weak ground team, slow on integration specs — "if you don't want my business, I have other options." Konami: barely pushed, no known distributor, hard integration, okay trial. Pricing tiers: Aristocrat/Curve $20K+ protected in best locations; Light & Wonder, Bad Dog and JVL sit in the same middle bucket — a new location typically opens with one top-tier plus a JVL and a Bad Dog and sees what happens.

**JVL at Inamax.** 21 active JVL terminals (NTI = net terminal income shared in chat). JVL over-indexes in Hispanic areas — largely location-driven word of mouth ("John down the street has this peach game — can you get it?"); Peach 1/2/3 cluster in one region. Ray answered Juan's Latino-games question: it helps, but it is location demand more than strategy. Six-game packs are right — manufacturers price per game count, so he takes "the six highest performing games."

**Fleet refresh and trade-ins.** Inamax is actively purging old Primeros/Banillas and IGT Prodigy Views (kept alive with parts bought from the Caribbean and Chicago); Ray refurbishes pulled cabinets himself — ~$1,000 monitor conversion to 43-inch plus a $3–4K Bad Dog board, $5–6K all-in. Val probed the trade-in appetite: Ray would want "a few thousand" per old cabinet against discounted new or high-performing JVL peach cabinets — and confirmed the decision is his. Val flagged the expired Christmas trade-in offer and authorised Felipe to extend it exclusively to Inamax.

**The trial agreement (the meeting's win).** Val's ask: place JVL free machines against top performers in top locations, with JVL allowed to physically extract detailed data (not through any system), paying for technician time if needed. Ray: "I would be okay with that — I'll definitely put two or three games," agreed to comparative data against competitors, and accepted **three machines in three different locations**. Felipe to finalise; lunch planned next week.

**Nebraska.** Ray asked unprompted for the Nebraska skill offering — the market is growing fast and he "can only stick so many Light & Wonders and Bad Dogs in a location." JVL went live in ~6 Nebraska locations a week and a half ago; Juan to send options and pricing right away.

**Inamax engineering.** 13–16 engineers, primarily software, reporting 50/50 to Ray and Nick — a real in-house development shop.
