# Meeting Summary — InComm / Val Levitan — Georgia COAM strategy, Skill Pay card, recycling ATM

## Summary

Informal one-on-one call. Jason walked Val through how InComm's card program actually runs today, then gave Val his read on the Fiery Pay threat and on whether JVL should sell or operate its recycling ATM.

**Scale and card volumes.** InComm is now live in roughly 4,000–5,000 locations (Val's follow-up question confirms they were talking about Georgia) and sees about 50,000 cards a week. Jason put the average at 1.5 cards per day per location, but prefaced it with "I don't know, I think" — treat it as his rough figure. He said it should be about 4.6 at full pool; the prior pilot ran at 0.5–0.7, so it has come up two to three times. Because they can see the swing in card use, they can tell which locations were already compliant before the law. In another month or two they expect to identify relative performance at every location and how far each is complying.

**Pax devices and brokers.** Jason does not like InComm's Pax devices — cheap, they work, nobody wants to pay for them, and the experience is poor. InComm is not in the device business: it buys Pax units from large wholesalers and hands them to brokers, who place them with locations. Named brokers: Bob at IFS, RWJ, Yenlo, and M3T (technically a broker, connected to InComm, and it services collections on the equipment it sells). Jason explained the broker model as a mortgage servicer: InComm underwrites all the cards and the locations, while brokers represent InComm and do the fieldwork.

**Val's probe: could JVL displace the Pax fleet?** Val asked directly whether InComm would rather stay in that business or have JVL replace it. Jason's answer was that InComm is not in the Pax business at all — the devices only exist to get cards activated.

**Device-agnostic position — the Netflix analogy.** InComm does not care whose device activates its cards. Jason said InComm would happily contribute a few hundred dollars — up to about $300 — toward a location buying a JVL device instead of a Pax, and would rather more locations used JVL equipment because JVL does the card dispensing. His framing: InComm is Netflix, and it does not matter whether the TV is a Samsung or a Sony as long as it comes loaded. Most of InComm's work is enabling providers that build their own equipment — he named JVL, "M three P" (likely M3T) and Excel. His closing endorsement was explicit: "I'm happy to have you sell as many machines as possible that will dispense cards or issue cards because I love issuing cards."

**Fiery Pay and the capital argument.** Jason called Fiery Pay a real competitor with essentially the same product today. His differentiator is balance sheet, not features: he told Kenny at Epic that Fiery Pay is as good as InComm at 10, 20 or 50 locations, but at 200-plus locations, if the gift card provider runs out of money to keep funds on the cards at the bank, those 250 locations go dark while InComm keeps running.

**Skill Pay card.** Val stated the new ATM-usable card is coming out mid-September and Jason's only confirmation on the call was the single word "September" — the firmer mid-September confirmation reached Val by message the same day (see the Notes below). Jason expects the card to be materially different from Fiery Pay's product and expects Fiery Pay to need 12–18 months to copy it, by which time InComm has another payment solution in development. His stated plan is to keep competitors chasing rather than innovating. Val asked whether Skill Pay can be accepted at JVL machines; Jason said there is an API JVL can integrate, either at the point of sale or at the machine. No integration work was committed to on the call.

**Locations running both providers.** InComm imposes credit limits — a location may only activate, say, $5,000 in cards before paying. Some locations do $10,000 a day and ask for more credit. InComm's answer is to make a deposit; instead, some locations sign up a second card provider (Jason said "they go and sign up a Fidelity thing"), so InComm now sees locations running both its product and a competitor's.

**Bank Secrecy Act paperwork (Monica).** Jason spoke with Monica and sent her a BSA plan template. His guidance: whatever the contract says, the real requirements are a named compliance officer — Monica — and a written plan submitted to InComm. InComm will most likely never read it and just files it as evidence a plan exists.

**ATM opportunity and the recycler question.** Jason sees a large opening in ATMs: as soon as a card can be used at an ATM, ATMs will be drained, and he does not think cash-in-transit operators are positioned for the coming transaction volume ("nobody has really"). Val described JVL's recycling ATM (RATM), which JVL will show at the Georgia expo: bill validators come out of the gaming machines to force recycling, all cash goes into the RATM, the RATM issues a QR code the player carries between machines, and presenting the QR back at the recycler pays out cash. Jason asked whether it could run as a cash-in point issuing TITO tickets; Val first said yes, then corrected himself — TITO will not work, because a TITO ticket needs a bill acceptor to validate it and the validators are deliberately removed.

Asked whether JVL should place and operate these or sell them to operators, Jason leaned to selling — he has never been in the ATM business, and running a cash route looks harder than what most Georgia operators do. He added that part of "the Indian market" may want to simply place machines. He also flagged that banks are often unwilling to lend cash for ATM fill, and that Herring Bank in Texas will do bailment for COAM locations with ATM needs.

**Herring Bank.** Val confirmed Jason's introduction to Maya led somewhere: Val has spoken to Campbell and JVL will be registering its ATM with them.

**Loyalty.** Val said Fiery Pay approached him for integration and he declined, staying with InComm. Both men will be at the Georgia show — Val coming down from Canada — though no meeting there was actually arranged. Jason's closing framing was a circular ecosystem: players put money in, get paid out, and put it back in.

## Decisions Made

- **JVL stays with InComm and declined Fiery Pay's integration request** — Val's stated position on the call ("I'm staying with income... I'm kind of your loyal customer"). (Val)
- **Working direction: sell the recycling ATM to operators rather than have JVL operate cash routes** — Jason's advice, on the grounds that running a cash route is harder than what Georgia operators normally do; Val's response was "So sell." Not locked: Jason said both "I would definitely sell the equipment" and, in the same breath, "I would maybe sell. I don't know enough about the ATM space." (Jason Hall, Val)
