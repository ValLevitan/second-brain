## Summary

Val introduced himself to two NTN operators he hadn't met during a recent Nebraska trip. He pitched three product/feature concepts:

**1. Electronic wallet / split-payout concept.** Nebraska operators avoid hand pays because any hand pay must run through the state's wager system with 1099 reporting. Operators currently disable hand pays entirely — a $2,500 win pays out in cash with no trace beyond the machine's internal audit trail. Val proposed splitting large wins into sub-$1,200 payouts plus an electronic wallet balance, or structuring "partnerships" among players to divide winnings below the threshold. Operators said it has theoretical appeal but would likely get abused by players gaming the system, and would need state regulatory review.

**2. Promotional credits.** For every $20 wagered, the machine adds $2 in non-cashable promo credits. Val said he did this successfully on horse racing machines in the 1980s. Both operators were receptive — "every player wants free credits." Operator 1 noted the accounting/back-end integration with their current system would need figuring out.

**3. Recycler kiosk.** Val pitched the Flex Recycler: a single kiosk per location replaces bill acceptors and dispensers in every machine. Player inserts cash, gets a QR code, scans it at any machine to play, and cashes out at the kiosk from the same bills it already collected. Eliminates bill jams, paper, cash collection crews, and kiosk refills. Val also offered free SMIB boards for per-machine reporting, claiming it would undercut what operators currently pay for that data. Operators called it "a good idea" and "interesting."

Val shared his background: founded CashCode (sold to Crane in 2006, which later acquired MEI/Mars Electronics), sat on the Gaming Standards Association board 2000–2006 developing peripheral-device standards.

Operators mentioned Dennis has handled recent technical issues (ticket printing, hand-pay settings) and Dana referenced an incoming South Dakota software update. Val confirmed the SD update is expected mid-to-late August and includes Keno games (not applicable in Nebraska since the state runs its own Keno).

No formal decisions recorded. This was an introductory pitch meeting.
