## Summary

**Small talk and other markets.** [Personal passage removed — owner ruling 2026-09-28: the JVL brain holds JVL matters only. Original kept in the source vault.] He talked about Nebraska, Wyoming and Montana. He said lottery states that let machines in may go to InComm as a vendor, and InComm would then need partners to buy machines. ARC's board member and lobbyist Byron Booth knows the Wyoming director well.

**InComm and Val's plan.** Val said he has lined up private family-office capital to finance equipment, so operators would not have to pay; most likely a rental. He has talked with InComm about their 5,500 locations. He wants a share of InComm's prize-card money (he guessed InComm earns 1–8% on about $5 billion). George said ARC gets about 50 cents or $1 per gift-card redemption from InComm, and that ARC's InComm contact is Jason Hall. Val's long-term idea: technology plus the fund plus two or three strong operators, take most of the market, then sell the company to InComm or to NCR (whose division was bought by Brink's). George did not commit to any of this.

**ARC's Skill Pay plan (George asked to keep this private).** ARC plans to get two legal opinions. The idea: print "gift card" on the TITO ticket, so the ticket itself is the gift card, which the player then swaps for a Mastercard. Goal: put Skill Pay cards in the market for players.

**Georgia Lottery and Intralot.** Val said he asked the lottery's Chris for a site controller again today and was refused. George (ex-Intralot) said this will not change: COAM revenue keeps rising, so the lottery sees no reason to do more. He said the lottery did agree that Intralot can test JVL machines in house with JVL engineers, but Intralot does this for every manufacturer. GLI approval is what counts. **George's tip:** ask Intralot for a site controller for Nebraska (Intralot runs Nebraska, ARC has no presence there). It is the same site controller, so it can be used to test Georgia machines, but rules differ. In the Georgia Intralot lab, "Chris" decides and delegates to Bill. Shane is Intralot's Georgia operations manager.

**Transactional test at one ARC location.** George needs an option for locations that do not want a kiosk. Today he uses a Pax device there. He wants to test JVL's Flex IQ instead: players move money between machines, and cash out at the IQ with a gift card. MudaGoose would be removed. George will first come to JVL's office for a 30–60 minute demo, then pick one location and get David's approval. He named a top-tier location doing about $80K a month that is asking for a kiosk. Val offered, for an average 6-machine location: six Flexports, one IQ and one tabletop printer, installed by a JVL technician. Fee: $90 per location per month for all devices (a monitoring fee). Val also offered to sweep the location's money, act as broker, and post to ARC's general ledger. George called the automated broker part "my selling point"; he is not sure ARC will use the accounting part. The kiosks stay as they are.

**Machine volumes.** ARC cannot take 65 machines before Christmas; there are no locations for them. George said they must first finish the current 60-unit deal. ARC now has a new team that decides where machines go, and it is slower. **George's estimate for 2027: about 8 machines a month from JVL** ("very accurate", not just conservative). ARC's total would be about 120–150 machines with today's numbers, from eight or nine suppliers. After the current promotion ends, purchases will be at JVL's full price at that time. A new JVL machine model is a new deal, not part of the current one. Val said 10 a month at full price would make him very happy, plus help with one or two other operators. George said JVL's price and service are among the best.

**Old Georgia fleet.** George said about half of the machines in Georgia are 6–7 years old. IGT asked him for about $300,000 to replace logic boxes on 50 machines.

**Trade-in for Texas.** Val offered to take ARC's old IGT and other machines, fit JVL boards, and sell them to an operator with a Texas route. Boards can be garbage; monitor, buttons, touch screen and cabinet must work. Value would be a credit against ARC's 2027 purchases (one-year credit; George said they would use it right away). It is a handshake deal, no one else involved; machines ship straight to Val's warehouse in Austin, Texas. George said the number could be 60 to about 275. **George will count what ARC has and send Val the number and the trade-in he wants.** Price not agreed: Val said Texas buyers pay at most $3,000–4,000 per machine and he needs his board and labor; George said $1,500 sounds logical, give or take, depending on cabinet type. Val said if 20% are bad he will ask for parts. George also has hundreds of spare game boards. Val said Texas law may change in March.

**Class A.** ARC has close to 35 Class A locations and is closing a few; it adds about two a week. George said some Class A locations earn more than Class B. Val will have his Class A machine for ARC in January. Val's goal: a cheap slim machine for about $3,000 plus a revenue share, no bill acceptor and no printer, with promo credits (for example $2 extra on $20). George was not sure about this. He wants a progressive like the one Primero tried (the GLC stopped it). ARC pays Primero about $1,150–1,250 for the game board and uses its own refurbished cabinets. George said two kiosks in one location will confuse players. David has offered Val a revenue share on machine revenue, like Primero.

**Washington DC.** Val holds an application. George said the market is tiny, about 50–55 locations; ARC has some, Light & Wonder told him it wants to enter.

**Other operators.** George knows every operator with 50+ locations. Kyle and Mark Brooks are friends and similar in size. Val said Kyle accepted a full-business deal, then got cold feet, and Val released him. George called Mike Patel very small. George dislikes Inamax. On United: people say Banilla is out from January. George will ask Byron Booth (on ARC's board; the same fund is behind United) if he can give Val his number.

**Social.** Basketball game on Monday, November 9; No business at the game. Next lunch is on George, at a Greek place.
