## Summary

A short cold-ish call from the car. Val had dinner the night before with Shawn Fellows of Diamond Amusements and did not realise Fellows knew J&J. When Val said he was trying to reach J&J about Nebraska, Fellows told him to call **Sam Westgate** — "the top guy there, he's in charge."

**Val's pitch.** He has a game approved for Nebraska and a transactional product for Nebraska. He was open about the weak start: the first iteration of the game did not perform, but JVL's history is that it delivers a game that makes money, and what JVL needs is a good partner on the project. On the transactional side he leaned on his own record — he owned **CashCode**, sold it to Crane National Vendors (now CPI, who later bought MEI) in 2006, and before the sale CashCode designed **Bill to Bill**, a recycling bill acceptor. JVL's transactional product is built around that bill recycler plus an **AFT**, with an optional point of sale, and it runs either eWallet or TITO. He closed the pitch with his six years on the **Gaming Standards Association** board in Las Vegas.

**Sam's answer — the process, not a decision.** Everything J&J evaluates gets shipped to their office in **Effingham, Illinois**, where they go through it with the CEO. Val is welcome to come down and demo, or ship it and talk by phone, whatever he prefers. Evaluation for Nebraska or any other state happens in Effingham. Val committed immediately: he will ship the games and the transactional equipment to Illinois, send his own people to set it up as a fully working Nebraska configuration, and come down himself for the presentation. That is the only thing he asked for.

**Illinois came up unprompted.** Sam asked whether the transactional product is available in Illinois too. Val said the whole push right now is Georgia, and that JVL is anxious to put it in every market. Sam pushed back with the obvious question — how does it apply in Georgia when there is no cash out there? Val's answer: Georgia gets a slightly different product, a payment kiosk, a point of sale and an AFT display. A modification for Georgia.

**Who actually decides.** Sam was direct that he is not the man to convince. **The CTO evaluates everything.** A second name, **Derek Bremley**, handles equipment purchasing for **Nebraska and Georgia** but not Illinois. J&J's footprint as Sam listed it: Montana, Nebraska, Vegas, Pennsylvania, Illinois, Georgia and Iowa. Sam asked Val to text or email his contact details; he will walk it to the CEO and have him call Val back. Val was driving, so Sam agreed to text first.
