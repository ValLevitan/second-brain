## Summary

Short alignment call between Val and his JVL accountant Sean Riley covering three subjects: replacing Igor as JVL's part-time controller, status of 2011680 Ontario historical tax filings, and JVL 2025 year-end work.

### Controller search — replacing Igor (Mexico)

Val opened by saying he is looking for a part-time, work-from-home, Canada-based controller with strong professional skills *and* good communication ability. Part-time is fine — he does not believe a full-time role exists for the position. He clarified this is not a CFO replacement: Alka is leaving naturally and that role is not being backfilled. The hire specifically replaces Igor in Mexico.

Val's reasons for replacing Igor: Igor lacks confidence in working independently, always needs a support person around him both mentally and professionally. There is also a significant language barrier — Igor does not speak English, and Val sees no realistic path to that changing in the timeframe needed (would take years even if Igor started learning today). Val estimates he wastes ~50% of the time explaining things, ~20-30% pacifying or "babying" Igor, leaving only ~50% on actual work.

Role scope: NOT data entry or posting work — JVL already has dedicated AP and AR people doing that. The controller role is reviewing entries, end-of-month reporting, clearing intercompany transactions, spotting wrong postings, and producing year-end / semi-annual books. Val noted JVL is close to automating most of this; even after full automation, a human is still needed to validate the automation's output, execute decisions, and own the books. The controller currently works under Alka's tight supervision — she tells Igor what to check and adjust, and Igor is "her physical hands."

Compensation: Val proposed roughly $60–65K/year for a half-timer who knows what they're doing. Sean's market reference: a basic full-time controller is around $85K/year, scaling up to $150K/year for a senior, near-CFO-level person. Sean confirmed $60–65K for a half-time experienced controller "is about right."

Sourcing approach: Sean offered to check his own network and his partners' clients for any controllers who are between roles or open to a move. He also described the standard approach — a recruiter LinkedIn profile to post the role, recruiter agencies for vetting (worth the fee given the noise of unfiltered resumes). Val rejected the ad/recruiter approach: "That's not gonna work for me." Val accepted Sean's offer to spread the word through his network but explicitly said it cannot be the system — only opportunistic.

Open question Val raised: how to reliably resolve the situation if nothing pops out of Sean's network. Sean had no further answer.

### 2011680 Ontario historical filing — CRA sign-on still owed

Val acknowledged he owes Sean a CRA sign-on for 2011680 Ontario. To unlock that sign-on, Val first needs to file his 2024 personal T1 — that's the verification mechanism. He hasn't gotten around to it yet.

Sean clarified the state of his work: he has already drafted the 2011680 returns from the bank statements Val sent over and reconciled the loan activity between JVL and 2011680. Sean files from 2017 (or 2018) forward — there is a gap from the entity's inception (~2006) through 2017 where Sean is not the historical filer. The CRA sign-on is just to confirm that nothing was filed in that gap so Sean can lock in the right opening numbers and check what losses or other tax-account balances might be carrying forward.

Sean confirmed he can file with his current best opening numbers if needed; the CRA check is purely a matter of confidence. Val has provided the initial starting point (~2006) plus all the loan history, and Sean believes that gives him enough to derive the year-one return.

Val explained the personal context: his "file grooming" is dragging on (his words: "It's a black hole — you start doing it and it's just never ending"). He expects to finish, and his goal is specifically to compile 2011680 because it is "the only missing element for formalizing my departure." Everything else is in order. He is currently working on visa documents — applying for a US visa with a path to a green card — and won't depart until the visa is secured. The visa is not yet finalized, so the 2011680 unwind is not strictly time-pressured, but it remains the eventual bottleneck.
