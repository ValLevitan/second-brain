## Summary

A catch-up call that turned into two pieces of real business: Val testing the Georgia recycler situation on someone whose judgement he trusts, and Val trying to recruit him to operate Class A machines in Georgia.

**Jamie's business.** He is in the fifties for Illinois locations with line of sight to seventy, and says they are **not far from being the largest operator in Illinois**. These are more than cafés — 90–95% of revenue from gaming, 5–10% from liquor, and they are deliberately diversifying into neighbourhood bars to be more defensive and reach a broader player pool. They run on loyalty. Management is the strain: he described a person (heard as "Moi" — spelling unconfirmed) whose job has outgrown itself, and the plan is to move him from COO to **Chief Revenue Officer**, because he is strongest on machine optimisation and revenue and is a business-development salesman at heart, and to bring in a multi-unit retail operator as COO. They have already upgraded food-and-beverage management with a hire out of Hidden Creek who has both casino and multi-unit F&B background.

Pipeline: about **twelve locations**, and they are looking outside Illinois — **Nebraska, Wisconsin and West Virginia**. His thesis is two hubs, the Plains and Midwest as one, the Northeast and Atlantic Coast as the other. His own assessment of the business: "You guys are great. We're good. We've got some work to do to be great, but we're good."

**Val's pitch.** JVL now has transactional equipment — changers, cash dispensers, cash recyclers, point-of-sale units — which combines with gaming into a stronger offering to operators. Automated ATM-like transactions, good for Nebraska and Georgia under the new regulations, and they are looking at something for South Dakota. It converts a location to **fully automated with no attendant**: takes cash, dispenses cash, issues an electronic wallet. JVL also has its own AFTs now, installed inside the machines with optical readers and mini screens. JVL is **not at G2E** — Val called it "for the big guys" — but will be in Vegas in March for the AMOA show. He invited Jamie to Georgia to see it set up.

**The recycler and the Georgia Lottery.** This is the substance of the call. Georgia introduced cards, and InComm introduced a conversion to an ATM card. Val manufactured a **recycler that takes those cards and dispenses cash**. He showed it to Joe Kim, who reacted violently. In Val's account the Georgia Lottery is now **backpedalling on the ATM card and the whole industry is upside down**. Both InComm and the Georgia Lottery have since asked Val politely not to build it or put it in the market — and both asked **after the fact**, having already seen it. Val's own reading, and InComm's, is that it is **illegal under the current law**; everyone is confused, and he thinks Joe Kim is confused too.

Jamie's advice was the opposite of caution: **force the issue.** Sell a number of them, make them change the rules, and it could open the door to Georgia becoming a VLT market (heard as "VLP"). He added the political read — with Kemp leaving, the picture changes; Kemp was no fan of gaming but always said that if it got out of the legislature he would let Georgians vote on cash gaming, and Jamie puts sentiment at **over sixty percent in favour of cash gaming in Georgia**. A catalyst could force the change.

**Class A.** Jamie knows the category and attributed the push to "Mike" (no surname given). Val's position was the same one he gave ARC that morning: he brought his game designer, JVL understands the technology from **limited-payout machines in Germany**, and they can make a stunning machine — the software and how to make it interesting is the part they know. It needs a major redesign and he will do it. The size of the prize: **tens of thousands of machines** could go into the market. Jamie: "You could certainly make millions," and noted Class A machines are cheaper to make and cheaper to administer.

Val's actual ask was to **bring Jamie back to Georgia to operate it**, with a "killer price" and changed economics. Jamie's reaction was that this may be too good to sit on until October.

**The meeting.** Val wanted the week of October 12; Jamie may be in Chicago that week and offered the 19th, then settled on Georgia on the **13th or 14th**. Val fixed it at **October 13 at his office in Georgia**, and Jamie said he would leave it with him this week and try to move things around — currently he is slated for Chicago, Toronto, Chicago, so it is a TBD, not a confirmation. Val promised to show him the transactional equipment.
