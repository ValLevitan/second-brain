Based on my thorough research through the transcripts
  using sequential thinking, here's a comprehensive
  understanding of why the Flex-Pay initiative for GA-COAM
  was started:

  The Origins and Reasons for the Flex-Pay Initiative

  The Core Problem (2023-2024)

  The initiative began when Georgia Lottery approved gift
  cards as a legal redemption method for COAM (Coin
  Operated Amusement Machine) winnings. This created both
  an opportunity and a significant operational challenge:

  1. The Manual Process Problem
  - Players win on COAM machines and receive a ticket
  - They must go to the store clerk/cashier to redeem their
   winnings
  - The clerk manually scans and loads value onto a GLC
  (Georgia Lottery Corporation) gift card
  - This process is:
    - Labor-intensive for clerks
    - Prone to human error
    - Creates friction in the player experience
    - Dependent on clerk availability and willingness

  2. The Money Leakage Problem
  - Once players receive gift cards loaded with their
  winnings, they can:
    - Leave the location and spend money elsewhere
    - Use the cards at any location accepting MasterCard
    - This causes significant revenue loss for locations
  - As stated in the April 2025 meeting: "merchants are
  worried about the inability to use gift cards at COAM
  gaming machines, which could lead to a loss of in-store
  spend"

  3. The Circular Economy Challenge
  - Operators wanted players to be able to use their gift
  card winnings to play more games
  - Without this capability, money was leaving the gaming
  ecosystem
  - The core use case: "a player wins $100 at a convenience
   store, loads it onto a MasterCard, and then cannot use
  that $100 to play at the COAM gaming machines"

  Market Drivers

  1. Legislative Opportunity
  - Georgia Lottery's approval of gift cards as a
  redemption method opened new possibilities
  - Gift cards were seen as "almost like cash" but with
  better tracking and compliance
  - The cards had to comply with specific regulations while
   modernizing the payment experience

  2. Competitive Advantage
  - United Gaming (representing ~25% of Georgia's 24,000
  machines) was looking to differentiate
  - Being first to market with an automated solution would
  attract more players
  - As noted in 2023: "If we find a solution together...
  we'll end up buying more JVL machines because that's what
   the players will prefer"

  3. Operational Efficiency
  - Reduce cash handling costs and risks
  - Eliminate the need for constant clerk intervention
  - Provide better tracking and accounting of transactions
  - Address service issues with mechanical components

  The Human Factor

  From the 2023 meeting with United Gaming:
  - "The person behind the counter is not really motivated
  to give the card"
  - Players had to actively push for cards instead of
  merchandise
  - This created friction and reduced gift card adoption

  Financial Impact

  Val Levitan's calculations from 2023 showed:
  - If machines generate $3,000/day with 90% RTP
  - Even at 20% gift card adoption: $540/machine/day
  through cards
  - At 3% processing fees: $16/day/machine in fees
  - For 3,000 machines: $48,000/day in processing costs
  - The opportunity to keep this money in-house was
  significant

  Strategic Vision

  The initiative aimed to:
  1. Automate the redemption process - Remove manual clerk
  intervention
  2. Close the loop - Enable gift cards to be used at
  gaming machines
  3. Reduce costs - Minimize transaction fees and handling
  charges
  4. Improve player experience - Make redemption seamless
  and immediate
  5. Retain revenue - Keep winnings within the location's
  ecosystem

  Evolution of Solutions

  The initiative evolved from initial concepts in 2023:
  - Started with exploring plastic card readers
  - Considered paper tickets (like TITO)
  - Moved toward QR codes and optical readers
  - Eventually settled on a comprehensive kiosk + reader
  solution

  This initiative represents JVL's response to a
  fundamental shift in the Georgia gaming market - the
  transition from cash-based redemptions to electronic
  payment methods, driven by regulatory approval of gift
  cards and the urgent need to prevent revenue leakage
  while improving operational efficiency.
