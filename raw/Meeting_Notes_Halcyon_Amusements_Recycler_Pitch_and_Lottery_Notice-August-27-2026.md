## Summary

The operator showed Val a notice the Georgia Lottery sent to locations a couple of weeks ago: with gift-card systems, the lottery now demands end-of-day register reports, bank statements, and the commissions retailers charge for cash-back off gift cards — the lottery has realized retailers take $5–$30 (even $50–$100 on big wins) to convert cards to cash and wants in. Val asked for a copy; the operator agreed to send it. Chris Plonic at the lottery had originally said the lottery does not care what happens after a card is issued; that has changed.

Val made his data pitch: Ailer-type reports are self-proclaimed and mathematically wrong (Val cited a report claiming JVL paid out a million dollars); from September 1 JVL machines ship with LTE modems, and JVL offers to buy its own data back at $337/year/machine — real geo-positioned net win, popularity, and settings data. Val's settings argument: 25-cent minimum bets with 94–96% RTP during a location's ~3 useful hours a day demolish net win — a 90% RTP at 40–50 cent minimum vs 95% at 25 cents is a ~50% difference in net win, not 5%. JVL can customize bets per game (feature game at 25 cents, rest at 40+); the operator asked for the ability to turn off certain bets — confirmed. Val also walked through the triple-progressive concept (lines/ways, jackpot-rush chaser, link) and the ~15-game package.

On cash handling: the operator uses PTI + PTI Sentry kiosks in Texas with staff manually pre-loading ATMs; Val pitched the recycler (player buy-ins fund payouts from lockable cassettes, no pre-loading, no sorting, no shrinkage) and noted the first test location installs first week of October with Joe Hollow / Jordan Barberio (association president).
