## Summary

Val's second partner call (after Steve Muzzo, who is away — Val meets him Monday). Val told Louis directly: he has to make a cash call at JVL of about $1M. He acknowledged Louis and the others entered the business as gratitude for the Trump project rather than as an investment; Louis corrected that he has nothing with Trump — he was in strictly for commercial reasons, and because of Val ("it's you who I believe in").

Background Val gave: he held off as long as he could. Trump hasn't been making money; JVL is his only occupation. Since COVID the casino market dried up (casinos stopped buying equipment), so JVL switched to the street market — variety stores and gas stations — and expanded in Georgia. The company was profitable roughly three years running and repaid the ~$3M loan to Doug Skrepnek and Muzzo. Last year competitors squeezed the Georgia market, and the bigger problem was an unknown software glitch that cost significant sales before it was discovered. It is fixed and results are good again, but recovery takes time. South Dakota is licensed and showing good results (small, exotic market); Nebraska is licensed with product going in now (small market). A transactional product — converting gaming money onto prepaid cards — is just rolling out; revenue probably 2027. Val sees no hundred-million-dollar exit strategy. The company's cash is gone: burn is roughly $500K a month against ~$250K total revenue. The company is losing money this year.

Debt/ownership picture: the interest-bearing loan to Doug and Muzzo (who hold first security) has been repaid, but accumulated interest of about $2.8M is outstanding. After earlier dilution rounds Val owns roughly 56–58% of the company.

Val is selling his house and going into debt to meet the cash call himself. He told Louis he doesn't know whether Louis should participate or not.

Louis declined: he has tens of millions tied up at modular precast and no bandwidth ("twenty six hours of a twenty four hour day"). He asked for the last 3–4 years of financials — he hasn't seen financials in years — both to take a look and as evidence for a tax loss. Val agreed to send them and offered open access to the books, even the computer.

Observations from Louis's side: the cash call will wipe everybody out — new equity priced so low that existing shares become worthless — and what does raising $1M do against a $500K monthly bleed; Val conceded it may just be "delaying death." The second participant recalled making the same judgment at Integra (curtain wall) — no good money after bad; it cost him $7M, and Louis took a hit alongside him ("Louis took a hit with me on that one"). Louis's side expects Val will put out a proper capital call notice; Val expects "fireworks" from the Muzzos and Doug, though Louis noted they're probably in the weakest position of anyone ("you can't take blood from a rock").

Possible help Louis offered: he knows the Mississaugas of the Credit well and says three more casinos are coming to the Niagara area (not yet public; years away, but Canadian buyers might prefer Canadian product). Also, the son of Andre Kapinski (who did the license for the first Niagara casino) has something similar to JVL's transactional card going — Louis will find out more; a merger could be at hand.

Tone: Louis was empathetic and respectful of the courage it takes to make these calls; he offered phone calls and introductions wherever they might help.

## Decisions Made
