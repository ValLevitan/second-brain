## Summary

Held in Russian (a few English words at the start). Speech recognition is poor in many places. Some sentences are unclear. Genadi shared a SolidWorks viewer drawing of the street machine cabinet. He could not move the model because it was a viewer only.

**Service access is the main problem.** Genadi said there is no good access to the electronics. The part must slide out very far, and there is no room for it. Val agreed. He said the hardest part is not the depth. It is how to open the panel and where the board sits. The goal is service without putting your head inside the machine.

**Val's idea: panel slides down, door folds down.**
- The play panel is a separate part on rails on both sides. It slides down vertically, all the way to the floor.
- The vertical front door is a separate part. When the panel is down, the door folds down 90 degrees and is flat.
- All the electronics (the board) lie flat on the door, like a shelf, under a small metal cover. Not in a sandwich.
- The door can be **stamped metal**. Val also said it could be plastic.
- Then there is nothing at the back of the machine.
- Genadi said he understood and liked the idea. He also asked why a door is needed at all.

**Dashboard stays fixed.** Genadi suggested the dashboard could slide out with the electronics. Val said no. The dashboard must not move. It is removed only once in a while, for install or repair. It must be screwed or closed. It must not work like a drawer.

**Val's final layout: deeper cabinet.** Val said: keep the cabinet deep enough for the printer. He said the bill acceptor must support **TITO**. So back-load is out. Only **front-load** is left. The **port, printer and front-load validator go on the dashboard**. The **printer on the left, bill acceptor on the right**. Speakers go under the monitor.

**Printer options talked about and dropped.**
- 90-degree ticket printers: Genadi said only Nanoptix made them. Nobody else does. He does not know if the product still exists.
- Printer at 45 degrees with only the exit nose: Val said this is not a good solution (paper may slide or fall).
- A simple ticket printer that is not TITO: then the ticket cannot be used as TITO.
- Genadi raised a printer in the middle, between the legs. Val stopped this. He said the printer location is a proven model and not worth more time.

**Play panel: buttons or video panel.**
- Genadi: a panel with touchscreen is about **$100** for about 30 cm length. A panel for about 21.5 inches length is about **$180**, because they take the whole panel. Val said $180 is too expensive.
- Buttons: Genadi said Taiwan-made ones are about $6–7 each. Better ones cost more. Val: 8 buttons is about $50, plus about $10 harness, so about **$60**.
- Val: buttons cost us about $60 with harness, video costs us about **$120**, so most likely we will go with video. This is not final.
- Genadi said the supplier is on a festival now. He will talk to them again.

**Cabinet material.**
- Plastic is out. Genadi said a mold this size is about 250,000. Val said shipping containers of plastic is very hard.
- Val prefers **plywood**. Metal is possible. Val said the block design Genadi drew fully satisfies him.
- Val: the street machine must be easy to make in large runs anywhere. Every place (Puerto Rico, Texas, Georgia) has a small kitchen-cabinet maker who can cut the boards.
- Val also mentioned splitting the machine in two: a separate pedestal and the machine.
- Stability: Genadi said a separate leg can be added. A small back shield may be needed if the machine is not against a wall. Val suggested a metal plate like on FlexPay.

**Shallow vs deep.** Genadi said a shallow cabinet has a big advantage: half the size for shipping from China and for warehouse space. Val kept the deeper cabinet.

**Disagreement on time.** Genadi said layout is the most important thing, and he needs to look at several options. Val said he has no time for philosophy and the effort is not worth it. At the end Genadi said he has no more questions and will work. Genadi said he cannot present the design on Monday. Val said they will talk on Monday anyway.

## Decisions Made

- **Deeper cabinet, deep enough for the printer.** — Service and printer fit matter more than depth. (Decided by Val Levitan)
- **Bill acceptor must support TITO, so front-load only; back-load is out.** — TITO is required. (Decided by Val Levitan)
- **Port, printer and front-load validator go on a fixed dashboard: printer left, bill acceptor right.** — Proven layout. (Decided by Val Levitan)
- **The dashboard does not move and is not a drawer.** — It is removed only for install or repair. (Decided by Val Levitan)
- **Plastic cabinet is out.** — Mold cost and shipping. Val prefers plywood. (Decided by Val Levitan, Genadi Peari agreed plastic is hard)
- Considered and superseded: dashboard sliding out with the electronics (Genadi) — replaced by fixed dashboard with slide-down panel and fold-down door.
- Considered and superseded: printer in the middle between the legs (Genadi) — replaced by printer on the dashboard, left side.
- Considered and dropped: 45-degree printer; non-TITO ticket printer.
