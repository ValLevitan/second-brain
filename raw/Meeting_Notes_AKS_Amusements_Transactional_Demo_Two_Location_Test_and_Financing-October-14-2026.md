## Summary

**Why JVL net win fell.** Val explained. About 18 months ago net win started to drop. JVL first copied a competitor's simpler skill feature; revenue did not rise. JVL then released about four new game packs; still no rise. Then JVL found that **the Linux system and the AMD processor did not work together and froze the screen**: the attendant saw the game on, the player saw a dead machine, until the nightly reset. That was fixed. The next game release **set RTP to 95% by mistake**, against 90–92% at competitors, so games had to play almost twice as long for the same net win. That is fixed too. Val is bullish now.

**Upgrade for the two AKS machines.** Felipe said the two installed cabinets will get **build 4333** and the new games: **Lion's Riches** (from Peach One, now vertical) and **Twin Bison** (new, Bison Ways style, 3×3). The 15-game cabinet shows 10 games at a time; the rest stand by. AKS mentioned a topper (about $2,800, better effect); Val said to go for it.

**Modem and data.** New machines carry a cellular modem with a data plan that JVL pays for. JVL sees which games are popular and replaces weak ones in the next version. By law the link is one-way: JVL collects data and cannot control the machine. AKS can get the reports free for a period; they can opt out.

**Transactional demo (Andrei).** A family of devices:
- **POS** at the cashier, with a personal PIN for each cashier.
- The player gets a **ticket that is a temporary e-wallet**; the Flexport on each machine scans it to load or collect credits. One ticket for the whole session; it can be kept and used the next day. **Valid up to 30 days** (a location setting).
- At redeem time the cashier can split the balance: some to an **InComm card**, some to a lottery ticket, the rest kept on the e-wallet. A card cannot go back to a ticket; InComm blocks that today.
- **Backend**: a master sees all its locations, a location sees only its own; reports, device health, per-game data through the second SAS port.
- **Self-service slim kiosk**: does what the POS does, with a card dispenser; fits between machines. JVL suggests keeping both POS and kiosk. One button prints a report for an inspector.
- Lost ticket: history and reprint through the POS, or by PIN.
- **Install**: brackets pre-made for each cabinet model; about **10 minutes per machine**, a location in a couple of hours. Works on any machine with the second SAS port (SAS 6.0+). Single-SAS machines can use pulse through the bill acceptor, but lose data.
- Untested cabinets: JVL borrows one for 1–3 days and tests it first. **JVL installs the first location itself.**
- A Wi-Fi version (JVL's own network) will come later; Ethernet stays as backup.
- **Production version:** features finalized this Friday (Oct 16); final enclosures next week or the week after. Remote software updates are already in.

**What AKS uses today.** POS, the IFS kiosk (through EasyLink) in a handful of locations, and Fiery Pay. "Mixed bag" — reliability problems with all of them. AKS now tests at one location before rolling out.

**The offer (Felipe).** For each location with a JVL cabinet: **one FlexPay or Slim, one Flex-IQ and six Flexports, at no cost**, for a limited time (post-show promotion). **Service fee $90 per location per month** (support, updates). Bought separately, an average set (6 ports + IQ + Pay, printer included) is **roughly $15,000**. Val: take the equipment even for locations without JVL machines.

**Trade-in.** AKS has IGT COAM machines. Val offered to buy them for the Texas market, where a local operator converts them with a JVL board; Texas buyers pay about $1,000. An AKS speaker said $2,000. Val: tell me what you want.

**Price increase and financing.** Felipe reminded AKS of the price increase from January 1 (tariffs) and asked how many games they can take before year end. Financing: up to 12 months, after a credit check, or a third-party option. AKS wants 12 months same as cash, noting they have about 50 JVL machines bought at $22–25K. Val: the free equipment is like a 50% discount, so it is "either a discount or financing"; but for a credible company he sees no problem with 12 months.

**Next step.** AKS wants the full system in **two new locations** to get 30+ days of data before year end, and it must work well first time. Val clarified it would be two machines plus two machines, plus transactional for four. **AKS will try to answer by tomorrow with two good locations.** Felipe asked for the cabinet models at those locations as soon as possible, for the brackets.

**Close.** Val: JVL is committed to Georgia — buying this building and settling here — and will keep developing the product with the market.
