02-09 Strategic Partnership Meeting: JVL and Diamond Amusement Market Performance Review-Summary

Strategic Partnership Meeting: JVL and Diamond Amusement Market Performance Review

This executive meeting between gaming machine manufacturer JVL and operator Diamond Amusement focused on reviewing JVL's product performance in the Georgia market, analyzing competitive pressures from major manufacturers, and establishing a data-sharing partnership to improve future game development and market positioning. JVL sought detailed performance feedback and agreed to place trial machines across multiple location tiers in exchange for granular operational data that would inform their next generation of products.

Introductions and Meeting Context

The meeting convened as an executive session to review field performance, leadership initiatives, support priorities, and strategic next steps for JVL in the Georgia gaming market. From JVL, participants included Val Leviton (President), Juan Martinez (VP of Product), Michael LoPleau (Game Producer), and Felipe (Juan Martinez). Diamond Amusement was represented by Sean Fellows, CEO.

Val Leviton brings 42 years of industry experience as second-generation leadership at JVL, which his father founded in 1984. The company launched Cash Code in 1990, becoming deeply involved in the transactional business before selling that division in 2006. Val served six consecutive terms on the board of directors for the Gaming Standards Association. Following the Cash Code sale, he refocused JVL's strategy on transforming from a countertop manufacturer into a full gaming machine manufacturer. Val characterized his role as bringing top-tier talent to the company and ensuring he remains "the dumbest person in the room."

Juan Martinez led the meeting, bringing 17 years of operational experience with Sonoma Hard Rock, where he set up new projects across the US and internationally. Prior to that, he worked on the manufacturer side for IGT, Valley, side games, and Light & Wonder, providing him with comprehensive industry perspective from both operational and manufacturing viewpoints.

Michael Gottlieb has 34 years of game development experience, starting with 15 years in the coin-operated video game business. He spent seven years at Valley, creating Betty Boop's Love Meter, Betty Boop's Fortune Teller, and notably Wonder Woman, one of his studio's most successful games.

Sean Fellows is in his 38th year in the industry, having started in traditional amusements and consistently operated distributed routes. He was instrumental in securing the legislation that established Georgia's current regulatory structure for coin-operated amusement machines and has been involved in state and national industry advocacy since the late 1990s. Sean recalled purchasing early JVL countertops from Bob Whitehead years ago and operating Cash Code equipment, establishing a longstanding business relationship with the company.

Val Leviton acknowledged JVL's track record of delivery with Cash Code, countertops, and the first round of Georgia games, but admitted the company had fallen behind and was committed to regaining momentum in the market.

Georgia Market Analysis and Competitive Benchmarking

Diamond Amusement reported experiencing declining revenues as a terminal operator, attributing the softness primarily to external factors including the government shutdown and unseasonable bad weather over the most recent 90-day cycle. However, Sean acknowledged these declines appeared consistent across the industry as a whole based on their data tracking and five-year seasonality analysis.

A more structural challenge emerged from market saturation. The gross number of coin-operated amusement machines in Georgia has grown significantly year over year, with the number of retail locations offering machines also expanding. The state currently operates over 41,000 machines, up from 36,000-38,000 during the 2021 COVID period. Despite this 14% increase in machine count, the state average win per unit has declined from a COVID-era high of $137 to approximately $108 currently--a 21% decrease.

Sean identified two primary drivers behind the decline: first, the absence of government stimulus money that flooded the market during COVID; and second, genuine market saturation that is beginning to cause revenue dilution. He projected this saturation would lead to market adjustments over the next 18 months, including store closures as weaker operators exit.

An additional trend impacting the competitive landscape involves the entry of larger, more sophisticated manufacturers into the Georgia market. These manufacturers are deploying equipment with notably higher Return to Player (RTP) percentages--in the 94-96% range--compared to legacy equipment that operated in the low-90s range. This shift is changing the player experience by providing more seat time for less money, effectively using higher RTP as a market share acquisition strategy. Sean anticipated these manufacturers would gradually reduce RTPs in subsequent software releases once they established their player base.

Performance Data Comparison

Diamond Amusement provided detailed performance benchmarking across their portfolio of 1,200 units. JVL's 41 deployed units averaged $167 win per unit per day--54% above the state average of $108. However, this performance lagged significantly behind top-tier competitors.

IGT's portfolio told a more nuanced story. Their "Price is Right" title package (109 units deployed) averaged $221 per unit. When including IGT's lower-performing titles, their full portfolio of 182 units averaged $176 per unit--still 5% ahead of JVL.

Aristocrat demonstrated the strongest performance at $305 average across 65 units deployed as of December last year. However, Sean clarified that Diamond Amusement deployed 25% fewer Aristocrat units compared to IGT, and critically, all Aristocrat machines occupied top-tier locations--the highest-performing sites in their portfolio.

Novomatic, another mid-tier manufacturer, performed comparably to JVL with an average of $169 across 30 units.

Sean characterized the competitive landscape as dominated by the "big three"--Aristocrat, IGT, and Light & Wonder--which trade places as top performers depending on location characteristics. The next tier consisted of JVL, Novomatic, and "The Hollywood Game" from Bad Dog. Everything else constituted "filler" pieces or legacy equipment.

Konami's market entry proved unsuccessful and they have reportedly already exited Georgia. Sean attributed this to a combination of insufficient distribution, technical issues, and internal problems with their distributor partner, Bravery Gaming, preventing them from ever having a fair opportunity to establish themselves.

Data Limitations and Strategic Disadvantages

JVL acknowledged a critical disadvantage compared to traditional Class III casino operations: lack of access to granular performance data. In casino environments, robust reporting engines tied to player tracking and accounting systems provide manufacturers with detailed performance metrics to fine-tune games. In Georgia, manufacturers only receive raw data from Intralock reports that cannot necessarily be tied back to specific locations, bet levels, or individual game performance within multi-game cabinets.

Felipe highlighted that historical data quality issues compounded this problem. For example, when a "Mother Goose" unit was misconfigured two years ago, JVL's games misreported data until the setting was corrected, but this erroneous data remained permanently in GLC reports without retroactive cleanup. Without the ability to analyze which of the six games in a cabinet drives performance, or how games perform across different location types, JVL operated with significant informational blind spots compared to their larger competitors.

Analysis of Player Preferences and Winning Game Attributes

The discussion revealed several key factors driving competitor success, particularly around RTP strategy, game mechanics, brand recognition, and content diversity.

Return to Player (RTP) Strategy

JVL's current H3 machines operate at 92% RTP and perform reasonably well. However, newer competitor entries are launching at 94-96% RTP as a deliberate market penetration tactic. Sean explained this strategy makes sense in the Georgia distributed route environment, which differs fundamentally from casino settings. Without the ability to launch promotional campaigns or create special incentive programs around new game banks, manufacturers use higher RTP to attract players to unfamiliar games and create new experiences.

Sean emphasized that 92% RTP alone doesn't provide a complete picture of a game's appeal or profitability. The volatility profile matters significantly--not all locations are identical. Some prefer higher volatility with higher win potential even at 92% RTP, while others want lower volatility that provides more seat time and a grinding play pattern. Market analysis needs to identify which software sets perform better in higher bet volume locations versus lower-volume environments.

When asked whether JVL should consider launching new cabinets at higher RTP ranges with an agreed-upon reduction schedule, Sean didn't object, noting it could be a viable tactic. However, he stressed the importance of matching volatility profiles to location types. Felipe suggested structuring RTP by bet level, rewarding higher-betting players with better theoretical returns on JVL's seven-button digital deck. Sean saw value in this approach, particularly for high-volatility games where players expect to go 15-20 spins without a win but then receive substantially greater payouts when wins occur.

Game Mechanics and Features

Current market trends strongly favor "overlay skill" elements and persistent state features. Sean described how many manufacturers now incorporate skill-based overlays that trigger after a winning outcome--not necessarily traditional nudge or swap mechanics, but interactive bonuses the player must complete.

The most successful mechanic currently trending across platforms involves "hold and spin" style features, exemplified by games like Fire Link, Coin Lock, and Cash Eruption. These features lock symbols and continue adding coins in a frenzy-style accumulation. Felipe specifically observed during field visits that the "Cash Eruption" game within IGT's "Price is Right" package was consistently the most popular, with players gravitating to it whenever available. This observation directly influenced JVL's decision to introduce their "Diamond Link" game, which employs a similar mechanic but on a larger scale with what they believe is a more attractive presentation than a simple three-reel setup.

Progressive jackpots remain essential. Sean confirmed that consumers definitively chase progressives, and successful game packages typically feature some kind of progressive meter on the top three to five payouts. Only one competitor in the space has experimented with non-progressive titles that offer incremental payouts based solely on bet level, but this approach hasn't gained traction. Players expect and respond to progressive elements.

Brand Recognition and Theme Preferences

Larger manufacturers possess a significant advantage in brand and title recognition that proves challenging for mid-tier companies like JVL to overcome. When Diamond Amusement brought IGT to the Georgia market 12 years ago, titles like Red, White and Blue, Double Diamond, and Cleopatra carried immediate player trust and familiarity. Players didn't face a learning curve figuring out how games worked or what to do--they already knew and trusted these brands.

Theme preferences are evolving. Asian-themed games, which once commanded as much as 40% of the market, have declined to approximately 30% or less. Conversely, cartoon themes are experiencing an uptick. The key principle, as Michael Gottlieb summarized, is that anything encouraging familiarity helps player adoption.

Sean noted that the Georgia distributed route market runs approximately three to five years behind land-based casino trends. The "hold and spin" mechanics and bonusing jackpots currently gaining traction in Georgia likely peaked in traditional casinos years ago, according to recent iBridge reports. This lag creates an opportunity for manufacturers with casino market intelligence to anticipate which proven mechanics will eventually succeed in the distributed route environment.

Cabinet Configuration and Game Mix

Both dual-screen and vertical configurations are common across successful manufacturers. However, cabinet style alone doesn't determine success--the game mix within a multi-game package proves equally critical. Sean emphasized that even within individual establishments, player bases are quite diverse. Successful game packages contain a mixture of game types to appeal to different player preferences.

Specifically, he recommended including both three-reel and five-reel games within a five or six-game set. This diversity allows the same cabinet to serve players seeking different experiences, maximizing the appeal across the varied player base present even in a single location.

Location-Specific Performance Considerations

Michael Gottlieb raised an important economic point about the relationship between machine cost and location tier. Given market softening and capped earning potential due to limited foot traffic, placing extremely expensive machines (metaphorically "Ferraris or Lamborghinis") in mid-tier locations doesn't make financial sense. Each location has a natural earning ceiling based on its player traffic.

Sean confirmed this reality, emphasizing that Georgia represents local convenience gaming, not destination gaming. There's no unlimited foot traffic to tap. If a location has five or six machines, it will only generate a certain amount of revenue regardless of the equipment quality. Adding three more machines to reach nine doesn't proportionally increase revenue or profit--it simply divides the existing player spend across more units.

This dynamic explains why premium new equipment from any manufacturer gets placed in top-tier locations first. Operators want to maximize ROI quickly and assess performance under optimal conditions. If a new machine's earnings don't remain consistent with other devices in that premium location, it gets cycled back out and replaced with a second unit from a proven top performer like Aristocrat, IGT, or Light & Wonder.

Future Strategy: Transactional Systems and Product Roadmap

Georgia Gift Card Regulation

With the July 1st implementation of Georgia's gift card program approaching, Felipe sought Sean's perspective on market readiness and potential enforcement. Sean clarified important details about the regulatory framework and industry preparation.

The requirement for gift card systems is statutory, not voluntary, and will be enforced beginning July 1st. However, contrary to some assumptions, there is no statutory requirement that locations install kiosks. Many retailers are instead incorporating gift card capability through APIs integrated into their existing point-of-sale infrastructure.

InCon reported serving over 4,500 locations with gift cards on a monthly basis, with that number ramping up weekly. The Georgia Lottery Commission is working around the clock to prepare from an enforcement standpoint, and the industry is preparing from an educational perspective.

Mike Harum in the COF division has been sending educational communications every other week leading up to July 1st, reminding retail licensees and master operators of the effective date. These communications are published on the Georgia Lottery website at gacoam.com. The lottery, in coordination with GMOA and certain retail associations, has scheduled three educational seminars around the state--one along the coast, one in central Georgia (likely Macon), and one in the metro Atlanta area--to ensure retailers understand compliance requirements and avoid future citations.

JVL Transactional Product Suite

Building on their extensive Cash Code experience, JVL announced plans to introduce a comprehensive suite of transactional products designed to address most operator needs in the new regulatory environment. The product line will offer operators choice and flexibility rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.

Solutions will include TITO (Ticket-In, Ticket-Out) systems, electronic wallet options, kiosk configurations, and simple point-of-sale devices for card dispensing. JVL emphasized they are "absolutely indifferent" to which solution individual locations or operators prefer--their goal is to provide options that fit varying operational models.

Critically, JVL will establish its own end-to-end merchant relationships and transaction processing rather than relying on third-party processors. The systems will handle information in either TITO format or electronic wallet format, depending on operator preference.

JVL is also considering auxiliary services like accounting information systems and machine service tracking that could benefit master operators. However, they sought guidance on two strategic questions: whether to involve master operators in the rollout or go directly to retail locations, and whether master operators actually want these auxiliary services or would prefer to avoid the complexity.

Sean provided decisive clarity: master operators will want to be involved, and he personally would not want a manufacturer engaging directly with his retail customers. The master operator relationship must be managed through the operator's team. Additionally, since these systems will connect to COAMS (coin-operated amusement machine systems) anyway, master operator involvement is effectively mandatory for proper integration.

However, Sean acknowledged a potential service model where JVL could offer turnkey deployment and management as a service to smaller operators lacking internal operations staff to administer such systems--provided the master operator was engaged from day one and explicitly approved that arrangement.

Technical Compatibility

When Sean asked about hardware requirements, Val confirmed JVL has developed solutions compatible with both dual-SAS and older single-SAS machines. While legacy single-SAS equipment is increasingly phasing out of premium locations, it remains prevalent in middle-tier locations where players prefer familiar equipment. Ensuring backward compatibility extends JVL's addressable market across Diamond Amusement's full portfolio.

Development Timeline

Val acknowledged their projected rollout date had been "half a year ago" and has mysteriously kept rolling forward. However, he expressed confidence that May 2025 represents a realistic timeline, noting they are "very very close" to readiness.

The phased rollout plan includes:

- Immediate: Begin showing products and conducting beta testing in controlled environments
- May 2025: Alpha testing with select operator partners
- End of 2025: Full market rollout

Sean expressed interest in participating as a test partner, contingent on understanding the specific technical requirements and integration architecture. Felipe committed to following up with detailed specifications.

Next-Generation Game Portfolio

Felipe previewed JVL's forthcoming game lineup, designed to compete directly with the successful mechanics and presentation quality of top-tier manufacturers. The new aesthetic employs an "apple-like liquid glass effect" across the entire portfolio, representing a significant visual upgrade.

Key product lines include:

Fiery Ice Link: A persistent state feature game in the "cash eruption" style that proved so successful for competitors. This directly addresses the player preference Felipe observed in field visits.

Jackpot Rush Series: Multi-level progressive games where jackpots are winnable at any bet level, reminiscent of Valley's classic "Quick Hits" mechanic. This addresses the market's demonstrated preference for progressive elements while ensuring accessibility across all bet levels.

Revamped Classic Peak Games: Updated versions of JVL's established titles with modernized presentation and the new liquid glass visual treatment.

Sean responded positively to the preview, noting the layout and configuration appeared simple and intuitive for players. He predicted players would migrate toward these games even if they couldn't articulate why--the combination of familiar mechanics, attractive presentation, and intuitive navigation would drive natural adoption.

Felipe positioned these new releases as exclusive early access for data-sharing partners like Diamond Amusement, creating a quid pro quo arrangement where operators providing performance data receive first access to new, potentially higher-performing content.

Action Plan for a Data-Driven Partnership

JVL formalized their request for a collaborative data-sharing partnership with Diamond Amusement to address their critical information deficit in the Georgia market. The proposal centered on placing JVL machines in strategically selected locations where detailed performance data could be collected and compared against established competitors.

Specific Requirements

Felipe outlined JVL's core need: placement of JVL machines in tier-one locations positioned directly alongside IGT, Light & Wonder, and preferably all three major competitors simultaneously. This head-to-head comparison in premium locations would provide the cleanest performance data, controlling for location quality as a variable.

The data requested included both metrics JVL could collect independently through field visits and proprietary operational data only Diamond Amusement could provide--detailed win per unit figures, game-level performance within multi-game cabinets, player behavior patterns, and time-of-day/day-of-week performance variations.

Value Exchange Proposition

In exchange for this data access and Diamond Amusement's effort in data collection and sharing, JVL offered several compensation elements:

- Free trial machines for testing periods
- Extended warranty options beyond standard terms
- Spare parts kits to minimize downtime
- First access to new game releases (as previewed with the Fiery Ice Link and Jackpot Rush series)
- Negotiable additional terms to ensure Diamond Amusement felt fairly compensated for their partnership

Felipe emphasized that JVL recognized the value of this data and wanted to ensure Diamond Amusement received appropriate compensation for the effort required to collect, organize, and share performance insights.

Diamond Amusement's Commitment

Sean responded positively, immediately identifying that Diamond Amusement currently operates three JVL machines at one of their office locations. He committed to working with JVL to extract and share available data that would help analyze performance patterns.

Additionally, he offered to facilitate field visits in limited capacity, either by taking JVL personnel to locations during Diamond Amusement's regular service visits or otherwise enabling in-person data collection as needed.

When Val emphasized the importance of "multiple test locations" rather than just one, Sean expanded his commitment substantially. He proposed deploying JVL machines across all three location tiers--low, mid, and premium--to collect comprehensive data across the performance spectrum. He offered to provide historical baseline data for each tier, clearly identifying examples such as: "$80 win per unit average location, $140 location, and $500 location."

This three-tier approach would enable JVL to understand not just how their games perform in absolute terms, but how performance scales across location quality, which volatility and RTP profiles work best in which contexts, and where in Diamond Amusement's portfolio JVL equipment delivers optimal ROI.

Felipe and Val both expressed that this comprehensive, multi-tier testing arrangement was "absolutely terrific" and exactly the partnership structure they needed to make data-driven product development decisions.

Next Steps and Ownership

Felipe confirmed that Nico would follow up with Sean on next steps, establishing a clear point of contact for operationalizing the partnership. This would include defining specific parameters for the test deployment, establishing data reporting formats and frequencies, finalizing the compensation package for Diamond Amusement, and setting timeline expectations for the test period.
