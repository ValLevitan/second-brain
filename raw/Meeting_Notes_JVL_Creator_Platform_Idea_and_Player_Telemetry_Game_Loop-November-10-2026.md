## Summary

**Anton's idea: a creator platform.** Anton said it is an idea, not a ready product. It came from notes he kept while working on NOVA 2. JVL has a large library of certified math and games from about 45 years. Put it in a library others can reuse for reskins: keep the certified math and framework, change only the art, so GLI should not charge much. Flow: a developer picks a model and describes the look, an AI agent generates the visuals over a few rounds, the platform runs tests, the developer submits the reskin for certification, then the game ships. He compared it with Stake Engine (needs its own math and an SDK, more engineering). He said his numbers were made up. He mainly wanted to build it as an engineering project, and even if it has no business value he wants to bring some ideas (agentic development, agent-to-user interaction) into the NOVA 2 studios.

**Val's answer.** Val thanked Anton. JVL is not in the online market and Val never made money there, so this does not interest him. In other hands it could work, but others would build it faster.

**Where Val is going instead.** With JVL's own module now on machines and the move from casino to street business (game rooms, bars), Val wants to send games straight to the machine. The loop: machines on location collect many "probes" — which buttons players use, which features they like, maybe even where their eyes look on screen, plus other math or physical methods. Data goes to JVL, a new game or a reskin or an extra mechanic is made automatically, sent to GLI, approved, and sent to the machine with minimal human work. GLI step: tell GLI what changed, GLI approves for that sector, JVL gets the approval with checksums, the machine reports its checksums, they are matched against the approval, and the game is enabled — all automatic. Igor and Evgeny's mock-ups already go partly in this direction.

**The unsolved part.** Val said making a game and its math is now possible with little cost. The hard problem is how to judge what players like: which statistics to take and what they mean. Example: 2 of 8 games are popular — is it the mechanic, the theme, or the math? Val believes no competitor can do this yet, because it needs the module on the machine and fast game making, so JVL is moving the right way.

Side note: Anton has a JVL machine with arcade games (countertop) in the bar under his home, installed about a week ago.
