## Summary

Andrei called the meeting to align on what JVL will present at the Georgia expo in three weeks (August 18). The list settled on four transactional products: Flex IQ, Recycler, Dispenser, and Flex Pay.

**Flex IQ.** Present it in its connected version — machines wired together through Flexports with a Mother Goose, showing how cards are issued using test cards that do not look like real ones. The point is to show the workflow. Two games are enough for the IQ demo.

**Payment hub dashboard.** Andrei walked through his dashboard mockup: an operational interface where an operator starts the day. It shows location setup, IQ status (online/offline, software version), a money-flow overview by period, transaction detail, top locations by performance and share, top gaming machines, fleet state (how many IQ units online vs offline), and a location map for planning site visits. Val was initially hesitant about leaning on reporting — worried it could look skimpy if there isn't much to show — but after seeing the mockup he agreed it is impressive and worth a proper screen. Juan had already seen it and rated it a marketing advantage over a PAX device. Decisions: show only the "future" extended version (on Mother Goose), do not show two dashboard versions, and do not display a live or fake transaction in the reports (the booth is not an authorized location).

**SMIB version.** Andrei offered to build a SMIB (non-Mother-Goose) mockup that could also show money-in, not just money-out. Juan said to sell IQ first and offer SMIB as a later upgrade. One step at a time.

**Recycler demo flow.** Val laid out the use case: insert $20, print a QR-code ticket, play across machines that have Flexport (traverse — Georgia House Bill 353 calls this "replay"), create a win, then for the demo issue a Skill Pay card via IQ, present it to the recycler, and the recycler dispenses cash. The proper September use case has the Skill Pay card issued automatically from the recycler rather than IQ. The Skill Pay card (coming from InComm around September) is ATM-ready and convertible to cash; the recycler acts as an ATM. For the show they will fake the conversion to Skill Pay (subject to InComm's permission) using a hand-painted/laminated distinct (e.g. green) card. The recycler currently has no card dispenser, so IQ would need QR-reading added for the demo. Val said the recycler's mechanical design must evolve to house an optional QR printer plus a card dispenser. On fees, Val wants to stay silent at the show and set them once the market is understood.

**Dispenser.** Shown for other markets (Texas, Nebraska), not Georgia — a springboard into those markets. Flow: start a session on the dispenser, get an e-wallet and QR ticket, traverse machines, return to the dispenser, scan, and get cash. Recycler will not read fake bills (real bill acceptor); the dispenser might, so Andrei will ask Roberto how they demo it.

**Flex Pay.** Already shown at multiple shows, dispenses cards, works with Flexport. No concerns.

**Booth and staffing.** Felipe worried about space with recycler, dispenser, and flex pay all present; Val prefers a larger booth (~20x30) over fewer machines, and Felipe will check with organizers. Felipe will also check the size of a large screen received from Canada. Show staff: Andrei, Felipe, and Sebastian. Sebastian is returning from Colombia; Juan will coordinate his travel so he attends on the 18th (Val considers his presence important since he will handle service and this is a chance to meet operators).

**First IQ beta operator.** Val pressed on who the beta operator is. Felipe said AKS is the best shot — good relationship, close to the office, tolerant location and operator. Val said to close the deal and set up the IQ ASAP as the first priority. Felipe is speaking with them Monday to close and secure/register the location. Andrei received InComm's reply about the test location and will proceed.
