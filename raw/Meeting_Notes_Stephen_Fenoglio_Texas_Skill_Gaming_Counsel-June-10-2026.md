## Summary

A cold introductory call that turned into the clearest legal briefing JVL has on the Texas market, and the source of two operator introductions a week later. Felipe put Val onto Fenoglio; Val opened with *"I don't know maybe we can help each other"* and by the end had an open invitation to Austin.

**Who Fenoglio is.** Thirty-one years in gaming, licensed 41 years, and outside counsel to **AMOT** — the amusement and music operators' trade association in Texas — since about 1995–96. He works with the lobbying team on legislation nearly every session but is not a registered lobbyist; he calls what he does *"near lobbying"*: drafting bills and amendments, crafting arguments, negotiating language. Two different Texas district courts have designated him an expert witness in gaming law. He also handles some oil, gas and utility matters. His own summary: *"I guess I am considered one of the experts on gaming law in Texas."*

**The legal test, which is the whole point.** Texas is a **pure skill** state, not a predominant-skill state — and Fenoglio was emphatic that this distinction is the entire game. The standard he can send an opinion on: **if the skillful player wins, each and every time, more than it costs to play, it is not a gambling device.** Conversely, *"if it's ninety nine percent skill, but one percent chance in determining the outcome... that would be considered an illegal gambling device."* Nebraska, which Val offered as JVL's closest analogue, is not the same test — Fenoglio knows the Nebraska framework and drew the line himself.

The practical layer on top of that is enforcement blindness. Police and prosecutors cannot tell the difference between an old eight-liner, a pure-skill game and a Class III slot — *"they all look the same"* — which, in his words, *"creates opportunity and chaos."*

**Pace-O-Matic is the benchmark, and it is also the conflict.** Fenoglio named Pace-O-Matic as the manufacturer that has actually built a 100% skill game and proved it. He is one of roughly fifteen outside counsel for Pace. They won in state district court and the court of appeals upheld it, on exactly the skillful-player ground. The case came out of **Fannin County**, where police seized about twenty games and forty to fifty thousand dollars in cash countywide; Pace told the locations and operators to stand aside, funded a full defence with lawyers and two expert witnesses, and tried the case in a single day. The judge held the games were not gambling devices and ordered everything returned. The state appealed and lost, producing **binding precedent across nineteen northeast Texas counties** and persuasive authority statewide. Fenoglio also mentioned a state agency opinion issued that same day addressing the question.

The consequence for JVL is a hard limit on what Fenoglio can do: because he represents Pace, **he cannot give a binding written legal opinion to another manufacturer.** He will look at a game on an operator's behalf and give a verbal observation — *"I think these features are good and I would definitely not have this feature"* — but nothing in writing. Val's route around it, which Fenoglio accepted, is to come to him through an operator: *"I don't mind meeting with you and will give you the same opinion I would give the operator, but I am not going to put anything in writing."*

**Market shape and the two business models.** Fenoglio estimates **at least 200,000 games in Texas claiming to be skill**, possibly more, on the back of other manufacturers asserting their machines are "virtually identical" to Pace's. Val's reaction: *"Texas is not a state; it's a country."* On models: **Banilla** has more games out in Texas than Pace, but sells the game to the operator and the story ends there. **Pace does not sell** — it operates through a series of operators and provides full legal defence. Fenoglio's implication for JVL was direct: as a manufacturer that sells, JVL would be in Banilla's position, not Pace's.

**The county patchwork.** Texas has 254 counties, and enforcement posture varies by individual official — a DA who will not touch it, a county attorney who will, a chief of police who thinks everything is illegal, a sheriff who understands the distinction. *"So you have a patchwork; there are certain counties where you should not operate, unless you want a criminal case."* Because the state is so large, his advice to operators and to Pace is simply to avoid those counties — there is *"plenty of fertile ground"* elsewhere, and no reason to spend fifty to a hundred thousand dollars defending a criminal case. Some clients take the advice and some do not. He was equally clear that there are genuinely good, multi-generational operators who will not take unnecessary risk, and others who *"will just operate as long as we can"* and rely on him if they get caught — his answer to those being *"just make sure you got plenty of money."*

**What Val was after and what he got.** Val introduced JVL as a well-established, privately owned manufacturer in business 42 years, covering amusement through Class III, licensed in a number of US states. The Texas state trade show (AMOT, early June) had been *"just a fishing expedition"*, and Val's honest position was that JVL has a product that looks right for Texas but *"we don't even know any operators in Texas, so we're not even sure how to proceed."* Fenoglio offered to put him in touch with **the president of AMOT** and with good operators he represents, and suggested Val eat with those people as well as with him. Val offered to fly down for a steak and a glass of wine; Fenoglio had a calendar open, and there are nonstop flights from both Toronto and Miami into Austin (Southwest from Miami — Fenoglio looked it up during the call). The constraint: Fenoglio takes a **summer sabbatical from July 8 to the end of September**, out of Austin and in the mountains, so any Austin visit had to happen before July 8.

The introductions came through a week later — on June 17 Fenoglio group-emailed Val to **Jordan Barberio** (Barberio Amusement, San Antonio area) and **Joe Hello** (Capitol Amusement, Austin), with mobile numbers, which produced a scheduled discovery call with Capitol on June 24.

## Decisions Made

- **JVL will approach Fenoglio through an operator, not directly as a manufacturer.** He has a conflict from representing Pace-O-Matic and will not put anything in writing for JVL; the verbal read is available either way.
- **Texas is a pure-skill jurisdiction and JVL's Nebraska product cannot be assumed to qualify.** The test is that the skillful player wins more than the cost of play every time; 1% chance in the outcome is fatal.
- **County-level risk selection is the operating model in Texas**, not statewide compliance.
