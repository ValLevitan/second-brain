## Summary

**Why Christos brought Louis.** Louis's father contacted Christos on LinkedIn years ago; their company has repaired equipment for Intralot for 10–15 years. Christos wants a group of partners to run routes for lotteries: masters, a repair depot and a manufacturer. **JVL is the first manufacturer he has approached.**

**Christos's route plan.** He hopes to land **two route projects in 2027** out of about ten ideas. Most realistic: **Wyoming, Idaho and Nebraska**. He said route capex pays back in about 2–2.5 years, against about 4 years for a lottery system, and thinks Bally's would fund it. Ideas per state:
- **Nebraska:** a Georgia master as the operating partner. Intralot already runs the system there.
- **Idaho:** strong security, because the market is more grey.
- **Wyoming:** Intralot's own route general manager (Vlad). Intralot sells lottery tickets in about 500 stores; perhaps 150–200 suit games (bars), about 3 machines each — **about 600 machines**.
Val said he is very interested in the operation side, and that **he wants to be partners with Intralot itself**, not only bring revenue to its central system. He named Sean as a strong operator.

**Timeline.** Christos is presenting a 2027–2028 plan to Intralot's new lottery division president and new group CEO (starting January). **JVL is already in that presentation as a potential partner.** Next: a meeting with people above him (for example the US CEO) within about two months, one level up each month; **3–4 months to finalize**. Nothing is signed.

**Louis's repair depot.** Depot repair only, no field service: advance exchange, light configuration, some inventory and logistics. North American repair depot for **Aristocrat and Light & Wonder**; also Intralot and Scientific Games. **40,000 sq ft in Gwinnett** (Oak Grove Parkway, off Jimmy Carter), 60 staff (about 30 technicians), looking at space across the street (60,000 sq ft) and Nevada in 2028. Revenue coming up to about $8M; he has tripled the company since the pandemic. Moving to NetSuite. Repairs printers and bill acceptors (JCM, MEI, TransAct): **flat rate about $150–300 per unit, new parts, 90-day warranty, no charge if it cannot be repaired.** Val said a Montreal company charges about CAD 120 (about US $80). Most volume is now surveillance cameras (about 20,000 units a month), including Israeli companies.

**JVL background (Val).** Val told how he moved JVL from Canada to Georgia in three months when tariffs came, the Lawrenceville building (10,000 sq ft, now being bought; office or assembly undecided), parts mostly from China and South Korea, power supplies now US, cabinets starting in the US. For the street market JVL plans machines with only the AFT (Flexport) — no printer or bill acceptor inside — plus its own POS and slim self-checkout. He also told how JVL got into Georgia through Lucky Bucks during COVID.

**InComm, the ATM card and cash.** Val said InComm's John asked him two years ago to build a cash-dispensing unit. After JVL showed it at the August show, InComm called to say JVL could not have it. Val said he warned InComm of a large lawsuit: if InComm issues an ATM-type card, it cannot tell him not to build ATMs. **InComm's ATM card (done with Bulldog) has been stopped; as of today InComm took the card out**, and an announcement was expected the next day. Christos said the lottery's new proposed rules will not allow ATM cards because they are too close to cash. Both said gift cards feed a black market (stores buy them back at a discount).

**Christos's advice.** Build a kiosk the lottery would want to promote (for example, show lottery products on the big screen); aim for one recognizable redemption terminal in Georgia. Val's idea: **promo credits that go toward a lottery ticket**. Christos would rather the money go into more games than into inducements. He urged masters to attend the lottery's advisory board meetings and stand together.

**Other topics.** Christos's longer vision: lottery-branded route machines, later Bally's-branded, linked to Bally's casinos and i-gaming through loyalty. Intralot loses its Ohio contracts (casino side already; lottery side to Scientific Games, going live around March–June 2027 — he put the EBITDA loss at $25–30M) and will bid there again. Texas Lottery has asked Intralot about Georgia's system. Interest in Puerto Rico and the Dominican Republic. Through GAMOA meetings the lottery **raised the win limits** that needed approval above $10,000; a request for linked jackpots was refused.
