**Date:** March 10, 2026  
**Author:** Val Levitan, JVL Gaming

## The Problem We Are Trying to Solve

You are running an engineering organization where you are paying for work, but you cannot see the work happening. You only find out what got done when someone decides to tell you — and by then, it's too late to course-correct.

This is not a technology problem. It is a **management visibility problem.**

Right now, developers work largely inside a black box. A task gets assigned, and sometime later — days, sometimes weeks — you get a result. In between, there is no reliable signal of progress. You don't know if the work started. You don't know if the developer is stuck. You don't know if they're working on three other things simultaneously. You don't know if the task was written clearly enough for anyone to execute it. You only know something is wrong when it's already late.

Compare this to almost any other part of the business. In sales, you see pipeline movement. In operations, you see inventory. In finance, you see cash flow. These numbers don't tell you everything — but they give you a basis for an intelligent conversation. Engineering, right now, gives you almost nothing equivalent.

The deeper issue is **cultural, not technical.** People have learned — not out of malice, but out of habit — that keeping their work private protects them from hard questions. Hard questions take time to answer. They expose gaps in planning, gaps in skill, and gaps in how tasks were written. Most people instinctively avoid that. So work stays invisible as long as possible, and leadership is left managing by feel rather than by fact.

What we want to build is a company where **visibility is normal, expected, and non-threatening.** Where a developer committing code early — even imperfect code — is the professional standard, not the exception. Where anyone in the company, at any level, can look at the same dashboard and ask the same intelligent questions: Why does this have 464 open tasks? Why has this Story been in progress for 8 days? Why did this take 20 iterations? Those questions are not attacks. They are the foundation of a healthy, improving organization.

Companies that build a culture where honest questions are welcomed, and honest data is available, grow exponentially. Those that don't end up managing by whoever shouts the loudest.

**In short: we are trying to build the infrastructure for accountability — not to punish people, but to create the conditions where the company can actually learn, improve, and make good decisions with real information.**
