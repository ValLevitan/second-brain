## Summary

A short phone call, two days after Halcyon's first order. Yash opened with a warning about JVL's product, then gave Val the most useful Texas market numbers JVL has on record, and ended by proposing they meet Thursday.

**The warning — software does not sell in Texas.** Yash's brother had been in Texas the previous week. His read, which he had already given Felipe: the retailers out there are not sophisticated. They do not understand that the software matters. What they want is a big cabinet with a big screen. Halcyon is still happy to buy, but Yash did not want Val disappointed after shipping. Val's answer was that he preferred hearing it up front, and both men made a point of valuing the straight talk.

**Texas net win — $250 per device per day.** Yash pulled the morning's report during the call: Halcyon's live net win averages about **$250 per device per day**. Val checked it twice — per machine, per day, net — and put it against Georgia at roughly **$100–114**. So Texas is running about **2.5× Georgia**. Yash qualified it honestly: he culls bad locations rather than tolerate them, so his own number may run high, and Val should plan on **at least $200 a day per device**.

**Legislative risk — real but not imminent.** Texas legislates every second year, so there are long gaps. Last session's push to ban skill games failed. The Ken Paxton opinion letter is the live problem: the state itself will not act because an opinion is not a law, but **local police and sheriffs can and do enforce it**. Yash gave a worked example outside San Antonio where the department settled it at roughly **$50 per device** — a slap on the wrist. He does not expect widespread enforcement, and his reasoning is the pattern in Virginia, Pennsylvania and Missouri: states call the games illegal, then reintroduce bills asking how the state makes money from them. Games have been in Texas since the 1970s; he expects tighter rules rather than removal.

**The political read.** Yash does not expect the current Republican wave to hold, and notes that historically Republicans have been better for gaming while nobody knows what the Democrats would do. The Sands family is spending heavily to get a casino licensed — they bought the San Antonio basketball team — which raises the pressure. Yash is working two channels: **Dennis Bonnen**, a former Senate leader and a personal friend, and **Jordan Barberio, the AMOT president**. Both have lobbyists engaged. The answer comes next summer.

**Cabinet spec.** Vertical, **43" or 47"**, with 47" preferred.

**His actual ask — a unified industry front.** This was the point of the call. Texas is a good market, a lot of operators from other states are moving in, everyone assumes they know everyone, and the effort is fragmented. Yash wants the industry pushing in one direction with a single message. He proposed meeting Thursday when Val is in town — Lawrenceville or anywhere — to go further on it. Val agreed and asked for eggs and coffee, telling Yash to pick the spot.

## Decisions

- **Texas planning number for JVL: at least $200/device/day net win**, with $250 the figure Halcyon actually reports. Georgia baseline ~$100–114.
- **Texas cabinets: vertical, 47" preferred over 43".**
- **Meet Thursday (Sept 17)** to work on a coordinated industry position for Texas — Yash picks the venue.
