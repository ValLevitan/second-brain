## Summary

**Association (GAMOA) intel.** Ryan sits on the board (12 elected members, 3-year terms; Robert Berry is the manufacturer representative — sells parts, no machine business; a new manufacturer rep nomination is being voted now). Budget: 50/50 dues and the annual show; all volunteer except one salaried admin (Christina). Political champion: Representative Alan Powell (chair of the oversight committee); lobbyist Joe Butler (Butler Avenue); law firms Les Snyder and Chris & Milowitz. The association does not donate to campaigns directly — members pool contributions per event. Ryan will have Christina send Val the board-member summary and show/association info personally.

**Operator profile.** Small operator, deliberately volatility-driven: had ~25 locations last year, sold a batch to a partner, now 12, targeting 20 in 6–8 months. Five or six locations produce 60–70% of revenue. High-roller locations (notably Vietnamese cafes) run $1 minimum / $10 max bets with jackpots set or reset at $12,000; his recurring ask is **adjustable jackpot reset values** — resetting to $2,400 after a $10K hit kills the chase; he wants resets around $8,000. His two largest locations are partner-run, so he cannot test new equipment there; new locations get 6 machines first, then 8–9 ("baby steps"). Brands: Light & Wonder top, JVLs the level below, Bad Dog liked. Kiosks: uses IFS; TITO via EasyLink ("easy win").

**Val's pitch.** The triple-progressive vertical (bet-dependent, symbol-driven link, time-on-device) as the high-roller machine — bet-scaled progressive displays as social signaling; class-3 pedigree (Bally lineage, 15–25% of casino floors). Val's condition for beating L&W/Aristocrat: equal RTP settings, and location-specific tuning via the new modems (real-time stats + remote tweaks; remote initial setup). Val offered a free Diamond-Link-to-new-machine swap test in an established high-roller location — blocked by partner control, but Ryan will consider his upcoming new location and asked whether old cabinets can take the new software (yes).

**Modular cashless demo (Andrei).** InComm card activation by barcode; Flex port on MudaGoose for cross-machine replay; recycler as closed-loop licensed ATM (buy-ins fund payouts, lockable cassettes, no refill); ATM card redemption coming Sept 15 (non-reloadable). "Lego bricks" positioning; ~$1,000 full unit, couple-hundred-dollar slim versions; SAS-2 connection; promo credits possible via SAS promo meter (not advertised to legislators). Val's overarching frame: JVL is technology- and carrier-agnostic (ticket, QR, card, phone), player is king, and multi-provider validation manipulation is coming (locations playing processors against each other — already happening to InComm with PAX/Fireplay). Ryan's stated preference: skip physical cards entirely, go digital reloadable wallets; for now he passes on equipment but wants numbers once his new location lands, and flagged the ATM piece plus Class A as the two things the industry needs settled. On Class A, Ryan is personally opposed (loophole, threatens the $200M/year lottery funding and Hope Scholarship, gambling-hall backlash risk) — a useful contrast to ARC's position.

## Decisions Made

- No purchase this visit; Ryan passes for now, revisits when his new location opens ("I always want to stay abreast").
- Val's swap-test offer stands for an established high-roller location when Ryan controls one.
