## Summary

The conversation centered on a tactical plan to seed JVL machines into the field ahead of the August 15 Georgia gaming show, plus secondary discussion on show booth logistics and Texas market readiness.

**July 1 free-machine rollout — the core proposal.** Val opened by visualizing what happens "if we give you a product on July 1st," clarifying he meant the exact machine he had previously shown Felipe via Teams chat. The structure: a set of free, complete machines deployed to operators who keep all the revenue, with the locations chosen by JVL (not the operator). The trial period would run 3–6 months, after which the operator buys. Juan added that included in the deal would be a no-charge upgrade to the final software, which is targeted to be ready in August. Val responded that JVL should institutionalize this as a policy — a software warranty bundled with machine purchase, typically running 6–12 months from purchase date for upgrade rights. Felipe initially misheard "forever" instead of the actual 6–12 month window, which Val and Juan clarified.

**Operator targeting and the "good location" problem.** Juan asked whether Felipe could find operators willing to take incremental machines in July given the gift-card-deadline rush. Felipe's read: June and July will be chaotic because operators wait until the last minute to install gift-card solutions before the regulatory deadline; technicians will be overcommitted at companies like United (which has its own technicians) and at the third-party service companies that smaller-to-medium masters rely on. Operators like Ultra, who already have a gift-card solution, are not in scramble mode. Felipe believed he could still find 5 locations even in this window. Juan pushed for more — at least 10–15 — for valuable data.

Val reframed the conversation. The right question is not "how many machines" but "how many top-tier locations can we get into." JVL cannot afford to be placed in average locations because that will produce misleading data and damage the product story. Val's heuristic: any 50-location operator has perhaps 5 strong locations (top 10%) and one exceptional one — the rest "fall off a cliff." So the real metric is the count of strong relationships Felipe has where he can dictate placement into top locations.

Felipe cited Allenson/Malik as a model: Felipe asked Malik not even to remove the Flex V-43 already in place but to put JVL into Malik's best location, with software upgraded (older revision, not the dash-A version, but no freezing reports). After about two months, Felipe checked and the location was averaging $600–700/day in hold (up from ~$320 average), strong numbers. Felipe pushed Malik on Friday to send the comparative location data; Malik promised but had not delivered as of the meeting. Felipe estimated he can credibly push 5–8 masters into placing machines in their top locations. Val pressed further: if Felipe builds a list of 10 mid-size operators (50+ locations each) where he has strong relationships, that yields roughly 20 candidate top locations, of which 10 machines could be placed in July with at least 8 different operators. Felipe agreed this is achievable; Juan agreed with the math. Val also raised that Felipe's relationships with the technicians servicing these operators may be as strong or stronger than with the decision-makers, and a small kicker to technicians could grease installations.

**The "swap" maneuver for locations already running JVL machines.** Felipe initially struggled to follow Val's logic when Val said an operator with a JVL V43 already in their best location should still take a free machine. Val spelled it out: the operator moves their owned V43 to their second-best location, and JVL's free machine takes the prime spot. "Nothing beats free." For the Allenson case where Felipe already has a Flex V43 in a premium location and the operator does not want two vertical machines stacked there, a software upgrade rather than a swap makes sense — but as a general rule, the incremental free machine should be the primary push, with software upgrade only as a fallback for resistant operators.

**The August 15 show.** Val tied the July rollout to the show: 10 machines deployed July 1 yields 45 days of live performance data going into the Aug 15 show. Felipe confirmed this transforms his pitch — instead of relying on rendered images, he can quote actual revenue from real locations. Val then framed the day's broader conflict: Juan had given the engineering team an "escape" earlier today on the schedule update, and Val wants Juan to push hard tomorrow for a firm schedule commitment because the July 1 plan depends on it.

**Show booth and product mix.** The booth is currently 215 (20x20), $7,500 gross, $1,000 sponsor discount, plus a hanging banner cost — netting ~$9,000. Booth 115 is open at ~$10,000 gross (likely with discount available). Booth 215 cannot expand because the booths behind it are taken. Val noted the show is small ("you walk the whole show in 30 minutes") and effectively a one-day show, so booth size matters less than location, which is good. They parked the upsize question. Val asked why JVL needs more brand exposure — every Georgia manufacturer already knows JVL.

Product mix at the show: V43 plus D27 (Felipe and Val both want D27 retained, possibly with adjusted proportions — e.g., two banks of vertical V43 vs. one bank of D27). Juan had questioned whether D27 is worth showing given operator reactions; Val pushed back. Transactional products: e-wallet, Tito (Juan thought Tito was on the back burner; Felipe was unsure), Tito Flex Pay, non-Tito Flex Pay, broader pay station, recycler, dispenser, POS. Felipe wants a more memorable booth design / giveaway strategy this year — referenced Bad Dog's prior-year cabinet raffle which drew traffic on day two. Layout has historically been U-shaped against the back wall but could be redesigned.

Premium games at the show: in addition to the games already being tested, JVL will show two premium games. Juan added Jackpot Rush and Fiery Ice Link as themes within the family that can serve as fallback if any negative data comes back from the primary themes.

**Texas Maples.** Felipe asked whether he can begin talking to people about the Maples being prepared for Texas. Val said wait a few more days because a new QR code reader is being integrated, but he is confident the games will perform. Once cleared, Felipe should "be singing like a canary" because a discounted machine is an easy sell. If this revision underperforms, JVL will iterate to find one that does.

## Decisions Made

- **Pursue a rollout of approximately 10 free trial machines** to top operator locations, kept by operators 3–6 months with all revenue, then converted to purchase. (Decided by Val, endorsed by Juan and Felipe) — *Target date revised May 5: last week of June 2026 (was July 1).*
- **Primary offer is an incremental machine; software upgrade is the fallback** when an operator's premium location already has a JVL machine and a swap won't work. (Decided by Val and Juan)
- **JVL chooses placement locations**, not the operator — only top 5–10% locations qualify; average locations are off the table because they would produce misleading performance data. (Decided by Val)
- **Software warranty policy:** JVL will treat software upgrades as a warranty for 6–12 months from machine purchase. (Stated by Val as a rule going forward)
- **Booth 215 stays for now** — upsize question parked; show is small enough that the current 20x20 location is acceptable. (Consensus)
- **D27 will be shown** alongside V43 at the August show, with adjusted floor proportions favoring vertical machines. (Decided by Val and Felipe over Juan's question)
- **Hold off on actively pitching the Texas Maples** for a few days pending the new QR code reader integration. (Decided by Val)
- **Each of the 10 trial placements requires a brief written agreement** with the operator confirming schedule and commitment. (Added by Val, May 5 email — see updates)
