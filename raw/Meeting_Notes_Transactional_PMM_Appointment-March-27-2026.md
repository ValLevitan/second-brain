# Meeting Summary — Alignment: Product & Market Manager Role for Transactional Initiative
**Date:** March 27, 2026, 9:32 PM Eastern
**Duration:** ~39 minutes
**Segment:** Segment 2 of 2
**Attendees:** Val Levitan, Andrei Klimovich

## Summary

Val formally discussed Andrei's transition into a new role as Product & Market Manager for the Transactional product line. This was a structured conversation covering the role definition, compensation, market landscape, responsibilities, career path, and practical next steps.

**Role & Start Date**

Val confirmed that Andrei will begin his new responsibilities starting April 2, 2026 (Val specifically avoided April 1 due to superstition). The position title is **Product & Market Manager — Transactional**, where "Transactional" is the name of the product initiative, not a descriptor of the role type.

**Compensation Structure**

- Base salary: $90,000/year (Val noted this is above current market rate for this type of role, drawing on his local market experience to set it)
- Commission on the first $10M in sales: 1%
- Commission on all sales above $10M: 0.5%
- Val's target: grow the business to approximately $20M in installs within 3 years

**Markets & Geography**

Three active ("hot") markets, all growing rapidly:
- **Nebraska** — small state, only a couple of operators, but serves as a starting point
- **Georgia** — very active; approximately 80% of the most important customers are already known
- **Texas** — described as the "Wild West" with the most intense growth expected and the highest revenue potential

Additional states with activity include Pennsylvania, Michigan, and possibly Virginia, though these are not yet primary targets. Andrei should expect to travel approximately **75% of his time**, visiting operators and building relationships across these markets.

**Product Status**

The product line exists as a series of products in "file status design" — essentially past the initial rollout phase but still in active design. There is no ready-to-ship product today. Approximately 70% of product requirements already exist (in people's heads or informally documented) and need to be formally captured and consolidated. The first shipment is expected to go to Texas around **June 1, 2026**, with an estimated initial revenue of **$250K–$500K**.

Competitors exist and started earlier, giving them a market presence advantage. However, Val believes JVL is superior technologically and will also compete on pricing. Manufacturing will be done in the USA.

**Role Responsibilities**

Andrei's core responsibilities:
1. **Define product requirements and capabilities** from the market — visiting operators, listening to their needs, and translating feedback into actionable product requirements for the engineering director
2. **Own marketing strategy** for the Transactional product line
3. **Dual reporting**: reports to both Juan Martinez (VP of Product) and Val Levitan
4. **Corporate representative role**: Andrei is not a direct salesperson. Sales are handled by existing channel partners — Philippe in Georgia, Denis in Nebraska, and a distributor in Texas. Andrei's value is as a corporate figure: showing up professionally, listening to operators, gathering intelligence, and building relationships. Val emphasized that no competitor sends a product manager to meet operators directly, making Andrei's visits a significant event for customers.
5. **Influence pricing and marketing programs** — Andrei has substantial influence over commercial strategy (pricing policies, marketing programs) but acts through strategy formulation rather than direct selling.

Val used the Steve Jobs quote — "Marketing is not about what you want, it's about what they need" — and noted that customers often don't know what they need and must be guided. Andrei's role is to listen first, filter the noise (Val estimated 90% of what operators say will be irrelevant), and then shape product and marketing strategy accordingly.

**Client Positioning**

When speaking with clients, the company is to be described as "American" or "American-Canadian." No discussion of the Eastern European engineering center. Val noted this topic never comes up at Andrei's level of interaction.

**Travel & Client Engagement**

Val expects Andrei to be on the road 75% of his time. The first field trip could happen by end of April if Andrei feels prepared and has laid the groundwork. Val warned this is a "two-edged sword": it takes multiple visits before operators start engaging seriously, but there's also no point showing up with nothing to offer. The suggestion is to start by introducing himself and the upcoming product, then return for deeper conversations once there's something concrete to demonstrate.

Professional dress (smart casual) is expected. Val noted that operators typically wear jeans, so showing up well-dressed from the "corporate office" creates the right impression and signals that Andrei has direct access to company leadership.

**Career Path**

Val outlined the progression: Product & Market Manager → Product & Market Director → VP of Product. Juan Martinez (current VP of Product) has 35 years of industry experience and came to JVL already at VP level.

**Legal Consultation**

The employment agreement (NDA/indie) requires Andrei to confirm he consulted with a lawyer. Val made clear he cannot force Andrei to spend money on legal consultation, but the document contains a field where Andrei must sign attesting to it.

**Next Steps**

Andrei confirmed he can begin allocating 30% of his time starting Monday (March 30) without impacting his current Echo responsibilities. He has a meeting scheduled with Juan Martinez on Monday at 10:00 AM in the office to begin learning about the Transactional product. Andrei will think over the weekend, consider any additional questions, and tentatively accepts the role.

Val closed by expressing confidence in Andrei, citing his honesty as the primary reason for the appointment — "An honest person can always be taught. The reverse is not true."
