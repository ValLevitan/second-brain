## Summary

Follow-up to the August 7 call. Val came back with a sober two-year cash forecast and the two agreed on a bridge-loan structure instead of a cash call.

**The forecast.** Cash basis, January 2026 to December 2027; January–August are actuals, September onward forecasted. Val calls it average — deliberately not optimistic. It shows a cash burn of $2.5M from August 2026 through December 2027 (~17 months). Inside that burn sit $1.6M of "unusual payables": when Val knew the factory would move, he pre-built heavy inventory to ride out the move and the tariff war, financed through a buffer distributor insured by EDC (Export Development Corporation) — effectively a factoring-style deal with fixed three-year payments that cannot be restructured or prepaid. Six payments remain and then they fall away. The forecast also budgets repurchasing new inventory (the pre-built stock is almost gone).

**What the forecast excludes (upside).** (1) $1.9M of approved SR&ED/media tax credits awaiting payment, plus about $1M more to be filed starting January 2027 — $2.9M total not in the numbers. (2) The transactional-product rollout, which should generate roughly $1.1M of recurring revenue by end of 2027 — also excluded because Val can't yet predict the rollout. Bottom line both agreed on: cash-flow neutral by end of 2027 with somewhere between $400K and $1.5M in pocket; the $1.9M credit comes very close to wiping out the $2.5M deficit. Doug's reading of the monthly shape: underwater monthly until the $1.9M lands, briefly positive, then an inflection next summer with a possible $100–600K shortfall if the 2027 credit hasn't arrived.

**The bridge loan.** Doug does these for a living and can get it done in 14 days once he has the paper. Structure discussed:
- Preferred debt — sits in front of the shares and any other debt.
- Security: the approved CRA SR&ED refund ("I'd rather have that than their house"). Legally mandated pay-direction in effect: when CRA pays, JVL sends the check straight to the debtor.
- Pricing: 4% fee plus 10% annual interest, open loan repayable on 30 days' notice — paid off in 30 days it costs 4.75% total; three months ≈ 6.5%; six months ≈ 9%.
- Facility idea Doug floated: up to $2M available, drawable ~$600K per quarter against the cash-flow forecast, repayable in totality the moment the credit lands.
- Possible lender group: Val, Doug, and Doug's contact Ray Marks (illustrative $400K each = $1.2M).
- If a shortfall reappears next summer, the same lenders can re-up against the 2027 credit once it's approved ("as good as a bond. It's a government note").

**Val's own money.** Val is putting in $400–500K of his own cash this week — he needs cash now and has no time to arrange outside money first. Doug said Val should charge the company the same 4% + 10% terms ("I think that is more than fair"), and if a $1.2M facility follows, Val's $400K stays in alongside Doug's and Ray's.

**Doug's conditions.** He won't commit until he sees the CRA approval ("I don't wanna... get caught with my pants down. As soon as you send that to me, I'm good"). Val is sending the CRA approval he has in hand (he recalled $1.6M of it on the call, wasn't sure where the rest of the figure sits) plus the month-by-month cash-flow forecast. Doug will look at it as soon as he can.

The partners are not expected to write checks, and Doug repeated they likely don't care how Val funds it — pref debt over their heads will get a "yeah whatever Val, you do you."

Val's frame of mind was noticeably better than August 7 — sober rather than glass-half-empty, but deliberately avoiding an optimistic posture so as not to promise what he may not deliver.
