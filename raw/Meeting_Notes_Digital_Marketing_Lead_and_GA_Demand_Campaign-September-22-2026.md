## Summary

Val asked who can run Meta and Google advertising. Andrei then read Val's document on the Georgia demand plan and they worked through both halves of it — reaching location owners, and reaching players.

**Who runs digital marketing.** Andrei described three resources:
- **Sergey Vysotsky** — the digital growth generalist. He does not set up ad campaigns professionally, but he knows the tools well and can build a whole strategy. He is currently helping Andrei with Eco growth. Andrei's answer to every question Val asked about him: about 38 years old, lives in Warsaw, the younger brother of Andrei's university classmate, ex product manager at Apalon (a large Belarusian mobile company later bought by Apple), known to Andrei for many years. Andrei called him "an absolutely autonomous unit" — he takes a task, and if he does not know something he either learns it fast with AI or finds someone who can help. He works half-time and runs his own AI startup for hair-loss consultations; he is committed for another two months, then it gets reviewed. If he leaves, Andrei says he will hand over softly and recommend someone he knows, and will stay reachable.
- **Anton** — Meta only. He sets up Meta ads, watches the balance, and bids to win placements to a set strategy. He is not creative and does not help invent the creatives.
- **Google** — previously done through the Belarusian agency **Webcom**. Andrei was not happy with their work and stopped using them at the start of this year. Google advertising was also very expensive and did not pay back, so JVL stopped it.

Val's reaction: those are good qualities if the person can be relied on, and he would work with Sergey.

**Part 1 — reaching location owners.** Andrei's doubt: the numbers that are publicly available will mostly be the locations' own numbers (you just call the gas station), so it is unclear how realistic it is to find the owners.

Val said that with AI voice calls, logic and a script this is realistic today and no longer science fiction — call the general number, find out who the decision maker is, get his consent to be contacted, then keep a permanent line to him. It may take more than one call, but an AI-powered call is cheap. There are about 7,000 licensed locations in Georgia; Val would count 20% as a success.

Andrei asked whether it is even legal for a gaming machine manufacturer to contact a location owner. Val called it a very good question and answered in two parts. Business to business is allowed. But most owners use one general number and are not necessarily business lines, and you can tell from the number whether it is a cell or a landline — landline is fine, cell is not; doing it through an AI calling service is illegal, because federal law forbids it. His instruction: use a separate, offshore, third-party company, calling not in JVL's name, presented as a marketing company that has the information and wants to find the owner. Andrei accepted this and said they would think through the cover story.

Val was clear on the sequence: **JVL can only appear once the permitted contact list exists.** Andrei asked what forms that list — Val: the owner allows his phone to be used for informational messages or calls.

**Part 2 — reaching players.** Andrei's warning: Meta and Google police gambling advertising hard. Promoting gaming machines as gaming machines will be blocked. The creative has to avoid saying gambling while still letting the player understand. He restated the goal as he understood it: inform players living around the gaming halls, build JVL brand awareness and desire, up to the point where a player asks his own location why it has no JVL machine.

Val gave two hooks for players: JVL has **15 games, not the usual 6**; and with the transactional system the player can get **promo credit**. Because gambling is banned in Georgia, this can be presented as free amusement games — computer games.

Andrei suggested Class A would fit perfectly, since it is not regulated the way Class B is — advertise Class A as amusement, and on site the player also sees Class B. Val said there is an interesting phenomenon here he has not fully worked out: Class B is the skill amusement game set by law, while Class A is in principle not amusement at all but pure gamble, because the law allows a win up to fifty dollars. Class A was never developed because at a fifty-dollar maximum nobody found it interesting.

**Sharing the recording.** Andrei asked to send this video to Sergey so he would already be deep in context, then arrange a three-way call. Val objected — in this recording he speaks openly about how to get around the law. Andrei immediately agreed not to. Val's replacement: a memo saying what solution is needed. If Sergey finds no way around it, so be it. If he finds a way and does it in his own name, Val does not want to know the details, as long as it produces the list of locations.

**Presentation.** Andrei asked whether Val wanted to add anything by voice. Val said his written comments were clear. Andrei will send the next version.

## Decisions Made

- **The recording and transcript are not sent to Sergey Vysotsky — he gets a written memo instead** — Val speaks openly in it about getting around the law. (Decided by Val; Andrei agreed at once.)
- **Considered and superseded: sending the video to Sergey** — replaced by the memo.
- **Location-owner outreach runs through a separate third-party / offshore agency, not in JVL's name** — federal law forbids AI-service calls to cell numbers, and JVL must not be exposed. (Decided by Val.)
- **JVL offers nothing and appears nowhere until the consent list of location owners exists** — the list is the first deliverable. (Decided by Val.)
- **Sergey Vysotsky is the proposed owner of the outreach task** — Andrei recommends him; Val wants to be present at the briefing call before work starts. (Andrei proposed, Val accepted.)
- **Player advertising is positioned as amusement / computer games, not gambling** — gambling is banned in Georgia and Meta and Google block gambling ads. (Val and Andrei.)
