## Summary

A working call that locked the commercial skeleton of the Texas play.

**The four test machines.** Tested over the weekend, going out "next week": one into each of two gas stations, two into a game room. Scanning in/out works.

**FlexPay tier 1 (introduction, ~$11,000).** Val named the JVL kiosk "Flex Pay." To avoid shocking a Fujitsu-habituated market, the launch configuration is Fujitsu dispenser + regular TITO printer + regular bill acceptor. Machines connect through the in-machine **FlexPort** screen device, eliminating bill acceptors and printers inside the machines. Rich to sanity-check the model with a couple of operators. Reference points he gave: "Jim Meg" (GemMega?) kiosks ~$9K, "ours" (Access?) ~$14K — $11K sits in between.

**FlexPay tier 2 (right after, ~$9,000).** Same product configured with a bill recycler + printer: eliminates practically all location labor — no bill acceptors or printers in machines, no dispenser restocking, attend only for collections.

**Brokerage terms agreed.** Rich will not stock or prepay — he brokers: JVL invoices and ships to the operator, Rich takes commission. His ask: **$1,000 per box ≈ 8% on a $15,000 retail** — Val: "eight percent — it's a little bit fat, but a man has to live." Retail target $15K (market has $8K machines; quantity discounts apply; Val won't lose a serious operator over price). Future: "partners on the massive Texas business" talk deferred until results.

**Connectivity and the service charge.** FlexPay ships with a cellular modem; Val wants ~$3/day (~$1,000/year) per terminal as a connection/service charge, delivering machine data ("a mini CMS") to the operator's phone. Rich validated: competitors charge ~25¢/day per machine, so at 6–7 machines per Texas location the economics are comparable — and he reframed the pitch: sell it as "online access to your route," a route-management system that saves wasted collection trips. Open design question: who holds the cellular subscription — Val leaning to subscribing on the operator's behalf and passing the cost through. Rich has a cellular-connectivity contact to pass along.

**Pipeline.** Rich meets the ~87–97-location convenience-store group Sunday/Monday (presentation being built from JVL website assets; Val offered graphic-designer support). Timeline confirmed: April for FlexPay.

## Decisions Made

- **Rich's role: non-stocking broker at $1,000/box (~8%); JVL invoices and ships direct to operators.** (both)
- **FlexPay launch config: Fujitsu dispenser + TITO printer + bill acceptor at ~$11K; recycler version ~$9K follows immediately.** (Val)
- **~$3/day per-terminal connected-service charge, positioned as route management.** (Val, validated by Rich)
