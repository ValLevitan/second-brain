## Summary

Val asked Michael for his unfiltered read after the week's operator meetings. This is the internal counterweight to the polished customer calls — a diagnosis of JVL's Georgia sales function.

**The Felipe diagnosis.** Michael's frame: two kinds of problem people in this business — order takers (answer the phone, take the order) and scorekeepers (observe, take credit when things go well, announce doom with no solutions). He pegs Felipe as an order taker — a hard-working technician-type who services what comes in but "doesn't have the self-confidence, the ego, the swagger, the relationships" to force outcomes, doesn't come with solutions or calls to action, and cannot get meetings with principals ("he gets an appointment with a technician"). Val confirmed the last point as conclusive and called Felipe's posture "service, polite, on one knee — that's not how you get an order."

**The trade-in fumble (case study).** The customer (garbled as "Mark Black" — context says Ray/Inamax) told them he would happily trade in Banilla cabinets at ~$2,000, yet JVL's Christmas offer at $4,000 got no reaction — because Felipe never actually reached him (SMS and email only). Val performed the recovery play a real salesperson would run in mid-January: "this is the deal of the century… I held my year-end open for you… I can backdate it." Worse, on the call Felipe publicly blamed the customer for missing his messages — both agreed this was an emotional-intelligence failure ("you never take something neutral and make it negative"; the right line is "that's on us, the timing was goofy"), that the customer visibly registered JVL's flusteredness, and that pressure smells like weakness ("why is Felipe laying into me — are things okay at JVL?").

**The narrative problem.** Michael's sharpest warning: Felipe may be feeding Juan anecdotal claims that drive product decisions — "we were told unequivocally that the only game in our library that works is Peach One. That's bullshit": Inamax's own data showed Peach 3 first, Peach 1 second, Peach 2 marginally third. Neither customer complained about JVL product at all — the week's message was market saturation and Banilla purging, not "you suck." Both agreed to stop reacting to unverified anecdotes: "some of our reactionary behavior has no foundation in truth."

**The commoditization idea (Michael's proposal).** Bad Dog's model — a $3–4K board plus $1K monitor conversion into an existing cabinet — is commoditizing the replacement cycle, and operators are sitting on warehouses of retired Banillas. Michael, drawing on Williams' self-sabotaging elitism in the video-game era (unique control panels, protecting cabinet margins, "other people ate our lunch" with conversion kits — Val: "I was one of the guilty parties that brought conversion kits to the market"), proposed a financial and practical analysis of a **JVL board that converts Banilla cabinets** for the second-tier replacement wave. Explicitly flagged as unknown-margin — analyze, don't assume.

**Sales doctrine sketched (for whoever does the job).** Walk into the warehouse ("holy shit, that's a lot of machines — you're paying storage on this old shit — let me turn it around"); prescriptive selling ("you need another thirty-forty machines — let's figure out what's holding you back"); raise the trade-in until it clears ("$2,000 not enough, $4,000 not enough — how about $6,000? It's just a matter of price now"); lean on pedigree and evergreen numbers against risky cheap conversions and unproven entrants; the Godfather construction — an offer with nothing but upside. Michael's father's story for flavor: Gottlieb marked games with UV ink to catch transshipping distributors — "you've got to be in that warehouse."

**People decisions left open.** Val: "I don't know what to do… I don't have anybody to replace him with — or add to him." Michael's suggestion: ask long-standing trusted market principals for recommendations — a small community, and asking is not weakness. On Rich Winley as the answer: both skeptical — "he's a talker," no patience, no strategy shown.

## Decisions Made

- **Stop making product/market decisions on Felipe's anecdotal claims until verified against operator data.** (both — "take a deep breath… no foundation in truth")
- **The all-Peaches-perform fact is established** (Peach 3 #1 at Inamax), overturning the "only Peach One works" narrative. (Val)
- **Evaluate the Banilla-cabinet conversion board financially** before any commitment. (Michael's proposal, accepted as analysis)
- **Trade-in price is a dial, not a wall** — willingness to go to $6,000 against machines already paid for many times over. (Val)
