# COAM Gift Card Solution - Incomm Meeting Notes
*April 7, 2025*

## Problem Definition
- John and Val discussed the problem of merchants being concerned that gift cards cannot be used to play at COAM gaming machines, leading to a potential loss of in-store spend.

## Merchant Concerns
- John explained that merchants are worried about the inability to use gift cards at COAM gaming machines, which could lead to a loss of in-store spend.
- This concern is based on feedback from multiple operators in the industry.

## Use Case
- John provided a use case where a player wins $100 at a convenience store, loads it onto a MasterCard, and then cannot use that $100 to play at the COAM gaming machines, highlighting the core issue.

## Solution Options
John outlined three potential solutions to address the issue:
1. Players asking clerks to swipe their cards for cash
2. Contracting with a merchant acquirer to install card readers
3. Using a barcode on the card to process transactions directly with Incomm

### Solution 1: Clerk Card Swipe
- Players could ask clerks to swipe their cards for cash, which they could then use to play at the gaming machines.
- This solution requires no changes to the current system.

### Solution 2: Merchant Acquirer Card Readers
- Contracting with a merchant acquirer to install card readers on kiosks or gaming machines.
- This would allow players to swipe their cards and transfer funds directly to the machines, though it involves additional costs.

#### Merchant Acquirer Challenges
- John explained the challenges of using a merchant acquirer, including monthly maintenance fees and transaction fees, which may deter merchants from adopting this solution.

#### Cost Implications
- Merchants would face monthly maintenance fees ranging from $50 to $200 and a 3% transaction fee for each transaction processed through a merchant acquirer, making this solution less attractive.

#### Merchant Revenue Impact
- Merchants would receive less money from transactions due to the 3% fee.
- If a player transfers $100, the merchant will only get $97, impacting their revenue.

### Solution 3: Barcode Solution
- Using a barcode on the card to process transactions directly with Incomm, bypassing the need for PCI compliance and reducing costs.
- This solution involves a modest monthly fee for accounting and settlement.

#### PCI Compliance Advantage
- Using a barcode on the card avoids PCI compliance issues because the barcode is not considered payment card information, simplifying the process for merchants.

#### Cost Reduction
- This solution eliminates the 3% transaction fee and only involves a modest monthly fee of around $20 to $25 for accounting and settlement, making it more cost-effective for merchants.

#### Transaction Process
- Transactions would be processed directly with Incomm, where the barcode is scanned, and funds are transferred without involving a merchant acquirer, streamlining the process.

## Security Measures
### PIN-Based Security
- John clarified that the barcode solution would require users to enter a PIN to protect against unauthorized use, with the PIN being set on the first use of the card.

### PIN Setup
- The PIN for the barcode solution is set on the first use of the card. The first time a user enters a PIN, it becomes their PIN for that card.

### PIN Change
- Users can change their PIN anytime by calling the IVR or visiting the website, providing flexibility and security for cardholders.

## Business Structure
### Role of Participants
- Val and John discussed the roles of the various participants in the business scheme, including the location, the machine owner, JVL as the supplier, and Incomm as the program manager and technology provider.

### Participant Roles
- **Location**: Houses the machines
- **Machine Owner**: Manages the machines
- **JVL**: Supplies the machines or kiosks
- **Incomm**: Acts as the program manager and technology provider for the gift card product and APIs

### Incomm's Role
- John clarified that Incomm does not build kiosks but focuses on financial technology, product management, and money movement between parties, ensuring compliance with regulations.

## Implementation Considerations
### Kiosk Solutions
- Incomm does not build kiosks, and the operators are responsible for designing their own solutions based on their specific needs and preferences.

### Operator Responsibility
- Operators are responsible for designing their own kiosk solutions based on their specific needs and preferences, as Incomm does not build kiosks.

### Design Flexibility
- Operators can choose different configurations for their kiosks, such as integrating card readers or using digital wallets, depending on their operational requirements.

## Expansion Plans
- Incomm is looking to expand into other markets, including Texas, Florida, Pennsylvania, Nebraska, and Iowa, to improve the player experience and reduce dependence on cash.

### Target Markets
- Target markets for expansion include:
  - Texas
  - Florida
  - Pennsylvania
  - Nebraska
  - Iowa

### Product Adaptation
- Incomm is adapting its products to suit different markets:
  - Using cards that can be immediately used at ATMs in Pennsylvania
  - Addressing large win payouts in Nebraska

### Merchandise Markets
- John mentioned the print-on-receipt product for merchandise markets, allowing players to choose from various gift card providers, enhancing flexibility and player satisfaction.
