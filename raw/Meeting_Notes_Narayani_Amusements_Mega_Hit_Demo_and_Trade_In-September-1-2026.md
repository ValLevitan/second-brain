## Summary

This is the demo promised on August 26. It did not close, because the people who came were the operations and service managers rather than the owner.

**Note on the account name.** During the meeting Neel disclosed that the company **changed its name to Akshar Gaming last week** (spelled out: A-K-S-H-A-R) following a change of ownership after four or five years. The second company, **Ananta Amusement**, is Vishal's and has about 400 machines. Akshar has **~150 locations and close to 1,000 machines**, with a different owner from Vishal. Neel and Jai make purchasing decisions for both, but not without Vishal.

**Mega Hit Games demo.** Felipe led. The cabinet shows 8 games, with 10 the recommended configuration out of a library of 15 — games can be enabled and swapped remotely, and JVL tracks which are performing so it can call and suggest a change, needing only a hard reset. Two more games are being added, including Lion's Races (successful in this market, reworked for vertical) and a new title. **Jackpot Rush** was the centrepiece: a multi-level progressive of the type popular in Class III casinos, where the jackpot scales with the bet level — 25¢ bet caps at $75, a $5 bet pays up to $1,500 — which Felipe framed as protecting the operator from paying a quarter-bettor a huge prize. Val added the design intent: it is built for high rollers, who want to be visibly distinguished and to win more in absolute dollars, and he considers the 25¢ button on this cabinet wrong. Pricing given: **$14,572** for the 15-game version; a cheaper 10-game version exists but loses the ability to change games, which Felipe advised against for $250 a game.

**The trade-in did not close.** Val pressed hard for a decision because he needs to move the traded games into a **secondary market** deal he has open in another state, and reconditioning costs him about $1,000 per game. Neel said 21 games sit in the warehouse, fully functioning with minor software errors, but could not commit without Vishal. The gap: **Vishal asked $5,000, Val agreed $3,000** — Val's words, "I think you asked for five thousand and I agreed to three thousand. I think we're quite a bit apart, twenty six and twenty one. We shall make a deal somewhere in between five and three." Felipe offered to go to their office next week to close it. Val's pressure was explicit and repeated: *"That's why I'm rushing you to make that deal. I get it. Otherwise I will take somebody else's deal."*

**Transactional walkthrough.** Andrei ran the same ladder as the other visits. Neel's own detailed account of their existing **Acres-style card system** was the valuable content: about $5,000 one-time for six machines including POS, card readers and player cards, **$13.30 per machine per month**, they remove MudaGoose completely and run their own wires (leaving it connected causes double payouts and confuses the location). Players use a plastic card; balances persist between visits; gift cards must come from InComm or another provider because the card system does not issue them. He also described the **EasyLink limitation** that annoys players — a $500 ticket must be played through enough hands before it can be redeemed, where the card system lets a player draw down $50 at a time. They have two EasyLink locations and are removing one.

Neel also raised the InComm credit ceiling as a live constraint: locations get only $3,000–$6,000 per week, but some pay out $50,000, so they need higher limits.

Val's competitive close: Flex port at **$950**, Flex-IQ at $1,000, and a post-show special — buy the machine at $16,000 and get **six Flex ports plus a Flex-IQ terminal free**, for whatever machines they already have, with JVL supplying brackets and the customer installing.

**The coaching session (last ~35 minutes, after the customer left).** Val gave Felipe and Andrei direct, unsparing feedback. It is worth reading in full, but the substance:

- **Do not answer phone calls in front of a customer.**
- **Never say "no, but"** — say "that's a great idea, and in addition we do this."
- **Stop repeating yourself, stop raising your voice, stop gesticulating.** Val demonstrated that people back away from loudness: "the louder you speak, the more repulsive it is."
- **Posture:** hands in pockets or behind the back signals hiding something; hands forward is defensive. Stand straight, normal tone, look them in the eyes.
- **Speeches are too long.** "Start from focusing on a gem, on something that is a killer. Stick with it. Less is more."
- **Never ask "do you have any questions?"** — if he has none, either he is stupid or you did badly. Let questions come.
- **Lead with the differentiator, not the feature list.** Val's Steve Jobs argument: Jobs sold "ten thousand tunes in your palm", not fidelity or screens; BlackBerry sold security and lost. JVL's one line is that this is **the only machine where the operator can actually fine-tune his games**, because the modem gives real per-game statistics — game popularity, bet levels, time of day — none of which exists in the standard money-in/money-out report Val calls an "exit report."
- Val also argued that any report containing one line of garbage should be dismissed entirely, which Felipe pushed back on.

Felipe's counter was fair and Val partly accepted it: the operators do not know what a remote initial setup or a modem is, they came to see a game, and using internal vocabulary loses them. Andrei's own read at the end was that this meeting was less explicit than the previous one and the visitors would probably tell Vishal only that "there is a game machine." Val agreed the realistic takeaway they will carry back is that JVL has something you install in the machine that lets a player move between machines — "That's all they remember."

Val closed with an invitation to contradict him: *"By all means, contradict. There's nothing. I don't get offended by you contradicting me."*

## Decisions Made

- **No deal closed.** Neel and Jai cannot commit without Vishal; they take the games and the offer back to him.
- **Trade-in still open between $3,000 and $5,000 per cabinet**, to be settled "somewhere in between"; Val is time-pressured by a secondary-market deal in another state.
- **Felipe to go to their office next week to close**, with Val available.
- **10-game configuration recommended over 15** only if they accept losing game-swap capability — Felipe advised against it.
- **Post-show special extended:** machine at $16,000 including six Flex ports plus a terminal, free, fitted to whatever machines they run.
