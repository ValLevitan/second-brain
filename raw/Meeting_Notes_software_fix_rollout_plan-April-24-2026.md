# Meeting Summary — JVL Software Fix Rollout Plan
**Date:** April 24, 2026, 4:34 PM Eastern (8:34 PM UTC start in transcript)
**Duration:** ~1h 1m
**Attendees:** Val Levitan, Felipe Miranda Giorgi, Juan Martinez

## Summary

Felipe convened the meeting to walk Val and Juan through a phased rollout plan for the new Flex cabinet software that contains the kernel-level fix for the freezing issue. Felipe had prepared a PowerPoint deck — Val pushed back hard on the format, calling the slide content "glorified" filler and asking Felipe to state the problem and resolution in plain terms rather than presenting through marketing-style bullets (benefits like "stronger route performance potential," "improved player flow," etc.). Val also specifically directed that any external communication must NOT mention the watchdog timer or generic "stability improvements" — these phrases reveal an inadequate prior design (a watchdog timer should have been there from the start) and are not appropriate to surface to operators. The acceptable framing is the kernel/processor incompatibility story: a specific processor/kernel-version (4.x → 5.15) issue that has now been identified and fixed.

The fleet scope is approximately 2,065 Flex cabinets identified so far (Felipe believes the actual number may be higher; Irina's report from Minsk was missing some serial numbers and Felipe is reconciling). The cabinets span multiple software baselines — most are still on the older 1919 build, which Felipe noted historically had very few freezing reports. The freezing problem is concentrated on the newer software builds (1776 and the new-nudge family), which is why those cabinets are the priority for the upgrade. Felipe has the cross-reference of current software version, current preset settings, target software version, and target preset settings per serial number per master operator.

The biggest operational challenge is that operators are notoriously unresponsive to written communication. Val's characterization: operator owners drive Mercedes and "show off like peacocks," while the technicians who actually do the work are "treated like peasants." Letters and emails frequently go unread; even when an owner agrees to something verbally, the instruction often does not filter down to the technical staff. Felipe's previous experience with operators like Sean (head tech Tara has gone silent), AKS (eventually delivering), and others bears this out. Juan reinforced the point: the question is whether the agreement at the top reaches the technicians in the field.

Val's central decision in the meeting was the commercial framing: JVL will give the upgraded software to all affected operators **free of charge**. The software ordinarily sells for $2,500 per cabinet. Val rejected Felipe's proposed "carrots and sticks" approach (free for a window, then charge after a deadline, and/or refuse to support legacy software). Val's reasoning: operators do not respond predictably to either pricing pressure or deadlines — "they are a special species." The operative argument that does carry weight is uptime-as-revenue: if a cabinet was freezing and out of service 30–40% of the time, restoring it does not give a 30% revenue lift — it gives more, because the recovered hours are disproportionately the peak earning hours when players walked away from a frozen screen. Val will produce an official written statement by **Monday, April 27, 2026** that Felipe can use as the foundation for his operator communication. Val also noted he had committed to drafting this letter approximately a month earlier; this meeting was partly Felipe pressing for delivery of that promised letter.

A significant portion of the meeting addressed the 10 cabinets Felipe has already upgraded with the new (kernel-fixed) software in the field, in the past one to two weeks. The split is approximately three V43s and three D27s confirmed, plus a few more — Felipe could not produce the precise breakdown on the spot, which Val challenged sharply ("you know which toilet paper is in your bathroom"). These 10 are early production validation, not commercial rollout. They have been running for ~2 weeks without freezing.

A side discussion clarified a misunderstanding from a prior conversation Val had with Juan about Peach 1 Vertical with new-nudge. Val had been told Peach 1 Vertical was the top performer; this dated to a conversation roughly eight months ago, before the freezing fix existed. Felipe confirmed: with only ~1 week of data on new-nudge-with-fix, there is no statistically meaningful basis to claim new-nudge performs better, worse, or the same as old-nudge. All prior Intralog reports showing new-nudge underperforming were running on freezing software, so they tell us nothing about the post-fix behavior. Val's conclusion: at this stage, JVL has no data on whether new-nudge performs differently from old-nudge once freezing is removed from the picture.

Val criticized Felipe's prioritization sharply: Felipe upgraded 10 cabinets for various operators rather than going first to the flagship customers JVL had explicitly spoken with on the phone — the operators who had been running freezing software with full knowledge from JVL that a fix was coming. Val called this offensive and directed that those operators be the immediate priority for re-upgrade once the bulletin is released. The sequence Val wants: (1) release the bulletin/letter, (2) immediately re-upgrade the cabinets at flagship operators that JVL had already converted to the freezing version, (3) collect COM IDs as part of the upgrade.

The COM ID question dominated the second half. The original goal was operators scanning QR codes per cabinet so JVL could correlate field installations to serial numbers. Operators have largely refused or failed to follow through on QR-code scanning. Felipe's pragmatic substitute is to require the COM ID at the time of upgrade install — the COM ID is a CMS-assigned identifier created when a machine is commissioned. By tying COM ID to JVL serial number, Felipe can track field performance per machine via Intralog reports (which include COM ID in parentheses but not the JVL serial number). Limitations: (a) the COM ID changes each time a machine is decommissioned and recommissioned, so it is not persistent across moves; (b) machines move between locations and even between masters, complicating long-term tracking; (c) operators do not always voluntarily share COM IDs even after agreeing to. AKS has delivered COM IDs in the past. Sean's tech Tara is non-responsive. Val reluctantly accepted COM ID as a workable substitute for QR codes ("your solution is better than QR code — well, it's not, but I understand why you're doing it"), and framed the deal: free software in exchange for the COM ID.

Felipe also floated two other ideas, both deferred by Val as out of scope for now:
- A field-helper / install-flow tool that walks the technician through the upgrade and requires entry of the COM ID and tech name as a final step (potentially with a raffle incentive for participating techs).
- Pulling a database report of all RIS license scans per day in CMS, to cross-reference which cabinets were actually re-installed with the new software. Val said this is a new development and not on the table now.

Logistics: Felipe estimated ~1,600 USB sticks at ~$2.50 each plus RIS/dongles per cabinet plus several copies of each software per master, totaling roughly $5–6K in USB material cost. Felipe is NOT proposing to ship USBs blanket — he is signaling the upper-bound budget. The actual delivery model is mixed: some operators (e.g., United Gaming) reload software daily and just need the file emailed. Others want USB dongles delivered. Some have their RIS hanging inside the machine and don't need a new RIS if presets don't change between current and target version (Felipe has a table mapping which cabinet+preset combinations need a new RIS vs. not).

Juan raised a related observation from the earlier South Dakota serial-number incident: the RIS in CMS was dynamically updated when Minsk corrected garbage characters in a serial number, and the operator had to re-download the same RIS number to get the corrected file. Felipe confirmed: any change to the serial number — even removing a hidden comma, dot, or dash — produces a different serial in CMS and therefore requires a fresh RIS download. Same RIS file works fine when the serial is unchanged.

There is also a precedent for charging operators for software copies: when United Gaming asked for ~20 dongle copies of each of several software versions to avoid having to image them in-house, JVL charged $25 per copy. This is rare and is treated as a parts charge, not a software license fee. The first copy of any required upgrade remains free.

In closing, Val and Felipe agreed: the recovery of full uptime is the only commercial argument that will resonate with operators, and the free-software framing is the carrot. The letter Val will deliver Monday is the trigger that lets Felipe begin operator outreach on the rollout.

## Decisions Made

- **JVL will provide the kernel-fix software upgrade FREE OF CHARGE** to all affected operators. (Decided by Val) Rationale: operators do not respond predictably to pricing pressure or deadlines; the trust-and-uptime narrative is the only argument that works; the upgrade is in JVL's interest because it removes a defect.
- **Val will draft and deliver an official written statement to Felipe by Monday, April 27, 2026.** (Decided by Val) The statement will frame the freezing as a mechanical out-of-service issue (not a software performance issue), describe the kernel/processor incompatibility cause, announce the free upgrade, and serve as Felipe's authoritative reference for operator communication.
- **External communication must NOT mention the watchdog timer or generic "stability improvements."** (Decided by Val) Rationale: surfacing the watchdog timer points to an inadequate prior design — a watchdog should have existed from the start. The communicable narrative is the kernel/processor incompatibility (4.19 → 5.15 LTS) only.
- **No deadline or "free until X then charged" model.** (Decided by Val) Rejected as ineffective.
- **Priority order for rollout: flagship operators that JVL already converted to the freezing version come FIRST**, immediately after the bulletin is released. (Decided by Val) Newer-build cabinets are second priority. The 1919 baseline is lowest priority because the freezing rate there is low.
- **COM ID at time of install accepted as the field tracking mechanism**, in lieu of QR-code scanning which has not worked in practice. (Decided by Val, proposed by Felipe) The free upgrade is offered in exchange for the operator providing the COM ID per cabinet upgraded.
- **No new tooling work approved in this meeting** — the field-helper install flow and the CMS RIS-scan report idea are both deferred.

## Engineering Signals

### Confirmed Signals

- **ADR — Linux kernel upgrade 4.19 → 5.15 LTS on Flex cabinet platform.** Root cause of the freezing is an incompatibility between the integrated graphics processor on the affected cabinet platform and the 4.19 kernel. The motherboard vendor confirmed in writing that 4.19 was inappropriate for this processor and pointed JVL to the current LTS (5.15). The fix has been deployed and validated on the 10 field test cabinets running ~2 weeks without recurrence. (Val provided the technical narrative; this is fully decided and is the basis for the letter.)
- **REQ — JVL must collect COM ID per cabinet at the time of every new-build upgrade install**, and link COM ID to JVL serial number in JVL's tracking system. This is the contractual quid-pro-quo for the free software. (Val and Felipe both agreed.)
- **REQ — External communication policy: do not surface watchdog timer, "stability improvements," "auto recovery," or similar phrasing in operator-facing material.** Permitted narrative is the kernel/processor incompatibility. (Val explicit.)
- **CAP (aspirational, called out as a strategic engineering goal in the letter) — Fleet-level health visibility for connected cabinets.** Val's letter states that JVL is investing engineering effort to make cabinet health information discoverable in real time by every operator on every EGM, so problems like this surface in days rather than months. This signals a forward-looking capability commitment that should be tracked.

### Suspected Signals

- **Possible CAP — Field-helper install flow that walks the technician through the upgrade and requires COM ID + tech name entry as a completion gate.** Felipe proposed this as a way to mandate COM ID capture and possibly add a raffle/incentive for participating techs. Val did not approve in this meeting (deferred), but did not reject the underlying need. — *Val, does this warrant tracking as a future capability?*
- **Possible REQ — CMS report of all RIS license scans per day, to cross-reference which cabinets actually got re-installed with the new build.** Felipe raised this as a tracking aid; Val deferred. — *Val, worth logging as a deferred requirement, or drop it?*
- **Possible REQ — Persistent cabinet identity that survives decommission/recommission.** Felipe noted COM ID changes every time a cabinet is decommissioned and recommissioned, breaking long-term performance tracking when machines move. Not raised explicitly as a requirement, but it is a real gap. — *Val, is this on the radar for the broader visibility initiative?*
