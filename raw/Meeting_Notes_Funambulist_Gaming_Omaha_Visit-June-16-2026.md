## Summary

A visit that did not find its meeting. Val arrived at Funambulist Gaming's Omaha office for a scheduled appointment with Andy Suydan and nobody there knew about it — Andy's wife had gone into labour early. Chris, then Heather Ackerman, met Val in the front office instead. Val took the excuse well and made it a virtue: *"if he hadn't been there, he was a dead man walking and we want him alive."*

Heather was open about her position — she works directly for the owners, has been with the company a long time, and dabbles across the business rather than sitting in sales. She was careful not to answer questions she wasn't sure of: *"I don't want to give you incorrect information."* What she did give Val was the org map, which is what the visit ended up being worth.

**Account profile as described.** Funambulist runs the length and breadth of Nebraska — *"from here all the way to the other side of the state and up and down"* — with about **300 locations**. Nebraska is the whole of the operating footprint; the corporate office is in **Virginia**, and the company is owned by **Winners Marketing**, which Heather believed to be the manufacturing arm. There are **two owners**. It runs as a family-style business: *"we may not be super corporate structured, but we do have a very healthy book."* Warehouse and operations are out of the Omaha site. Funambulist is already a JVL customer — they bought machines through **Dennis**, Val's predecessor.

**The path to the decision-makers.** Heather redirected Val to **Scott Twine**, Director of Business Development, at every turn. Scott is Virginia-based but spends about half his time in Nebraska, is not an owner but is a decision-maker, and is covering Andy's work while he is out — he was due in Nebraska the following week, after Val had left. Val asked whether he could fly to Virginia to meet the owners directly; Heather deferred that to Scott as well. Heather scanned Val's QR business card, took his details, and committed to forwarding them to Scott and getting the two connected.

**Val's positioning.** Val gave the standard opening — he owns JVL, second generation, his father started 42 years ago as a new immigrant taking the only job available, his daughters are in the business and one is an attorney taking over — and the differentiator he uses in every first meeting: *"I listen to the decision makers, I pinpoint three pain points for them, and I address it. And I make all the decisions in the company, so I can address it. Dennis, for example, cannot."* Heather placed herself in the same bracket as Dennis: *"like me, I'm like Dennis. I cannot — but I know who to get."* Val also told her plainly that JVL is replacing Dennis, that Dennis will be gone, and that the South Dakota office is moving to Nebraska because JVL likes the market and intends to grow there.

Val asked for a warehouse tour; Heather was willing but had other commitments. No next meeting was set in the room. Val was flying out the next morning and said a return trip would not be natural for him — the next step would be Virginia or a call.

## Decisions Made

- **Scott Twine is the point of contact**, not Andy Suydan, for anything involving business development or reaching the owners.
- **Introduction to run through Heather** — she takes Val's details to Scott rather than Val cold-contacting him.
- **No second Nebraska trip for this account** — Val stated he does not make frequent return visits; the next meeting is a call or a Virginia visit with ownership.
