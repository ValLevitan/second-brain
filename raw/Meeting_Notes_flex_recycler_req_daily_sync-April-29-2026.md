**Tito vs e-wallet — credit transfer model.** Andrei asked whether the Recycler will print a **TITO** (Ticket-In/Ticket-Out) ticket for use at game machines. Val and Juan walked him through why TITO is the wrong frame for this market:

- TITO is a 30+-year-old IGT design built for casinos with 500+ machines. Starting price ~$900K for a 500-machine casino, and most casinos run >$1.5M for the full system. It requires a centralized server, redundant server rooms, kilometers of SAS wiring under double floors, dedicated full-time staff, dual physical electrical entry, and dual fiber-optic entry. *"Definitely not a gas station or a convenience store."*
- Today in Texas, one operator JVL knows of has machines that print a **QR code** on a thermal receipt (not a true TITO ticket) which the player carries to a separate Count-R ticket-redemption kiosk (manufactured by Count-R, distributed by Vanilla) for cash. Machine ↔ kiosk talk over serial / SAS or by QR pairing. Juan added that Andrei is supposed to be researching Count-R "with the [contact] from Austria."
- Val's preferred framing for the Flex-Recycler: an **e-wallet** as the umbrella concept — the ticket can be a printed QR code, a barcode, a four-digit PIN, a plastic card, a photograph of a QR on the player's phone, an SMS-delivered PIN, or "just a number." There is **no standard, no rule** — any of these can work, and the choice will depend on other constraints. Juan agreed: *"definitely think of the e-wallet as the umbrella covering everything below it."* Andrei updated his mental model — for him, "wallet" had previously meant only "e-wallet" in the digital sense.
- **Voucher seizure semantics:** the e-wallet token must be **seized / confiscated** by a machine on entry — it is not a transferable instrument. To move between machines, the player cashes out (a new token is issued) and inserts the new token at the next machine. Val: *"there is a confiscation and there is an issue every time. That's how you monitor a transaction."*
- **Auth model:** pair a **QR code with a PIN**. Val: *"the moment you pair QR code with PIN, you have your security."* Andrei raised shoulder-surfing risk; Juan's analogy: not worse than ATM keypad exposure with five people queued behind. Andrei pushed back: at an ATM, theft requires direct robbery, but at a game machine someone who saw your PIN can simply walk to the next machine and use it — different threat model. Open question.
- **Phone-photographed QR** as a ticket form was floated by Val and is already partially in production — Juan: the four Rich Winley installations today use a thermal-printed QR on receipt paper that the player takes to the kiosk. Val proposed pushing further: the ticket could simply be a QR shown on a screen that the player photographs with their phone. Edge cases (broken phone screen, scanner reliability, players who refuse smartphones). Juan and Val mostly dismissed the no-smartphone segment ("drug dealers", "they'll just point a gun and borrow the phone"). Not formally adopted.
- **No-printer alternative:** Val pointed out that you don't even need a printer — the player could have a PIN delivered by **SMS** and bring nothing physical to the machine. This drops the printer requirement entirely if SMS is accepted.

**Why the Recycler architecture exists — Val's deep-dive.** Andrei asked whether the Flex-Recycler should support cash-out for QR tickets that a *machine* printed (not the Recycler). Juan's answer: **no** — Flex-Recycler is not going to coexist with kiosks at the same site, because if a player can cash *into* the machine via its bill acceptor, the Recycler will run dry. *"It needs to be an entire JVL ecosystem ... we need to train them to go to the recycler and the recycler only."* Val then delivered a long structured explanation:

- A win means the player walks away with cash, which requires a **float** (bank of cash supply). In conventional gaming, every machine has a bill acceptor with a cash box; cash settles inside each machine. To get cash out, the player needs a transfer mechanism (a ticket) that the dispenser can trust → hence TITO, hence server, hence centralized infrastructure.
- The competitor / status-quo Texas operation requires: bill acceptors in every machine, printers in every machine, optical readers / barcode readers, a server, and a cash bank. Plus a **drop team** physically opening 20–40 machines per location. Each cash box can hold up to **900 bills across 4 denominations** stacked together — Val's worked example: 800 bills × $10 average ≈ $8K cash exposure *per box*, times 20–40 machines per location. In a casino, the drop team is **three people** (one employee removes the cash box, one accountant follows, one armed security guard follows the other two). Casinos go out of service **4 AM – 7 AM** during the drop because the section must be roped off. *"They go home when they're done. So they're slamming those boxes, destroying machines, breaking locks, breaking keys."*
- Bill acceptors and dispensers also have **bill-condition constraints** — bills going *into* a bill acceptor (worn) are not the right condition for going *out* of a recycler-style dispenser (need to be slightly worn but not brand new — brand-new bills get vacuum-stuck). The industry term is **"ATM-grade cash."** Sorting by denomination is required. Val's pedigree note: *"only a few companies in the world can do it well. And the reason I know because I used to own the company. Otherwise, you would never hear this lecture."*
- Operators rely on armored services for daily refill, which adds delivery cost plus the bank's float-service cost plus locked-up operating cash. Juan's casino reference point: **Hollywood Hard Rock has 13 kiosks**, each filled with **$150,000 per weekend / concert / big event**, plus interest paid to the bank to borrow the money. **Emergency armored-truck request on a Saturday or holiday: ~$10,000 for the truck alone**, double on emergency. *"Granted, these are much smaller scales, but the expenses are still the same."*
- The status-quo Texas alternative is operators doing it themselves: opening every machine, sorting bills by denomination on a back-room table, and refilling the dispenser by hand. **Audit tickets** are produced because the location expects its cut on the spot during the drop — so the cash count must be auditable at the moment of drop. Machines are out of service during this whole process, so the operator is also losing revenue.
- **The Flex-Recycler eliminates almost all of this.** Remove bill acceptors and printers from the floor machines (saving conservatively ~$1,000/machine, "much more than that" in reality — at 20 machines that's ≥$20K of hardware avoided). The Recycler stores incoming bills in cassettes that auto-refill the dispensing cassette as bills are paid out. Because gambling RTP favors the house, the Recycler is, by definition, always filled. For high-activity locations, JVL can install **two recyclers in one body** to double capacity. No bank, no sorting, no float, no armored service, no drop team, fewer mechanical service incidents (mechanical devices are the source of service expense). The Texas distance argument matters here too: end-to-end Texas drives are **10 hours** at highway speed, so onsite service has to be minimized.
- **Server requirement:** Val's stance is that the Recycler does not strictly need an outside server — a barcode could auto-expire, and a server could live inside the machine and reset every 24 hours. JVL is nonetheless building a server-side component for reporting / analytics that operators want, and Val noted candidly: *"it allows me to charge money on a monthly basis."* Andrei pressed on the reconciliation question (one player, one QR token used across multiple machines) — Juan clarified that JVL *does* store that state somewhere ("we have to balance that"), which is a real architectural question for the team. Val: state can live inside the machine; an external server is a *want*, not a *need*.

**Bill-acceptor / Recycler co-existence.** Andrei asked whether an operator could request "your optical scanner and PIN screen, but I want to keep my bill acceptor." Val and Juan: *technically* possible, but commercially refused — a co-installed bill acceptor will exhaust the Recycler. Juan: *"we don't want to co-mingle a bill acceptor and machine and a recycler at the same location ... it's a clash of products because that's the only way you keep the Recycler full."* Player re-education will be required (signage, messaging on JVL toppers) to direct players to the Recycler first.

**Mixed-fleet support.** Andrei asked whether JVL supports competitor cabinets in mixed deployments. Juan confirmed: yes — JVL **sniffs meter pulses** and can **tap into bill acceptors** as needed. Val added that JVL's **Flexport** product (touchscreen window mounted in the standard cabinet opening) has been verified compatible with the **four main cabinet suppliers in Georgia.** Juan's market read: in Texas, "every day a new location opens up," so the ideal sales motion is to pitch new locations / new route-operator deployments where hardware can be prepped in the warehouse without the unwanted components — making "all-JVL ecosystem" the phase-one wedge. Existing-location retrofits become phase two.

**Revenue model for Flex-Recycler.** Andrei asked where JVL makes money. Val and Juan: hardware sale is one-time but operators have told Juan that competitors (Vanilla, Pace) prefer **lease** with deposit — Juan wants JVL to consider a deposit + lease model. Recurring revenue comes from a **connectivity fee** charged to the operator (server, analytics, machine-up/down telemetry, low-cash alerts) and a **processing fee** on transactions, charged to the player. This mirrors Georgia, where processing fees are already established. Texas is "Wild West" on transactional fees — Juan owes Val a check-back with his Texas contact (Rich Winley) on whether transactional fees are charged today and at what rate. Val's logic: if there's no transactional fee today, somebody is absorbing a real cost (armored truck, labor) — the question is who.

**Income debit-card overlap.** Andrei probed whether the Income debit-card model (Georgia, regulated July 1) — combined with bank ATMs already on every Texas gas station — undermines the Recycler's value. Val and Juan disagreed: most US players strongly prefer cash to plastic; cards are tracked and most ATMs have cameras; "good luck convincing somebody to take a plastic card versus cash in America." In Georgia, the regulated debit-card path has produced a black market — counter clerks legally buy plastic cards back for ~95¢ on the dollar (Val: *"I can absolutely buy your plastic card if you have $100. I could absolutely buy it for $95. You will sell it to me, gladly. There is a market for it"*). Juan added the same dynamic happens with lottery tickets and other regulated payouts — locations get caught and fined but resume the practice. *"Cash rules."* Income has confirmed to JVL it will come into both **Texas and Nebraska** with JVL, layering its existing ~$2.50/transaction fee plus a separate ~$5 ATM fee. ATMs are a separate business already on every Texas gas station.

## Engineering Signals

### Confirmed Signals

- **ADR — e-wallet is the credit-transfer abstraction, not TITO.** The Flex-Recycler will not adopt the TITO/IGT model (server-centric, casino-grade infrastructure). Instead, an "e-wallet" abstraction covers any concrete token form (QR, barcode, PIN, plastic card, photographed-QR, SMS-PIN). Decided jointly by Val and Juan; reasoning: TITO requires server + kilometers of wiring + operations staff (entry-level cost ~$900K, typical >$1.5M), all of which are infeasible at gas-station / convenience-store scale.
- **ADR — no co-installation with bill acceptors at the same location.** A Flex-Recycler site cannot have active machine-side bill acceptors; doing so would drain the Recycler. Mixed-manufacturer floors are supported only via meter-pulse sniffing and Flexport; bill acceptors must be removed. Decided by Val with Juan endorsing.
- **ADR — no co-installation with third-party redemption kiosks (Count-R / Vanilla).** Flex-Recycler deployment requires a JVL-only ecosystem at the location. Decided by Juan with Val endorsing.
- **ADR — voucher seizure semantics.** The e-wallet token is confiscated on machine entry and re-issued on cash-out. Tokens are not transferable across machines without re-issue. Decided by Val.
- **ADR — phase one go-to-market is new-location greenfield deployments.** Pitch route operators bringing fresh sites online (warehouse-prep), not retrofits of existing kiosk-equipped locations. Decided by Juan.
- **CAP — meter-pulse sniffing and bill-acceptor tap-in for mixed-manufacturer floors.** JVL already has this capability; necessary for Flex-Recycler deployment in locations that have non-JVL cabinets. Stated by Juan as established fact.
- **CAP — Flexport cabinet compatibility.** JVL's Flexport touchscreen window has been verified compatible with the four main game-cabinet suppliers in Georgia. Stated by Val as established fact.
- **CAP — dual-recycler-in-one-body for high-activity locations.** JVL's product can house two recyclers in a single chassis, doubling cassette capacity for busy sites. Stated by Val as a known capability.
- **REQ — Player: reliable cash-out.** The Player persona's primary tolerance threshold is a friction-free cash-out at any moment. Any "out of cash" or interruption condition is unacceptable. (Juan's wording, accepted as the persona's red-flag attribute.)
- **REQ — vandal-proofing.** The hardware must withstand physical abuse including the possibility of a machine being kicked, hit, or knocked to the floor. Promoted from persona-attribute discussion to a confirmed requirement input. Existing precedent: Gorilla Glass on existing JVL screens where applicable.
- **REQ — audit tickets at cash drop.** Operators must produce an auditable ticket reflecting the location's cut at the moment of cash collection. Stated by Juan as an existing customer ask. Whether this applies to the Flex-Recycler model (which eliminates manual drops) is open — but the requirement exists in the operator's expectation set.
- **Engineering Solution — auth via QR + PIN pairing.** The e-wallet's security model is the pairing of a QR code (or equivalent token form) with a PIN. Stated by Val: *"the moment you pair QR code with PIN, you have your security."*
- **Engineering Solution — SMS-delivered PIN as printer-free token.** Val proposed SMS as an alternative to physical printing — eliminates the printer bottleneck entirely if accepted as a primary token form.
