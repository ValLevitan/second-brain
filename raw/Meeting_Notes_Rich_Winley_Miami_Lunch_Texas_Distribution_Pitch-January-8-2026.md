## Summary

A three-hour getting-to-know-you lunch that turns into Val's recruitment pitch: stop running five businesses, put on "the JVL hat," and build Texas.

**Rich's current footprint (business).** Ex-Georgia master licensee (partnership ended badly); left Georgia operating because the economics are brutal — new locations demand ~$100K cash up front, seven-eight games at ~$18K each, bonuses, and pay-again contract renewals. Today: a Texas route run through an operator on his behalf, a Pennsylvania skill-game room (state-level legality pending a court opinion; ~80,000 games in PA, he doesn't see them disappearing), and a gift-card venture with a Bahamas technology partner — digital-first **Visa**-branded gift cards where InComm has no digital side: app reload from the player's debit card, one persistent card acting like a player's card, kiosk card readers. He cited InComm's Georgia load volume and ~1,100 installed locations (about a third of the market) at a $2 transaction fee, with reload fees permitted from July. He is also close to Banilla and carries financing debt with them ("my biggest nut is my financing machine, Banilla") plus a Pace-O-Matic distribution agreement in Texas.

**Val's positioning of JVL** (as told to Rich): ~80 employees, most for decades; flat structure ("I like to receive information directly"); everything in-house from idea to implementation; ~40 games in 8-packs across vertical, dual-screen and countertop; more manufacturing capacity than demand. The transactional line is ready as pre-production (50 kiosks in inventory, tooling done, architecture agnostic — TITO or e-wallet, SAS or POS emulation, plus the giveaway SMIB board); first release by June, a rounded product set by December. Pyramid printer integration for the machines ~30 days out. Current markets: South Dakota, Georgia, Nebraska; targets: Texas, Pennsylvania when stable, Alabama HHR with Ainsworth in about a year; Class 2 is on the roadmap.

**The pitch — focus and free cash.** Val's argument, made repeatedly and bluntly: Rich nets a fraction of what he could because he is spread across states and ventures. Val's yardstick — "the only sensor of success is free cash after tax… everything else is just noise" — and his 75-kilometre rucksack analogy: energy is temporary, don't discover at the end that the weight wasn't worth carrying. If the Texas market is really ~5,000 machines a year at ~$15K, that is a ~$75M revenue channel before kiosks and payments — "you're showing me a $75 million business and you want to open a hairdressing salon on the side." At ~2% of a $100M Texas business, Rich pockets ~$2M — against the ~$250K he takes home today. Val's conditions: focus ("I am not prepared to give JVL to somebody who's not focused"), wear the JVL hat with pride, revenue-attached compensation, and real product ownership — shaping features, next generations, and market priorities with engineering, not just sales. Trust framework: mutual assumptions, tested within a year — "if I cannot give you a performing game and you cannot move a performing game, then we're not for each other."

**Market theses Val laid out.**
- *Premium pricing:* JVL can match any cost but shouldn't — "there is no reason discounting the price if people will buy at the price you offer… we should be premium." Demand for variety exceeds supply, so five more competitors wouldn't matter; locations only grow, and growth demands variety.
- *Operator evolution:* manufacturers obsolete machines like iPhones; locations squeeze operator splits downward; over 5–20 years operators morph into location owners and manufacturers sell direct. Georgia is the exception only because vertical integration is banned there.
- *Banilla's Texas numbers doubted:* the "20,000 sold" is largely Georgia software resold and run out-of-state (North Carolina, Alabama) — Rich confirmed the practice from his own route days.
- *Georgia diagnosis:* JVL is out of favor in a gossipy, cliquish market — "we probably didn't do favors for the right people"; Interlot/Interlock reports mis-mark tables so the performance data is unreliable; Felipe is honest and follows up but is "not on the click"; Adam (competitor-side) is excellent — Val tried to recruit him.

**Payment-system caution.** Both share doubts about the incumbent Pyramid QR architecture: operators report players photographing sequential QR codes and scanning ahead to drain kiosks; Val: "I don't think it's a good system… but right now I don't have to be the judge of it, I just need to prove my machines." JVL holds a serious backup thanks to its bill-acceptor heritage and could "switch to a cheaper system" if needed.

**Marketing ideas from Rich.** Skip booths: he once rented a designer boutique for a private shopping night for Georgia masters (free venue, champagne, ego-feeding) — talked about for years; Wisconsin operators rent Caesars cabanas instead of AMOA booths. Val liked it: "whatever's going to create cash."

**Agreed next steps.** Test first, no commitments — "nothing's running away from you": Rich provides three Texas test locations; JVL supplies printer/QR integration for the test; Rich will float the game quietly (Val warned him not to demo it to 50–60 operators at the Texas meeting without an agreement — "at least get paid for your services"); machines possibly at the Feb 5 show; a Texas trip (Austin — where JVL owns a building — and San Antonio) to follow; Rich to call Jamie for the reference; stay in touch next week.
