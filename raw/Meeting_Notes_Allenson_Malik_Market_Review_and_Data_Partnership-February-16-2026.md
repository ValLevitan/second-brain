## Summary

Third meeting in the February operator series, and the most brutally useful operator history lesson of the set. Malik runs 40–50 JVL P1/P2 machines "in every single location" of his route (locations from $5K to $30K), bought through Felipe, and speaks as a friend of JVL whose buying stopped for concrete reasons.

**Why JVL purchases stalled (Malik's history).** When Light & Wonder, Aristocrat and "Prices Right" (IGT) arrived, "they killed the JVL, they killed the Banilla, they killed the Primero." Then Bad Dog took Banilla's tier: 13 games on one board, splittable 7/7, cabinet and board sold separately, price comparable to Banilla. Banilla's Fusion 1–4 "all failed," and Banilla now swaps old boards for ~$1,000 hard-drive updates to defend its base. IGT converted its dead Fort Knox cabinets with $4,000 Cash Eruption software ("we got Price is Right in the name of Cash Eruption") — so operators refresh for $4K instead of buying a $12K JVL. Novomatic ("Nomadic") sells casino-look machines at $8,500 with good early reviews next door to him. And the decisive JVL moment: when Val visited his office years back, the offer was a ~$50K three-machine progressive bundle, take-it-whole-or-nothing with commitment letters — "then we went to IGT and signed with IGT." Val did not remember the episode; Felipe confirmed (~$13K/machine era). Verticals: he tried vertical Peach 1; players did not take to it, cause unknown.

**What actually sells in Georgia (Malik's view).** Not brand: customers send snapshot photos of game screens without knowing the game's name — "it's the math or the way the game plays." Demographics vary by area (he can map area ethnicity, not player gender). On the Aristocrat 94–95% RTP rumor: he doesn't believe it ("otherwise we would not be in business") — though the exchange exposed that he thinks in actual hold (~15%) and says the machines come preset; his techs never touch a setting. He will ask his technician how it even works.

**The gift-card doctrine — the "last connection."** As a master he sides with his locations: the gift card is compulsory July 1, the kiosk is NOT. Locations want the final redemption at the cashier's POS, not a kiosk, because the cashier is the last human connection — "thank you for coming, do you want to buy lottery, chips, soda… you can go to Walmart with this card" — daily customers, verbal upsell, relationship. A kiosk interrupts it. That is "the solution everybody is looking for."

**Digital wallet.** "The best thing" — tap machine to machine, money to the phone, possibly no SAS interface at all; he knew Billy Liu was attempting it but not how far he got. He wants to see JVL's version and agreed to test the phone-wallet product in a few months.

**Attendant-fraud probe (Val).** Val tested the cashier-redemption model's weakness: an attendant keying $120 instead of $100. Malik's answer, at length: register/POS/report reconciliation catches mismatches next day, and the risk is identical to what stores already carry on lottery tickets, beer sales and the money-order machine — a known, accepted exposure, no worse than today's MudaGoose-ticket-for-lottery-ticket dance. "Because it's the same, it's acceptable."

**The data agreement.** Juan asked for per-title performance inside the box (Intralot per-game data is distrusted — inconsistent pay-table IDs even on JVL's own reports). Malik agreed: his technician will photograph service-menu/per-game data at one or two locations "for Felipe… it's not about money, it's about friendship," including competitor machines, with JVL's QR-scan program used on JVL cabinets. Val settled the legality question on the spot: "machines are yours, data is yours, money is yours" — purchased machines, owner shares freely (rented would differ). Malik's one condition: his data must not become a general database traceable to him. Val promised reciprocation; Malik will send Felipe the updated cabinet report.

**Roadmap preview.** Juan showed the new direction: Fiery Ice Link with perceived persistence (Cash Eruption-like), a multi-level progressive line (classic Bally Quick Hits), traditional peach games with new themes, up to 14 titles per box with per-title enable/disable via Felipe (RIS USB configuration). Malik recognised the top six as existing, bottom eight new — and said he wants in on the next software version.

**Expansion tell.** Malik asked unprompted about out-of-state: he is planning to expand and has already met Light & Wonder about pricing. JVL: South Dakota live since October (office open; ~11K-machine market), Nebraska live since January 15 (<5K machines, booming); Nebraska games are Class-3-grade with a Simon Says-style memory skill (no nudge). Pricing details to come via Juan.
