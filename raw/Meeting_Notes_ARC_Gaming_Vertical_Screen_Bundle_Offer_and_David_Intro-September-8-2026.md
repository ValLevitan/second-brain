## Summary

**The machine.** Val re-set expectations on the new vertical-screen machine: ARC has been testing an initial version with a partial game set, not the final build. The final build carries fifteen games and Val expects it through approval by about the middle of September. That is the machine JVL will actually sell them, and Val expects it to test better than what ARC has in the field now.

**The offer Val is considering for Georgia.** A single location set sold as one package: the vertical-screen machine, plus a kiosk (convertible to an ATM but presented as a payment kiosk), plus Flex IQ — the small point-of-sale terminal — plus five additional FlexPorts on top of the port that ships with the machine. Val was blunt about the intent: it has never been done before, and it is a deliberate buy-in to get inroads with transactional equipment before the market settles. He was explicit that he was not asking ARC for anything and knows ARC has its own kiosks — he wanted George's read so ARC would not be surprised, and because he values George's judgement. His two stated worries were making a beginner's mistake in a market he is new to, and cheapening the brand by giving too much away.

**George's read of the Georgia market.** There is room for everything right now. The market is a mess — in the last three or four weeks he has personally seen a stream of different solutions pitched, including what he called the false story about the gift card and the ATM, so one more offer changes nothing and this is exactly the right moment to put something new in front of people. On penetration: Georgia is still overwhelmingly a cash market, most locations take cash, and by his estimate under ten percent of the market uses transactional equipment of any kind, from any vendor. That cuts both ways — large room for growth, but no reliable way to tell which locations actually want equipment. Even inside ARC's own base, not all locations want any solution; some want nothing and care less. Val's counter was that locations can keep doing exactly what they do today, only redeeming onto a card instead of paying cash, which is better for them.

**The kiosk is not the issue.** ARC already has its own ATMs on location. George told Val not to worry about it — the ATM-convertible kiosk is neither a selling point nor a deal breaker for ARC.

**Whether to take it to David Sloan.** Val asked directly whether he should even present the offer to David. George said it does not matter either way and there is nothing to lose — David has seen so many solutions lately that one more makes no difference, and he may well look at it and decide to test it. George described David as a very reasonable man. He has already told David that Val wants to talk and will reach out, so David is expecting contact; George deliberately stepped out of the middle of it. Val initiates, and the two of them set the date and time between themselves.

## Decisions Made

- The Georgia bundle offer stays on the table as a market-entry play: one machine + kiosk + Flex IQ POS + five additional FlexPorts as a single location set. George's counsel — nothing to lose, right timing — did not change the shape of it.
- The kiosk is to be presented as a payment kiosk, not as an ATM, even though it converts.
- Val approaches David Sloan (ARC CEO) directly. George has made the introduction and stepped aside; Val initiates by note, call or text and they agree date and time between them.
- ARC's own kiosks and ATMs are not treated as an obstacle to the offer.
