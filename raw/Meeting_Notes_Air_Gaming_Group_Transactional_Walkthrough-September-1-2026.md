## Summary

Val established early that his visitor was there for training rather than to decide, and asked directly who makes the decision — Karim, the sole owner. He then handed the demo to Andrei and let it run, intervening on strategy.

**The product ladder, presented as a Lego set.** Andrei took them up the range: **Flex-IQ** as the entry device for a location that has nothing (cashier signs in, transaction appears automatically, no manual entry, card activated by barcode, receipt printed) — described as "a quick solution for locations with MudaGoose" that just need to comply. Next step: **Flex ports on every machine** so a player can move between machines, with redemption at the kiosk. Then the **kiosk**, configurable with different modules inside — printer, bill acceptor, card dispenser, cash dispenser.

Val's framing, repeated twice, was the sales spine of the meeting: *"Think about it as a Lego set, and you and your location and your players. You choose the Lego pieces that you want in your sandbox."* No PIN, PIN, plastic card, paper ticket, QR to phone — all optional. He was explicit that he would state his own preference if asked but that it is not his call.

**Val's recommendation, when asked.** A **standalone self-service kiosk** that takes the money, issues the e-wallet and exchanges it for a card — because it grows with the operator, needs no attendant, and stands next to the machines. Later he refined it: bill acceptor, printer and cartridge dispenser in a self-service kiosk, validators left in the machines, Flex ports added, and **no ATM function until GLC and its counsel rule on cash dispensing** — advice he gave against his own product interest.

**Pricing given (retail, explicitly).** Flex port ~$1,000 each; Flex-IQ ~$1,000; kiosk with bill acceptor and printer, no dispenser ~$5,000; with card dispenser more; **with cash dispenser ~$12,000**; plus **$90 per month per location** for connection. Val was careful to say this is street retail for a single unit, and that a real customer would sit down and talk.

**Compatibility.** Compatible with all Class III manufacturers — Light & Wonder, IGT, Aristocrat, Novomatic named, plus Banilla. The unit is fully self-contained (no interface board, no power supply), fits any Class III machine, connects to the second SAS port, ~5-minute install, with custom brackets supplied pre-designed per machine type and drawings provided so the customer can make their own.

**Where it got real — Texas.** The visitor raised fraud on their Texas machines: tickets are being copied. Val's answer was the strongest part of the pitch. Copying works elsewhere because validation is local — the kiosk cannot tell a reprinted number from a real one, so it honours both. JVL's cloud is the source of truth: the printer asks the cloud what serial to issue, the cloud issues it with a value, and at redemption the reader asks the cloud whether that encrypted number is legitimate. Andrei added the principle plainly — the source of truth must exist somewhere outside the location and outside any piece of its hardware. Val offered a **guarantee against ticket duplication** ("I can actually give you a guarantee that people cannot copy tickets... I can sign my name on it") and proposed running an **experiment in Texas**. Air Gaming has about **100 Texas locations**, and their current Big Daddy kiosks are being hacked.

**Regulatory limits stated honestly.** Card dispensing is approved and could ship in a week or two. **ATM cash dispensing is not approved.** Whether the ATM ends up inside the kiosk or as a mandatory separate component is the Lottery's call, not JVL's.

**Data privacy objection.** The visitor worried about vendors seeing which locations perform well. Val's response was that the horse has bolted — JVL, InComm and GLC already hold that information, the data in the cloud is encrypted, and the concern belongs with GLC rather than with JVL. He made the point with a crude analogy that landed badly enough that Andrei noted the visitor did not like it.

**Sales angle.** Removing per-machine printers in favour of one printer per location was pitched as a direct cost saving. Val's commercial argument for Flex ports: a player who has to cash out and walk to the attendant keeps his winnings; a player who can move freely between machines keeps playing. New machines ship with cell modems, giving betting-trend and game-popularity data — Val gave the example of raising the minimum bet from 25 to 75 cents between 6 and 8 PM when a machine is busy.

Val closed by pushing for the real meeting: *"Let's make a deal. I'm here next week. Let's set up a meeting in your office."* The visitor agreed to call, and they settled on **Wednesday or Thursday** the following week — Val is back Tuesday and leaves Friday.

## Decisions Made

- **Nothing bought** — the attendee was there for training and is not the decision maker; Karim decides.
- **Val advised waiting on ATM/cash-dispensing** until GLC and its lawyer rule, and recommended the self-service kiosk without ATM in the meantime.
- **Follow-up meeting agreed at Air Gaming's office**, Wednesday or Thursday the following week.
- **Texas named as the near-term opening** — ticket-copying fraud is a live problem there and dispensing is legal.
