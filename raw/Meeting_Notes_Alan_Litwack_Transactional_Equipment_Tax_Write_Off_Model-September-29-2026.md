## Summary

**Background.** Val said JVL got into transactional equipment, a subject he knows well from CashCode days and from the Gaming Standards Association board. Georgia's new law requires player winnings to go to a prepaid Mastercard or Visa debit card. Under the US "big beautiful bill", slot machines get a one-year write-off. Alan said Carney is doing a similar thing in Canada for some asset types (computers, mining): write-off in the year the asset comes into use.

**The model.** A friend of Val's who makes a lot of cash wants a write-off. JVL's equipment retails at $15,000 and costs $4,000. The friend pays $4,000 now and takes a $15,000 write-off that year; he owes the rest to JVL over time. With that cash JVL produces the equipment and gives it to an operator almost free, in exchange for a 5-year contract at $150 a month. There is no profit participation. Val said this could let JVL monopolize the market. The machines will be in various Georgia locations, run by a third-party operator. Legally the friend may need an active business to write off the full amount.

**Alan's concerns.** Alan first struggled to follow the model. He said it is fine as long as JVL gets the full $15,000 within some period. Val said the write-off is the friend's issue, and he does not want to spend money researching it. Alan: JVL must not be accused of helping someone disguise something with the IRS. It needs to be checked enough, and structured better. Alan will need his US people to look at how the write-off works.

## Decisions Made

- **The structure must be checked enough that no one (IRS) can say JVL helped disguise anything; the deal must be structured better.** (Alan advised; Val agreed "understood")
