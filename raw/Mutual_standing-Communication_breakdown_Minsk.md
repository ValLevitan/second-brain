# Mutual Standing: Communication Breakdown in Minsk

**Date:** 2026-03-05
**Participants:** Val Levitan, Pavel Frolov
**Based on:** [[Describing the cultural problem in Minsk]] and [[communication_break_down_Minsk-March-5-2026|Teams discussion]]

---

## What We Agree On

1. **Feedback loops exist, but they are fragmented by level.** One loop operates at the executive level, others exist between developers, QA, and Pavel/Irina. Each segment communicates within itself, but signals between segments travel poorly.

2. **On clear points of contention, Minsk does speak up.** Contentious issues trigger meetings — almost always live conversations, not comment threads. Roughly 90% of decisions on services and GUI went through Val, Juan, and the team, often over several iterations. Some form of two-way dialogue does exist.

3. **The problem is not contention — it is the everyday feedback loop.** Points of contention are black and white, easy to resolve. The permanent state of work lives outside contention, in the continuous flow of smaller decisions and interpretations. That is where the loop breaks.

4. **The current model is hierarchical, and signals get filtered at each level.** Information moves vertically — manager to subordinates — and each layer filters what passes through. The break point is between the Minsk middle layer (Pavel, Irina) and North America: the accumulated context from developers does not transfer upward across the boundary.

5. **Architectural decisions percolated in Minsk are usually not documented.** These decisions are not made in a vacuum or in a minute. The team sits on subjects, deliberates, and arrives at fundamental choices that affect the company for years — and those decisions, along with the reasoning behind them, are usually not documented or communicated to North America.

6. **Small local decisions accumulate into critical changes.** Individually, many questions seem minor enough to resolve locally. But when undocumented, they accumulate into changes that can be perceived as deviations from the original requirements.

## Where We Landed

The diagnosis shifted from "Minsk is silent" to something more precise: **Minsk communicates internally, but the output of that communication — especially architectural decisions and accumulated local judgments — does not cross the boundary to North America in documented form.** The feedback loops are not absent; they are fragmented and filtered. The task is not to create communication from nothing, but to bridge the segments so that what is already being discussed in Minsk becomes visible to North America.

Pavel expressed genuine willingness to improve and simplify processes, increase reliability, and bring more transparency. The next step is to define what "bridging the segments" looks like in practice.
