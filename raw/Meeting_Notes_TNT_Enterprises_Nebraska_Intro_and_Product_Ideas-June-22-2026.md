## Summary

Val's owner-to-operator introduction after his Nebraska trip missed them in person. Pitch frame: "you have access to the owner… I can bypass a lot of red tape."

**Status.** No open complaints: Dennis has handled the small things; the ticket-printing failure and hand-pay issues are resolved. South Dakota software update (with Keno games) coming ~mid-to-end August pending approvals — Nebraska can't take Keno (the state runs its own Keno and stays away).

**The real pain (theirs): the state, not the manufacturer.** Nebraska's 1099 requirements and the state "wager system" hinder players; TNT turned hand-pays off entirely so nothing routes through the wager system — big wins are paid in cash, traceable only inside the machine, which the state cannot access.

**Val's three ideas (explicitly "shooting from the hip — don't kill me"):**
1. *Split-win constructs.* Pay $1,199 and hold the remainder in an electronic wallet metering out below the threshold; or a declared "betting partnership" formed at game start (contractually splitting a $2,500 win three ways). TNT's reactions: it would be worked/abused hard, needs digging into Nebraska rules and regs, "in theory it's good" — parked for thought. Miko's caution: a regulation would have to affirmatively allow it.
2. *Promotional credits.* $2 promo per $20 in, on the SAS promo meter, non-cashable (play-through only), shown vividly on screen — Val did this on 1980s horse-racing machines with "smashing success." TNT: "that would go over well — every player wants free credits"; accounting side workable ("it's just another meter").
3. *The recycler kiosk.* Replace their dispensing kiosks: player validates cash at the kiosk, gets a QR temporary wallet, roams machine to machine (free SMIB board in each machine, small optical reader), cashes out from the same recycling cassettes — no bill acceptors or printers in machines, no refill runs, shorter collection pattern (swap cassette and go), full per-machine reporting cheaper than their current fee. TNT: "interesting theory… that's a good idea"; they want printed reports available.

Close: all three ideas parked with TNT; they have Val's cell and email.
