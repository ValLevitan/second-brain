# 02-27 FlexPay Cash Kiosk Rollout: Two-Tier Product Strategy and Texas Market Entry via Brokerage Model

## Core Synopsis

Val and Rich Winley have finalized the commercial framework for a two-tier kiosk rollout in Texas. The immediate play is the "FlexPay" unit — a cash-dispensing kiosk configured with a Fujitsu dispenser, standard bill acceptor, and TITO printer, priced at approximately $11,000 retail. Rich Winley will broker these units on an 8% commission basis without holding inventory, positioning himself as the sales conduit to Texas operators while Val retains manufacturing and invoicing control. The second-wave product — a $9,000 fully automated variant equipped with a bill recycler and networked connectivity — will eliminate on-site labor but carries a $3-per-day service fee for remote monitoring, a cost Rich Winley deems acceptable given existing market benchmarks of $0.25 per machine per day. Rich Winley is presenting this proposition to a high-value prospect controlling 80-90 convenience store locations on Sunday, and we are betting on field performance to validate pricing, not theoretical calculations. The April timeline for full rollout remains intact, contingent on weekend testing of four pilot machines currently deployed across gas stations and game rooms in Georgia.

## The Commercial Architecture: Brokerage Model & Pricing Tiers

### The Distribution Agreement

Val has rejected the stocking distributor model outright. Rich Winley will operate as a pure broker: soliciting orders, routing them to Val for manufacturing and direct shipment to operators, and collecting an 8% commission ($880 per $11,000 unit) upon invoicing. Val acknowledged the commission as "a little bit fat" but accepted the markup under the rationale that "a man has to live." This structure decouples capital risk from sales velocity — Rich Winley bears no inventory exposure, and Val retains full control over production scheduling and operator billing. Quantity discounts remain undefined but were explicitly flagged as negotiable for serious operators.

### Product Tier 1: The "FlexPay" Cash Kiosk

The introductory configuration targets immediate market acceptance by mirroring familiar hardware patterns:

- **Core Components:** Fujitsu cash dispenser, standard bill acceptor, TITO printer.
- **Retail Price:** $11,000 (subject to volume discounting).
- **Strategic Rationale:** Val explicitly stated, "I don't want to shock the market," opting to deploy the Fujitsu dispenser — the only dispenser Rich Winley's network has historically used — to minimize operator retraining friction. This design avoids the bill acceptor/printer elimination strategy initially demonstrated, prioritizing adoption speed over operational novelty.

### Product Tier 2: The Fully Automated Unit

The second-generation unit will strip labor overhead by removing all in-machine peripherals:

- **Core Components:** Bill recycler, standard printer, networked connectivity via cellular modem.
- **Retail Price:** ~$9,000.
- **Operational Impact:** Operators can eliminate bill acceptors and printers from connected gaming machines. The bill recycler auto-replenishes, removing the need for manual cash stocking. Val claimed this configuration achieves "practically 100%" labor elimination outside of periodic cash collections.
- **Service Model:** A $3-per-day connectivity fee (~$1,095/year) per FlexPay terminal will fund remote monitoring and real-time operational data delivered to operators' mobile devices. Rich Winley confirmed this fee is competitive, citing existing market rates of $0.25/day per gaming machine (implying FlexPay consolidates monitoring across 12+ machines per terminal to justify the premium). Cellular modem subscription logistics remain unresolved — Rich Winley offered to connect Val with a telecom vendor he's fielded calls from "for three years."

## The Texas Sales Pipeline: Pilot Validation & High-Value Prospect

### Georgia Pilot Status

Four FlexPay units are undergoing final validation this weekend before commercial deployment next week:

- **Deployment Plan:** 1 unit → Gas Station A, 1 unit → Gas Station B, 2 units → a single game room operator.
- **Technical Status:** "Everything's scanning in and out," and transactions are processing. Rich Winley confirmed the units passed basic functionality tests, though the weekend represents the true stress test under sustained operator usage.
- **Market Signal:** Val anchored the entire pricing discussion to this pilot, stating, "If your collections are good, you're gonna sell it. If collections are not good, you're not gonna sell it for any money." We are using empirical cash flow data, not feature lists, to determine commercial viability.

### The Sunday Meeting: 80-90 Location Operator

Rich Winley disclosed escalating inbound demand via the website, with multiple parties now requesting gaming machine availability. The marquee opportunity is a Sunday/Monday meeting with an operator controlling "80-90 locations, all convenience stores," who is exploring bulk gaming machine purchases for route deployment. Rich Winley is preparing a formal presentation borrowing graphics from Val's website, with Val offering direct access to in-house graphic designers for customized sales collateral. Val confirmed no supply-side constraints: "There is no shortage of games."
