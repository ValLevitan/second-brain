## Summary

The proper move: prop the company with new cash. **Litwack's design: a secured convertible debenture.**
- New money enters as **debt, secured, at mezzanine rates**, with warrants or conversion rights **at today's value — "in the toilet"** (penny-a-share order of magnitude).
- Downside: money comes out first (as before). Upside: conversion at the depressed valuation is massively dilutive to everyone who didn't participate — including Val himself pro-rata, which is precisely the protection: **make the identical offer to every holder** ("here is what anybody who invests gets — do you want to participate?"), so nobody can complain in 20/20 hindsight that Val gave himself too good a deal. The narrative to circulate: how we got here, why we kept working "so we could realize value for our shareholders," we're on the doorstep, terms must be favorable to raise the money, everyone may participate.
- Math check on the call: at ~58% current ownership (46% dilution when all warrants counted), a $1M-for-50% round with no other participants takes Val to ~75%+.
- Comparable Litwack told: a client company that raised for years at $1/share "always turning the corner," then took VC money via convertible prefs — original common now marked at one cent, a 99% decline. "That's what happens sometimes."
