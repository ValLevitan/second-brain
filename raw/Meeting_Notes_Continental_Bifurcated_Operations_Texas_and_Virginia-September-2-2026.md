## Summary

The opening segment of a visit Val and Andrei started in the parking lot waiting for Felipe. Salim runs **two companies** and was reached through Felipe the day before — he had been having issues with JVL and called Felipe about them.

**Scale.** Roughly **150 locations** between the two companies, close to a thousand machines in Georgia, plus about **1,500 machines outside Georgia — primarily Texas**, with Virginia as well.

**The core insight of the segment: the business is bifurcated by regulation.** Val opened with the assumption that Texas has a duplicate-ticket problem; Salim's answer reframed the whole conversation. Outside Georgia they run no ticket system at all — it is "still MudaGoose", connected to a printer, plus their own in-house SAS reporting system built for their own use. Payout is manual at a cash register, not by dispenser, because there is no regulation requiring otherwise. The machines themselves differ by state: **Texas has "win your money back"** (lose a bet, get the option to win the dollar back) and **Virginia has a free-play mechanic** where the player can see before placing the bet whether it will win.

**Why Georgia needs replay and the others do not.** Salim gave the clearest explanation in the batch of why operators want a replay machine, and it is a reporting artefact rather than a player feature. If a player puts in $100, plays $10 and cashes out $90, then carries that $90 to the next machine, the Georgia Lottery counts $190 in and $170 out. The cash never left, but the report shows inflated in-and-out on every hop, which upsets the stores. In Texas no authority asks, so nobody needs replay: "In Texas, there is no authority that's asking us to do whatever you want."

**What he uses in Georgia.** Not TITO — **Axcess**, started seven or eight years ago when there was nothing else in the market, now covering about 75% of his estate. He is looking at **Novomatic** as an alternative to Axcess, not because Axcess is failing but to have something in reserve; he rates Novomatic's user interface as much better. Cost comparison he gave: Axcess about $15 per machine plus $3,000–$4,000 for the system, versus Novomatic effectively free apart from printers.

**Where JVL could sell.** Val moved quickly to the opening: Georgia purchasing is closed ("Now we're not buying anymore"), but **Texas is open for refurbished machines** provided they carry the out-of-state feature. Salim was willing to try **five or ten** — explicitly not one, because a single machine proves nothing — with the benchmark that nothing yet has come close to Banilla. If a machine did 150% of Banilla's performance they would replace Banilla with it.

The segment ends mid-conversation with Val saying he does not follow the point being made; it continues in Segment 2.
