## Summary

Val's first face-to-face meeting with the Georgia Lottery COAM staff. He brought breakfast, spent the first twenty minutes on his own background, and then asked two direct questions: can JVL build Class A machines without damaging this relationship, and is JVL's transactional equipment legal under the prize-card rules.

**Background Val gave.** Born in Minsk, family left the Soviet Union on 25 May 1980, a week before the border closed; through Rome to Toronto by September 1980. Diploma in digital electronics. Could not find work, so started in coin-operated arcade games. In 1990 a customer proposed building a bill acceptor; Val declined, his father took the deal, and the company became very successful and was sold to CPI in 2006. Val sat on the board of the Gaming Standards Association in Las Vegas from 2000 to 2006. He came to Georgia COAM during COVID, was asked by a large Georgia operator to supply machines, and had a machine in the state five months later. His daughter Monica is a lawyer and works as counsel.

**Class A — the main question.** Val has played a Class A machine in a location (a Primero) and was confused by it. He asked plainly whether building Class A would make enemies at the Lottery. The answer was no: Class A is lawful today, and a manufacturer entering it is "playing within the rules." But the staff were candid about the limits of that answer. Class A machines are not connected to a central accounting system, there is no certification process, and there is no book of Class A regulations for Val to read. Until about a year ago a Class A licence went to anyone with a pulse who paid the fee. The staff said they may ask questions, and would like to see specifications and a technical demonstration of how a machine functions, even though they have no legal right to demand them.

They also warned Val about timing. The legislature may address Class A in the January session. One staff member made the point that the more manufacturers who enter Class A between now and the end of April, the more attention it draws and the more urgency the legislature will feel to act. Another observed that when the legislature does open a bill, everything but the kitchen sink gets thrown in and the whole thing gets muddied.

Val's own position: he is not going after Class A customers, he is going after Class B customers, because a licensed operator is regulated and has more to lose. His personal view is that Class A should be regulated, legislated and taxed, and that this would be good for the business.

**Prize cards, ATMs and the Joe Kim letter.** Val described JVL's transactional line — a point-of-sale device, AFT ports on every machine over SAS, and a processing unit that takes cash and issues what looks like a TITO ticket but functions as a temporary electronic wallet with a QR or barcode. The player carries it between machines, then cashes out at the POS, an attendant, or a self-serve kiosk that validates the ticket and dispenses a card. Val showed this at the trade show and accepts it "could have been misunderstood, or I've created too much excitement."

The staff explained the rule as they now read it: the gift card cannot be exchanged for currency **at the location where the prize is won**. Taking that card elsewhere — another store, a different gift card, an ATM across the street — is not what the statute prohibits. Val pushed on this, noting every store in Georgia already has an ATM. The staff acknowledged the awkwardness; one said of the letter, "Welcome to our world."

**InComm.** Val said he ran his business model past InComm two years ago and was told on the record they were fine with it, and he feels misled. The staff pushed back gently and fairly: their own counsel's position shifted only recently, so InComm was probably giving Val correct guidance at the time. InComm has a black prize-play card and a white card with ATM functionality; based on Joe Kim's recent comments, they may keep one product and drop the other. Val said he is now being approached by other card providers, and that "everyone is focused on taking business away from InComm — money is not in issuing a card, it's in managing cash." He described the relationship as having become political and said a lunch with three of them was "extremely political" and they told him uncomfortable things. One staff member confirmed the wider mood: "we're definitely in an uncomfortable period of time."

Val has asked for a meeting with Joe Kim three times through a general inquiry address and got only an acknowledgement that his message was directed to an appropriate department. The staff offered to give him a direct phone number and email and to connect him with the right people.

**Equipment demonstration.** Val offered to bring his transactional equipment in and set it up. The Lottery's own lab has limited space and a heat problem — "we put too many games in there, gets real hot" — so they host prototypes at Intralot instead, where they work with Bill. Val asked for a binary answer and got one, and agreed to set it up at Intralot. They also mentioned they have an older version of the JVL cabinet and had asked about getting an updated one.

Val is not going to G2E ("I can't afford it"). The staff apologised for missing the JVL booth at the Southern Amusement Expo. Val closed by asking everyone to scan his QR card and send him their details.

## Decisions Made

- **JVL will not start Class A production before the January 2027 legislative session.** Val stated it twice and it was his final position: "I doubt very much that I can do anything until January, so it will probably hear the legislation before even doing anything." His stated sequence is to have this conversation first, hear the legislation, understand where the Lottery stands, then decide. (Decided by Val)
- **The Georgia Lottery confirmed that manufacturing Class A is lawful today and would not damage JVL's standing with them.** Qualified: their understanding may change with the legislation, and Val acknowledged he is not relying on them for it. (Decided by Georgia Lottery staff)
- **The transactional equipment demonstration will be set up at Intralot, not at the Georgia Lottery building.** Val asked for a binary answer and confirmed, "I'll set it up with Intralot." (Decided by Val and Georgia Lottery staff)
- **JVL targets Class B customers, not Class A customers** — Val's stated commercial frame for anything he builds. (Decided by Val)

## Open Questions

- **Should JVL develop ATM or cash-out capability?** Val asked directly — "should I be working on ATMs, or should I not be" — and did not get an answer; the conversation moved to InComm instead.
- **What is InComm's new product?** One staff member had heard InComm is working on something to get around Joe Kim's recent letter, but did not know what it is.
- **Will the January 2027 session restrict Class A?** Nobody in the room would predict it. The staff expect the question to be addressed and warned that a crowded bill could make it worse.
- **Will the Lottery's prize-card position shift again?** Their counsel's stance changed recently, and they said openly that a meeting held two weeks earlier would have produced a different answer.
