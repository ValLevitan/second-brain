## Summary

**Who Lucky Coin is.** Third-generation family business: founded early '90s by Kyle's grandfather (Jones, Tucker/Scottdale; died 2021) who got in after seeing Butch Fochet dump machine cash on a card table; Kyle's grandmother owned it, his mother owns it now; Kyle (41 next week) runs it with younger brother Curtis; their father (63) still works the warehouse. ~50 locations, ~250 machines, all over Georgia. Val almost certainly did business with the grandfather through JVL and CashCode (bill acceptors, eight-liners, countertops). Buying criteria: cash-in/revenue and **service reliability**. Redemption stack: EasyLink + GBT kiosks (30–40% of locations), PAX handhelds elsewhere. Class A: skipped it initially (distrusted Jinko/Primero motives), now sees it as complementary and would place some.

**The service complaints — Val took them straight on.** JVL games generate more service calls than other brands. Specifics: a menu button that throws the machine "out of whack" forcing a flash; Intralot decommission/recommission turning 15-minute fixes into two-hour ordeals (Val saw it himself the day before); the trial game's QR-code stats scan appearing to fail (actually a one-day processing delay — misunderstanding now solved by modems); Altair legacy units beyond help ("Altair needs to come out"); and — hardest — JVL support "never takes accountability", refuses third-party integration help (vs Banilla, who "bend over backwards"), and tech Aaron was rude to Felipe. Val's answers: machines should not break, period; he now finally understands the flash/recommission burden and will address it, but will not promise a date; meanwhile he offered his own techs on the road, replacement inventory, a free software upgrade for Kyle's new machine, replacement of the problem machine, and gave Kyle his private number. He also explained WHY resets exist (component allow-listing against validator-swap fraud) and offered to remove features Kyle does not need — and to report Kyle's actual validator acceptance rates via modem.

**The offer — buying the way in.** For every location that buys the new machine ($15,750 list): **6 Flex ports + 1 POS/slim terminal free per location** (~$7,000 value → effective machine price just under $9,000), $90/month per location (not per machine), full reporting, money deposited automatically. Kyle to be the transactional "guinea pig" in tolerant locations; JVL will "babysit" the first location overnight until it runs. ATM cash-out: Val meets the lottery corporation Thursday for breakfast; InComm asked JVL to hold the ATM feature back two years — Val may wait only a month, seeing no legal grounds. Kyle: "If it could spit out cash, I'd throw that in every one of my locations."

**Next step.** Val visits the Lucky Coin office **Wednesday, September 9 at 8:30 AM** to meet Curtis, the technicians, and route guys (Kyle: "they'll tell you exactly what they're dealing with"). Kyle sends the invite. Post-meeting, Val told Felipe the emotional reaction to service criticism is useless — take the feedback, fix the problem ("Val's stated read: 50 locations, 30 could be exclusively JVL equipment from the start, 30 machines").
