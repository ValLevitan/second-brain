## Summary

This was Val's first call with Sergei, a contractor brought in by Andrei. The first part was an introduction. Sergei studied at BSU in Belarus. He worked about three years on startups with his brother, who was a developer at Meta. He has not been an employee for three or four years. He has known Andrei for about twenty years, through his brother.

**Terms, as Sergei stated them.** He aims for about half of a full-time load (about 20 hours a week of a 40-hour week). He bills **$37.50 an hour**. He says the rate is open to review each month with Andrei. If he goes over the agreed hours, he takes it from his own time. His real goal is to build his own products. He works for JVL partly because, as a resident there, he must show income and pay tax every month.

**The task Andrei gave him.** Andrei sent him a list of more than 7,000 Georgia COAM locations. The goal was to find contacts and get consent from each location so JVL can contact them. Sergei found some contact (phone or email) for about **50% of the locations**. Most are not decision-makers. They are often just the phone of a gas station from Google Maps. Sergei said he does not want sales work: calls, outreach, email threads, travel and networking wear him out. He is good at data, analysis, automation and AI tools.

**Val's answer.** Val said Sergei does not need to do any sales work. If outreach is needed, Val wants a service or an AI engine to do it. Val stressed that there are **federal restrictions**. JVL cannot contact the locations, and nobody can do it in JVL's name. Any third party must not say it is linked to JVL. Val does not want to call locations even if they opt in. His target model is inbound: locations call JVL, and JVL's AI voice takes the call, qualifies and closes the client.

Val explained the four parties: the location, the master operator (buys the machines and runs the locations), the player, and JVL as the manufacturer that sells to the master operator. The location tells the operator what it wants, but often does not know what it wants. **What Val wants from Sergei:** a reliable opt-in list of locations, and a way to find (by geography or other means) people who are most likely to play at those locations. Sergei described the bottom-up path: players ask at the store for JVL machines, and the location owners then ask their operator.

Sergei asked about Class A and Class B machines. Val said the split is roughly right but did not want to go deep into the law. Sergei asked whether a location can work with only one operator. This was not answered on the recording.

**How to work.** Both said they do not yet have a clear view of how to do this. Val said he sees some parts at 30% or 60%, but nothing fully. Sergei proposed short iterations instead of a large block like 100 hours: about 5 to 10 hours over about two weeks, with short check-in calls with Andrei. After each step they decide the next step or stop. Val agreed and called these "spikes", a normal practice. Val said they can also stop and say "we tried, it did not work."

Sergei keeps his research in a separate Google Drive with restricted access. He offered to send Val the link. Val asked him to go through Andrei first, and to bring Val only what is useful.

- **The work runs as short R&D spikes** (about 5–10 hours over about two weeks), reviewed with Andrei. After each spike they continue, change course, or stop. (Val Levitan, Sergei Vysotski)
- **Sergei reports through Andrei, not to Val directly.** Andrei reviews the research and brings useful results to Val. (Val Levitan)
