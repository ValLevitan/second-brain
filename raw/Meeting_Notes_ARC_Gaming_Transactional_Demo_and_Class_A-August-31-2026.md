## Summary

Long, candid working session with George Zazanis. Main threads:

**Data and reports.** Both sides dismissed the Ailers-type magazine reports as manufacturer-fed and unverifiable; ARC diversifies machine placement across location tiers to keep its own averages honest. Val explained JVL's data-buyback model ($337/year/machine via the new LTE modems).

**InComm exposure and daily processing.** George walked through ARC's broker role: ~$10K/day exposure cap per location, daily ACH swipes against location bank accounts off InComm's daily file, manual reconciliation — labor-heavy and two days behind. Val pitched JVL's cloud processing: automatic reconciliation against the InComm report, automatic ACH, automatic posting into accounting, same-day money into the operator's bank, plus the ability to remotely disable machines for non-paying locations. George was visibly interested ("I like the idea"), especially a free swap for the ~90 PAX locations paying $13.99/month/machine.

**The kiosk lock-in.** ARC is stuck: a previous ARC CEO signed a ~20-year kiosk agreement with his own other company (EXIO connection; "Bill", friends with David Sloan), ARC pays monthly and cannot exit — "it is what it is." Val proposed working around it: keep the kiosk shells, replace the PC board with JVL's, remove per-machine TITO printers (ARC's #1 service headache is printer jams) in favor of barcode readers, add one printer in the kiosk. Any approach must come through David (ARC CEO) instructing Bill — George warned Bill would otherwise steal the idea.

**Class A.** ARC is aggressively expanding in Georgia Class A (partner: Primero; $50-per-spin cap worked around via a two-bucket win system; unlimited licenses and machines per location; no lottery tax). Their top Class A site is a 24-hour tire shop near Blue Ridge doing ~$10K/day on ~40 machines. GLC's reaction is the open risk; a linked progressive jackpot is on hold at GLC. **Val committed: "I will have a Class A deal within three months — January; we're going to do business."** Val also plans to visit and film a friendly Class A location. Parallel: German AWP market (Gauselmann/Novomatic, 150k machines) as the model.

**Market intel.** United (owner Bandi) rumored near bankruptcy and shedding top locations; Lucky Box's earlier collapse followed the same loan-then-money-disappears pattern. Honest masters per George: Bad Dog (Brian Deaton), and above all **Shonfellows — the most politically connected operator in Georgia** ("if you need one relationship, choose him"). Ultra (CEO Ray Constantino) — the owner's wife actually runs it; meet at their office. GLC: Chris Bosnick (now director) is the operational man to know; Mike Parham retiring. ARC: 200+ locations, #2–3 in the state. DC market: ARC has ~27 locations, one bad manufacturer (Omega), wants JVL to license there; Light & Wonder and Primero being pulled in.
