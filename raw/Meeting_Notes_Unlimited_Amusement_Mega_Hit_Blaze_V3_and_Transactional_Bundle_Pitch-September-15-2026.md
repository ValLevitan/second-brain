## What happened

JVL pitched the Mega Hit Blaze V3 cabinet packaged with the transactional hardware suite to a Georgia operator. The customer knows the market well and pushed back on practical points throughout. It closed warmly but without a commitment beyond a single trial location.

## The offer as presented

- **Hardware at zero cost.** The full transactional suite — tabletop POS, self-serve kiosk, embedded controllers — comes with the machine purchase. No upfront capex for the transactional layer.
- **$90 per machine per month**, covering everything under discussion: cloud vault, software updates, and interactive dashboards showing the whole estate. The dashboard also alerts on faults such as a bill acceptor degrading.
- Val positioned this against a competitor quoted at **$10 per machine** for a far narrower service, arguing that once a location starts asking the operator to pay, the operator should be paying for the thing that costs least — and that nobody else in the market gives the equipment away.
- On wallets: Apple Pay or Google Pay is possible in principle but sits with the integrator, and Val was explicit that it would cost considerably more.

## The customer's position

- They want to **start with one location** and see how it performs before going further. Their reasoning is how the market actually spreads: a machine that works in one room brings calls from the rooms nearby, exactly as they saw with Diamond Link.
- They were candid about not knowing which card provider they will end up with — the InComm question is open for them.
- They raised the daily-cap and jackpot-validation problem, which Val answered with the cloud database holding the record and updating on the spot.

## Val's counter-argument, worth keeping

Val accepted the single-location trial but pressed for something else alongside it: **schedule the rollout across all their locations month by month, in advance.** His reason was supply, not sales pressure — JVL is making the same offer to every operator, so inventory and rollout capacity will tighten, and an operator who has not reserved a slot will struggle to get machines when they want them.
