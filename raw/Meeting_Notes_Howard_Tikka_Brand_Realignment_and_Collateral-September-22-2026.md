## Summary

Val called Howard Tikka, whom he has known for about 31 years and who did JVL's brand work roughly 30 years ago, to ask him to pull JVL's look back together. A long catch-up took up about a third of the call; the business part is short and clear.

**What Val wants.** One uniform style and look across everything JVL does: stationery printed and digital, paper stock, business cards, trade show booths, trade show attire, advertising, and a style guide to hold it together. Val was explicit about the limits — **the logo cannot be touched and the slogan cannot be touched**. They carry his father's name and meaning. This is collection and rearrangement, not a rebrand. Howard asked twice whether Val wanted an incremental evolution or a big leap; Val said no leap is needed. His words: for many years there was "no chef in the kitchen", so everything got disjointed and lost, and the pieces just need collecting, dusting and putting back in order.

**Why now.** Val said plainly that JVL is still small, he cannot get it to the point where he could retire, and he is back on the road meeting clients himself. He is running engineering, sales and marketing at once and has nobody who knows the company well enough to fill out the website, which he called clean but only partly informative.

**The market story Val gave Howard.** Class III casino gaming is a mature, saturated market — since COVID, casinos found they can earn the same with 3,000–4,000 machines instead of 5,000, so they are not buying. The growth is in street markets: Georgia (47,000 machines, 7,400 locations), Nebraska, South Dakota VLT — small mom-and-pop locations. Val framed this as JVL going back to where it started, from a more advanced position. The same countertop machines now also sell direct to homes through Amazon at about $4,000 — "not killing it, but okay". JVL is also expanding into transactional products, which Val compared loosely to the CashCode direction. He wants to show the product at c-store trade shows and home and design shows, because the push-pull plan with operators, locations and players gives JVL access to players for the first time.

**Also mentioned.** JVL is headquartered in Georgia with manufacturing there; Val said he and the company are in the process of departing Canada and moving to the States. He gave an example of a 25-year-old relationship turning into an order of a container of machines a month for a year, from the CEO of the second-largest company in Georgia.

**Where it landed.** Howard said he does not have much free time — he works from home twice a week and can fit in an hour here and two hours there — but will think about how to do the work and send Val a proposal. Val asked him to work out how many hours he has available and come back.

## Decisions Made

- **The logo and the slogan are off the table.** They are non-negotiable for Val — his father's company — so the work is unification, not a redesign. (Decided by Val Levitan)
- **No rebrand, incremental only.** Val rejected changing colours, fonts or direction; JVL's name and history still register with people, the look just needs collecting and rearranging. (Decided by Val Levitan, after Howard Tikka offered the alternative)
