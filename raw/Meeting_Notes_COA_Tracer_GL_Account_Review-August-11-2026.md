## Summary

Val called a short check-in with the accounting team (Igor, Laura, Cris) on the Chart of Accounts "tracer." Much of the call was spent on translation — the team works in Spanish and translates through Google, and Val repeatedly asked Igor to slow down and use far fewer words ("70% shorter").

**Purpose and deadline.** Val gave the team until 6 PM today to send their answers to the AI CFO. He made himself available in the meantime for questions. He framed the exercise as a productive exchange of practical and academic suggestions on how to build the proper GL structure for the JVL set of companies — "not a ping-pong match" — and stressed his goal is not to judge who is right or wrong but for everyone to reach the best solution together. Questions can be written to Val (in Spanish; they get translated) and he will reply.

**The tracer comments.** The AI CFO added a comment to every single line of the tracer. The team should insert their own comments in response. Val asked that the team's reply come back as one combined answer for the whole group, not a separate answer per person. Igor suggested the team meet again after this call to agree on their comments before sending the final version to the AI CFO.

**Why the AI CFO is in the loop.** Val explained the AI CFO is still learning: right now it has academic knowledge — it has read the entire Microsoft Dynamics / Business Central manual — but not yet JVL-specific knowledge. The more it works with JVL, the more its academic knowledge merges with practical knowledge. Val wants to run the account-narrowing exercise with the CFO precisely so it learns why the team makes each choice and what they do and don't agree on.

**The plan and terminology.** The goal is to narrow the roughly 800 current accounts down to about 143–147 GL accounts, then split each account into dimensions. Igor pressed on the next steps after the chart of accounts is built — the sub-accounts (which he called auxiliaries / sub-ledgers) and posting groups. Val clarified the Microsoft Business Central term: instead of "sub-accounts," Business Central uses "dimensions" (tags/tabs) configured per account, and each of the ~147 accounts must be individually assessed for which dimensions it needs (for example, credit cards or assets). After the accounts exist, the next task will be to ask the CFO to help add dimensions based on Microsoft best practices for a company like JVL; the CFO has access to Val's second brain.

**Next.** Val wants a full decision on the GL accounts by tomorrow, after which he hopes the AI CFO can produce an import file for Lev to load. The team confirmed they understood and agreed the plan is good. Val will wait for their reply this evening.
