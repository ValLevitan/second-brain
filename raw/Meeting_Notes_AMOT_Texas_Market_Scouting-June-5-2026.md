# Meeting Summary — AMOT Show + Texas Market Scouting

**Date:** June 5, 2026 (~1h 59m)
**Attendees:** Val Levitan, Juan Martinez, Felipe Miranda Giorgi, Andrei Klimovich (location photos also shared by Sebastian, who did not attend)

## Summary

Felipe and Andrei reported back from a Texas trip built around the AMOT trade show, plus location visits in Austin, San Antonio, Corpus Christi, and Houston. They did not go to Dallas or the Mexican-border towns, so the read covers South Texas only. AMOT itself is a small show; Felipe thinks the bigger opportunity is the AMOA show in Vegas each March, where many Texas operators actually buy. Val agreed JVL should attend AMOA next March (2027).

**The market.** Texas is a gray market split between "skill" games and Class 3 machines (called "8-liners," used loosely for any Class 3). The two coexist — some locations are pure skill, some pure Class 3, many mixed. Skill is seen as legally safer because a Texas attorney/lobbyist defends it in court and "vets" games (name unclear in transcript — rendered "Steve Brunoglio" / "Stephen Pinoglu"). That lawyer wants manufacturers to strip any "bet / wager / chance" wording from game menus so the games can be defended as skill. Felipe has his contact details. Houston was recently raided — machines seized, locations closed or lying low — but Val expects it back in business quickly. Gas stations and small stores lean skill; game rooms and social clubs lean Class 3. Most Class 3 games are vertical. Ultimate Fire Link, and Chinese knockoffs of it, is everywhere — roughly half of all cabinets in many locations.

**Competitors.** Biggest machine makers at the show were Banilla and Pace-O-Matic (Pace-O-Matic always paired with its own kiosk). Also seen: C3 Gaming (appears to have left Georgia for Texas), Red Plum (a local Texas cabinet maker), and Omega cabinets out of Georgia (sold heavily into Texas via Ivy Promotions). JVL ended up with a good front spot at the show after a rocky setup.

**How players pay.** Almost everything is cash. Very few Tito setups; most locations print a ticket from a pyramid printer that you take to a cashier or a cash-out kiosk. A few oddities: a "silver pellet" scheme (one owner, redeem at an attached jewelry shop, no fee); a "match" mechanic (location matches your bet but raises the minimum cash-out); and "sweepstakes"-style PC stations with electronic wallets. No location charges any per-transaction fee. Kiosks run about $7K. Cash dispensers are common; recyclers are rare.

**Transactional-product read (Felipe / Andrei).** Their take: a cash dispenser is welcome and needed; debit-card products (FlexPay, and Flex IQ as currently built) are not, because it's a cash market and players won't trade real cash for a card. The biggest operational gap they saw: players can't move between machines — they must cash out at each one individually.

**Val's pushback and strategy.** Val agreed debit cards are wrong for Texas, but pressed two points hard:

- *Per-transaction fee.* No one charges a fee today because the market is new and operators just chase demand. As stable operators move in, they look for efficiency. If JVL gives them a menu option to add a small ATM-style charge ($2–5), smart operators will adopt it to cover costs, and the market copies fast. Juan's example: one ATM at Tampa Hard Rock earns Seminole Gaming $400K/month in fees.
- *Flex IQ does not have to be debit-card-only.* Val corrected Andrei firmly on this. IQ can issue a QR ticket or a plastic card as an e-wallet, let players roam machine to machine, and act as a central accounting / reconciliation point — so a location stops opening every machine by hand to tally cash. Paired with FlexPort, a single ticket issued once at the counter can move around all machines. For Tito environments he'd suggest a standard Tito printer + bill acceptor, with IQ as the central accounting layer.

The exchange got tense. Val was frustrated the team returned with observations but no concrete product proposals, and used his "shoe salesman in India" parable to make the point ("nobody wears shoes" vs "the opportunity is crazy, nobody wears shoes"). Andrei said they were building toward solutions but didn't take the meeting to be a proposals meeting; Juan noted he'd rushed the meeting the day after they landed. Val apologized for cutting them off, then asked them to bring proposals next time and, ideally, to call in opportunities live from future trade shows.

**Next sales steps (Felipe).** Penetrate fast with cheaper equipment (Maple), starting with operators met at the show who showed interest, plus Georgia operators Felipe already knows who run machines in Texas. Whether Maple goes in as Class 3 or skill is still open and needs Val's call; if skill, get the defense attorney to vet the game (lab certification, gameplay videos already made for the Nebraska attorney, and menu screenshots). Most equipment in Texas is shipped in from Georgia / Vegas / Illinois, so a large Texas office and inventory is probably not needed — maybe a small showroom.

## Decisions Made

- **Debit cards are the wrong approach for Texas.** It's a cash market; nothing beats cash. (Val, with Juan and Andrei agreeing)
- **Flex IQ will be treated as more than a debit-card product** — QR / plastic-card e-wallet, machine-to-machine roaming, and centralized accounting. (Val, directing Andrei)
- **JVL will attend the AMOA show in Vegas in March 2027.** (Val and Felipe)
