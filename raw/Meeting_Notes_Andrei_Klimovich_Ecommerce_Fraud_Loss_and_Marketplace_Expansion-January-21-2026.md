## Summary

A one-on-one operations review of the e-commerce machine business (JVL Games) with Andrei Klimovich, in three threads: a payment-fraud loss, marketplace expansion, and staffing.

**The fraud loss — seven machines.** Andrei walked through the sequence: an invoice was issued in QuickBooks, payment was received and confirmed cleared into the Bank of America account belonging to JVL Games (Andrei checked with Chris that the money truly landed), and only then did he ship seven machines through Amazon's fulfilment. About two weeks later QuickBooks notified that the payment was cancelled *by the payer* and clawed the money back. The account owner on the receiving end appears to be a scammer ("I didn't buy anything from him, I don't know anything about him"). Andrei holds a delivery address (real), plus an email and phone number that may be fake; he had not yet called. He called it the biggest single loss the channel can produce — about $10,000 if ten machines are written off. Val's instruction: write the machines off against profit and see what is actually left; the ledger already carries a separate column for replacement and warranty costs, and theft will now add to it. Val also questioned why the tracking item is called "Dead Project"; Andrei wants to pursue the counterparty ("distributor or not distributor or forum") and finish the story.

**Marketplace expansion.** Val's push: "Let's focus on creating a new business. We need Best Buy, we need to go to X. X is ready." Andrei's report: after fighting through three managers, the marketplace account was unlocked — they proved the machines are not gambling devices — and the first advertising campaigns are rolling. Bing carries them too; Yahoo is under consideration. His caution: unlike Amazon, these platforms are not self-serve — they are closed, declarative systems where you must get in through a person.

**Staffing — Valeria.** The plan: Valeria returns to the office at roughly half load (about 10 hours a month, ~$400/month) and keeps working. Val wants her actively integrating with Salesforce for the next six months — at least two integrations per month, funded from the existing marketing budget (nothing delivered yet). She will also take over replacement logistics: sending a machine for replacement, collecting the old one, filling FedEx shipping labels on the corporate FedEx account (a two-hour skill), and generally acting as first-line support. On backfilling her business-development side, Andrei is still unsure who to look for; earlier candidates are not ready to move into managing business development.

## Decisions Made
