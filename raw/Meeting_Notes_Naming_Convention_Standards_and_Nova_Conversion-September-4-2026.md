# Meeting Summary — Game_Development (Naming Convention Standards and Nova Conversion)
**Date:** September 4, 2026, 2:07 PM – 4:27 PM Eastern
**Duration:** ~140 minutes
**Attendees:** Val Levitan, Michael Gottlieb, Lena Zhukova, Juan Martinez, Efim Podvoiski, Irina Shishko (joined for part)

**Raw transcript:** [[Meeting_Transcript_Naming_Convention_Standards_and_Nova_Conversion-September-4-2026]]

## Summary

The recurring Game Development meeting, in English. It runs long and covers three distinct blocks: the root cause of the Georgia V3 delay, product and interface design decisions, and Val's closing instruction on the standards repository.

**Mismatched assets and the naming convention.** Lena Zhukova opened by describing the mysterious asset mismatches the team had been chasing. The root is that Fire Island games were developed first as single games, and many assets share the same names, for example "transition" or "big green talker", without any qualifier saying whether it belongs to Larosa or Crescent City Carnaval. Val immediately named it as a naming convention problem, and Lena resisted the framing, explaining that the product changed and assets are being reused from single-game products in Fiery Ice Link.

Val pushed until the group settled it. Efim Podvoiski put it cleanly: the convention was there, but it was inadequate. Michael Gottlieb agreed: the naming convention was fine for a single game release, and the trouble started when multiple games sat under one umbrella and the convention was not set up for that.

Val then said the group would not like what he was about to say, and made the point he returned to repeatedly: identify the issue honestly, fix the root, and never run into it again. Lena said it was already done and would not recur, because the team has only just started building multi-category products. Val asked whether that meant a new standard existed and was documented. Lena's answer was that it was not documented, it was a verbal agreement. Val's response, which he said he could not help being sarcastic about, was that JVL finally has a verbal agreement.

Michael said this had only happened in the last couple of days, and added, with feeling, that this had been the worst development process he had experienced at JVL for a single game release. Val disagreed on the history: standards at JVL are always agreed verbally, become tribal knowledge, carry through the company, and he has been trying to root that out for years. Michael respectfully disagreed, pointing out that he and the team had developed well over a dozen games without this problem. Val's answer was that this is precisely because they kept their word and everyone knew and honoured the verbal agreement, and that this is exactly what does not survive. His challenge: point me to a repository that lists the standards, because if the repository does not exist it will be impossible to keep standards in the long run. Michael agreed.

Lena's position was that a document exists for Omni and Nova, from when they started developing in Omni such as Fortune Football and Kaliocha Omnia, but not for Rage Studio, and that with a shortage of artists and a release to finish she did not think time should be spent creating it now, offering to start after the release next week. Val questioned why the file naming convention should be different for Nova versus Rage Studio at all, and Lena explained that the asset types and storage structure differ. Val's position was that everything can still live in GitHub, and that nothing stops standards for Rage Studio living there too. Lena clarified she was talking about asset structure rather than standards; Val said he was talking about naming convention standards.

Lena's practical worry was that renaming assets now would cause bigger problems, because for some games branches are cut from already finished games and names already exist. Michael cut that off: there is no reason to rename assets, this was a folder structure problem and the folder structure has been addressed. Val's counter was that the folder structure problem will continue until standards are developed. Michael agreed that what Lena is articulating needs documenting, and proposed a Confluence page describing the default folder structure. Val challenged why it has to be Confluence; Michael said it does not have to be anything in particular, and that all it needs to be is an articulation of what the asset folder structure is.

**Where to branch from.** The second root cause proved harder and was never fully resolved. Val wanted to understand what methodology should be used to decide which branch to start a new release from, and who should give that guidance. Michael's answer was that the information exists in Jira, that it is why the requirement gets generated, and that going forward he would specify it. Val said twice that this was not helpful and that he wanted to understand how to make the decision, what information to use, and where to find it.

Efim Podvoiski explained branching to Juan Martinez, who asked directly what a branch is. Efim's explanation: branches exist in source control so changes for other products do not contaminate current development; because JVL has different products going different ways, and sometimes returns to a product or moves it to another jurisdiction, someone must decide which snapshot to start from, and it is not always trivial, because you may have a similar product on an old code base with old bugs and missing features, against newer products with different games. Lena offered the working rule: the branch closest to the current requirements of the product being developed. Efim agreed.

Along the way Juan surfaced a live field defect: in the V2 version the European currency was selected for Jackpot Rush, and the help text says "win at max bet", which is not what Georgia calls for. Georgia is "at any bet". Juan confirmed this bug was present at the trade show and is in the ten machines on trial for V2.

Val summarised two issue types: the absence of a written, referable file naming convention, and the branching decision, which he said he still did not fully understand.

**Why the release slipped to September 10.** Val worked backwards through this carefully, saying he was reading between the lines and asking not to be killed for it. Lena listed the remaining work: finishing updated graphics for Queen of Luxor, an expanded touch area on the nudge arrows added the previous Friday, and some bugs. Val's reading was that the bugs would not have pushed the date, and that the graphics change for Queen of Luxor is what did. Lena confirmed the decision was made knowingly, that the product could be submitted without the new graphics, and that the team chose to update it. Val asked whether he had been part of that decision; Michael and Juan both said yes, though Michael clarified the September 10 date itself was never discussed, only the wish to add Queen of Luxor and fix bugs after the show.

Michael reported that in the last couple of days he finally has a Georgia V3 build that has settled down and behaves as it should, and that it is ready to submit now with no more bugs. Val accepted the constraint of the Rage development environment, saying that as long as JVL continues on Rage the aggravation will continue, but held that the naming convention problem specifically could have been prevented very easily.

Remaining work Lena named: the wider touch area, and a change Val had requested to the horse animation. Val was blunt about the animation, saying the horse does not kick or land naturally and the physics is wrong, and disbelieving Lena's report that it had been approved and liked. Anastasiya is splitting her time between that animation and Queen of Luxor. Juan raised that, given Felipe found several problems in V2 at and after the show, Felipe should get the ISO build and test a full machine with topper before it goes to Eclipse. Val agreed that costs an extra day or two, and closed the thread with the conclusion he had been driving at: Felipe would have received an ISO today or Monday had it not been for Queen of Luxor.

**Product and interface decisions.** Michael asked where the new Georgia skill initiative stands. Val said it is on hold: he wanted to understand what might come out of it, but needs a customer commitment to purchase and does not have one, and is meeting the CEO next week. Michael and Efim still need more game footage to resolve maths questions, specifically free spins, racking and hot streak at the highest bet without autoplay. Val undertook to record it.

Lena showed a mock-up of the dual-box skill feature built on Lions Riches, with progressives removed from the top and large touch boxes lowered for ergonomics. Val's feedback: increase the transparency of the larger rectangle, make it less square and more rectangular, and reduce it from being the primary focus. Michael and Juan converged on moving the box and increasing transparency, which Val said was the right direction while repeatedly noting he is not an expert. Lena explained an earlier version had been rejected because it covered the dashboard, which Michael confirmed cannot happen because the win must go to the award meter. Val volunteered that he found Primero's implementation better than Piggy's, despite not liking the company, because the four choices are positioned and shaped for easy pointing and sit low on a tall screen. Michael's counter is that Piggy requires no thought while Primero requires four choices, and that competitors deliberately make the boxes big and prominent as a stopper.

Juan raised the button deck question: Primero has a mechanical deck, and should JVL keep the V-deck long term. Val said he loves the V-deck, but is analysing why JVL machines make less money than Aristocrat. His observation from touring Georgia locations is that Aristocrat has a mechanical deck and a small scale on the main screen that defaults to a dollar and can be moved up or down. On JVL's deck it is natural to start from the leftmost, smallest button, and stepping up feels like entering a danger zone, almost warning the player they are about to spend three or four dollars a game, which he suspects is counterproductive. Juan agreed there is something to it, noting some manufacturers let dollars display on buttons and that operators can toggle it, and that seeing $3 makes a player more aware of spend. Val was careful to flag that he was theorising for the first time and it might be worthless. Efim asked whether a partial swap could be tested on some machines in the same release; Michael said that would be a very expensive test, and asked instead what the downside is of simply doing it Val's way. This was framed for V4 rather than the current release.

Lena showed the proposed game selection screen for South Dakota D2703, noting six games fit comfortably on one line on that monitor and that with a small squeeze there is room for around fifteen. Val called it beautiful and made one forward-looking point: the number of Keno games will grow a lot, based on his tour of South Dakota locations, and the layout lends itself to multiple carousels. He would not use left and right arrows but a visual infinity, and noted Lena had implemented a carousel on the amusement product years ago that worked beautifully. Michael raised Val's own struggle with Primero's carousel; Val's answer was that it was implemented incorrectly. Michael relayed Juan's preference for letting the operator switch which games are offered to keep the mix fresh, and Val agreed and suggested the top screen could then carry advertising.

On Georgia, Val said JVL should not offer upgrades for D27 machines, and that the next trade show should show a redesigned interface. This produced a genuine disagreement. Michael said changing the look between form factors is a violation of branding, and that the design just completed has not even been deployed. Juan reframed what Val was asking: how to make Curve appear more premium so it can sell for a thousand dollars more than a V43. Lena's view was that premium should come from the games rather than only the home screen, possibly licensed games. Juan agreed licensed games are not realistic yet. Val floated what he called a crazy idea, that Curve should be a collection of large-format premium games only rather than a collection of many games. Michael pushed back that the market is going the other way, with competitors reaching further back into older libraries. Left as a thinking item rather than a decision.

On conversion, Val reported that automatic conversion from Rage to Nova has been tested on two games, one vertical and one dual screen to dual screen, and both went smoothly; he expects Keno to be easier, flagged as his own professional opinion. Tentatively from October 1 South Dakota VLT converts from Rage to Nova. Lena's constraint is that developers work on Nebraska in October, so the conversion starts a little later; she will do all design screens for South Dakota Real Keno meanwhile. Val confirmed Nebraska is the last release on Rage and everything after that is Nova, and Michael confirmed C43 Nova will be introduced at the Georgia show.

**The standards repository.** Val made a closing statement he asked to be treated as a point. JVL has a repository in GitHub called Knowledge Base, and under it a folder called Standards, which is where all standards go. It is open to everyone. When anyone uses Claude for any function, they should point it at the standards repository and let it refer to the standards, and anyone who wants to read them manually is welcome to. It includes the taxonomy and the file naming convention. Anyone without access should have it, because it is open to everybody. He added guidance on how to write them: write to a narrow point, do not make it a broad standard, keep adding narrow granular points. Michael echoed the word "granular". Lena said the team actually has documents of this kind already and simply needs to move them into the proper repository. Val added that JVL is steadily moving away from Confluence and will probably leave it completely as the switch to Nova proceeds.

## Decisions Made

- **The root cause of the asset mismatches is an inadequate file naming convention, not a one-off mistake.** The convention was acceptable for single game releases and was never extended to multiple games under one umbrella. (Efim Podvoiski's formulation, agreed by Michael Gottlieb and Val)
- **The asset folder structure and naming convention must be written down, not held as a verbal agreement.** Michael agreed to document it; the location is GitHub rather than Confluence, per Val's challenge. (Val, agreed by Michael Gottlieb)
- **Assets are not renamed.** This was a folder structure problem and the folder structure has been addressed. (Michael Gottlieb, resolving Lena Zhukova's concern)
- **Branch selection rule stated: choose the branch closest to the current requirements of the product being developed.** (Lena Zhukova, agreed by Efim Podvoiski. Val said he still does not fully understand the decision method, so this is a working rule rather than a settled standard.)
- **The Georgia V3 slip to September 10 is attributed to the decision to update Queen of Luxor graphics, not to bugs.** Val established the team knowingly chose this and that he was part of the decision, though the resulting date was never stated at the time. (Val, confirmed by Michael Gottlieb and Juan Martinez)
- **Felipe gets the ISO build and tests a full machine with topper before it goes to Eclipse,** accepting an extra day or two. (Juan Martinez's proposal, accepted by Val)
- **The Georgia skill initiative stays on hold** pending a customer commitment to purchase; Val meets the CEO next week. (Val)
- **Dual-box skill feature design direction:** increase transparency of the frame, make it less square and more rectangular, lower it, and keep it off the dashboard so the win reaches the award meter. (Val's input, converged with Michael Gottlieb and Juan Martinez)
- **The button deck question is deferred to V4, not the current release.** Michael rejected a partial hardware swap within one release as too expensive. (Michael Gottlieb, with Efim Podvoiski's test proposal set aside)
- **South Dakota D2703 game selection screen approved in direction,** with six games on a line and room to grow to about fifteen; Juan still evaluates which games go in. (Michael Gottlieb and Lena Zhukova, endorsed by Val)
- **Operators will be able to switch which games are offered, to keep the mix fresh; the top screen may then carry advertising.** (Juan Martinez's preference, agreed by Val)
- **No upgrades offered for D27 machines in Georgia.** (Val)
- **Nebraska is the last release on Rage. Everything after is Nova. C43 Nova is introduced at the Georgia show.** (Val, confirmed by Michael Gottlieb)
- **South Dakota VLT converts from Rage to Nova, tentatively from October 1,** starting a little later in practice because developers are on Nebraska through October; Lena does South Dakota Real Keno design screens meanwhile. (Val and Lena Zhukova)
- **Standards live in the Knowledge Base repository in GitHub, under the Standards folder, open to everyone.** Anyone using Claude points it at the standards repository first. Standards are written narrow and granular, not broad. Existing documents are moved into the proper repository. JVL is steadily leaving Confluence. (Val, closing statement)
