## Three Coverage Items Resolved

**1. Tenants Legal Liability ($1M):** No separate premium. It is an automatic extension included in every CGL policy. Cannot be removed for a premium reduction; there is nothing being charged for it.

**2. Non-Owned Auto ($5M limit, $250 premium):** This is the only item with an explicit premium line. Cyndi explained the exposure: if an employee drives their personal car on a work-related trip and causes a serious accident (e.g., fatality), once the employee's personal auto insurance exhausts, liability flows up to JVL. Val acknowledged $250/year for $5M coverage is sound math but wants to see the actual policy wording confirm the coverage before committing. Cyndi offered to get a revised quote removing it, but recommended keeping it.

**3. Employer's Bodily Injury ($2M):** Same as Tenants Legal; automatic CGL inclusion, no separate premium. Standard on all commercial CGL policies.

## What CGL Actually Covers

Val pressed Cyndi on whether CGL is the right coverage type for a pure software licensing entity. Cyndi's explanation:

- CGL covers **bodily injury and property damage** caused by Labs' operations or products to third parties
- The primary value is **defense costs** in lawsuits, even frivolous ones; Cyndi cited a current case at $250K in defense costs alone where the insured is not liable
- Coverage limit is $5M per the quote
- This is **primary** coverage; no umbrella needed (Everest had a $3M umbrella, but Labs' reduced operations don't warrant that limit)

**Examples of what IS covered:** cabinet tips over on a player, cabinet catches fire and burns down a casino. Even though Labs doesn't contract directly with casinos, as the software developer they get dragged into lawsuits alongside everyone else.

**What is NOT covered:**
- Faulty software causing financial loss → requires **Errors & Omissions** (E&O), separate policy
- Player loses money gambling → not a CGL matter
- Software bugs causing service outages → E&O territory
