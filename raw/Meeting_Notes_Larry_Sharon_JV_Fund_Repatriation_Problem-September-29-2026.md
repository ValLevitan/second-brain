## Summary

The partner said that, while on a meeting with Trevor and Ben, he realised the license discussed yesterday belongs to the **joint venture entity**, not to "entity A". The JV entity is the one that sells to master distributors.

He recapped the structure:

- Entity A forms a **joint venture (entity B)** with an entity Val chooses. Entity B distributes and manages the equipment.
- JVL books a **$4,000 cash down payment per unit** plus about **$12,000 in receivables** (first said $11,000). He is still checking that booking it as receivables, not a loan, works for depreciation.

Val pointed out the real deal is: JVL gets $4,000 and the rest is split 50/50. The debt payment is then really Val's own 50% going back to JVL. The partner agreed the payment must look like an arm's-length transaction, and the open problem is **how that money gets back to JVL**.

Ideas raised, none settled:

- Partner: JVL gives credit toward future equipment purchases instead of cash back. Val asked how that could still be written off.

The partner said the others ("the three of you") did not discuss this method; they leave it to the two of them to find what works for both sides.
