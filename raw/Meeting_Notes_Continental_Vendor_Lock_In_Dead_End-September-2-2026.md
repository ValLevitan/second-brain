## Summary

The second half of the visit, in which Val concluded — out loud, to the customer's face — that there is no business here today.

**Texas economics.** Salim's Texas operation is run by one person who does everything: finds locations, prepares machines, lives on the road, working entirely on referral ("one customer is happy calls the second one"). About 20 more machines were being prepared for the following week. The numbers are nothing like Georgia: net win around **$15 per day** per machine, with Salim's share after the split about **$120 a month** at 20–25%. Machines are cheap by comparison: a refurbished cabinet around $3,000 plus a **$1,000 conversion card** to move it from Georgia specification — Salim buys in volume and pays about $1,000 for cards others pay $1,200–$2,500 for. Banilla does not sell direct; there is a large secondary market of Georgia resellers.

**The lock-in.** In Georgia he buys **only Aristocrat** — "that's the only one that's performing", and crucially the locations, not the players, are the ones asking for it. Aristocrat also extends **six months at zero percent** on machines at roughly $18,500–$20,500, and he mixes dual-screen and vertical depending on what the customer wants.

**Val's read, delivered plainly.** *"Doesn't look like we can do business here. It seems like your mind is set on Aristocrat and Axcess system."* Salim did not fully close the door — he said it does not mean it will not change, he stays in touch with Felipe every fifteen days ("Great guy. He's the one who got JVL back in the market"), and if a new JVL game arrives that people want he will look. He also volunteered that a couple of JVL verticals had come back from locations recently, which Val accepted without pushing.

**Why Axcess wins for him.** Salim gave the mechanism: because Axcess lets a player tap or insert a card and carry the balance from machine to machine, it produces a report the Georgia Lottery inspector will accept — showing the real $70, not the inflated $170. Nobody else has solved that except Novomatic. Val's response — "You know that we have a better system" — got an "Obviously, yes. I saw it in your demo," but no movement.

**The Novomatic commercial model, which is the real competitive intelligence here.** $210 per month for 36 months, then $150 per month, with no equipment to buy except printers. What Salim likes most is that **the $210 does not come out of his pocket — Novomatic bills the location directly**, and the locations accept it because the replay function saves them money. Every location without Axcess is being pushed to Novomatic.

**Val's counter-pitch and its problem.** JVL is $1,000 per machine plus $1,000 for the POS, requiring rewiring, ideally on the second SAS port (bill-acceptor port possible but not recommended), plus $90 per month per location. Salim's reaction was to compare it against a $5,000 nine-machine Axcess install. Val's answer was honest and probably too honest: *"Access gave you a wholesale price. I'm giving you a retail price. I'm giving you retail because you're not my customer."* He added that when it comes to buying, they can sit down and talk. The meeting ended cordially with apologies for the late start.

**The debrief with Andrei, after.** Val's read is the most commercially interesting part. He believes Salim's request for replay is really about the Intralot reporting gap, and that the replay solution is being used as **a wonderful way for locations to keep giving out cash and sweeping the rest under the reporting** — an excuse for the regulator rather than a genuine cashless move. Andrei agreed and connected it to the data: this is exactly why so few cards have been issued in three months. His conclusion was that it is an opportunity for any replay solution — operators want one to have an excuse — but it will not translate into more cards being issued. Andrei's closing assessment: *"They have a great capacity, but it looks like they're not ready to change their current partners."* Val: *"That's absolutely correct. They have their highway."*

## Decisions Made

- **No business today.** Val stated it directly; the account is locked into Aristocrat for machines and Axcess for the card system.
- **Retail pricing given deliberately**, on the basis that Salim is not a customer; wholesale is available if he decides to buy.
- **Keep the relationship warm through Felipe**, who contacts Salim every fifteen days.
- **Internal read recorded:** the replay demand is a compliance-cover play, not a genuine cashless migration, and will not lift card issuance.

- **Texas economics:** net win ~$15/day/machine; operator share ~$120/month at 20–25%; refurb cabinet ~$3,000 + ~$1,000 conversion card.
