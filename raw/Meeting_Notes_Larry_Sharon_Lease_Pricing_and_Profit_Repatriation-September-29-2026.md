## Summary

**Two problems.** Larry named them:
- **Problem A:** how the partner entity gets its half of the money back from JVL. Still unsolved.
- **Problem B:** the math. For the note to be legally repaid within the machine's 10-year life, **$16,000 does not work; $12,000 does.** Larry is fine with $12,000.

**Larry's repayment model.** Using $150 per month less $15 operating cost ($135 net), he can assign all $135 in year one to repay JVL's note. From year two about $40 per month goes to tax distributions and gains. The note is paid off in **month 99**, within the machine's life. He offered to send the Excel model. A higher fee ($175 or $200) only pays it off faster.

**Val's pricing problem.** Val told customers $90 per month service charge; now he needs $150. Customers who multiply $150 by 60 months do not reach the $15,000 equipment price (that is 100 months). Val would rather not charge $200 because he wants to win the market. He likes 100 months at $150: it matches the highest competitors, who have less equipment and limited capability. Val said a competitor charges upfront and also about $14 per machine per month.

**Val's first idea:** a lifetime contract with all service, updates and advance replacement, with the right to exit by paying the rest of the equipment price (for example $15,000).

**Larry's proposal — a lease.** Present it like a car lease (for example BMW with service included): a monthly payment for a set term; at the end the customer returns the equipment in good condition or buys it out at a set price. Offer a scale of terms and let customers choose. Val agreed this avoids saying "free": "we do nothing for free; minimum $150." Val floated a higher price for short terms (for example $199 for 3 years). They settled on modelling **3, 4, 5, 7 and 10 years**.

**Write-off on returns.** Val asked about equipment taken back early. Larry: after the year-one write-off nothing is left to write off; returned equipment can be reused elsewhere. Val noted that would then be profit.

Larry will think about Problem A tonight and get back to Val.

## Decisions Made

- **Pitch the customer offer as a lease with set terms and a buy-out or return at the end**, not as free equipment. (Proposed by Larry Sharon; agreed by Val Levitan)
- **Model lease terms of 3, 4, 5, 7 and 10 years.** (Val Levitan and Larry Sharon)
