## Summary

Val, new to Georgia, asked the Becks to help him meet the **right master-license holders** — "nice people" worth seeing. They agreed to **email him a vetted list** with who is doing what and, for each, **who the decision-maker is**. Their read: of the roughly full field, only about **two dozen (20–30) masters are worth meeting**; another ~50 are too small or too insulated. Names surfaced: **Dixie Music** (everywhere, big), **River City** (broken into many small LLCs — a common "break off an LLC so you can jettison it if it gets in trouble" tactic), **All Star** (formerly Epic, now All Star again), plus manufacturer names Jenga Labs, Ace to Ace, Hayes. The operator base is mostly Indian/Pakistani with some Eastern European.

**Market intel:**
- **Texas** is "fully established," county-by-county (the state doesn't control gaming per county), and moving toward **card systems** — worth watching; it mostly runs **decommissioned Class III games** (old WMS/IGT machines, hoppers pulled, "built like tanks"), likely bought at auction. Notably little talk of E&P devices there.
- **Puerto Rico** stays gray/unstable — repeated state attempts to impose an Intralot-style interlock setup keep falling apart ("the right people aren't getting greased").
- **Protocol secrecy** gripe (shared with JVL's own JCM frustration): IGT/JCM don't publish; the Becks reverse-engineered NetFlex to emulate a dollar-bill acceptor; the SAS protocol only became piece-able because **Montana requires public disclosure** (though vendors published only a subset, like Georgia's).

Val also mentioned he is **looking for a second salesman** in Georgia (office in Lawrenceville) and both sides praised Felipe. Social close (candy from nudo.com, machines in the shop).

## Decisions / Outcomes
- The Becks will **send Val a vetted list of ~2 dozen Georgia master-license holders** with the decision-maker for each. (Jim & Mary Beck)
- Watch Texas as it moves to card systems. (mutual)
