## Summary

This meeting was called by Val to align the accounting team on reporting structure, priorities, and communication expectations as the company transitions away from reliance on Alka, the external accounting consultant.

**Alka's Contract Wind-Down.** Alka's consulting contract, originally set to end in March or April, was extended at Val's request until June 1, 2026 — though at half her previous involvement level. Val wants the team to progressively and exponentially reduce their reliance on Alka between now and June 1st so the transition is gradual rather than abrupt. Val noted that on April 6th he met with Alka and she told him she would instruct the team to stop copying her on day-to-day correspondence. When asked, Igor and Laura confirmed they had not yet received that instruction from Alka — Igor mentioned they had a meeting with Alka scheduled for later that day where she might communicate it.

**Avoiding Conflicting Directions.** Val's primary concern is preventing situations where he and Alka give conflicting instructions to the team. He emphasized this isn't about Alka giving wrong orders — it's that they don't speak daily, so neither may be aware of the other's priorities. Val wants the team to take direction from him directly, without needing Alka as an intermediary, and to build direct working relationships with each other.

**Cross-Training vs. Media Tax Credit Priority.** The team had been working on a cross-training initiative set up by Alka, where Igor's extensive process knowledge would be transferred to Laura, Cris, and Mary. Val acknowledged and appreciated Alka's intent but overrode the priority: the media tax credit report is more urgent because it directly brings cash into the company. He noted that Laura and Cris are already highly qualified professionals who know receivables and payables well, so the cross-training can be deferred. Igor apologized for taking Laura's time on cross-training, and Val used this as a teachable moment about communication style — stating that in North American business culture, brevity is preferred. When Val says "I understand, I was wrong," there is no need for further apology loops or circular discussion.

**Media Tax Credit Report — Igor's Assignment.** Inna had sent Igor a previous media tax credit report (from two years ago) in a standard format and asked him to update it with current data for 2023 and 2024. The report covers marketing, distribution, and BLR-related GL accounts — account names, descriptions, and amounts by year. Igor initially described the task as complex because each account has associated vendors and payments need to be verified against specific periods. Val initially thought the ask was simply pulling a GL account from Business Central, but learned the data resides in the old Navision system (maintained by Lev), not in Business Central. Igor confirmed he has access to Navision and it still works. Val acknowledged his misunderstanding ("I was wrong") and asked Igor for a realistic deadline. Igor initially offered "tomorrow" but Val pushed back, saying he doesn't need it rushed — he wants a comfortable, realistic commitment with no excuses. Igor agreed to deliver by Friday night (April 10, 2026), working independently, alongside his other regular duties.

**Laura's Assignment — SOPs and Receivables.** Val directed Laura to focus on two things: (1) writing SOPs (Standard Operating Procedures) for receivables processes, and (2) drafting collection letters to send to clients. Val was explicit that no letters go out until he personally approves them. Laura mentioned she had already started an SOP draft and shared it with Igor, Cris, and Mary for feedback. Val firmly shut this down — he doesn't want Laura occupying other team members' time with review cycles. The feedback loop should be Laura drafting directly and submitting to Val for approval, not circulating among peers. Laura also wanted to discuss invoice splitting with Felipe, but Val indicated everyone is busy and she should work independently given her qualifications (he noted she holds a master's degree plus a specialization). Laura will begin working on the collection letter drafts immediately.

**Communication Expectations.** A recurring theme throughout the meeting was Val's insistence on direct, concise communication. He expressed strong frustration with circular conversations — repeated apologies, excessive explanations, and going around the point. He wants: state the issue, get the answer, move on. Val used humor (calling himself a "dictator" vs. "socialism") to make the point that decisions flow through him and don't require group consensus or committee review.
