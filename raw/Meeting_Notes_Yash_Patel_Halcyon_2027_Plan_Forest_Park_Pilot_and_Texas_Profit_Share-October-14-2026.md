## Summary

**Why Val asked for the meeting.** Val wanted to know Yash's plan for 2027 and how JVL fits in it.

**2027 machine plan.** Yash has not tested JVL's new vertical machine yet. He just sold some locations with their inventory and is adding new ones. He is repairing his old Banilla games and sending them to his Texas route, because nobody wants them in Georgia. He has **at least 20 new locations coming in 2027, so at least 20 JVL games**; every location he has carries one or two JVL games. Val said that is about two a month; Yash said orders may come in batches (for example ten at once), and he works out quantities and finance terms with Felipe.

**Vertical conversion.** Val told him to go with the vertical and Version 3. **Val took it as his undertaking to convert all of Yash's JVL vertical games to the current version.** The machines are in locations; Yash keeps no inventory. Val said new machines have modems inside, and the data is confidential. Yash said this would have helped Novomatic: its first games in Georgia did very well but had many physical errors, and he had to pull them.

**Transactional.** Val said JVL has a strong transactional product, not yet tested, and asked for all of Yash's transactional business, exclusively, with "something very special" in return. Yash uses Axcess today. He is not committed to anyone; it is location by location, and he will not buy more until his installed systems run for about three more months. His costs with Axcess: **about $6,000 of equipment per location (no kiosk), plus about $1 per device per day** — about 7 devices, so about $210 a month. His pain points: **3–4 hours to install each location**, parts from many suppliers, and retailers who want whatever the neighbour has. He would pay a bit more for a system that just works every day and gives CCL-level data. He rarely uses kiosks: in rougher locations players jam the card in the wrong slot and his tech gets an emergency call.

**Georgia Lottery and the cash ban.** Yash sits on the COAM board; the board had a call yesterday on the lottery's new cash-redemption proposal. He said it mostly writes down what already exists. Val said he has a close relationship with the lottery and had dinner with them yesterday. Yash described **"COAM Trucker"**: a tablet that turns a Georgia Lottery gift card into a store gift card, and then into cash rung up as "store merchandise". He thinks many will do this quietly. Val called it a black market. A line on the recording says the lottery corporation mentions "March of next year".

**November test location.** **Agreed: one JVL test setup in November, with the POS and the kiosk, tightly scoped.** Preferred site: a location in **Forest Park**, south of the airport — a gas station c-store with a laundromat and a barber shop next door, good foot traffic. It is waiting for the city business license, then the lottery's print date for the COE license. Fallback: **Macon**, near Yash's warehouse (another Macon location comes in January). Most games there will have a second SAS port.

**Texas partnership.** Val proposed: in a location of about six machines, put **two JVL machines side by side with one JVL recycler in the middle**, no bill acceptor and no printer, maybe with a pop-up display. Go into another operator's location. **Yash fronts the equipment cost; profit is split 50/50.** Val's rough cost: **about $3,000 per unit, so about $9,000 per setup**; one more machine adds about $3,000. It runs **Texas software, not Class III** — do not judge it against Class III. How it works: the player puts cash in the recycler, gets a TITO-like ticket, the Flexport optical reader reads it as a temporary wallet on either machine, and the player cashes out at the recycler's touch screen. Val will convert the D27 dual-screen trade-ins to vertical, install Texas software and supply the recycler. **If it fails, Val converts the machines to Georgia COAM for free**; Yash brings them to Val's shop. Yash will talk to his partners (his brother and one partner Val met) on Friday and will spend about ten days in Texas in November. Target: **mid-to-end of January**. He suggested starting east, near Louisiana, where stores are bigger, or at Nix (Margaret Green's high-volume location, about 8 games).

**Bangalore.** Yash also puts his own software in his locations. He asked for JVL's tech side to work with his tech team in Bangalore, and to send hardware samples there for QA. Val said it is just the SAS protocol and a harness from the Flexport into the second SAS port.

**Texas market notes (from Yash).** Players are stuck on Banilla. Primero does badly in Texas (he ordered 30–40). Someone he knows is buying bootleg Aristocrat copies for gas stations; both called that trouble. Other cheap games survive only because they are cheap filler.

**Other.** Long talk (AI tools, India and China). [Personal passage removed — owner ruling 2026-09-28: the JVL brain holds JVL matters only. Original kept in the source vault.] Val liked a Montblanc line — "we are not a fashion brand, we are an established brand" — and said JVL will use it.
