## Summary

Larry called Val with a reworked structure for the transactional-equipment JV after talking to John. Mostly English, some Russian.

**One entity, not two.** Larry researched further: an LLC can allocate depreciation separately from profit and revenue, so one entity is enough. Structure: one JV company, Val and Larry 50/50, with a special allocation of depreciation to Larry's side. The allocation can be changed in the partnership agreement every year to match what each side needs.

**Pricing and proof of concept.** JVL invoices the JV at $4,000 per machine, not $12,000. The JV puts out 50 to 100 machines as a proof of concept. Val called it a 100-machine trial; Larry said no handshake is needed and to do more if JVL can (125, 150), because more machines make a stronger case to a bank.

**Financing path after the proof.** Larry will take the business to the banks he already uses for his railcars (Bell Bank, Seacoast). The JV puts about one third down, the bank finances the rest, and the note is repaid from inside the company. If the banks say no, the partners keep financing machines themselves and still capture the depreciation. Second idea: also approach InComm, offering a first right of refusal on a future acquisition in exchange for financing. Val: InComm has at least two competitors to approach as well, one he could not name with certainty and NCR, which is being bought by Brinks.

**No inflated values.** Val worried he would have to invoice a higher amount to book income at JVL. Larry: invoice the real $4,000 per machine; the JV puts 33 percent down and the bank finances 67 percent; JVL receives the full invoice amount and books its profit and taxes on exactly what it receives. Val: with Larry guaranteeing the loan, zero down is also possible.

**Risk.** Val wants a small test so partners do not lose money if something was not thought through. Larry: disappointments happen daily in his real business; 100 machines is a large enough sample to find and fix the problems and then go to a bank with monthly recurring revenue. Val's view: past about 1,000 installations the business finances itself.

**Next.** Larry sends the structure and Excel tables for 3, 5, 7 and 10-year lease terms today. Val tweaks them. Once agreed, Gabe opens a Florida LLC and drafts the partnership agreement. The company gets a license if it needs one.

## Decisions Made

- **One JV entity (Florida LLC), Val and Larry 50/50, with a special allocation of depreciation.** — An LLC can allocate depreciation apart from profit, so a second entity is not needed. (Decided by Larry Sharon, agreed by Val Levitan)
- **100-machine trial as the proof of concept; more if JVL can deploy more.** — Limits the money at risk; large enough sample for a bank case. (Decided by Val Levitan, Larry Sharon)
- **JVL invoices the JV at the real $4,000 per machine; no inflated values.** — JVL books exactly what it receives. (Decided by Larry Sharon, Val Levitan)
- **After the proof, Larry approaches banks (Bell Bank, Seacoast) and possibly InComm with a first right of refusal.** (Decided by Larry Sharon)
