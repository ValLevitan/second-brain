## Summary

**The operator.** Blue Sky has about 100 locations; Gold Rush about 30 (new Megahit places). Chirag owned Gold Rush and is president of "HRE", a convenience-store retail association with about 6,000 stores; he is still a partner. The team is new in the industry, recently bought the company, and is also looking at North Carolina, Virginia and Texas. Bad Dog machines do very well for them. Their cash flow is tight.

**V43 cabinet demo (Felipe).** 10 games, 5 shown now; more can be turned on later based on results. Progressives: Jackpot Rush, a spin-and-hold type (heard "Fiery Iceland"), Lion's Riches, Diamond Leap. The new build with Twin Bison and Lion's Riches went out only two days ago. Retail price $15,750; topper about $800 extra. Felipe advises against JCM bill acceptors because the new $10 bill will need an upgrade JCM may not offer.

**Transactional demo (Andrei, Val).** Flex-IQ at the counter, kiosks (gift-card dispenser, cash, recycler), POS for wallets, Flexport in each machine. One e-wallet ticket moves money between machines without the counter, and lasts 30 days (configurable). At redemption the cashier splits the money: lottery, gift card (InComm Prize Play now), or keep on the wallet. **Val: JVL is building nine categories that the location owner defines** (for example gas, lottery, a store card). The Flexport fits the Class III cutout with a universal bracket; for non-casino cabinets like Banilla it sits in a plastic case that sticks out. Works now with dual-SAS machines; a second version will cover others and later go wireless.

**Pricing and show special.** For 9 machines: about $17,000–18,000 for kiosk, Flex-IQ and 9 ports, plus a fixed $90 per month (no variable fee). Show special: buy a cabinet at $15,750 and get Flex-IQ, Flex Slim, the port in the machine plus 5 more, free (about $15,000 of value). **A 9% price increase starts December 31.** **Val: no financing on machines already discounted this much.** JVL installs the first location. LTE modem (new machines only) sends zero-revenue alerts; JVL pays the data.

**Fiery Pay and the market.** The operator uses Fiery Pay at $69.99 per month, with TITO possible but no printers. They say a Fiery Pay Mastercard can move money back into a machine with no fee. Val doubts it: moving cash out of Mastercard needs a merchant number. The operator will send a video within a week. They say the market is down 30–40% because of the gift-card change and online gambling, and expect new Georgia legislation in March.

**Refurbished machines.** JVL has 20 trade-in machines, fully refurbished, 90-day warranty, no topper; not combined with the 50% show special. The operator wants all 20 at about $9,000 each with 12 months financing, as a trial before buying new. **Val does not think $9,000 is possible** (some units have broken monitors, touchscreens or PC boards); he is thinking of 14 or 16 monthly payments instead, and asked them to say exactly what they will buy. A deal needs a credit check, the company as guarantor and a bank reference. The operator plans to upgrade 65 machines across locations in 2027. Val cited test results: 12 of 14 test machines were bought; one buyer committed to 65 games. He explained JVL's soft cost (about $6,500 per machine) and approval costs (GLI about $9,000 per game in South Dakota; about $10,000 per cabinet for Georgia Lottery).

Val is next in Georgia the first week of November; he discusses the deal with Felipe on Oct 16.
