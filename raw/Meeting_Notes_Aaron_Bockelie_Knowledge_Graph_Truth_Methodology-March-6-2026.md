## Summary

**Aaron's demonstration.** He showed a research paper ("Intent Amplification and Neural Hallucination: Feedback Dynamics in Human-AI Systems") written entirely by agents traversing his knowledge graph — content loaded, hundreds of queries run, a full paper in ~30 minutes needing only adjustments. Val: "This is incredible." The graph mechanics shown live: concepts with provenance (evidence + quotes), typed relationships (requires, exploits, contrasts with, composed of…), real-time grounding/diversity/authentication scores, cluster plots of concept regions with positive (supportive) and negative (detracting) grounding, and linguistic bridges (causation, evidentials, logical/modification/operation arguments).

**Val's practical challenge — "how do I extract truth from transcripts?"** Aaron's answer, distilled:
- You don't extract truth; truth **emerges as weighted structure**. Every statement is embedded and similarity-matched against everything else; consistent restatement across contexts grows a concept's score, contradictions leave "weak points."
- The fortune-cookie analogy: enough fortune cookies read against a life reveal the life.
- Diversity beats repetition: a hundred people saying the same thing means something; a hundred *different* concepts all connecting to the same target concept makes that target "truthier."
- The energy theory: "it's less energetic to tell the truth than to tell a lie" — agenda-pushing produces a detectable signature (same phrasing, low true diversity), and the graph computes it.
- Positioning vs the transcript library: the archive is the library; the graph is "the books pulled off the shelves, photocopied and highlighted" — the short-term strategic instrument. With a live transcript shunt formulating questions in real time, it becomes a second intelligence sitting in on conversations ("Exactly — especially based on facts," Val).

**Val's restatement (accepted by Aaron):** load all transcripts, apply the graph, blocks of weighted positive/negative concepts form, and overlap tells you how close to truth you are on a subject at a given time.

**Application chosen.** Aaron proposed starting with the [personal real estate]/manufactured-housing thesis (ontologies for financial value, strategy, method; Claude researching external evidence; trusted-adviser conversations folded in; the graph then confirms or **contradicts** — "you either have humility and go 'I was wrong,' or you go get the evidence"). Val's decision: **"I suggest we do this with JVL."** Next step: a scoping meeting to pick the aspect to analyze.
