# Meeting Summary — October Rollout Staging and Unit Ramp

**Date:** September 9, 2026, 8:43 AM Eastern
**Duration:** approximately 27 minutes
**Attendees:** Val Levitan plus members of the JVL engineering and sales team. Plaud labels speakers by number only, so nothing beyond Val's voice can be attributed with confidence. Named in the discussion: Andrei, Felipe, Genadi, Sebastian, Anibal.

## Summary

Val ran the team through how the first transactional installation gets staged and what the ramp looks like for the rest of the year. He held the meeting to scheduling only and deferred anything else.

**The October 1 assumption.** Val told the team to work to a first installation on October 1. He said he does not know whether it is October 1 or September 28 and that it does not matter — assume October 1. The hard constraint he restated: Flex IQ and FlexPort cannot both go in for that date, so the location gets either FlexPay or IQ, not both. The bundled offer still stands; it just gets delivered in stages.

**Unit ramp.** Val set the scale for the last quarter: 100 units in October, 500 in November, 1,000 in December. He was explicit that a "unit" is not a location — it counts ports, Pay, IQ and the machine bundled together. Working from about six ports, one IQ, one Pay and one game machine, that is seven units per location. He also said plainly he is not claiming this is how it will actually turn out; the numbers are there to convey the scope and the pace he wants.

**Everything goes to SAS, on wires first.** Val told the team to stop talking about non-SAS connection — everything is SAS. All ports connect only to the second fastboard. Through October and probably November, installations run wires, one wire per device, in parallel. He said the WiFi migration is what comes after, and told the team not to raise WiFi with customers yet.

**Testing has to start September 15.** To install physically by October 1, experimenting starts the middle of next week. The critical part is that FlexPorts must be tested in non-JVL machines — Val asked for at least one or two, preferring Aristocrat and Light & Wonder, even for a single day. They cannot be bought and cannot be commissioned, so the plan is to borrow one, bring it in, and fake-commission and fake-test it. Felipe is to arrange the machines. The drawings and brackets have to come from Light & Wonder, IGT, Banilla and Aristocrat, who must release them; Val said they will, and the drawings go to Genadi.

**Test the negative cases first.** Val was firm that the first thing tested is negative cases — door open being his example — and said he did not want to go into granular detail beyond that. Routers also need testing on two different manufacturers; he told the team to avoid Cisco on cost ("crazy money") and to use no Chinese manufacturer. He noted a ten-port router will be hard to find, and that a borrowed router is only available for a day or two, so the tests have to be run hard and fast.

**LTE modems.** The machine, Pay and IQ all need the LTE modem, and the gateways for IQ and Pay are needed for certain.

**Requirements.** Val said the most important thing is that every functional requirement is tied to the capabilities for this particular October 1 rollout. He was told the requirements would be finalised that day. Val said explicitly he does not care about the non-functional requirements here — functional means user experience, interfaces, the use cases. He said he sees no bottlenecks and the job is just to deliver.

**Hardware readiness.** Genadi's first priority is changing the mould — making another mould with the screen removed — and his second is installing the stands and all the internal components and harnesses. Brackets are already done and sent to the manufacturer. Stock as stated in the meeting: 100 plastics, 20 hardware, unlimited plastic for the Pay unit, about 100 screens; boards are being redesigned and arrive in about two weeks. Val pushed back on the panic, pointing out that October 1 needs five and ten and four units, not the full run. The printer is the open risk — Val is not sure the actual printer will arrive installed, and if it does not, the fallback is to install the other configuration, since Pay and IQ do not work together anyway.

**Why both Pay and IQ in one location.** Someone asked why JVL would install Pay and IQ in the same location. Val's answer was about control, not function: he believes there will be a need, and he does not want anyone else's equipment offered to an attendant, or an attendant deciding to keep their PAX alongside. He wants an exclusive agreement with each location plus a non-disturbance clause — "a lot of legal mumbo-jumbo that neither you nor operator nor location will pay attention to" — so that when it comes to enforcement he holds all the aces. He also wants the screens reserved to JVL even for informational use.

**How the giveaway is pitched.** The operator buys the machine and gets the ports for the other machines. Val corrected a colleague mid-explanation: the operator does not have to buy additional ports. If a location has nine machines it gets more ports; if it has three, it keeps the spares. Val then cut the wider commercial question off, saying he respected it but would not handle it in this meeting, and returned to scheduling.

**Installation labour.** Val's instruction was specific: hire junior electrical tradesmen with an electrical certificate — not technicians, not programmers, not university or college people. Laying wire is the longest part of an installation and electricians do it fastest and best. They are only needed for the first quarter, because after that everything moves to WiFi. For each installation he suggested sending Sebastian in one direction and Anibal in the other. A colleague said he has another person in his back pocket but would rather not use him, and raised hiring temps to build the ports; Val agreed they would have to, while noting temps are usually not efficient.

**Open risk raised by the team:** getting the shipment over the ocean handled by next week.
