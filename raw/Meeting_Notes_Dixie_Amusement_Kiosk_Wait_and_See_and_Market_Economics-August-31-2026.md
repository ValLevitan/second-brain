## Summary

The most informative operator conversation in this batch. Salim is a master licensee with about **70 locations**, VP of the state association, and runs a **buying group of roughly 30–40 operators** (about 190 people in the wider association). He is currently **testing two JVL machines**, installed about a month and a half ago, and Val opened by telling him they are averaging **$290 per day**, which Salim accepted without argument: "I'll take it."

**Why he will not buy a kiosk.** Salim's position is a deliberate wait-and-see, and his reasoning is the useful part. The regulators are not giving clear answers and, in his view, deliberately keep it murky because that is where the fines come from and they do not want to pick sides. He has seen this before: when the law came in 2013 it took two years to get clarity. Gift cards became effective July 1 and there is still no settled answer on cash with or without an ATM. He does not want to invest, then invest again, then invest a third time. His blunt summary of the whole vendor field: **"nobody has yet figured out an end-to-end system."** He has seen JVL's three units and everyone else's.

He also laid out a political timeline that matters. The gift card started as a pilot five years ago, with the stated intent of eliminating cash because the Georgia Constitution does not allow it; it was regulated two years ago and became effective July 1. Lawmakers and regulators are now, in his words, annoyed, but they are silent because the **midterm election is in November** — nobody wants trouble before it. **January is when the reckoning comes**, and he expects a hard conversation about why the industry asked for gift cards to remove cash and then went straight back to cash. He also warned that the Lottery is only the regulator, not the lawmaker or the enforcer; the enforcers are the GBI, local police and sheriffs, and if they decide the constitution is being breached it becomes an uphill battle against the state and the attorney general.

**What he runs today.** InComm via PAX, obtained free and contracted **directly by the location, not by him**. He has added Fiery Pay in two locations. MudaGoose stays in place underneath — if Fiery Pay does not approve something, the location falls back to MudaGoose and PAX.

**The credit-risk argument.** Val pressed on what happens when a location does not pay, having heard from InComm that many do not. Salim's answer was clean: it is not his problem and not the distributor's — the location contracts directly with InComm, InComm sets the credit limit, and if the location does not pay, its credit is cut. Val explained why the manufacturer cannot take that position: on a $75,000 machine sale he can afford to go to court, but on four cards at $500 each the distributor earns $4 and cannot chase $2,000. Val's conclusion is that the **only viable route is exclusivity in the location** — remove MudaGoose, rewire to JVL, install the POS and Flex ports, and retain the ability to shut the location off. Salim agreed nobody wants to be in that business and said the others simply let the location and the provider deal with each other directly.

**Where the money actually is.** Val took apart the prize-card economics using a Fiery Pay fee sheet Salim had attached to a card and handed over: $2 activation, a **$2.95 monthly dormancy fee**, a $6 lost-card fee, and 3–5% per transaction — which Val calculated as roughly **9.6% per year** on a $1,000 card. He extended it: Georgia paid out about **$5 billion**, providers keep the float, the residual balances and the commission, and he put InComm's overall take at around 15%, or roughly **$750 million a year** — against which giving away 5,000 PAX devices at $300 each ($1.5 million) is nothing. His verdict: "I'm in the wrong business."

**Market numbers exchanged.** ~47,000 machines across ~7,400 locations (5.9 per location), up from ~35,000 three years ago — about 4,000 new machines a year. Salim reported operator revenue down 20–25%, blaming spending, gas prices and seasonality; Val challenged this directly, citing the M2 money-supply report and roughly $700 billion a year being added, and said nobody outside Georgia reports a decline. Salim then reframed it: total money is up 4% year on year, but it is **diluted across more machines and more locations**, while equipment costs have risen (JVL at just under $16,000, Aristocrat at $22–23,000, IGT ~$14,180). Machine replacement runs at about **20% a year** in his group because legacy equipment is still being cleared out; Val put the mature-market figure at 10% and Salim agreed it will fall there as the industry ages — it is only thirteen years old.

**Access to the buying group.** Val asked directly how to get in front of Salim's colleagues. Salim was discouraging about dinners and lukewarm on open houses — "people are tired of open houses" — and Val agreed, noting JVL has been in the market five years and is a known entity. The realistic route Val named: Salim personally telling his friends to come for an hour. Salim is on the **four-person association executive board** meeting after the trade show (Wednesday–Friday that week) to debrief the show; the ex-president Sean and current second-year president Mike Patel were mentioned.

**A side thread.** Val asked Salim to sanity-check the $1.5M building JVL is buying (10,000 sq ft, freestanding, three truck-level doors, metal beam). Salim, who paid $1 million for his own comparable building and does his real estate at an 8–9 cap, called $1.5M a good deal and good value on replacement cost, though he noted the north side (Gwinnett) is where the businesses are and buildings there run 50% higher. Val invited him to look at the building; they agreed on **Tuesday, with lunch**.

Salim also offered to introduce Val to Fiery Pay — he is friends with them, and with Adam Faus who brought him onto the board ten years ago. JVL had previously walked away from Fiery Pay because they asked JVL to sign an NDA, which Val refused as a technology company working on the same thing.

## Decisions Made

- **Salim will not buy kiosks or transactional equipment now** — a deliberate wait until the regulator, lawmakers and manufacturers settle, so he does not pay two or three times.
- **Val's model for transactional equipment requires exclusivity in the location** — MudaGoose removed, JVL rewires, POS and Flex ports installed, remote shut-off retained. Anything less leaves JVL carrying credit risk for $1 per card.
- **Open houses and dinners rejected** as the way into Salim's buying group; the route is Salim personally convening his friends.
- **Val to visit Salim's building on Tuesday**, with lunch, and Salim to look at JVL's $1.5M building.
