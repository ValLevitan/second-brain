## Summary

Val called Doug first, before any paper goes out, to tell him he has to make a cash call at JVL. The company does not have enough cash to operate. Val expects most partners will not write a check and will end up diluted. He offered full open-book access — Doug or his CFO can inspect anything; Doug said the books are not the issue and he is not suspecting anything improper.

The numbers Val gave: total fixed burn is about $500K a month against current revenue of about $250K, so a net burn of roughly $300K a month (rounded). Val is thinking of putting in $1M himself. JVL has $1.9M (he also said $1.8–1.9M) in approved tax rebates — a media tax credit covering two years plus SR&ED for the last year — approved, just waiting on payment timing. Together that is roughly $2.8M, about a year of runway to turn the company around. Val expects the net burn to fall quickly (300 → 200 → 150 → eventually zero) as orders recover, and believes a return to prior revenue means about $3M a year of profit, plus new upside: the transactional product being introduced at the August trade show (revenue realistically 2027), South Dakota open, and Nebraska in progress.

The cause of the crisis: a Linux 4.7 kernel bug in combination with the AMD processor silently froze machines, leaving the game picture on screen. Machines lost revenue for half a year before anyone understood why — Val suspected games and content first. The fix took two weeks once found, and machines now show very good results, but sales must be won back ("take our product for free" uphill battle). No recourse against the vendor. Val also noted the factory was moved to Georgia and the local factory closed, which cut the expense line.

Val says the company has no outside debt — only internal debt — and is not buying equipment or inventory. There are no company-owned profit-share machines left to use as security (Mexico and Peru closed, Colombia being milked), and the engineering is in Belarus, which makes the company hard to sell.

Doug's core advice: don't send a bare cash-call letter — some partners may respond badly after years of silence ("we don't hear from you for five years, now you send a letter saying you want money"). Instead borrow short-term money, which needs no partner approval, and repay it from the SR&ED refund when it lands. He proposed pref debt — debt sitting in front of everything, secured effectively by the approved CRA refund with a pay-direction so the lender is repaid automatically when the money comes in. Doug said he might co-fund it (illustrative: $300K from him and $300K from Val to bridge until the refund). Val asked directly if Doug would participate in the form of pref debt; Doug said yes — that is exactly what he is describing. Doug also recommended the CRA complaints-department route to speed up payment; both had used it before (Doug got a dated payment letter five weeks after complaining; Val got money within five days last year).

Sizing: Val floated that after the trade show the company may only need $1M for a short period; Doug worked the numbers down further — maybe not $1M, maybe $600K to get through ninety days. Doug urged him to take the weekend to think ("refill my glass"), wait at least until after the trade show, and then sit down together.

Val's mood was low ("I don't know how to make it right. I am ready to cry"; "I'm tired... It's my last attempt"). He is 63. On partners: Doug guessed the Muzzo family likely won't respond, Joe DiTomasso had choice words eighteen months ago, Steve would likely say "do what you got to do" but not answer a cash call.

## Decisions Made

- **No cash-call letter yet — Val takes the weekend to consider short-term/pref debt secured by the SR&ED refund instead** — Doug's proposal; Val agreed to sit by the fire, think it over, and not jump in. (Val Levitan, Doug Skrepnek)
