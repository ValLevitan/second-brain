## Summary

Klimovich called the meeting at Pavel's request: the capabilities are written, and the team needed to set priorities and assign them to releases so development could gain tempo and everyone would know what is delivered in which version. Most of the session was spent going down the capability list on screen and deciding, item by item, what stays in MVP1 and what moves out.

**Where the requirements stand.** Klimovich still has five capabilities plus their requirements to write. After that he does the UI/UX description of the screens with the texts. Last priority is the operations dashboard update — he said releasing with the current dashboard would be acceptable and it can be finished afterwards. He is spending the whole day on it and expects to need about half of Monday, after which the full package is complete: all requirements, all screens, all texts.

**Two releases created.** Pavel created two releases in the product capability space — Transactional MVP1 and Transactional MVP2 — dated October 1 and October 30, and began linking capabilities to MVP1 and creating features under them. Irina corrected the sorting criterion directly: MVP1 is what must be done by October 1, not what Pavel had or had not understood. Pavel clarified his uncertainty was only about the fragmented gateway capabilities. The release was also renamed to put "Transactional" first.

**What separates MVP1 from MVP2.** Pavel's line: MVP1 runs through the linked network and a location may have only one kiosk — either IQ or FlexPay. Everything else goes to MVP2. Irina restated the priority order as machine + port + IQ first, machine + port + Pay second, both before October 1. Val confirmed later in the meeting that for October 1 they are finally committing to one configuration, Flex IQ — and said plainly that this is driven by the bottleneck the team says exists; if there is no bottleneck, MVP1 gets reconsidered. The port stays in either way.

**Who does what.** Dima Chudzinau works Flex IQ first. Sasha Biratka takes the FlexPort tasks and ports Dima's completed work into FlexPay; Sergey Tsymanov covers the FlexPay server side. Klimovich's own priority is to ship the IQ UX first and only then turn to the kiosks.

**Card limits, and the split that got dropped.** The current integration auto-fails transactions under about $5 or $10 — logic that does not work with the portal and has to be revisited. The maximum is $1,000 per card. Klimovich proposed splitting a payout across several cards; Val disagreed. Pavel's simpler answer was adopted: issue the card for $1,000 and leave the remainder on the player's ticket, so the player can keep playing or present the ticket again for another card. Chudzinau noted that on FlexPay the cashier drives this, so a dialogue appears offering to put the rest on the next card or leave it. Klimovich confirmed the full flow — $1,000 to the card, remainder on the ewallet, the existing logic prints a receipt showing both amounts, the cashier hands back receipt, card and ewallet, then scans again for the next card. The split logic was pulled out of MVP1 onto MVP2, with Klimovich saying it is not certain it belongs in MVP2 either. The second screen is being removed and that work is already under way.

**Capability structure and naming.** Pavel found the gateway capabilities (gateway ewallet creation, gateway ewallet info display) too fragmented and suggested folding them into one "Ewallet Operations" capability. Klimovich explained he could not find historical capabilities covering that functionality, so he wrote new ones on a unified model — gateway as an object, with kiosk gateway and IQ kiosk gateway underneath, and a common capability requiring every gateway to create a wallet, read a wallet and so on. They settled on keeping them separate, because it makes the different test executions cleaner to delineate; the features underneath will be test executions and autotests written with Claude against the regulations. Pavel created an initiative for the ewallet-operation capabilities so they need not be duplicated across FlexPort, which the hierarchy would otherwise block. Val pushed back on "FlexPort" as a bucket name — too narrow — and asked for something broader on the ewallet side; Pavel agreed he can move everything from Flex into Ewallet Operations, and the same applies to the FlexPay capability. The capability count is around thirty.

**Acceptance criteria live in regulations, not capabilities.** Bosik has three identical WiFi capabilities, one per product, and agreed to reformat them into one. Chudzinau challenged the rule — if a third product needs the same WiFi modem a year later, do you reopen the old capability or create a new one? Irina's answer settled it: capabilities carry no acceptance criteria; the criteria and requirements live in the regulations, which stay. Chudzinau said he had not known about that change. Pavel's mechanism, originally Dima Kovalchuk's proposal, is a Component field in the capabilities project so items can be tagged FlexPay and so on rather than prefixed in the title.

**Shared components across devices.** Bosik raised that Flex IQ, FlexPay and future devices share a great deal — WiFi, screens, the QR-code scanner, remote update — and that changing one UI element should not mean editing every repository. Klimovich fully supported this and said the screen logic and design should be unified across devices at different resolutions. Val pointed to Dima Kovalchuk as the person who knows the subject and said he would likely propose doing it as a component. Bosik stressed his point is broader than screens: it is about the product build and propagating a change from one place into all products.

**What came out of MVP1.** Bill escrow moved to MVP2 — it is kiosk-only. PIN protection moved to MVP2 as well: it is for the self-service kiosk, and on Flex IQ the cashier will not enter a PIN for the player, so there is no player PIN screen. The hook stays so PIN can exist, defaulted off; Klimovich had already described IQ as PIN-free in the regulation. Val stepped in to separate two things that were being confused — Klimovich moving items out of MVP1 (correct) and Pavel pointing out the two workstreams run in parallel (which only affects scheduling).

**Moto-guse.** Val asked for explicit confirmation on the transcript that MVP1 never has Moto-guse, and got it. He then pressed to rip the supporting code out rather than switch it off, on the principle that code left behind always crawls back and gets in the way, and said he would accept a day or two for it. Pavel put the cost at 2 of 14 — about 20% of the time. Chudzinau said he already has a variant where they just switch mode, and would rather spend the time on more important work. The removal was assigned to MVP3.

**Lease blocking.** Val asked whether the lease feature is on in phase 1. Chudzinau said it is formally already integrated, built on the Moto-guse mode and being reused. Val asked for it to be written in so it gets tested, and extended the scope: blocking must cover the FlexPorts too, not just the terminal, so players cannot play. Klimovich will add requirements for FlexPort screens showing adequate states. Irina asked what state the machine is left in — the bill validator in particular. Val's view is they can disable through SAS; Pavel thought the bill acceptor can be disabled; Val asked for it to be tested. A related exchange cleared up an old misunderstanding: automatic blocking on non-payment was switched off during the Dolla Play production payment testing, on Val's own instruction, and Val confirmed the standing rule — never block automatically, but blocking manually through the hub by setting a location's lease to expired stops everything.

**Remote update and remote config are two things.** Val separated sending an update (no configuration) from configuring afterwards. Remote update goes to MVP1, remote config to MVP2 — Chudzinau's reasoning being that there is nothing to configure yet, and once they decide what is configurable, implementation is not a problem. Software and firmware management for Flex IQ was moved onto MVP1. Per-location gateway configuration is the capability that sets a location's parameters; requirements come later, including PIN at the location's discretion, and a separate list is needed of what can be configured per kiosk, per POS and per location.

**Machine compatibility is the live risk.** Pavel flagged that FlexPort compatibility will be implemented for some machine models and not others, may need different logic per type, and may be work that lands after MVP2 per cabinet type. Klimovich agreed they hit this already in MVP1 and said the most they can do is find out as early as possible which machines sit at the first installation locations. The Georgia office has no non-JVL cabinets, so he is arranging with operators either to visit a warehouse with many different machines or to have several machines lent to the JVL warehouse for a day or two — a window of one to two days maximum, in which they install a FlexPort, run a quick compatibility test, and move it across as many machines as possible to collect information. Everything must be ready for testing by then. The first installation itself will be a real location with at least one JVL machine plus machines from other manufacturers.

**Network access for that testing.** Pavel said they need the VPN into the Georgia office by then so they can work directly from the internal network, and asked how it works off-site at a guest warehouse. Bosik's answer was a router with an LTE modem, which becomes a requirement: router with LTE modem, configured. He will check whether the router he already requested has it. Klimovich said this needs solving very quickly.

**Remote firmware update of FlexPorts.** Bosik asked whether MVP1 covers updating FlexPort firmware remotely; Pavel said it will be needed, 100%. It currently sits on MVP2 out of concern there is not enough time to test it properly. Bosik split it into two tasks — selecting a master device, then distributing the update — and said the master-device functionality needs to be in MVP1. Pavel laid out the staging: Dima extends the current firmware-dissemination mechanism to all FlexPay and Flex IQ (this is what Alexey is checking and fixing now); then remote update as step two; then the whole FlexPort fleet on one unified update system, with kiosks distributing to ports. The absolute fallback is USB update of the FlexPorts, and Klimovich said nobody will object to driving out to install an update during the pilots. Chudzinau added SSH to the terminal plus a script as a further backstop, and said the important thing is that FlexPort can at least act as a client. Bosik asked specifically for the transcript that FlexPort firmware be placed by the workflow straight into the same Amazon S3 storage that holds all kiosk firmware, and distributed from there, so there is one storage location.

**LTE modem in every device, or one router per location.** Bosik questioned the plan to put LTE modem support in every device — with several Flex kiosks at one location it makes more sense to put in a router with an LTE modem that all devices use. Val's position was that it is not worth it because data is cheap and installing another device plus writing software for it costs more; Bosik answered that no software is written for a router, it is a bought device where you insert a SIM and it hands out internet. Pavel called it the right question and pointed at the case of FlexPay and Flex IQ at one location. The exchange was cut off and this did not reach a clear decision.

**Capability workflow statuses.** Dmitri Kovalchuk was called in because the green "Accepted" status in the middle of the capability workflow misleads — it reads as work done when the work is only starting. The meaning was fixed rather than the status removed: Accepted means Pavel reviewed it, discussed it and accepted it; taken into work is the move to To Do, which happens when the team says these capabilities are going into MVP1. Kovalchuk will automate it so any feature moving to In Progress moves its capability from To Do to In Progress, and back. He cannot do it on the fly — it needs testing — so it was left for Monday, along with moving all MVP1 capabilities into To Do.

**Automated tests.** Pavel asked whether they want automatic tests for the ewallet use cases. Val said yes, on the grounds that anything not automated gets done by hand and hands always create a bottleneck. Features will be created and scheduled by Irina and assigned, with Alexey Khamin named as the likely owner; Irina said to assign to Lyosha in any case and have Dima supply whatever is needed. Bosik had Dima add Alexey Khamin and Sergey Tsymanov to the capabilities project during the call. Pavel noted automated testing will cover only a small part for now, tied to Flex IQ. Nothing is scheduled beyond October 1, and next week is heavily parallel.

**In-house rehearsal before October 1.** Val asked how they practise before installing anything on October 1 — assemble it, set it up, watch it work, train on it. The Georgia office already has assembled Flex IQ units, so it is a matter of installing the new software, connecting the TITO printer and moving the QR scanner; Pavel said Genadi should control that. FlexPort software gets released as functionality is added, and Elizabeth can already flash if needed. Chudzinau expects a simpler route once the first build is out, updating the Georgia terminals automatically. On staging, Bosik described the current mechanism: a GitHub Action creates an artefact that lands on Amazon, and a web interface to the transactional terminal management system has a button to choose which update version goes to which device. A demo was offered and Val declined — he had another meeting.

**Overtime.** Pavel proposed Dima work Saturday and Sunday on the services needing work for FlexPort and Flex IQ communication and card activation on the ewallet path — for extra money rather than time off, about 14 hours. Val agreed and went further: he wants everyone working all days for extra money, said the schedule is wrong if only one person is needed, and asked for it to be accelerated across the board, while acknowledging he cannot force anyone. Pavel will also work the weekend on FlexPort and on checking whether he can block the bill validator. Zhenya cannot work this weekend; Sasha is also unavailable, and his tasks are FlexPay, which MVP1 does not need. Val closed by saying that from today to December 15 there is a great deal that has to be done.

**Side note on WiFi coverage.** Before the meeting proper, Bosik was working out whether WiFi will reach across a location. Klimovich described a typical Georgia site — a petrol station with four machines by the wall in the retail area and another five around a corner behind a wall, all counted as one location, with about five metres between points and walls in between.
