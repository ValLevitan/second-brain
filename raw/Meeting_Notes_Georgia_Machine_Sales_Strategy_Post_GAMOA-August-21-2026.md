## Summary

Felipe reported back on the V43 "Mega Hit Blaze" slot machines after the Georgia GAMOA show, and the session turned into a hard back-and-forth between Val and Felipe about whether there is a real plan to sell machines in September, October, and November.

**Show read-out (Felipe).** Mega Hit Blaze drew attention but not yet purchase confidence — the market runs on hype and performance perception, and Aristocrat is the hot brand right now, which makes price matter less than expected. JVL's two-month grace-period financing was not compelling against competitors' longer 0%/longer-grace offers. Felipe logged 21 conversations (~19 companies) at the show; roughly the same core Georgia masters attend each year. United Gaming was absent. AGS is reportedly entering Georgia next year (Felipe first said "Scientific Gaming"; Juan corrected that SG is now Light & Wonder).

**ILAS/ILERS report.** JVL was missing from the ILERS report circulating at the show and customers noticed. The standing agreement is to show JVL's footprint only, not performance — because there are not enough new games in the field yet to look good on performance. Juan already contacted ILERS; the team agreed to keep footprint-only for now.

**Cell modem on every machine.** Val confirmed: from September 1, every machine that ships gets the modem and SIM installed, but JVL only *activates* the modem for customers who sign the data-sharing agreement.

**ARC.** Wants to buy the 5 machines currently on trial and order 15 more, delivery starting the first week of September with the cell modem and V3 software (Felipe to coordinate with Cris).

**Bulldog Gaming.** One of the largest Georgia masters; they had 5 machines that froze in the field. Brian Deaton wants the 5 back mid-October and a 90-day trial so he can decide in January and place a real order. Val approved the Bulldog 90-day trial without hesitation — but stressed trials must be selective and well-considered, not the default answer for everyone.

**Trials vs. terms — the core argument.** Val pushed hard against giving away brand-new machines on trial. His preferred play for masters who say "your games aren't making money" is to convert their existing under-performing *vertical* machines to the new software for free, on the condition that JVL installs its cell modem — no new hardware cost, and it proves the new software makes money. For warm/hot customers he wants to move on terms and discounts, not trials, and he questioned whether cold customers should be approached at all. He asked Felipe to classify the pipeline by temperature (cold/warm/hot) and market influence ("market movers" like Bulldog), and to mark which masters have convertible vertical cabinets.

**JVL Dollars as a sales lever.** Felipe wants to use the JVL-dollars program with Raul (Texas Play), a decision-maker who influences ~10 masters, alongside Mega Hit Blaze. Val's angle: sell Flex ports that carry JVL cash, so a customer with, say, $2,000 in JVL cash applies it toward a $13,000 machine (buys for ~$11,000) — putting two products in the market instead of discounting one.

**Forecast pressure.** Val pressed repeatedly for September/October/November numbers. A tentative September list reached ~31 machines (e.g., VGL ~5, One GA Coin ≥3, Dixie ≥2, ARC 10 more, Time & Amusement ~4). Val argued that in a 4,000–5,000-machine/year market, JVL should be able to sell 400–500/year (~40/month) at a $13,000 machine, and could not see why October/November had no strategy beyond "talk to people." Juan proposed a concrete "show special" — one flat price, one strong package with terms, plus a customer testimonial quote (e.g., from ARC) — pushed out as an email blast so good that customers call in. Val agreed a quote could be requested from happy masters; Felipe agreed to draft the blast next week with the already-approved discount/trade-in/pay-in-full/data-sharing terms.

**Daily-hold dispute.** Val had been using ~$240/day net win; Felipe said the report shows $146–$147 (he had told Val $167 in chat earlier). Val argued you should take a percentage of gross money-in rather than read day-to-day net win. Unresolved. Felipe also said the fixed-bug new-version games are not clearly out-performing the older 19/1776 software, and the dual needs new games too.

**Tone.** This was tense. Val described Felipe as demoralized, tired, and "broken," and said he came out of the show bullish while Felipe seemed the opposite; Felipe pushed back that the final clean V2 shipped only Monday before the show, that he needs next week to work the masters, and that he is tired of changing directions. Val ended by saying he was "lost," that his comments were not useful, and left the meeting early, leaving Felipe and Juan to continue.

## Decisions Made

- **From September 1, install the cell modem + SIM on every machine shipped; activate only for customers who sign the data-sharing agreement.** (Decided by Val)
- **ARC order confirmed** — buy the 5 trial units plus 15 more, delivery from the first week of September with modem + V3 software. (Reported by Felipe; delivery via Cris)
- **Bulldog 90-day trial approved** (5 frozen machines returned mid-October). Trials remain the exception, not the default — move warm/hot customers on terms, not trials. (Decided by Val)
- **Keep the ILERS/ILAS report showing JVL's footprint only, not performance, for now.** (Juan/team agreed)
- **Preferred sales play: convert customers' existing under-performing vertical machines to the new software for free, conditioned on installing the cell modem, rather than giving away new trial machines.** (Val — strategy direction)
- **Build a "show special" email blast** with fixed pricing/terms and a customer testimonial quote. (Agreed by Val, Juan, Felipe)
