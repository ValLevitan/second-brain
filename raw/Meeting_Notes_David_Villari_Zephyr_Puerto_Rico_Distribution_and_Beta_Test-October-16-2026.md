## Summary

**Who David is.** Worked 7–8 years at Banilla and ran their sales; Texas, Pennsylvania and Virginia were his markets. In 2024 he started a game-reporting product for gray markets (name heard as "Riken"); he left after two years of slow R&D. He joined Bad Dog in January 2026. Bad Dog is now called Zephyr (also heard as "Sanford Gaming"), because the old logo read as "Bad Games". Owner is Brian (Fort Worth, Texas), who built the Aloha restaurant POS and sold it to NCR. Chief product officer is Justin Harris, who made the first Grover/Banilla game. About 25 staff, 2 sales reps. A partner, Gaming Capital Group, may place their games in Class III casinos; no purchase yet.

**Puerto Rico market (David's view).**
- Class III. About 120,000 illegal street machines and only 5,000–6,000 legal, connected ones. The legal cap is 20,000, so far from full.
- Only three games make money there: Novomatic, Bad Dog/Zephyr, EGT. No good games otherwise.
- A licence allows 100 machines; one holder can have several licences. State tax is 22%.
- Price must be under $10,000; about $9,000 "American price". Zephyr sells at about $8,000, FOB Miami / Fort Lauderdale. The distributor pays freight, port and duties, and gives operators terms; Zephyr gives its distributor 6-month terms. One ocean container holds about 70–72 machines.
- Need GLI certification sent to the Puerto Rico Gaming Board. For a Class III-certified maker like JVL it is a light, easy step.
- Machines need a bill acceptor or printer (ticket redemption). Kiosks would need to cost about $5,000; most places pay by hand at the counter.
- Zephyr's distributor there is Christian (Harvard graduate), partnered with a Puerto Rico real-estate owner; 3 licences. David also knows a Jaime ("Jamie"), who is looking for a new manufacturer. Michael Snyder (Las Vegas) tests games and places them in Latin America.
- The OTB business (heard as "Camineros"), linked to IGT, is for sale for about $45–65 million; a Georgia buyer with Goldman Sachs money is bidding.

**Market talk.** David says Banilla is losing share: Missouri shut down (about a third of their business) and Pennsylvania machines must be out of locations as of October 13. Grover was sold to Light & Wonder; its owner Garrett Blackwell started Palisades. David says Zephyr games hold about 16% (about 94% RTP). Verticals play better than dual screens, except Aristocrat and IGT. He also said Fiery Pay's back end was built by Bad Dog with RKS (Jim Beck, maker of MudaGoose); Bad Dog will sell kiosk hardware for Fiery Pay.

**Val's terms for Puerto Rico.** Val wants one reliable partner there. He does not want to finance, collect or chase. He will pay a 20% commission: $9,000 price, minus 20%. He wants to push the JVL transactional system too: port just under $1,000, table POS just under $1,000, kiosk about $8,500 (depends on content). Val did business in Puerto Rico before through CashCode bill acceptors and older machines.

**Next step.** David proposed a small beta: 1–3 machines in a location for about 90 days to see if the game plays. He will call Christian and Jaime on his drive and connect Val and Felipe with the right person. Commission would go to the person who pushes the product; David wants nothing for himself.
