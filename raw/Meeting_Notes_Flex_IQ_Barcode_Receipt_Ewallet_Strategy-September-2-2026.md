## Summary

Andrei Klimovich called the meeting to work through the changes Flex-IQ needs before the first pilot location can go live. The trade show produced a lot of feedback and, as he put it, the device met reality for the first time. He had posted the list to the chat beforehand and wanted the team to agree what it takes to fix each item and when the pilot can start.

Val opened with a framing point he deliberately parked rather than debated: the first installation runs on top of MudaGoose, but it is now clear to everyone that JVL will not and cannot work with MudaGoose long-term, and that the approach was wrong in the first place. Flex-Port has to stand on its own wiring, running its own cables to the machines. He did not want that to become the subject of the call — only that people keep it in view. Andrei's useful reply: roughly 80% of the changes being discussed will carry over to a port-based setup anyway, so the work is not wasted. Val added two consequences: some of these changes point toward a cheaper Flex-Port, and Flex-Port probably should not support a machine that has only one SAS port.

**The three pre-pilot changes.**

*1 — Activate InComm cards by barcode.* Cards for non-kiosk devices ship sealed, and nobody but the final recipient the player may open the packaging, so only the barcode is exposed and activation has to run off it. Kiosks are unaffected and keep their current flow. This raised a real question from Dzmitry Chudzinau and Pavel: the barcode does not carry the card number in the form a user recognises, so the last four digits shown on dashboards (the way Visa/Mastercard and banks ask for them) would not be available, and incident investigation could be hampered. Val pushed back hard on the premise, asking why the team assumed a player would come back to the location at all. Andrei settled it: in real life a player with a card that will not work calls InComm directly and the location has nothing to do with it; and physically, the barcode on the card is the same barcode as on the envelope, so after activation JVL holds enough identifiers to tie a record to a card and let an administrator recognise which card is meant. Pavel and Irina accepted the answer. Consequence for the software: reports and the operations dashboard drop the 16-digit number and use the barcode identifier instead, showing the last four digits of the number stored under the barcode. Hardware-wise the scanner has to move — Genadi has already ordered new boards arriving next week that allow mounting the scanner on the front panel; Val described rotating it 90 degrees and bringing it forward, aimed downward rather than at the player's face. Dzmitry had already tested scanning JVL's test cards successfully and estimated the code work at around an hour.

*2 — Print a ticket receipt.* The consent logic changes: the player no longer accepts anything on screen, because activating the card is itself deemed acceptance of the terms. In exchange the player must be handed a printed receipt showing clearly how much money was loaded and what fee was taken. Andrei had attached a mock-up, which now needs updating to swap the four digits of the 16-digit number for four digits of the barcode number. This pulls in hardware: the receipt has to print on the same printer MudaGoose uses. Andrei and Genadi agreed to roll the harness back to version 1, and the newly ordered boards should carry a switcher that passes the printer signal through Flex-IQ when IQ is active and disconnects when it is not, so MudaGoose can still print its own tickets bypassing JVL. Pavel noted the switch does not exist yet and needs designing — how to control port switching on the new board — and that with board lead times of three to four weeks after Genadi receives them, the work will likely have to be done remotely, possibly on a bench in Georgia, since a direct VPN link from Georgia to the Minsk office has just been initiated. Pavel also observed that the code for receipt printing already exists and is simply not enabled.

*3 — Simplify the user flow.* Nothing is required from the player: no confirmations, no interaction with the second screen. Andrei had named the existing screens and posted the naming to Confluence so everyone uses the same terms, and had written out the positive and negative flows with their nuances. There are also wording corrections where terms appear that are better not mentioned in Georgia — cosmetic, but worth finishing before walking into a location.

**Two items that were hanging.** On remote update, Dzmitry reported the work turned out harder than he and Andrei expected because they chose to go through Amazon, which pulled in terminal-side and Kubernetes work across all layers. He is running final tests and has positive cases: CI builds a new version, publishes it to Amazon storage with fast download and no access needed to GitHub for anyone; the operator gets a role in JVL's transactional system with a dashboard listing terminals and what is installed, picks terminals, picks the latest (or a chosen) bundle, and deploys; the terminal downloads online, and the operator can force a reboot or schedule it, usually overnight, after which the terminal reports itself updated. He has deliberately built it to extend to future terminals such as Flex-Pay and the recycler. Rollback to the last working version will be tested. Andrei asked whether next week's board change affects this — Dzmitry and Pavel: no, only new firmware adding support for the board. On the payment hub, Pavel confirmed everything Nathan built is deployed; Andrei will put enough money on the card and Pavel will re-run the API request right after the meeting so the stuck, errored transactions move to pending and retry ACH the following morning (thought to be 08:00 in Dwolla's region, probably Georgia or Central).

**The strategic half.** Andrei then opened the question of how Flex-IQ should work with Flex-Ports in a closed-loop system. Flex-Ports let players move between machines, which he called an excellent requirement the market wants and players like, but the e-wallet needs a physical carrier, and the options each have problems. Option one: IQ with its printer at the cashier is the only place a carrier can be created, so the player must approach the cashier for a wallet or an empty ticket — it works, though the experience is odd, and redemption is straightforward (player returns, cashier scans the carrier on IQ, IQ shows the amount, a packaged card is activated). Option two, a hybrid: a slim Flex printer kiosk on the floor that only prints empty wallet carriers, with IQ reduced to redemption — more autonomous, and over time players form the habit of printing a carrier before playing. Pavel's alternative of handing out a stack of pre-printed tickets was rejected by Andrei on three grounds: they get lost, they get soaked, crumpled and thrown away (a dirty experience), and most importantly nobody owns printing and restocking them — the location cannot be relied on, and if tickets run out the problem lands on JVL. Pavel then raised a QR code passed to the player's phone, which Andrei liked in principle — once a smartphone enters the equation it opens up a JVL web page with a player cabinet, topping up credits and so on — but flagged a hypothesis that players in Georgia specifically are not well disposed to using their own phone for play.

Val cut in to make sure everyone left with the same impression: the team agrees the smartphone is a good solution, but nobody knows today whether players will accept it, and JVL has to start moving somewhere. The one thing the team can currently grasp with its own hands, right or wrong, is printing pellets/tickets. Where it goes after that is not yet clear. He mentioned an idea of giving each Flex-Port its own emitted identifier as a possible future direction, but not a solution for today.

Val then set out the business constraints, explicitly refusing to propose engineering solutions and asking to discuss only use cases and business pillars. He expects JVL will have to give this hardware away free or subsidise it with some kind of rebate, so unit cost must be minimised — it is most likely coming out of JVL's own pocket, whether JVL gives it to the operator or the operator gives it to the location. He agreed Andrei's proposal of a slim complex with a printer and card dispenser is a good solution. He added a hard requirement: JVL must have remote access at the location so that if a location stops paying, JVL can disconnect the system and freeze its operation. And he described the target topology — the host is the centrepiece, with a variety of local devices attaching over some parallel network: today's wide Flex-Port with extended capabilities, a narrowed budget port, a bare ~$9 SMIB inside a machine, a Flex-IQ (or a couple on large locations), and Flex-Pay in a slim product carrying a validator, card dispenser, reader and a screen for player interaction — plus reporting dashboards and accessibility from mobile devices. The number and mix of attached devices must be flexible, and the range of device types broad. In time IQ may grow into a mobile device, fully replacing it; if players adopt digital wallets, screens can drop away toward SMIBs, perhaps on the SAS port, with the machine itself communicating through an API.

Andrei added a further business pillar Val endorsed immediately: support for multiple card providers, not just InComm, since JVL needs to work with everyone in the market. Dzmitry asked whether the wallet should be a cloud solution tied to a location, and whether it survives a hardware swap. Andrei, following Val's instruction not to comment on engineering, still confirmed: replacing hardware at a location must not affect wallets already created and still active there. On whether a wallet exists only at the location where it was created — currently yes, and he does not propose changing it, since each location wants to keep its own player; Val said he is not sure that is the design JVL wants, and Pavel suggested a later server-side option marking locations as "friendly" so they can share wallets. Andrei then realised aloud that wallets are still stored locally with no cloud at all; Pavel argued cloud is necessary because it is what later makes phones possible, and Andrei agreed it is not even up for discussion — it must be so.

Two engineering constraints closed the discussion. Pavel asked whether Flex-Port needs a screen and a touchscreen. The team is going with the SAS variant as universal rather than chasing the ~10% of the market without a second SAS, and Flex-Port (or the Flex SMIB) gets the ability to connect a scanner. Andrei had prepared a matrix of three port variants by presence or absence of a screen; he believes needs could technically be met even with no screen using LED communication and pre-printed instructions, but called that emotionally a step backwards, and a simple non-touch screen would close all their tasks — unless a PIN is required, which forces a touchscreen or a physical keypad. Pavel noted a small 4-inch touchscreen is probably cheaper than a button panel. On PIN generally: Val would keep it as an option, not kill it, switchable per location as operators want; Andrei clarified that a wallet created on IQ by a cashier cannot have a PIN, so that path is PIN-less. Andrei also said the second screen should die — in real locations with protective screens and armoured glass it is barely usable — and there is no regulation requiring it now that a card can be issued without the player's consent.

The last constraint was memory. Pavel warned that the cheap SAS SMIB board cannot support remote firmware update: it has about 2 MB of flash and uses built-in RAM, so it is a flash-once part, whereas the full Flex-Port has external flash and SRAM chips and can be reflashed. Andrei asked how much remote firmware update would raise the cost; Pavel said significantly — flash memory is a substantial share of the board cost, and Genadi and Evgeny Alekseev are currently working on swapping one chip for another with four times less memory to save money. Fallbacks discussed: send someone with a laptop to reflash, or simply drive out and swap the ports if they are easy to install. Val redirected the question productively — instead of guessing how often firmware updates will be needed, work out what specifically could need updating and the frequency follows. Testing the examples: the scanner driver will never change; SAS might, but Dzmitry thought it more likely JVL would implement missing commands than that SAS itself changes, and any new statistics, counters, equipment or capability would need an update. Val's read was that going from 2 MB to 4 MB is insignificant money. Andrei asked at what memory size remote update becomes possible and whether that can be a requirement; Pavel referred the question to Genadi Peari and Evgeny Alekseev. Val summarised that JVL will have two ports — today's wide Flex-Port and a slim one — and Andrei offered the cleaner line: the boundary is Flex-Port touch versus Flex-Port non-touch.

**Priorities and close.** Asked directly by Andrei where to point effort, Val named IQ, ports, Flex-Pay, and immediately the Flex recycler — pointing at Texas, where the market can be entered today and there is fertile ground to replace incumbents and place JVL recyclers with JVL ports. He said Andrei should fly to Texas straight after his vacation, and half-joked that it should be instead of the vacation. Andrei's practical objection: he has nothing in hand to take there. Pavel said JVL needs a clear view now of which scheme it goes in with — wallet or TITO. Val said today it is wallets; ideally TITO would need a bill acceptor and printer on every machine, and while operators dislike printers on every machine they also will not remove validators — which Val personally believes they will eventually do, but not in the next year or two. Because the validator stays and the machine can take money, the need is for a Flex-Pay slim. Pavel drew the conclusion that if the wallet is the main direction, Flex-Port must carry a scanner, so either the SAS SMIB board is insufficient or a new revision with scanner support is needed; Val agreed.

Andrei asked to schedule the next call for Friday; Val moved it to Monday. Told Monday is a public holiday in Belarus, Val replied that JVL has no days off and no vacations, because money does not rest. Andrei asked Val to have the transcript posted into the chat and to set the meeting for Monday morning at the same time; Genadi is to be included. Val then checked back that the three items Andrei raised at the start would be done that week, and Pavel restated them: re-run the API request so Andrei can verify ACH the next morning; finish the software update by end of week and start testing it next week; and the IQ update itself — barcode activation, ticket receipt printing, and the flow/screen cosmetics. Dzmitry confirmed all three are doable code-wise by end of week with the hardware being the only sticking point, and since Monday is a holiday in Belarus, Andrei will test first thing Tuesday. Andrei also set a plan B: if the boards do not arrive or are not right, the unit can go out on the current board, accepting the limitation that if IQ dies it blocks MudaGoose — and if receipts must print, the old cable variant that breaks the printer connection has to be used.

Val closed with a forward note: a company in the industry that produces accounting reports for the whole sector — he named Island Park — wants JVL's API so it can offer those reports to its clients. He thinks JVL should give them an API, floated sending a batch file (then caught himself proposing an engineering solution), and Pavel suggested access should be scoped to transactions the individual customer has opened up. Val has the contact and will pass it to Andrei to work with, describing it as something he was throwing out for the future.

## Decisions Made

- **InComm card activation moves to barcode for Flex-IQ.** Cards ship sealed and only the barcode is exposed; kiosks are unaffected and keep their current activation. (Andrei Klimovich)
- **Reports and the operations dashboard drop the 16-digit card number** and use the barcode identifier instead, displaying the last four digits of the number held under the barcode. Andrei confirmed the card and the envelope carry the same barcode, so records can still be tied to a card for incident handling. (Andrei, accepted by Pavel and Irina)
- **The barcode scanner moves to the front panel**, rotated and aimed downward; Genadi's newly ordered boards arriving next week enable this. (Val, Andrei, Genadi)
- **Consent becomes implicit and a printed receipt becomes mandatory.** The player accepts nothing on screen — activating the card is acceptance — but must be handed a receipt showing the amount loaded and the fee taken. (Andrei)
- **Harness rolls back to version 1**, and the new boards carry a switcher passing the printer signal through Flex-IQ when active and disconnecting when not, so MudaGoose can still print bypassing JVL. (Andrei with Genadi)
- **User flow is simplified — no player confirmations and no second-screen interaction**, plus wording fixes for terms better not used in Georgia. Andrei's screen naming is published to Confluence as the shared vocabulary. (Andrei)
- **The second screen should be retired** — barely usable behind protective/armoured glass, and no longer required now cards issue without player consent. (Andrei, Val noting remote control of it remains possible)
- **PIN stays as a switchable option per location, not a killed feature**; a wallet created on IQ by a cashier is PIN-less by definition. (Val, clarified by Andrei and Pavel)
- **Flex-Port goes with the universal SAS variant** rather than chasing the ~10% of machines without a second SAS, and gains the ability to connect a scanner. (Pavel, agreed by Andrei; Val agreed a new board revision may be needed)
- **E-wallets must move to the cloud.** Currently stored locally; Pavel argued cloud is what later makes phone-based wallets possible and Andrei agreed it is not up for discussion. (Pavel, Andrei)
- **Replacing hardware at a location must not affect wallets already created and active there.** (Andrei)
- **Flex-IQ must support multiple card providers, not only InComm.** Endorsed by Val immediately as a business pillar. (Andrei, Val)
- **JVL must retain remote access to disconnect and freeze a location's system if it stops paying** — stated by Val as a hard requirement. (Val)
- **Unit cost must be minimised** because JVL will likely give the hardware away free or subsidise it out of its own pocket. (Val)
- **Priorities named: IQ, ports, Flex-Pay, and the Flex recycler, with Texas as the immediate market.** Andrei to fly to Texas after his vacation. (Val)
- **Wallets, not TITO, are the scheme JVL goes to market with today** — TITO would need a bill acceptor and printer on every machine, which operators resist, while validators are staying for the next year or two. (Val)
- **Next meeting Monday morning, same time, with Genadi included** — Val overrode the proposed Friday and dismissed the Belarus public holiday. (Val)

## Open Questions

- What JVL takes to Texas — Pavel asked for a clear scheme before travelling; Val answered "wallets today", but Andrei's point that nothing is in hand yet was not resolved.
- Whether a wallet should stay scoped to the location that created it. Currently yes; Andrei sees no market demand to change it since locations want to keep their players, but Val said he is not sure that is the design JVL wants. Pavel proposed a later "friendly locations" server-side option.
- How much remote firmware update raises the Flex-Port cost, and the memory threshold at which it becomes possible — referred to Genadi Peari and Evgeny Alekseev.
- Whether the scanner should be angled slightly forward rather than purely downward, to allow scanning wider items such as tickets — Pavel raised it, Andrei deferred it to when the work reaches that point, questioning whether supporting third-party machines' tickets is even a business requirement.
- Whether the new boards will arrive on time and be correct; plan B is to ship on the current board, accepting that an IQ failure blocks MudaGoose.
- Whether the scanner replaces the swipe entirely — Dzmitry asked; Andrei said he does not know if swipe is removed, only that he needs the scanner in the current vision.
- Long-term wiring for a MudaGoose-free setup: existing RS-485 is daisy-chained while JVL's design assumes a star through a central router. Val parked the discussion.
