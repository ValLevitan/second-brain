## Summary

The meeting worked through the Georgia production ramp for Flex, Echo and countertop machines through December 31, covering parts shortages, lead times, and assembly capacity.

Forecast and shortages. The working forecast is about 200 Flex bases through year end. Mikhail sized shortages against 200 units (base unit 200, monitor assembly 80, the 43-inch unit 150). The main risk is metal, but Elizabeth confirmed metal on hand covers about 100 machines, which carries production through August, September and into October. The critical date is October 1; up to that point there are essentially no metal issues, and any gaps can be covered by air or ocean freight. The one clearly long-lead item is the 43-inch touchscreen: only 15 in stock, roughly one month vendor lead time, and it must be air-freighted for September builds. About 30 pieces would need to fly for September, which Mikhail accepted as manageable. Countertops appear fully covered except for some standard harnesses from Amazon.

Lead-time method. Val pushed to record supplier and component lead times in MSBC, arguing it would take about two hours and give a usable report. Mikhail resisted a single per-vendor lead time, because the same part can come from different vendors and because shipping (air roughly 10 days, ocean roughly 6 weeks) is separate from manufacturing time and varies with order size (small orders about three weeks, a full container about six weeks). They converged on modeling lead time in parts: time to get into a production slot, production time, packaging, and shipping treated separately. Val noted you can incentivize a vendor to move you up the queue but cannot shorten actual production time.

Data quality. Many parts in the working file were never ordered through BC, and vendors are not assigned to item cards. Mikhail's process is to go through the roughly 400-component list, add the missing vendors, sort parts by vendor, and verify the counts he does not trust from Elizabeth's physical recount. He expects to have a clear picture of what to order and the lead times in about two or three working days, so by roughly Tuesday, August 4.

Assembly capacity and labor. Elizabeth presented a conservative Excel model: about 9.5 hours per Echo, 14.5 hours per Flex, 2.5 hours per Flex IQ, six people at 35 hours per week giving about 882 hours of monthly capacity against about 850 hours of demand. Val and Genadi agreed she has enough capacity even on these deliberately conservative numbers. Val framed his acceptance as "belt and suspenders": he does not accept that a Flex really takes 14 hours or that a week is only 35 hours, and does not want to be held later to those figures, but they still leave surplus capacity. In practice a normal week is 44 hours and overtime and half-day Saturdays can be added.

Hiring plan. Elizabeth has approval to hire four temporary people. Val recommended starting with six rather than four, expecting to cut the weak ones and end with about three strong performers, which with the two existing people makes about five. Sebastian Cortes transfers to Georgia on October 1 as an engineer for service and product-facing work, and Val refused to put him on warehouse duty ("feeding a child with a golden spoon"). Genadi recommended one dedicated, organized person to run kitting and warehouse hygiene, bringing parts to the assembly floor and returning leftovers, with daily cleanup rather than weekly. Elizabeth also raised the need to buy basic tool kits for new hires and lockers for staff; Val believes lockers were already shipped and will check.

The next meeting is Val and Mikhail on Monday (noted as a Canadian holiday).

## Decisions Made

- **October 1 is the critical schedule date.** Metal on hand covers about 100 machines, carrying production through August, September and into October. (Val, Mikhail, Elizabeth)
- **Air-freight about 30 of the 43-inch touchscreens for September production.** Only 15 are in stock and the lead time is long. (Val, Mikhail)
- **Model lead time in parts** (production-slot time, production time, packaging, shipping separately) rather than as a single per-vendor number. (Val, Mikhail, Genadi)
- **Start with six assembly hires, not four**, and reduce to about three strong performers over time. (Val)
- **Add one dedicated warehouse/kitting person** for parts staging and daily floor hygiene. (Genadi, Val)
- **Sebastian Cortes moves to Georgia October 1** in a service and product role, not warehouse. (Val)
- **Capacity numbers accepted on a "belt and suspenders" basis** — treated as conservative, not endorsed as accurate. (Val)
