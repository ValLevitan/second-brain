## Summary

Val's "single loaded question": what works and what doesn't in Georgia COAM. Rich Winley — who ran one of the largest Georgia routes, sat on the GAMOA board, and built Banilla's Skyriser series' math off his own route's Interlock reports — answered for an hour. This is the foundational GA-COAM relationship-market briefing; Val closed saying he now understands the landscape and politics but still needs the game itself.

**Manufacturer pecking order.** Aristocrat #1 (reportedly $300–400/day), then Light & Wonder, then IGT — but those games cost $20–24K and only belong in locations with 6+ machines doing $20–50K volume ("they break your monitor and I pay $2,000"). Banilla wins the middle: adjustable payout (low/medium/high, custom builds down to ~70% RTP — "an extremely low-paced game"), cheap buys every six months as their math "drops off like a rock" after 6–12 months. Bad Dog sells volume as low-cost (~$9–10K) — Rich thinks JVL does NOT need to price that low. Konami is struggling — big machine, wrong people. Cardinal left Georgia entirely; JVL is "doing better than Cardinal."

**How Banilla really sells: financing.** Self-financed terms (90 days to 2 years), flexible even for unlicensed Indian masters they know are "tied to it." Rich himself bought 200–300 Banilla games that way, and shopped IGT's 6-month/3% facility against them. During COVID Banilla bought every cabinet available and was the only manufacturer with stock for ~18 months — call it ~15,000 games sold, many resold by Georgia masters into Virginia and every other grey/skill market ("every street market you know of generally is a Georgia operator somewhere behind it"). The dark side: lawsuits chasing the paper. Rich's advice: don't become a "finance dummy," but understand that is the game. Michael's parallel: Konami bought into Class 3 with 0% and free months.

**Market mechanics.** ~78 masters after consolidation; only 20–30 are sophisticated, and those represent the 80/20 of the money. The rest are "Georgia Lazy" — five/six-year contracts, machines down nine months, no swapping. It is a replacement market, not new locations: master-to-master swaps driven by historical reports. Location owners hold power ("take a picture of a game down the street: I want this one or I cut the machines off"). Player forums police minimum bets. Sophisticated operators mix RTP by location (92 vs 88), machine-by-machine, and pull anything that cannot beat the Georgia average (~$132/day in Rich's time).

**The relationship market.** Georgia "doesn't do well with outsiders" — JVL is outside looking in; every successful manufacturer got in through a person (Banilla via Mike Mackey, IGT via Rob Bettson). The masters' real forum is the Indian WhatsApp groups, cricket tickets, the party after the GAMOA party at 2–3 AM, war stories, gossip — "almost like high school." Key dynamics: Bunty won't give time — he routes you to Ashish, who checks with Bunty's dad; "it's a rare case where an Indian buys from an Indian — they don't trust each other" (Lucky Bugs famously started with money stolen from an uncle). Juan's conclusion: JVL may literally need to hire someone with Indian-community clout (Upshot — who had a virtual processing company and knows everyone, sits equal to Adam in the Aristocrat/United structure — as a model). The generational white-guy techs (Tim, Sean, Kyle) are the ones who truly understand RTP, features, cabinet security, serviceability — they give the real product feedback; a technician demo day reaches them.

**Names to work (Rich's read).** Sean Fellows (Diamond) — Rich's first call, "he'll give it to me raw," growing, relationship-driven; ARC and Lucky Coin — open, flexible; Nick Damani — #3–4 in the market, worth it; Tim Jennings, Mark Brooks — not growing; Bunty — wait until the story is right. Rich committed to call Sean the next day and to pull Bad Dog's real numbers via a contact.

**Product guidance extracted.**
- Two RTP variants, not one: aggressive/high for sophisticated masters (get players winning and returning), and Juan confirmed masters would celebrate an ~89–90 option; a very low (70s) variant serves only small sub-50-location masters.
- Bet buttons: 25¢ / 50¢ / $1 / $2 / $5, max $5 — Rich would keep $1+ prominent (small-bet cash-out abuse), JVL already has this spread (Juan: currently seven denominations, small buttons far left). Rich's caution: "too many options is challenging."
- Regional taste is real: Primero only sells at the Georgia border, Pot of Gold still runs in Cherokee — manufacturers should tell operators which games perform in which region ("games that perform extremely well in North Georgia") for transparency.
- Michael's "sickly sweet math" thesis (from his WMS/Bally days): flashy math spikes then collapses in six months — a destructive replacement-chasing cycle that cost WMS share; Banilla shows the same signature. Non-repeatable experiences (Super Bowl ad, third Krispy Kreme donut) don't hold.
- Interlock's gap: it reports per-cabinet, not which of the multi-pack games earns — reading machines on site is how Rich learned which game in the pack sucked.
- Trade-in idea (Val asked): Rich's version — take the competitor's machine out, "I just want the fee," under a contract with reporting access; do it selectively (Sean would sign).
- The JVL rumor to chase: "I heard the JVL math falls off" — secondhand, Rich has never run JVL, but it needs a factual answer (where in the session, which game, what settings).

**Positioning insight.** Rich and Michael converged: Aristocrat/IGT abuse customers (forced systems, "add a zero"); a narrative of self-made independent operators aligning with Val's philosophy — listening, adapting the game to feedback, private dinners, showing up at their sites — is itself a differentiator. IGT once flew its whole development team in just to have dinner with Rich: "that meant a lot to me."

**Close.** Val: relationships understood, now the game itself — features and settings; "we're going to get there" on the 13th (Rich's in-person visit, arriving alone). Val to ask Felipe next day which of the named operators JVL has already sold to; Rich to research and revert. Juan deferred his own debrief with Val to the next afternoon (after the 1 PM GMI call) and wants Felipe's info on Diamond's setups (their website shows only D27/D3).
