## Summary

**Three ways for operators to get the machine.** Val listed them:
- Buy for cash.
- Buy with financing, about 3 years (roughly $15,000 divided by 36 months).
- Rent. Val said rent is the most attractive path and the one to embrace.

**Larry's math.** The machine now costs about $4,000 (not $14,000 or $15,000). Below 3 years, a rental does not get the cost back unless the monthly price is very high. At $150 a month, less a 10% operating fee, the net is $135. Val answered that $4,000 over 36 months is $111 a month, and the goal is to get back more than $4,000.

**One-year rental rejected.** Val asked if a one-year rental at about $450 a month (a hypothetical number) is too high. Larry: operators are not fools. They will not take a one-year term. They want to spread the cost. One year makes no sense, 3 years is borderline, and the sweet spot is 3 to 7 years, probably 5. Larry believes rentals will be "sticky" and operators will not return the equipment. Val was unsure how equipment would be returned at the end.

**Terms agreed.** No option shorter than 3 years, except a cash purchase. Val: an operator who multiplies $150 by 60 months gets $9,000, not $14,000, and 5 years is long enough. Larry said his earlier model had $150 to $180 a month for 5 to 9 year terms, and about $205 to $225 for 3 years. Val proposed $199 for 3 years (about $7,200). Larry: at a $4,000 cost, $7,200 over 3 years is good monthly recurring revenue and cash-flow positive. Both said they can live with it.

**Final plan (Val).** Rental at 3 years for $199 a month, and a 5-year rental. All costs are included: management, updates and upgrades. Buyers can get 3-year financing on $14,000 plus interest. Val's rough example: about 11% per year, which he put at $1,540, and about $362 a month.

**Payback.** Larry: on rentals the money comes back in about 2 years, then recurring revenue for 3 to 8 more years. Val: the goal is to become the top player in the market (the exact word is unclear in the transcript). If that fails, the result is still very good.

**Pilot.** Val plans to put test units on locations from November 1, aiming for 50 to 100 locations in November and December, then a larger rollout in Q1 2027. Larry asked what happens to the test units. Val said the money for 100 units is ready and he can fund it himself or with Larry. Larry: let's do it together. Val will tell Larry when it starts. In the meantime they can process the documents. If it goes nowhere, the money is not needed. If it grows slowly, 50 a month is also good.

## Decisions Made

- **No rental or financing term shorter than 3 years. Under 3 years, cash purchase only.** (Proposed by Larry; Val supports)
- **Rental: 3 years at $199 a month, plus a 5-year option. All costs included (management, updates, upgrades).** (Val Levitan; Larry agreed he can live with it)
- **Purchase: cash, or 3-year financing on $14,000 plus interest.** (Val Levitan; Larry: "That's fine")
- **Val and Larry fund the 100-unit pilot together.** (Proposed by Larry; Val: "Good")
