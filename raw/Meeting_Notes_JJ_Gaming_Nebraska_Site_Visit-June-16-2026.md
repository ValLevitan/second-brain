# Meeting Summary — J&J Gaming Nebraska Site Visit
**Date:** June 16, 2026, morning (meeting in Omaha, NE)
**Duration:** ~1 hour 24 minutes
**Attendees:** Val Levitan, Rick Rees (J&J Gaming, Nebraska Operations Manager)

## Summary

Val visited J&J Gaming's new Nebraska office in Omaha. Rick gave a facility tour — warehouse, gaming floor, cash handling vault, collection staging area. J&J is moving into a 24,000 sq ft building leased from Trestle (formerly Midwest Amusements), up from 1,300 sq ft. About 70 employees on the Nebraska side.

**J&J Gaming profile.** Owned by the Jansen family (Kay Jansen, Jay Jansen) out of Illinois. Started 100+ years ago in vending, pivoted to gaming when Illinois legalized in 2009. First machines set in 2012. Purchased Midwest Amusements/Trestle's Nebraska route. Also purchased Epic, a game development company, for vertical integration. They run their own 24/7 surveillance camera systems at all locations.

**Nebraska operations.** ~500 locations in Nebraska, ~100 in Iowa, ~600 total. Main suppliers: Banila (most units, ~$10K each), Light & Wonder (top NTI performer, $15-18K), Bad Dog ($10-11K, #2-3 in market), Apex (brand new, 7 locations in last 2 weeks), Premiro. Nebraska just opened a 5th machine per location. Average net revenue per unit across all J&J Nebraska locations: $77/day. Top truck stop locations do $25-30K/month. Location split currently 60/40 (location/operator); J&J expects to push toward 50/50 in 5-10 years through regulatory lobbying. State tax went from 5% to 10% effective July 17, expected to increase ~5% annually.

**Cash operations.** 14-person collection team, 7 two-man routes per day, covering ~70 locations daily. Teams bring pre-strapped cash from J&J's own vault, fill terminals, collect machine proceeds, return cash for counting and recycling. No armed security. One vehicle break-in incident since Rick arrived.

**Back-office systems.** All machines connected via Easy Link system (dual SaaS required in Nebraska). Terminals run Express RT software by Clint Lerman at $35/day per site. SMIB (Slot Machine Interface Board) modules in each machine connect to terminals and central server. James Jansen in Illinois handles all analytics and purchasing decisions; Jeff Harwick oversees technical.

**JVL machines at J&J.** Five locations currently. While reviewing Rick's analytics reports, Val acknowledged the first three months of machines were unstable and that May was the first stable month. Val noted JVL machines show the highest or second-highest average bet across the locations reviewed, but total cash-in is lagging — attributing this to incorrect game selection. Val told Rick a game swap is coming in the next software release, targeting September/October, and expects results to improve from there. Val mentioned two additional Nebraska customers are being developed but JVL is holding off until performance climbs.

**Val's pitch.** Presented JVL's cash recycling kiosk concept: player inserts cash at a kiosk, receives ticket, plays machines, returns to kiosk to redeem winnings via QR code. Kiosk automatically sorts and recycles bills across denomination cassettes, eliminating manual cash collection, sorting, and refilling. Only overflow cassettes need collecting. Rick was impressed and agreed it could eliminate significant labor cost. Val stated JVL's aspiration to capture 100% of J&J's new transactional terminal business and 50% of their new gaming machine purchases. Rick acknowledged the transactional side but said the 50% machine share would be the difficult part.

**Remote game rotation.** Val raised the ability to rotate games remotely via cell modem. The concept: a machine's approved software pack contains a full library (e.g. 20 games) but only a subset (e.g. 6) is enabled at any time. Since the entire library is already licensed and installed on the machine, enabling or disabling games within the pre-approved pack would not trigger a DOR re-approval — eliminating the need for site visits to swap games. Rick initially raised that Nebraska requires DOR notification and a new sticker for game changes, but that concern applied to installing new unapproved content, not to toggling games within an already-approved pack.

**Other markets discussed.** Iowa (~100 J&J locations, weird market with in-store purchase redemption model, Bank Shot machines only in C-stores). Texas — Val mentioned an appellate court declared skill machines legal in the last 60 days. Wyoming and Montana — JVL planning expansion from Nebraska base. J&J also has a small presence in Alabama (<100 machines).

**Dennis.** JVL's support contact (French name) has been responsive and helpful to J&J's technical team. He and another JVL person ("One," former VP at Seminoles for 18 years) came out for initial installation.

Rick agreed to introduce Val to James Jansen and Jeff Harwick. Meeting ended with Val inviting Rick to a steakhouse lunch.

## Decisions Made

- **Rick will introduce Val to James Jansen (analytics/purchasing) and Jeff Harwick (technical)** — to present the cash recycling system and discuss expanding the gaming machine relationship. Rick committed to reaching out that afternoon.
- **JVL will adjust game selection on Nebraska machines** — current games underperforming on cash-in despite strong average bet. New games targeted for September/October release.
