**JIRA backlog-grooming policy (recorded here, procedural resolution).** Val opened a debate about cutting old JIRA backlog tasks (his view: a backlog stops being useful after ~30 days and becomes "a notepad"). Michael and Lena argued the backlog holds fully-documented game structure/knowledge (useful when a shelved game is revived, and for onboarding new employees). **Resolution:** anything sitting **older than six months** can be deleted; anything **between three and six months** requires Lena's consent — such items get identified, labelled, and brought into her grooming cycle. Graphics/assets are **never** deleted (Omni graphics live in GitHub/Azure, each designer keeps their own storage); only the JIRA *tasks* are cleared. Val took an undertaking to **consult IT** on whether a JIRA "archive" state preserves the information attached to each task, and to have IT groom JIRA accordingly. [[raw/JIRA_schedule_review_Game_Development-March-20-2026.md]]

**Calle Ocho on Omni — market it, don't scrap it.** The team confirmed Kayocho (Calle Ocho) for Omni had served as the test bed to certify the Omni framework, estimate the FP7 processor's graphics performance, iron out Omni framework/data-center bugs, and establish the (materially different) Omni workflow. Juan pushed to **market the game** as part of the Fiery Ice Link package on Omni (it is JVL's only Omni-ready game, also needed for GLI load/functionality testing). Val also took an undertaking to fix the release-loading architecture — Lena currently downloads a review build from GitHub and runs a single file in a browser; Val will speak to the developers about a proper server-to-workstation push and about adding **dual/multi-screen "show mode"** support to the Omni build (Rage already has it; [[wiki/people/efim-podvoiski.md|Efim]] framed the need as flexible mapping of virtual screens to actual windows). Florida: a fresh Calle Ocho build was reviewed; Sasha Biratka has back-end bugs to close. [[raw/JIRA_schedule_review_Game_Development-March-20-2026.md]]
