# NTS Nebraska business-architecture walkthrough + bill-hopper pitch (early June 2025, date inferred)

A discovery call between [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan Martinez]] and **[[wiki/organizations/nts-nebraska-technical-services.md|NTS]]** owners [[wiki/people/ryan-cruz.md|Ryan Cruz]] and his sister **Sarah**, with [[wiki/people/denis-desmarais.md|Denis Desmarais]] on the line. This is the **on-site, filmed cash-in/ticket/cash-out walkthrough** that the [[wiki/meetings/2025-05-29-denis-nts-nebraska.md|May-29 Denis/NTS call]] planned NTS would host for JVL.

**Date inferred (undated transcript):** the audio file `nebraska.m4a` transcribed 2025-06-11; content places the meeting *after* the [[wiki/meetings/2025-06-04-eclipse-nebraska-testing-requirements.md|June-4 Eclipse call]] (which is referenced as already held). Dated 2025-06-11 (transcription stamp; content only fixes "after June-4"). QUEUE (Tier B) for owner confirmation of the exact day.

Substance — how the Nebraska business works today and where JVL's equipment can help — is folded into [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md]] and the [[wiki/organizations/nts-nebraska-technical-services.md|NTS]]/[[wiki/people/ryan-cruz.md|Ryan Cruz]] pages. Key points: cash-only market (no cashless yet), traditional casino-style tickets redeemed at ~130–140 NTS redemption terminals; NTS buys approved skill devices as a distributor, each location licensed by Sarah; location caps (4 games up to 5,000 sq ft, then +1 per 1,000 sq ft; ≥60% non-skill revenue except non-profits); a coming **6.5% tax on the net** NTS facilitates; **Easy Links** is the TITO/SMIB supplier (Pompano-Florida-based; ~$1,600–1,800 per location server handling ~9 games, ~$450 per SMIB board); cash dispensers are GBT and **CountR** (both German — "Gauselmann/Gewete" — expensive), refilled by ~2 dedicated NTS staff from ~500-bill cassettes; skill-game RTP runs ~88–92%, jackpot sweet spot ~$4–8k; bill acceptor MEI Cashflow SC66 + Transact Epic 950 printer are the market spares. **Val's bill-hopper pitch** (the core purpose): a recycling hopper where player-inserted bills refill the payout float (vs one-time-fill cassettes), from Val's 1990s cash-processing company (competitor to JCM/Mars Electronics, later sold to Crane) — NTS "would love to see it." Ryan also states his and Sarah's **father founded American Amusements** and developed the **original Bank Shot** game that went to the Nebraska Supreme Court (see [[wiki/meetings/2025-06-17-nebraska-lawsuit-progressives-102cd.md|the American Amusements lawsuit]]).
