# Texas used-machine distribution (via EDS)

JVL's plan (laid out by [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] on the ≈Mar 22 2025 call with [[wiki/people/julian-puerta.md|Julian Puerta]], [[wiki/meetings/2025-03-22-julian-puerta-texas-eds-distribution.md]]) to sell the **~400 machines freed by closing the Peru and Mexico offices** into the **Texas** game-room market through a distributor, shielding JVL "from a legislative perspective" while the market remains poorly regulated. [[raw/meeting_with_julian_puerta_texas_eds.md]]

## The market (Julian's research, as reported)

- Texas has an association providing legal support to operators (transcribed "**TGROA**"; charges ~**$50 per machine**); **ELSA** is "a small town in south Texas … pursuing a regulatory frame" with state-style taxing that other towns are replicating. [[raw/meeting_with_julian_puerta_texas_eds.md]]
- Product is **class-III-casino-style** (unlike Pennsylvania skill games); machines must be "class three gaming machines"; used machines run **$3,400 up to ~$10,000**; participation deals run **20–25%**. The market is "very vibrant … very volatile" given the unregulated environment. [[raw/meeting_with_julian_puerta_texas_eds.md]]
- Julian's source: a close Colombian-American friend, the distributor **Diana Safra** (Miami-based; ex-Granby Products / Justin Silverstein), who sells and also risks her own machines on revenue share in Texas; Julian learned "400 terminals can be moved within a year in Texas." A large Las Vegas corporation (Julian: "I don't remember if it was SANS") has set up in Texas to push regulation. [[raw/meeting_with_julian_puerta_texas_eds.md]]
- Val's read: authorities in select areas are tolerating the business, regulation is coming, so "it's probably a good time to establish our brand" — and when a market becomes regulated, margins fall and the distributor layer disappears, so the distributor structure is inherently temporary. [[raw/meeting_with_julian_puerta_texas_eds.md]]

## The deal structure Val dictated

- **[[wiki/organizations/eds-colombia.md|EDS]]** (the Colombian company, [[wiki/people/eliana.md|Eliana]]) symbolically **purchases the machines from JVL and becomes importer of record** into the US (likely needing an "EDS USA" entity, e.g. registered in Florida, with a US bank account). [[raw/meeting_with_julian_puerta_texas_eds.md]]
- JVL sends **~two technicians** (possibly redeployed from the closing Mexico operation) plus components to refurbish the machines after arrival; refurbishment costs are billed through. [[raw/meeting_with_julian_puerta_texas_eds.md]]
- On sales, **EDS keeps ~20% off the top; ~80% flows to JVL** as payment against invoices/receivables for parts and services (illustration: 400 machines × $5,000 = $2M/yr → ~$400K gross to EDS; JVL receivables ~$1.4M). EDS's share is **split 50-50 between Julian and Eliana**. JVL deliberately avoids a partnership form ("we want to supply components") and gains a legal platform to draw money out — including routing fees such as "tell EDS Colombia to pay JVL Belarus a development fee." [[raw/meeting_with_julian_puerta_texas_eds.md]]
- **Julian relocates to the US as a JVL employee** (salary structure discussed; figures held §15.8), acquiring market relationships on JVL's behalf and passing them to EDS; he may keep consulting for EDS from Colombian jurisdiction. If Eliana declines, **[[wiki/people/marta.md|Marta]]'s Automated Technologies** is the ready fallback distributor. [[raw/meeting_with_julian_puerta_texas_eds.md]]
- Val's guardrail: stay within "established, taxable, accepted business practices" that IGT/Konami/Bally-style companies also tolerate (Vegas casinos dumping floors to distributors whose machines end up in Texas), and **avoid internet/remote-content schemes** — "our online business is in concert with … regulations," including an agreement Val said JVL is "about to sign … with IGT and Aristocrat where they will shield us with their licensing" for online content. [[raw/meeting_with_julian_puerta_texas_eds.md]]
- Strategic frame: Texas is a **secondary market to extend the end-of-life** of JVL's current product; Pennsylvania (skill games) would get "proper skill product" later, also run personally by Julian — "if you're not running, I am not interested." [[raw/meeting_with_julian_puerta_texas_eds.md]]

## Sept 23 2025 — market/regulatory intel from Jay (Juan call)

On a [[wiki/meetings/2025-09-23-texas-skill-game-strategy-jay-call.md|Sept-23 call]], [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]] briefed an outside Texas consultant / former Pace-affiliated operator ("Jay") on JVL's product and got a candid market read. This is the **Nebraska-skill machine adapted for Texas** (same thread as the Sept-18 rollout above), not the EDS used-machine plan. [[raw/Texas_skill_game_strategy-september-23-2025.md]]

- **Product-fit:** the Georgia-flavor skill (single nudge) would **not** qualify for Texas, but the **Nebraska two-skill model would** — a primary skill (a 50-100% power-meter on wins, similar to competitor "Banela"/Vanilla) plus a **Simon-Says memory secondary skill** on non-winning outcomes paying up to **104% of the bet** (deliberately made "tough and slow" so most players skip a 1¢-on-25¢ win). Jay: it "checks off a lot of the boxes." [[raw/Texas_skill_game_strategy-september-23-2025.md]]
- **Machine lockdown / hold:** settings are locked per-machine via a JVL-supplied setup file (USB, done physically at the machine, never remote); operators can only enter TITO/SAS-ID data, not alter game, hold %, bet structure, or progressive contribution. Base game hold **88-92%**; Texas market expects a **minimum ~80%** hold (holds computed as if Simon-Says is out of the picture). [[raw/Texas_skill_game_strategy-september-23-2025.md]]
- **Lab certification:** JVL will hold an **Eclipse** ("game of skill") certification letter — the same software as Nebraska, so a one-time reference letter adding ~30 days. Jay recommends explicitly naming Texas in the letter as an edge over Vanilla (Pace argues rivals are "not lab-tested"). The two big labs named: **GLI** and **Eclipse** (Nebraska/Georgia prefer Eclipse). [[raw/Texas_skill_game_strategy-september-23-2025.md]]
- **Competition & economics:** incumbents ranked **Vanilla #1, Pace #2, Aurora #3** (plus "Paramount"). Pricing detail on [[wiki/topics/jvl-machine-pricing-strategy.md]] — Jay's blunt verdict is that JVL's **~$17,000 list is too high** to enter Texas vs ~$8-10k Vanilla and free/30-35%-rev-share Pace. Most operators use **Vanilla as their baseline** and connect via Vanilla's **TRT** (Vanilla sells the TRT and lets you connect anything; Pace will not let outsiders touch its TRT). JVL can talk to a TRT via SAS / hard-meter sniffing; JVL's own ticket-redemption transactional components won't be ready until **~January**. [[raw/Texas_skill_game_strategy-september-23-2025.md]]
- **Regulatory climate:** Texas is a gray market with no clear statute; enforcement often charges locations with something other than gambling. A **2025 legislative attempt to ban skill games died in committee**; the next two-year window is **2027**. Florida is tougher (a new gaming commission, seizures). [[raw/Texas_skill_game_strategy-september-23-2025.md]]
- **Trade shows:** Jay steers JVL to **Las Vegas in March** (and a Texas nightclub-and-bar show) as the real exposure venues rather than IAAPA. **Next step:** JVL software ready in ~10 days; Juan to send cabinet specs / screenshots / a video, then price down and trial a machine on Jay's TRT. [[raw/Texas_skill_game_strategy-september-23-2025.md]]
