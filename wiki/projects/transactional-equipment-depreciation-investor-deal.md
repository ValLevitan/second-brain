# Investor-funded Georgia transactional equipment — the depreciation deal

**What it is.** A way to put JVL's Georgia transactional equipment (point of sale, self-checkout kiosk, AFT) into locations without JVL paying for it all, with outside investors who need a tax write-off. First put to two sets of people on **September 28 2026** — at lunch to [[wiki/people/larry-sharon.md|Larry]] and his two partners John and Eddie (the "railcar" partners), and in the afternoon to an unnamed tax-investment adviser [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Larry_Transactional_Equipment_Investment_Structure-September-28-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Equipment_Depreciation_Investor_Pitch-September-28-2026.md]].

**Where it stands (owner ruling 2026-10-05: still under consideration — no decision made yet).** The latest working proposal, Larry's of Sept 30 2026, 8:42 AM, after three days of reworking: **one JV entity** (a Florida LLC, to be opened by [[wiki/people/gabriel-braun.md|Gabriel Braun]], one of Larry's managers (owner ruling 2026-10-05)), **50/50**, with a **special allocation of depreciation** that can be reset each year; **JVL invoices the real $4,000 per machine** — no inflated retail invoice, no $12,000 note, JVL "book[s] and pay[s] taxes off… of exactly what you received"; a **100-machine trial** as the proof of concept, more if JVL can; and after the proof Larry takes the business to his banks (Bell Bank, Seacoast — about one third down, the note repaid inside the company) and possibly to InComm with a first right of refusal. Larry sends the structure and the 3/5/7/10-year lease tables the same day [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Larry_Sharon_Single_Entity_JV_and_100_Machine_Pilot-September-30-2026.md]]. **Oct 7 2026:** the customer side moved on — no term under three years except a cash sale, $199 a month for three years plus a five-year option, all costs included, and test units from November 1 funded by Val and Larry together (still a working proposal; see the Oct-7 section below) [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Larry_Hardware_Rental_Pricing_and_November_Pilot-October-7-2026.md]]. Decision pages: [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-30-one-jv-entity-a-florida-llc-50-50-with-a-special-allocation-of-depreciation-and-jvl-invoices-the-real-4000.md]] and [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-30-a-100-machine-trial-first-then-bell-bank-seacoast-and-possibly-incomm-finance-the-rest.md]]. The Sept-28 model below — investors buy at retail, take depreciation on the full retail price, put in one quarter in cash — is the **starting point, set aside in the Sept-30 proposal on Sept 30**; it is kept here because the later calls only make sense against it.

**Where it sits.** This is a separate thread from JVL's own capital call ([[wiki/topics/jvl-2026-funding-crisis-capital-call.md]]) — it funds equipment, not JVL's balance sheet — and from the per-machine giveaway offers to Georgia operators ([[wiki/decisions/2026-09-02-free-ports-and-pos-with-every-machine-bought.md]]). The market and product facts Val recited at both meetings live on [[wiki/projects/georgia-market-rollout.md]] (Sept 28), [[wiki/topics/georgia-prize-card-processor-economics.md]] (Sept 28) and [[wiki/topics/flexpay-business-architecture-payout-devices.md]] (Sept 28, 12:50 PM).

## The business model Val put on the table (Sept 28)

- **Two prices, one choice.** "The business model is we either sell the transactional equipment and collect ninety dollars per month, or we give away the transactional equipment for free and collect one hundred fifty dollars a month. That is, in essence, that's what's on the table today" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Larry_Transactional_Equipment_Investment_Structure-September-28-2026.md]]. To the adviser he gave only the free option: retail $16,000, "we go to them and give that equipment to them, and we charge them one hundred fifty dollars per month" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Equipment_Depreciation_Investor_Pitch-September-28-2026.md]].
- **Cost and retail.** "The combination of all these devices, my cost is roughly four thousand dollars. We retail it at approximately fifteen thousand dollars" (lunch); Larry used "sixteen thousand dollars, which is what your retail report shows"; the afternoon pitch used $16,000 retail and "a spread of one to four" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Larry_Transactional_Equipment_Investment_Structure-September-28-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Equipment_Depreciation_Investor_Pitch-September-28-2026.md]].
- **Who pays the fee.** The master distributor, not the location: "So we don't have to worry about collecting from a 7-Eleven store" (a partner); Val: "Master distributor" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Larry_Transactional_Equipment_Investment_Structure-September-28-2026.md]]. To the adviser: the equipment goes "not to the locations, but to the master operators. Those are the same people that I sell my slot machines to" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Equipment_Depreciation_Investor_Pitch-September-28-2026.md]].
- **Term and service.** Val wants a five-year contract ("I would like to get five years") with advance replacement of everything, costing "approximately two percent replacement cost" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Larry_Transactional_Equipment_Investment_Structure-September-28-2026.md]]. To the adviser: "all future service for the duration of the contract is on me, advance replacement" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Equipment_Depreciation_Investor_Pitch-September-28-2026.md]].
- **The whole-market arithmetic, as a ratio not a forecast.** 7,000 locations × $4,000 = $28 million of equipment; $150 × 7,000 ≈ "a million fifty" a month, "roughly twelve million" a year; "let's call it two million dollars" of expense; "free cash of ten million dollars per year"; at a multiplier of ten "roughly a hundred million dollar business". The same numbers to the adviser, plus the write-off: buy-in $28 million, "the write off, depreciation, will be approximately one hundred twenty million" (the adviser: "one hundred twelve to one hundred twenty") [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Equipment_Depreciation_Investor_Pitch-September-28-2026.md]].
- **The realistic target.** "I don't see why we cannot get twenty percent of the market… Twenty-five hundred locations. So roughly, let's call it two thousand locations. Two thousand locations times four is eight million dollars" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Larry_Transactional_Equipment_Investment_Structure-September-28-2026.md]]. To the adviser, the open field: "approximately five thousand five hundred locations, which is seventy-five percent of the market, that is not filled"; filling them is a "hard cost of approximately twenty million dollars" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Equipment_Depreciation_Investor_Pitch-September-28-2026.md]].
- **A marketing shift Val voiced.** Today "I sell one machine with equipment for free", but the operator still has to pay $16,000 for the machine. "A better marketing strategy would be: Hey, buy machine. Don't buy machine. It's up to you. The equipment is for free" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Larry_Transactional_Equipment_Investment_Structure-September-28-2026.md]]. (Recorded as a Sept-28 update on [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-02-free-ports-and-pos-with-every-machine-bought.md]].)
- **With or without them.** "I'm getting ready to do it with or without you" (lunch); "I am moving with or without you. With or without them… You're not the only person I'm speaking to" (afternoon). If the partners pass, Val will do it "at a slower pace, or I'm gonna do it through some other" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Larry_Transactional_Equipment_Investment_Structure-September-28-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Equipment_Depreciation_Investor_Pitch-September-28-2026.md]].

## Larry's structure (lunch, Sept 28)

Larry had already walked the idea through with Val and given John and Eddie "the gist". His kick-off: "Currently, we have one avenue through which we capture depreciation that we desperately need. If your machine costs four thousand dollars, and you're able to invoice us sixteen thousand dollars… that provides us an additional twenty-five percent of depreciation that we're not seeing now. In order to make it an arm's length transaction, we would then have to take a certain portion of that one hundred fifty dollars… to repay the so-called twelve thousand dollar loan per unit" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Larry_Transactional_Equipment_Investment_Structure-September-28-2026.md]].

The structure he laid out, in his words and Val's [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Larry_Transactional_Equipment_Investment_Structure-September-28-2026.md]]:

- **Entity A buys the equipment.** "Val, as the manufacturer, would sell to an entity, entity A, that the three of us control." Val: "I can register JVL Distributing in Georgia… where my company and your company will be fifty fifty partners." Larry: "We can be fifty fifty partners in entity A." Entity A pays "sixteen thousand dollars, four thousand of it we pay upfront. The other… twelve thousand, is financed by Val's Manufacturing Company" — Val: "Not not financed. I open receivable." Entity A "will create the depreciation for us".
- **Entity B runs the business.** "Entity B is our joint venture with Val… engaged in the active business of distributing these machines and managing these machines and servicing these machines to the master distributors. This entity B will collect $150 a month from said master distributors. A portion of that $150 will be allocated to service the $12,000 debt… through a separate side agreement… In essence, it's a 50/50 split after all expenses for any machines that we help [Val] finance." "There's only two entities that need to be created."
- **Why it must be active.** "The whole point of this is to capture [depreciation]. So… we have to be engaged in an active business. It can't be a passive business." His railcar comparison: "The whole reason why we're able to take [depreciation] on our railcars is because we've now started to engage in the purchase of railcars that we use ourselves… we have to be able to say that we are actively involved in the business of the management of these rail cars." *(The transcript says "appreciation" both times; "depreciation" is the plain reading.)*
- **Same cash as railcars, better terms.** "Our cash outlay is four… It's actually no different than what we're doing right now because… we're going to a bank… and we're getting the difference financed. In this case… the difference is financed or open receivables through… his manufacturer." His close: "I like this deal enough because our cash outlay is no different than what we're doing with railcars. Our depreciation is 25% higher, and the revenue stream starts happening from month one rather than from year fifteen." A partner (Speaker 3, read as John by context): "I think we'd probably be interested in doing the deal, even if there wasn't this potential in the backend."
- **The $12,000 repayment is "minutiae".** "It's a paper difference. In reality… we're going to work out a way with Val how to… recapture that money." Val: "We need to do some financial engineering." Larry: "A hundred percent. But before we sit down and start spending time on financial engineering, I just want everyone to be clear on everything… I want to give us a little bit of time to think and to discuss amongst ourselves."

**The licence.** A distributor licence is needed for Entity A ("you become a distributor… it has to be this way"). Asked how long, Val: "Thirty days? I don't want to promise"; "I'll get you a license… Distributor license is not a problem." His idea was to license "my second company" and sell it; Larry: "in some industries, licenses are not transferable… they may want to vet who the new owners are. So we might be better off coming in and starting ourselves in an application." Val: "You're right… I don't think you should be investing anything into anything until you hundred percent" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Larry_Transactional_Equipment_Investment_Structure-September-28-2026.md]]. *(Evolved the next afternoon: the licence was moved to the JV entity, then Val said one may not be needed at all — see the Sept 29, 3:22 PM section below.)*

**The banking upside is set aside by both sides.** After Val told the InComm and prepaid-card story (on [[wiki/topics/georgia-prize-card-processor-economics.md]], Sept 28), Larry: "I think we only do the deal if we agree that the backend may never happen." Val: "I would also suggest for you to think that banking deal as something that is not going to happen… I feel uber uncomfortable promising something like that" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Larry_Transactional_Equipment_Investment_Structure-September-28-2026.md]].

## The lawyer's view — Alan Litwack (Sept 29, 11:41 AM)

The next morning Val put the idea to his lawyer [[wiki/people/alan.md|Alan Litwack]] as "another question" on the same call as the capital-call letter ([[wiki/meetings/2026-09-29-litwack-capital-call-letter-and-transactional-equipment-write-off-model.md]], segment 2). - **How Val framed it.** "as part of resurrection of JVL… we got into transactional equipment", a subject he knows "from CashCode days" and "from being on board of directors for Gaming Standards Association." The Georgia law "mandates… all the credits from the machines that a player wins… to be credited to a prepaid debit card, either Mastercard or Visa" (market background on [[wiki/topics/georgia-prize-card-processor-economics.md]]). Under "the big, beautiful bill in the U.S.", slot machines "have been given one year write-off" — Val's guess for why: "because of the relationship between the Adelson and Trump" — and an active business "can make an investment into, let's say, slot machines, and take the write-off that same year… effectively pay no taxes."
- **The investor, not named.** "a friend of mine who's generating a lot of cash right now. We went for a walk, and he says… I can use a write-off." The tape never names him; the numbers match the Sept-28 lunch structure, so by context this is that proposal (**inference**; see [[wiki/people/larry-sharon.md]]).
- **The model in Val's words.** "My transactional equipment, let's say I retail it for fifteen thousand dollars, but my cost is four thousand dollars. He says, if I give you four thousand dollars, I can take a write-off for fifteen thousand dollars immediately that year." The balance: "I credit him. He still owes me… he takes the write-off immediately." With the cash "I can give away the transactional equipment relatively for free because it's being paid by somebody else." Placement: "I'm giving this equipment to an operator for free, essentially, in exchange for a five-year contract at hundred fifty dollars a month"; "there is no profit participation." - **Alan's reading.** "How can he? If he only pays you four, how can he write off fifteen?" — then "You're essentially… lending him. He's paying you over time." (He once says "fifteen million" — a slip.)
- **The investor's standing.** "Is he the operator of the casino?" Val: "No… Legally, he may have to because he needs an active business in order to write the entire amount." Val: "Correct, but he will owe me the rest of the money."
- **Whose problem the write-off is.** Val: "The tax write-off is for him, not for me. I don't care how he does his stuff… I don't want to research his issue and spend money on it." Alan: JVL must not be "accused of participating" — "nobody in the IRS can say you helped them disguise something… Just means we have to… structure it better." He will "get some of my U.S. people" to look at how the write-off works. Val: "Okay, understood."
- **Still to come.** "There is a little bit of financial engineering that needs to be done, which I need to help me out with. So stay tuned for this." Alan's close: "this sounds like fun… there's going to be a lot of this going." His Canadian comparison (Carney's write-off "the year it comes into use" for "computer things… mining") is recorded on his page.
