# Nebraska skill-gaming market entry

JVL's plan to enter the **Nebraska** skill-gaming market by adapting its existing Georgia "Peach" software. Worked through on the Mar 14 2025 call with [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]], [[wiki/people/efim-podvoiski.md|Efim]], [[wiki/people/lena.md|Lena]], [[wiki/people/pavel.md|Pavel]] and [[wiki/people/irina-makarych.md|Irina]] (Irina — Shishko or Makarych unclear from source; per 2026-07-18 roles). Closely parallels [[wiki/projects/georgia-market-rollout.md]] (same software family) and [[wiki/projects/south-dakota-vlt-market.md]]. The software decision is [[wiki/decisions/2025-03-14-base-nebraska-on-georgia-conservative-nudge.md]]. [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]

## Why Nebraska

- A **skill market not too dissimilar from Georgia, but more favorable and closer to a normal casino environment**: **no "hands" requirement**, and **TITO** (ticket-in/ticket-out) for cash-out and cash-in is allowed today. [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]
- There is **no state central system today**, but Nebraska's documents say one will be deployed **within the year**; Juan understands **Intralot** may be in the running for it — which would make what JVL already does in Georgia easier. [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]
- Competitors already present/entering: **Banela Games** (from Georgia) is in the market today, and **Light and Wonder** is starting to work on entering. [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]

## Regulatory basis and the submission path

- Nebraska is a **GLI-11 3.0** jurisdiction for overall cabinet/software functionality, but its rules **remove certain sections** of the GLI-11 spec (Juan highlighted them; whether JVL must disable or simply ignore those is not yet clear). Juan posted the initial regulations to **Confluence**. [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]
- **Regulator-first submission:** on GLI's own recommendation, JVL should send the machine to the **Nebraska Department of Revenue** (the regulator) to look at the game **before GLI** does. The process: machine → Nebraska DoR confirms it meets the skill criteria → then the lab. The likely reason is that other manufacturers were **burned** — a machine failed to qualify as "skill" after they had already spent time and money at GLI. [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]
- **Testing lab:** the regulations spell out **GLI**, but Val prefers **Eclipse** so approvals can run in parallel with Georgia; Juan is to confirm with the Nebraska DoR whether Eclipse is an acceptable lab. See [[wiki/organizations/eclipse-gaming-lab.md]] and [[wiki/organizations/gli.md]]. [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]
- JVL will explain its skill approach to the **Nebraska regulator** (not to the lab, and separate from the skill element): because the game outcome is not truly random and not exactly predetermined, it "requires additional explanation." [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]

## The software approach

- Use the **existing Georgia software as the base**, not the in-progress Georgia updates, and present the **"no hands" version**. The "hands" is part of a **common UI used in all games** and can simply be **removed**. Release in **four releases of six games each** (packs of six, like Georgia). **Peach 4** is excluded (being redesigned); the base is **Peach 1, 2 and 3**. [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]
- Irina's recommendation (Irina — Shishko or Makarych unclear from source; per 2026-07-18 roles), adopted: take the **latest version (1.14, already in VDEC)** and just **revert the nudge to its old/conservative presentation**, rather than modifying the old version — it is easier and more reliable because everything else is already in VDEC. [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]
- **Kept** from the newest Georgia work: **dual-SAS**, **TITO** support, the **VDEC button changes** (flame only the highest three bet buttons, not the first two "big bad buttons") and the **touch-area** changes. **Removed**: the **hands** dependency and the **enhanced/new nudge** feature. [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]
- **"Georgia 1" vs "Georgia 2":** Val's framing — Georgia 1 is the current **conservative skill nudge**; Georgia 2 is the **advanced skill nudge** the competitors are introducing. The advanced feature would be deployed onto Nebraska **as it rolls out for Georgia**; whether it gets accepted is "less of a risk than relying on it from the start." Nebraska launches on the conservative nudge. [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]] **(Superseded May 8 2025 — Val reversed this and adopted the enhanced/new nudge for Nebraska after the regulator confirmed it is fine; see the May-8 section below.)** [[raw/game_design-may-8-2025.md]]
- **Touch modification** (changing the touch area for the big bad buttons) is treated as a **scope gap / design flaw** that should be on **all** machines regardless of class — so it is included in Nebraska too. [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]
- **Jira:** rather than "take the latest Peach and back these things out," the team will write a **full detailed requirement** listing all the enhancements, for future **traceability**. **Dutch Mill** (an operator/location, transcribed) already has the newest version. [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]

## Progressive and bonus configuration

- Progressives are permitted only on a **per-device, per-game** basis — **standalone only, no links** — and the word **"mystery"** does not appear anywhere in Nebraska's documentation. [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]] **(Superseded Jun 17 2025 — the American Amusements lawsuit removed the progressive/jackpot control section (102D), so JVL can now run progressives shared and even linked; see the Jun-17 section below.)** [[raw/legislation_nebraska-june-17-2025.md]]
- The progressive uses the **same limit as Georgia** (stops growing at ~**3,000**, "3,000 or 8,000" as set). Because Nebraska locations are small (**minimum 4 machines, maximum 15**), JVL will use the **lower Georgia top-award numbers** — a four-machine location cannot afford a **$10,000** jackpot that would wipe out its month. [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]
- **No SWIFT bonus progressive** (it is linked by definition). But the **SWIFT** setting comes from an **RGS/RIS parameter** and can be enabled per machine independently (like DSAP), so it "lives if it's needed." [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]

## Val's principle on legal interpretation

- Val directed the team **not to research legal interpretations** of Nebraska's skill definitions — there is established precedence and a legal system to judge what is acceptable; JVL submits its games and the gaming authority judges whether they are "skill" enough. He explicitly **dismissed Efim's set of definition questions** (chance vs skill, unwinnable games) as "a swamp" not worth the time; poker was noted as **naturally a skill game**. A **market trip** should be made **ASAP** before the design is finalized (Juan to travel; a Puerto Rico trip too). [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]

## May 8 2025 — Juan's market trip findings and the nudge reversal

On the [[wiki/meetings/2025-05-08-game-design-review.md|May-8 game-design review]], after [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]] visited **8 Nebraska locations over ~4 hours**, the team acted on what he saw. The dominant competitor is **Vanilla** (detailed on [[wiki/topics/slot-game-design-principles.md]]). [[raw/game_design-may-8-2025.md]]

- **Nudge reversed to the enhanced/new nudge (evolution of the Mar-14 decision).** Because the incumbent Vanilla games already use a nudge (a green sliding-bar "touch when it's green" mechanic) and the **regulator confirmed it is not an issue**, Val decided to **use JVL's new/enhanced nudge for Nebraska too** — "so we're consistent with what we're doing in Georgia." This **reverses** the launch-on-conservative-nudge choice in [[wiki/decisions/2025-03-14-base-nebraska-on-georgia-conservative-nudge.md]]: "I'll adjust the rec where I said we wouldn't do that. We should do it." (JVL judges its sparkly-arrow presentation "more direct and communicative to the player" than Vanilla's slider.) [[raw/game_design-may-8-2025.md]]
- **Denomination / bets:** **no multi-denomination** — the games show everything in cash (no glyphs), with **~7 bet levels ($1–$5)** and "no room" for denomination. Max bet seen in-market was mostly **$3**, with **$5** only at an upscale place ("Baker's," 2 machines). JVL keeps the **$5 and $10** bet groups available (toggled per location **via RIS**) but the market "is not ready" for higher bets yet. Val was surprised (Nebraska is wealthy), but Juan noted the visited locations were in poor neighborhoods; it's "a $20 player." [[raw/game_design-may-8-2025.md]]
- **Progressives / RTP:** Nebraska win-per-unit is ~**$40–50**, so the top progressive must stay small — the in-market top reset was **$2,500** (most machines below it), with a **$25 minor** hit often. JVL's plan: base **RTP ~90% + progressive contribution (max ~2%)**, or come in lower (88% base + 4% progressive; or 92% to win players over then adjust). A single progressive **level** today; adding lower meters would require introducing them. Vanilla's actual RTP to be learned via [[wiki/people/felipe-miranda.md|Felipe]] (very similar product in Georgia). [[raw/game_design-may-8-2025.md]]
- **$599 lock-up limit:** the regulator submitted to raise it to **$1,199** (didn't make the current bill; expected later). It's not a casino-style nightmare — in Nebraska the location's staff (bar/waitress) or a technician can **key off the lockup** and sign paperwork, so downtime is brief. A future sub-$500-win game was floated if the $599 cap sticks, but not prioritized now. [[raw/game_design-may-8-2025.md]]
- **Cabinet:** dual-screen is fine even though Vanilla runs vertical (Vanilla installs looked inconsistent/"ugly"; one location had no chair). [[raw/game_design-may-8-2025.md]]
- **Bonuses:** the team wants **both** the **Golden Husk** bonus (the corn reskin) **and** the **Swift bonus** on Nebraska games. The Swift ("quick") bonus is made a **requirement** to work on a **single machine** (not disabled by absence of a link), capped at **$100** so it hits frequently without lock-up interruption. [[raw/game_design-may-8-2025.md]]
- **Game shortlist / start:** production starts with **Crazy Spins** and **Midnight Money** (Lena will begin once Juan approves the final list; she can start one confirmed game). Juan swapped **Aztec Star → Clover Fortune** in his suggested six. Progressive reset levels sit well below $2,500. [[raw/game_design-may-8-2025.md]]

## May 15 2025 — the attorney rules nudge out; pivot to a Vanilla-style power slider

On the [[wiki/meetings/2025-05-15-game-design-nebraska-south-dakota.md|May-15 game-design review]], after [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]]'s Nebraska site visit, the whole nudge basis of this project was **overturned** — a major evolution of both the Mar-14 decision and its May-8 reversal. [[raw/game_design_nebraska_south_dakota-may-15-2025.md]]

- **The regulator's contact was not qualified; her boss (the attorney) overruled her.** JVL's assigned Department of Revenue contact [[wiki/people/brittany-walker-bell.md|Brittany]] (transcribed here "Brittany Waters") had given verbal "your game looks fine" assessments; during the visit Juan met **her boss, the attorney**, who apologized that "she should have never spoken about the game, she's not qualified for that" and, after reviewing JVL's video, said in legal terms that **nudge as presented does not qualify as skill** — because with a nudge "if you hypothetically nudged in the wrong direction, you could lose," and an unwinnable outcome "is what's not allowed." [[raw/game_design_nebraska_south_dakota-may-15-2025.md]]
- **Nudge is dropped entirely (supersedes the May-8 "enhanced nudge" choice).** "Nudge is done. We're not doing the nudge is no longer a topic of discussion for this market." Everyone (Efim especially) agreed. This voids both the Mar-14 conservative-nudge plan and its May-8 reversal to the enhanced nudge; the earlier nudge current-state now sits in the project History. See [[wiki/decisions/2025-03-14-base-nebraska-on-georgia-conservative-nudge.md]]. [[raw/game_design_nebraska_south_dakota-may-15-2025.md]]
- **The market and the compliant mechanic.** Nebraska's #1 supplier is **Bonilla Games** (~60% of the market; its CPU also loaned/sold into other manufacturers' generic cabinets); the dominant box is **Vanilla**. Both use a **"power slider"** where the win is always achievable through player dexterity (press dead-center = 100%; off to either side = 90/80/50%) — every winning combination is winnable, which is why it qualifies as skill. Newer manufacturers have moved away from nudge. JVL will replace nudge with its own **post-spin skill overlay** modelled on the power slider — the two candidate designs (Lucky Rainbow / Bullseye) and the market read are on [[wiki/topics/slot-game-design-principles.md]]. [[raw/game_design_nebraska_south_dakota-may-15-2025.md]]
- **Software approach (confirmed / extended).** Start from **Georgia Peach** games (furthest along; don't exist in Class 3) rather than Class-3 base games; **flatten the pay tables** and chop the top-end wins, then feed the removed top-win allocation (est. ~1–2% of total) into a **randomly-hitting "Swift"/Golden-Fast bonus** so a $10,000-type jackpot can't wipe out a small location. Keep games with absolute caps; avoid infinite-free-spin games (Midnight Money, Jungle Jaguar are hard to convert). ~70% of JVL's games can be made "palatable." Efim uses his **Malibu** math tool (predetermined outcomes) to retune. [[raw/game_design_nebraska_south_dakota-may-15-2025.md]]
- **Low-wallet market economics.** Win-per-unit ~**$46–$56**; 80% of locations visited had only **two machines** (gas stations, bars, restaurants — a side business), so high Class-3 win amounts (a $10k progressive or a "Summit Big Event") "could wipe us out for 30, 60, 90 days." [[raw/game_design_nebraska_south_dakota-may-15-2025.md]]
- **Submission scope.** **Six titles** for the first Nebraska submission on a **D27**; separately a vertical **V43** submission is now wanted for **South Dakota** (kept a distinct math track — see [[wiki/projects/south-dakota-vlt-market.md]]). Games in play: Liberty/Disco/Rodeo Spins, Pink Princess (Georgia-only, best-looking), Clover Fortune, gem/3×3 games; the **Golden Husk** bonus must still be inserted (with RTP space found). Cornhusk/Cornhusker stays as a concept, but the market showed "no loyalty" to Wild-West themes (observed games were fishing/Frankenstein/golf). [[raw/game_design_nebraska_south_dakota-may-15-2025.md]]
- **Next action (Juan).** Dig through the attorney's list of approved competing games' pay tables (posted on their websites), find the operating "sweet spot," and decide whether JVL goes slightly more generous; get extended video of the skill-bar behaviour for Lena's choreography. [[raw/game_design_nebraska_south_dakota-may-15-2025.md]]

## Golden Husk bonus economics + JVL-as-operator reservation (May 29 2025)

On the [[wiki/meetings/2025-05-29-games-design-recap.md|May-29 games recap]] the team debated how the **Golden Husk bonus** should be tuned for Nebraska's low-win-per-unit market. [[raw/games_design_recap-may-29-2025.md]]

- **Progressive base value.** **Georgia Peach's progressive starts at $2,400** — too high given Nebraska's ~**$50 win-per-unit** (Georgia's published average is ~$96; internet rumours put Nebraska at $36–42) and the **$599 machine lock-up** cap. [[wiki/people/michael-gottlieb.md|Michael]] proposed dropping the Nebraska base to **$500, then $300–$400, even $200** so wins stay under $600 (no lock-up) and the bonus hits more often. They'll ask the largest Omaha operator (**Ryan Cruz / NTS**, ~70–80% of Omaha) what level to set. [[raw/games_design_recap-may-29-2025.md]]
- **RTP allocation / frequency.** [[wiki/people/efim-podvoiski.md|Efim]]: Georgia allocates **5% of RTP** to the bonus, hitting **1 in ~600 games**, bonus EV ~15 bets; the base **progressive RTP is ~1%**. Options floated: raise to **6–7%** and/or increase frequency to ~1-in-300. Nebraska target base RTP ~**92%** (locals machine). [[raw/games_design_recap-may-29-2025.md]]
- **JVL will also be an operator in Nebraska** — so Val **reserved judgment** on aggressive RTP settings, not wanting to compete with the two largest Nebraska operators JVL has relationships with (and to whom it is selling machines in quantity); he's "trying to do everything possible not to put boots on the ground." Bets seen in-market were mostly $0.25–$3. [[raw/games_design_recap-may-29-2025.md]]

## June 4 2025 — Eclipse testing-requirement review (Nick + Steve)

On the [[wiki/meetings/2025-06-04-eclipse-nebraska-testing-requirements.md|June-4 call]] with the [[wiki/organizations/eclipse-gaming-lab.md|Eclipse]] lab — testing lead **Steve** and skill-game legal/patent expert **Nick** — Val and [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]] scoped what certifying JVL for Nebraska entails. [[raw/eclipse_nebraska_testing_requirement_review-june-4-2025.md]]

- **Three parts to the scope of work:** (1) **Nebraska's own skill requirements**; (2) **portions of GLI-11** (the bulk of the testing) — Steve to send JVL his extraction of the required sections; (3) the **Intralot site-controller / central system** (as in Georgia). SAS is **almost identical to Georgia** (a few extra Nebraska meters/events), so a clean Georgia-Intralot connection should confirm relatively easily. [[raw/eclipse_nebraska_testing_requirement_review-june-4-2025.md]]
- **What counts as skill (the confirmation JVL needed).** **Nudge and simple-swap games are defined as NOT skill**; what passes more easily is **memory games, assignment-type games, and power-meter-type games** — confirming the [[wiki/topics/slot-game-design-principles.md|May-15 pivot]] to a power slider. **Every play must have a winnable outcome:** on a winning combination the **power meter** is sufficient skill; on a **losing** play you must offer a **secondary skill** letting the player recoup up to **104%** of the bet (bet 25¢ → win 26¢). The most common secondary skill is a **Simon-says memory game** (3×3 grid; escalating rounds from ~9 up to 25–30 positions; deliberate intermissions every 5–10 plays to force a "brain dump"). This came from **Nebraska's own lawyer** — "every game must have a winnable outcome." [[raw/eclipse_nebraska_testing_requirement_review-june-4-2025.md]]
- **Where the secondary game can live:** on the main screen **or the digital button deck (V-deck)**, as long as the player controls it and the rules are shown (usually in the help screen). [[raw/eclipse_nebraska_testing_requirement_review-june-4-2025.md]]

## June 5 2025 — designing the compliant math (power meter + Simon, or "no losing outcome")

The day after the Eclipse call, [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]], [[wiki/people/efim-podvoiski.md|Efim]] and [[wiki/people/michael-gottlieb.md|Michael]] worked out how to build a compliant game ([[wiki/meetings/2025-06-05-nebraska-regulation-game-design.md]]; the design mechanics are on [[wiki/topics/slot-game-design-principles.md]]). [[raw/nebraska_regulation_game_design-june-5-2025.md]]

- **Market read: Banila (Bonilla) can disable the secondary feature.** A video from JVL's "man on the ground" at a potential test site shows **Banila** — the main Nebraska supplier, "at least 50% of the market" — with the ability to **turn the secondary (Simon) feature off** because "the operator found it annoying" (relayed via **Dennis** from a technician at NTS). Juan proposed JVL match Banila: ship the secondary skill to satisfy Eclipse + the attorney, but add a lesser-pronounced setting to disable it. **Val disagreed on legality** — disabling should be **player-selectable** (a choice), not **operator-selectable** (which "breaks the law") — and distrusts anything relayed second-hand through a mechanic; Juan to verify the actual game-setting screen in person. [[raw/nebraska_regulation_game_design-june-5-2025.md]]
- **The two design paths.** (a) **By the book:** offer the secondary Simon skill on losses, but present it as a **vague V-deck message** the player ignores by hitting spin (Val himself missed it while playing in-market). (b) **Design out the loss:** build the math so **every spin wins something** (≥5 credits, or even 1¢ — a competitor "Thistle" reportedly pays 1¢ to count as a win), which — by the letter of the reg — delivers a winnable outcome every play and could avoid the secondary feature entirely, at the cost of a **power meter on every spin**. Val and Efim leaned toward the "always-win" math (via a guaranteed **scatter pay**) as cleaner, pending Eclipse/attorney sign-off. [[raw/nebraska_regulation_game_design-june-5-2025.md]]
- **Anti-farming design.** The Simon secondary is deliberately made a low-paying, escalating "job" (~$5–10/hour), all-or-none, with intermissions — so a determined player can theoretically recoup 104% but rarely will; Val worried about the **max bet** (mostly $3, up to $4–5) enabling farming if the game "never loses." [[raw/nebraska_regulation_game_design-june-5-2025.md]]
- **The regulator "is on our side."** Nebraska is adding a **6.5% tax** and bringing the **Intralot** monitoring company on **July 1** — so it wants the machines compliant, not shut down; ~5,000 skill machines are already deployed. [[raw/nebraska_regulation_game_design-june-5-2025.md]]
- **Open verification items.** Whether winning a small fraction (e.g. 5 credits on a 40-credit bet) counts as a "win" that skips the secondary event, and whether the 104%-of-bet ceiling is firm, are **not in writing** — Juan to confirm with Eclipse. Juan will make another Nebraska trip (combined with South Dakota training), **bypassing Dennis** to question the NTS technician directly. [[raw/nebraska_regulation_game_design-june-5-2025.md]]
