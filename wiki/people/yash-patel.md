Because that's the future of this business." *(These lines sit in the drifting-label stretch; the attribution holds because Val separately says "You have a beautiful route, and you could sell it to a bigger player," to which the reply is the one below.)* On why he hasn't: "There is [a market], but they don't want to pay me what I want" — and "Money's not end all. Method: "It's hard to do it with the enchilad reports" — the vendor name is garbled in the transcript and is **not** resolved here — "so we're trying to scrape as much data as we can, and then I talk to my retailers who I've got a good relationship with and say, hey, look. Watch this game, and tell me if you know Betty Sue comes in there, and tell me how long she's playing… it's difficult here. It's not granular." ## Sept 17 2026 — ninety minutes in person: the association, Class A, Texas, and Georgia's operator history

The [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-17-halcyon-amusements-georgia-class-a-and-texas-route-partnership.md|Roswell breakfast]] (8:49–10:19 AM ET) is by far the longest recording of him and the one that establishes what he is worth to this corpus: **a GAMOA board member who runs a large Texas route and is willing to give a supplier numbers, names and advice against his own interest.** His route's operating data is on [[wiki/organizations/halcyon-amusements.md]] (Sept 17 2026); the Texas market read is on [[wiki/projects/texas-used-machine-distribution.md]]; the Class A material is also on [[wiki/topics/georgia-class-a-machines.md]]. ### He is on the GAMOA board — and his criticism of it is the sharpest in the corpus

"**I'm a member of [GAMOA]. I'm on the board, right? So this is nothing against them. It's not vindication.**" Then the charge: the association "**has made it to where they want to force every other retailer and master licensee and operator, manufacturer, to be a member… And it's like if you're not a member, if you're not part of this club, then we don't like you. You're ostracized.**" *(The recording renders GAMOA as "Demola" throughout, with "Gamboa", "Gamow" and "Gamal" as further variants of the same word; the derived notes read all of them as GAMOA and flag it as a probable new glossary entry.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Halcyon_Amusements_Georgia_Class_A_and_Texas_Route_Partnership-September-17-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Halcyon_Amusements_Georgia_Class_A_and_Texas_Route_Partnership-September-17-2026.md]]

**His count of the SkillPay split: 70/30 in favour, not 50/50.** Val put it at "split more or less in the middle"; Yash: "**I'd say seventy thirty, honestly**", reasoning from having been on the retail side and now the operator side. His evidence: "**All the other operators that I know, I'm friends with, that are not [GAMOA] members, they all want skill pay. All the retailers I know, they all want skill pay. There's nobody out there who says no.**" And his rule: "**the minority should just get absorbed into the majority… Because indecision is the worst decision.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Halcyon_Amusements_Georgia_Class_A_and_Texas_Route_Partnership-September-17-2026.md]]

### The statute, and the loophole he expects the market to drive through

He cited it from memory: "**if you read the OCGA, I think it's 57-21-71.1, subsection B… it clearly states a gift card cannot be redeemed for anything other than merchandise… Coin, cash, and currency by a COAM location.**" The operative words, in his reading, are the last four: "**that's where the hangup is going to be… Clearly states in the OCGA not by a COAM location. So what's going to happen? People that own the gas station that have game machines in there, have a suite next door, are going to make a non-COAM location and start a gift card buying business.**" *(The citation is as he gave it, hedged with his own "I think"; no statute text is verified here.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Halcyon_Amusements_Georgia_Class_A_and_Texas_Route_Partnership-September-17-2026.md]]

**His precedent is Ohio, around 2010** — internet cafés where "you would buy time to use a computer… **the guy at the counter would give you gold coins worth the value of your winnings**", with a separate gold-buying storefront next door ("**definitely a sister**"). When **John Kasich** became governor he sent notices to shut them down; some operators quit, and some kept running because the penalty was "**a misdemeanor, and it was like twenty five hundred bucks**" against operators "**making a hundred thousand dollars a week**." His conclusion: "**Same thing's gonna happen here.**" *(The neighbouring business is transcribed "Weeby Gold Star" and the notes flag it as unverified; nothing is substituted.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Halcyon_Amusements_Georgia_Class_A_and_Texas_Route_Partnership-September-17-2026.md]]

**And his fear is about who gets squeezed, not about the law.** "**they're going to try to squeeze out retailers that are [not] going to be able to afford it… some retailers that exist in the state of Georgia only survive because they have gas. Other than that, they're not going to be able to pay their mortgage or their bills because they have no inside sales.**" Val: "**You're preaching to a converted.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Halcyon_Amusements_Georgia_Class_A_and_Texas_Route_Partnership-September-17-2026.md]]

### His advice to his own side: stop lobbying

This is the answer to the **unified-lobbying front he asked Val to join on Sept 12** — and it runs the other way. On masters who want to change the law: "**some of these guys are like, oh, we're going to lobby to the state to get this law changed. I'm like, man, guys, save your fucking money.** Because now you're lobbying for one thing, then you get another master who… wants it done a different way. That's more beneficial to him than the industry… **Now you're bringing more attention to this wound. It's festering now. Just leave it alone. That's my strategy: do nothing.**" Val's own version — conflict belongs in the back room, no statements, stop asking the regulator for clarity, "**stop digging**" — met it exactly. **Note what this does to the Sept-12 ask:** the breakfast was arranged to take up a **unified Texas lobbying effort**, and the recorded conversation contains **no Texas lobbying discussion at all**; the lobbying talk is entirely Georgia and entirely against lobbying. **The Texas lobbying item is therefore not discharged and not withdrawn — it simply does not appear.** [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Halcyon_Amusements_Georgia_Class_A_and_Texas_Route_Partnership-September-17-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Halcyon_Amusements_Texas_Net_Win_and_Industry_Lobbying-September-12-2026.md]]

### Texas — his strategic advice to a supplier, given twice

**Do not sell cabinets into Texas right now; sell the software on a board.** "instead of trying to sell cabinets out there right now, **if you can get your software on a JAMMA pinboard**… that's compatible with the **JAMMA harness**. People will be more likely to buy it… **and then you can do the cabinets.**" He repeated it later unprompted: "**to move into that market right now, where people are trying to still buy games but they have too much inventory, is if you can get your software on a [JAMMA] board.**" *(The recording renders it "Java board" the second time; the derived notes read both as JAMMA.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Halcyon_Amusements_Georgia_Class_A_and_Texas_Route_Partnership-September-17-2026.md]]

The reason is inventory, not taste: "**there's master licensees in Georgia who have a lot of dead inventory of Banilla cabinets. So what they're doing is they're shifting them all to Texas**… now you have operators out there who have the cabinets, but **don't necessarily want to spend another twenty five hundred bucks on buying a new cabinet. If you can sell them software for the same amount, they'll buy more software and put it into the cabinet.**" Val's reply — "I know, but it's going to die eventually" — got "**It will eventually die.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Halcyon_Amusements_Georgia_Class_A_and_Texas_Route_Partnership-September-17-2026.md]]

**And the model to beat: Fusion Game Group at $10 per device per day.** He corrected himself to the number in mid-sentence — "**I think it's thirty bucks or fifty bucks a day. Not even that. Actually, it's less than that… It's ten dollars a day per device**" — and the terms are the point: "**They don't care about the split. They don't care about anything else**", and "**You can just give the games back at any time.**" What it enables: "**your guys who want to be operators but don't have any capital**" can undercut incumbents on the location split — "what percentage is this guy giving you right now?… **I'll give you 70.**" On contracts: "**You can make contracts, but they're not enforceable, so nobody signs them.**" *(His own arithmetic on the deal is garbled — "a hundred devices… you make a thousand dollars a month" and later "a thousand dollars a day, a thousand dollars a month" — and is **not** cleaned up here.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Halcyon_Amusements_Georgia_Class_A_and_Texas_Route_Partnership-September-17-2026.md]]

**What Texas wants in a cabinet:** "**They want the big screen, bezel to bezel, LED lights, bigger**", and by example "**this [is] a Banilla cabinet, 43 inch vertical single strip… Similar to the one you guys have. And that works.**" *(He also notes a lot of generic Chinese cabinets in the market, naming **Omega** as one supplier of them.)* This matches and refines his Sept-12 "big cabinet with a big screen" warning. ### Missouri — a new market he raised and offered to help with

He walked Val through it: an operator he places with **Torch Amusements** (owner's name he could not recall — "Mike Mark, something like that") took exclusive Banilla distribution for Missouri, bootleg Chinese Banilla boards proliferated anyway, crime and attention followed, he sued the state and won, then lost on a retest — "**these aren't games of skill. These are actually games of chance because the skill is only contingent upon the winnings**" — and operators were shut down, with a couple he knows "**being investigated right now under the Federal RICO Act**." The aftermath is the opportunity: "**Missouri's lawmakers [said] this guy was making a lot of money, and we were getting none of it. So they looked at the surrounding states that had legalized skill gaming, and they basically adopted a model that's kind of like a hybrid between Illinois and Georgia — get a master license, operator license, pay a decal fee per device, and the state of Missouri will take a certain percentage.**" Status, in his own words: "**I'm not sure if it passed all the way or not**", and "**No, they haven't issued decals yet.**" Asked whether there is a revenue split: "**I don't think there is. I don't think they've made it that sophisticated.**" He offered to help — "**Happy to do that**" — and Val said "**I'll look at it.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Halcyon_Amusements_Georgia_Class_A_and_Texas_Route_Partnership-September-17-2026.md]]
