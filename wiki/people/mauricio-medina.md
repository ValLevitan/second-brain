# Mauricio Medina

Mauricio Medina is JVL's **Mexican tax litigator**, of the Mexico-City firm **Medina Mendoza Abogados** (the transcript speaker label reads "Medina Mendoza Abogadas y Abogados"). He handles the **2019 tax audit** of JVL's Mexican slot-machine operation. He speaks little English (the July-3 call ran through [[wiki/people/felipe-miranda.md|Felipe]] as translator). This page resolves the earlier first-name-only "Mauricio" from the May-29 2025 strategy call (see [[wiki/people/humberto-sarkis.md]]). He is NOT the HUB International insurance broker of the same first name — see [[wiki/people/mauricio-hub-international.md]]. [[raw/mx_tax-dispute_status_review-july-5-2025.md]] [[raw/mexico_tax_case-may-29-2025.md]]

## The 2019 audit and the depreciation dispute (Jul 3 2025)

- **What the audit is about.** The Mexican authority challenges JVL's having depreciated its slot machines at the **30% personal-computer rate** (Articles 34/35). Mauricio's read: the law is literal — only desktop/laptop **personal computers** qualify for 30%; a slot machine doesn't, so it falls under **"other activities not specified" at 10%**. [[raw/mx_tax-dispute_status_review-july-5-2025.md]]
- **His defence (multi-component).** Argue the machines are **"multicomponentes"** — they contain printers, optical/barcode readers and a **storage unit** (per Article 37), each depreciable at **30%** — so the write-off returns to 30% once the machine is decomposed into parts. Second prong: the authority never legally **defined "computer"** (only an engineer could). [[raw/mx_tax-dispute_status_review-july-5-2025.md]]
- **Case status.** Audit started **July 2024**; a **conciliation letter was signed June 19 2025**, suspending the audit and granting 12 months; the audit **restarted June 20** on the remaining (depreciation) amount. Two of three findings were settled in conciliation (see the figures on the decision page); the depreciation point is still **open** — Mauricio expects a **resolution in ~6 months** carrying a **fiscal credit (assessment) of ~6 million pesos**, then appeals through the instances. [[raw/mx_tax-dispute_status_review-july-5-2025.md]]
- **Warnings.** Bringing the Canadian parent into the argument (Val's restatement idea) risks escalating from Mexico-City to **federal** authorities; invoices are registered digitally in the SAT system, and non/late registration carries penalties of **50–100% of invoice value**. Keep the matter in **Ciudad de México** jurisdiction; no Mexican precedent exists for a slot-machine-manufacturer case (Mexico doesn't document case law the way other countries do). [[raw/mx_tax-dispute_status_review-july-5-2025.md]]
