# Salim Khambawala (Dixie Amusement; VP of the Georgia operators' association)

The **master licensee** behind [[wiki/organizations/dixie-amusement.md|Dixie Amusement]] (~70 Georgia COAM locations, south-side Atlanta), an officer of the state operators' association, and the operator JVL treats as the market's product tester and a route into the association board. [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] met him at his office/warehouse on **August 31 2026** — see [[wiki/meetings/2026-08-31-dixie-amusement-kiosk-wait-and-see.md]] — and again on **September 8 2026**, when the first priced offer was put to him ([[wiki/meetings/2026-09-08-dixie-amusement-bundle-offer-and-card-economics.md]]). ## His reading of the Georgia regulatory position

The wait-and-see ruling this produced is on [[wiki/decisions/2026-08-31-dixie-defers-kiosk-investment-until-the-rules-settle.md]]. The reasoning is his, and it is the most complete operator-side account of Georgia's political mechanics in the corpus. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

- **Why regulators keep it murky.** "**The worst reason is they know that's where they make funds. So when they find someone, they get fined. And they don't want to pick sides of anyone. So it's always murkier and murkier.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **The Lottery regulates; someone else enforces.** "**the lottery is just the regulator. They are not the law maker or the enforcer. The enforcer is going to be GBI, the local police department, the local sheriff department.** If they get their hands into it and say, hey, the constitution which they are there to protect and enforce, we see it's [breached]… **It's an uphill battle to fight the state and the attorney general.**" See [[wiki/people/joe-kim.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **On whether a kiosk is even in scope, he told the regulator's own argument back as a reductio.** "One guy said, 'What if I put a scale, a weighing scale?… **What if I put a scale under my machine? And every person who comes in first stands on it and weighs himself before he starts playing? Does it mean that it's not a [COAM]? It's a weighing scale?**'… **Lottery says it's not a part of it because it's doing something else besides a COAM machine which is being played**… Them saying no, kiosk is not part of the coin, **but it will become a part**." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **How the industry keeps its legal ground, in his telling.** "in **2003**, the [COAMs] were banned from Georgia. We were taken out… **It took us ten years to build that relation into… the legislation**… Then you have to keep pushing it in a way to make it more legit and more strong. **So every year we have to go back to them during legislation and talk to them. Even if we don't have anything to ask, we still have to keep it.**" And the standing risk: "**they sign one executive order and say, 'Hey, these [machines] are illegal; they are gambling.' And if the governor… signs it, [the] charter goes.**" His conclusion on the lawmakers themselves: "**Some of these guys don't even understand business. They've never had a business, but they run and sit at the capital and make decisions for us**… So **we have to take them on dinners and educate them.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## Sept 8 2026 — the card economics he taught back to Val

At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-08-dixie-amusement-bundle-offer-and-card-economics.md|Sept-8 2026 follow-up]] Salim turned the meeting around: [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] made an offer, and Salim spent the next half hour explaining the gift-card business Val is trying to enter. He offered it as a favour — "**Since you are helping me, I'm helping you to understand that part also**" — and his evidence is four months of running two processors side by side plus a peer group: "I'm running from **[InComm]** some gift card. I'm running **[Fiery Pay]** gift card from last four months. So I have my data, and then we talk. He said **there's a group of master licensee. We compare and talk to each other.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

The numbers themselves — the ~$280 average load, the broker's $1, the 1.5-vs-3.5 cards a day, the Fiserv/program-manager distinction and the breakage question — are on [[wiki/topics/georgia-prize-card-processor-economics.md]] (Sept 8 2026). What belongs here is **his own position**, which did not move. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

- **He refuses the broker role, and InComm has offered it to him.** "**[InComm] is offering that to us, and we don't want to get involved in that. It's just a headache.**" His arithmetic: "I have **seventy stores. Three cards, two hundred and ten dollars**" a day of load — worth, as he put it later, "**it might be hundred bucks a month. Four cards a day**" to him. Against that, one service call wipes it out: "**Just to go out or do a couple of service calls for this headache. The card got stuck. I ran out of card. Can you get this card delivered? It cost me more than books just to do that part. Do I want to get involved in it? No.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]
- **And there is a second reason, which is about leverage, not money.** He does not want his locations to know he earns anything on the card: "then they start thinking, **you are also making money there. Now we don't want to make money. You directly deal with [InComm] or whoever the distributor is. We put out the equipment. If they give you money, take it. You keep it**… I don't want to get involved because… **Then, when I am negotiating**" — the turn breaks off, but Val restated the point back and Salim did not correct it: "**so you don't want them to know that you are making money, but if this money came from JVL to you, it would be nice.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]
- **Val's counter did not move him, and he did not close the door.** Val's pitch was that JVL removes every objection — "if I provide it for you, and if it arrives to your cell phone, and… **[InComm] guarantees it to you, it changes the whole thing**" — and that the money is his by right: "it's **your money and it's your locations and it's your business and it's your equipment**." Salim's read of the ask was blunt: "**You tell me to take the risk. That's why you are proposing that.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]
- **He would not accept a terminal shut-off for non-payment.** Val asked directly whether a broker could shut down his terminal when a location owes $2,800 and will not pay. Salim, twice: "**I won't like it because it's not mine** [fault]"; "**But as a master licensee, I won't like it.**" And his reason is regulatory as much as commercial — the shutdown becomes evidence the device is part of the machine: "that's why we call these lottery still in that **gray area, muddy area. That is this part of the equipment or it's not part of it**… if it's a part of the equipment, **they are the regulator**, so not only us not liking it, **even lottery won't like it**." Recorded against the exclusivity ruling on [[wiki/decisions/2026-08-31-transactional-equipment-only-with-location-exclusivity.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]
- **His view of where the money actually is: the rails, not the dollar.** He asked Val "**do you have a bank?**" twice, and explained the structure behind the question: "**[InComm], or what you call the banking system**… **that's where [InComm] is just a program manager. [InComm] is not the provider. Provider is one of the bank entities.**" And: "**if you have a bank, who gives you that capacity, then you are of the limits, then you really become in forms like [Fiery Pay] did.**" Val's own answer on the spot was "So I haven't gone that far yet." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

### Speaking as GAMOA vice president

- **The association wants the transactional equipment, slow-walked.** "[The association] **is happy it's happening, but I think we have to slow walk and time it**… some of the politicians or somebody getting mad and putting a piece of legislation out in the next election and **putting more taxes on our industry or putting restrictions is not a good idea**… But I think it's an excellent way to do things the right way than to do it the wrong way." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]
- **Why the timing rules out asking for cash at an ATM now.** "**July first twenty-six gift card became legally allowed to be in the market**; before it was pilot. **You cannot go to a legislator in August and say, 'Hey, by the way, the gift card… could go into an ATM and get cash.'**" See [[wiki/organizations/gamoa.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]
- **Servicing is the master's obligation by law, and that is where the grey area bites.** "**Any repair or maintenance in the equipment has to be master licensed**… Because of **security** and all those reasons, I think **in the law it was written in twenty seventeen, two thousand and fourteen**." The live legal question is whether the transactional device counts as part of the machine: "**Some people have challenged, saying it's a. Because it's connected and communicating, and some people are challenging. It's not. It's a freestanding device**… But somebody debated on saying, **where is this device getting the information from?**" Val's analogy — "the same as where your mobile phone gets information from" — drew "**So it's part of this.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]
- **On the September 2nd letter.** "we were talking about outside. **Paying cash to ATM. We saw the letter which came out on the second. September second. We saw that. So now… he is refining it. Going back and correcting what they said**, is somebody again going to pick a grey area from there and still try to do it? **That's what attorneys do**, right?… I could take that same letter and give it to **five different attorneys**." Val: "and you'll get **ten different opinions from five different attorneys**." *(The letter is named only by date; nothing in this source identifies its author.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

### Why the master will end up paying the connection fee

Val found the current practice extraordinary and Salim explained it as a negotiating posture, not a settled position. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

- **Val:** "some of the masters told me **the location pays a monthly fee on the connection fee on the transactional equipment**… **But I don't understand it. Why would location pay for anything?**"
- **Salim:** "**It's in the legislation for the kiosk, a master, or location, or both. It's just a push from the master. They are not going to pay. But it's just a push of a shield for negotiation for now.**" And he framed the whole thing as the middleman defending his margin: "**that middleman is now pushing the envelope. Hey, what are you providing? Just pay some electricity.**"
- **Val agreed on where it lands:** "**eventually, master will pay because master will buy equipment.** In this case, that's what I keep on telling my guys. **I said it's all temporary right now.**"

### Class A — his prediction that the grey area closes

His read, as an operator and an association officer: "**Class A is not as regulated as Class B. Class B, we pay the 13 percent plus licensing fees plus cost of doing business… Class A… I think their sticker is twenty-five dollars. They don't pay any taxes. They have zero regulations.**" Why he thinks it will not last: "**Class A was only formed to do jukeboxes or pool tables or those ball machines**… But **somebody has created a class A title, but plays like class B and put it in class A. Lottery is not liking it.**" The endgame he expects is on [[wiki/topics/georgia-class-a-machines.md]] (Sept 8 2026). [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

### One more market datum

**Equipment can appreciate, and he has seen it once.** "in my **30 years** of being in this industry, **I see game equipment appreciating**… that equipment because nobody had it, and **IGT did an end of life on it**, and around us we bought it. **Eight thousand was the price, and people were ready to pay sixteen thousand for it** because there was no competition and nothing. **Now again, people are ready to give those away and buy yours or Aristocrat.**" *(He calls the machines "those prodigies," which matches the **IGT Prodigy** the corpus already carries as Georgia legacy inventory.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]
