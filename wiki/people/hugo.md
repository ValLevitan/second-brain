# Hugo (Brazil VLT business-development contact)

Hugo is a Brazil-based gaming-industry business-development contact who pitched [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] on a possible JVL partnership for the Brazilian video-lottery-terminal (VLT) market on a **Sept 2 2025** call. [[raw/brazil_hugo_jvl-september-2-2025.md]] Surname not given; identified by context. Early exploratory conversation — no deal or commitment resulted.

## What he is / what he wants

- He was **hired ~3 months earlier** (≈mid-2025) by a Brazilian group that comes from **land-based gaming** and is **regulated in the online space**, to organize the business and bring in new opportunities. His mandate: find a **technology + games partner** so the group can **build VLT machines locally in Brazil** (importing finished machines is prohibitively expensive — he cited "**70% taxes**" on imported machines), initially as a **distributor of machines**, later obtaining its own licences and opening its own shops. [[raw/brazil_hugo_jvl-september-2-2025.md]]
- He proposed **buying kits/boards from JVL**, building the machines in Brazil, and paying JVL a fee — explicitly **not** shipping finished machines from JVL. He wants JVL's expertise and product built cheaply and locally; Brazil needs **very simple games** (5–20 lines, few jackpots) at this early market stage. [[raw/brazil_hugo_jvl-september-2-2025.md]]
- **His father is in the Brazilian VLT business** with **~3,000 machines already installed** and develops his own games; Hugo says he no longer works with his father ("I help him… but I'm not working with him anymore"), no bad blood. [[raw/brazil_hugo_jvl-september-2-2025.md]]

## Brazil VLT market points he gave (context)

- **PIX** (P-I-X) — Brazil's instant bank-transfer system from the Central Bank of Brazil — is how the machines take money in and out: no bill acceptors, a QR code on screen, the player scans and loads R$5/10/15; money in and out both flow through PIX. A weakness: the player must cash out to their bank account between machines rather than carry credit machine-to-machine. Val countered that JVL's own financial system does the same thing via a **temporary 24-hour wallet** (kiosk → QR ticket → optical-scanner modules on the machines → cash out to a **MasterCard** or bank/credit-card transfer), letting a player roam machine-to-machine before converting. [[raw/brazil_hugo_jvl-september-2-2025.md]]
- Market economics he cited: revenue ~**R$20–25 per machine per day** (street market, low competition, casinos still not approved in Brazil); a locally-built machine costs ~**$1,000** (cabinets, monitors, cables, boards all sourced/built in Brazil, some parts smuggled to avoid tax); the market is at an "infantry" stage where "anything you put in the street gets played." Competitors trying to enter: **AGS** and **EGT** (Bulgaria). [[raw/brazil_hugo_jvl-september-2-2025.md]]
- A regulatory note (unresolved on the call): Hugo believed the Brazil VLT certification is "**July 14**"; Val thought JVL's VLTs (e.g. South Dakota) fall under **GLI-11**, and said Brazil VLTs — treated as electronic scratch cards / lottery — likely need **GLI-11 not GLI-14**; both agreed to verify with [[wiki/organizations/gli.md|GLI]]. [[raw/brazil_hugo_jvl-september-2-2025.md]]
- **The business model was left unresolved.** Val's recurring point: with Brazilian counterparties he "can never find a way how I walk away with the money" — he asked Hugo to reverse-engineer the state-by-state taxes and tell him what profit is acceptable, and Val would work back to it ("figure out how much profit your company is comfortable with… I could agree to any terms"). Hugo to send an email after checking the numbers with his bosses. No follow-up commitment beyond that. [[raw/brazil_hugo_jvl-september-2-2025.md]]

§15.8: market/company economics only (PIX, taxes, per-machine revenue, hardware cost) — no personal compensation.
