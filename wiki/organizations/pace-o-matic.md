# Pace-O-Matic

The American skill-game manufacturer that set the legal benchmark in Texas: the only maker [[wiki/people/stephen-fenoglio.md|Stephen Fenoglio]] would name, in June 2026, as having actually built a 100% skill game and proved it in court. It matters to JVL twice over — as the standard JVL's Texas product is measured against, and as the reason JVL cannot get a written legal opinion out of the best-placed lawyer in the state. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Stephen_Fenoglio_Texas_Skill_Gaming_Counsel-June-10-2026.md]]

**Naming.** The [[wiki/meetings/2026-06-10-stephen-fenoglio-texas-gaming-counsel.md|Jun-10 2026]] transcript renders the company "**Panthematic**" at first mention and "Pace" plainly thereafter; the April 2025 InComm transcript renders it "**pace Omatic**" and "pays thematics". The derived notes for the Jun-10 call read it as **Pace-O-Matic**, which is how the wiki writes it. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Stephen_Fenoglio_Texas_Skill_Gaming_Counsel-June-10-2026.md]] [[raw/incomm_transcript_gacoam_payment_options-april-4-2025.md]]

## The benchmark (Fenoglio, Jun 10 2026)

Val asked directly: "Is there a manufacturer that, in your opinion, has successfully created a one hundred percent skill game?" The answer named Pace and nobody else. Fenoglio is **one of "probably fifteen lawyers who are on outside council for them"**. "We've won a case in state district court… The court of appeals upheld that their game is not a gambling device, **because the skillful player can win each and every time more than it costs to play the game**. This is the mark as far as the basis." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Stephen_Fenoglio_Texas_Skill_Gaming_Counsel-June-10-2026.md]]

## The Fannin County case

The corpus holds two accounts of the same Texas case, fourteen months apart, and they fit together rather than conflict.

**April 4 2025 — John Houseal of [[wiki/organizations/incom.md|InComm]], as the reason InComm was looking at Texas:** "It's Fannin, Fannin County. It's a **pace Omatic sued the Sheriff's Office to get their machines back**, and a judge issued a ruling that [Pace-O-Matic's] machines were skilled machines, not gambling machines, which is **the first time a court in the state of Texas has ever issued an opinion on these devices**." [[raw/incomm_transcript_gacoam_payment_options-april-4-2025.md]]

**June 10 2026 — Fenoglio, who was on the defence team:** [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Stephen_Fenoglio_Texas_Skill_Gaming_Counsel-June-10-2026.md]]

- **The seizure.** Police "came in and seized, I think about **twenty games countywide and maybe forty or fifty thousand in cash**."
- **Pace took the whole fight.** It told the locations and operators running those games "**just get out of the way, we'll call you when we need you. We're going to provide a full legal defense**" — with lawyers and **two different expert witnesses**.
- **Why the state chose that route.** "Police were so convinced they were going to win, they didn't even want to try the criminal case. It takes longer to try it. They thought they had a slam dunk **because the game awarded cash** and under the penal code, if it's a traditional slot machine and awards cash, it's a gambling device. **They had no idea that the pace games were different.**"
- **The result.** "**One day full trial**, end of it, judge says the games are not illegal, that they are not gambling devices. **Ordered the return of all the machines and equipment and cash.**"
- **The appeal made it precedent.** "The state unwisely appealed the case; they truly didn't know what they were doing. And we have a court of appeals decision… which is **binding precedent in the nineteen counties in northeast Texas**… **It's also used as precedent statewide.**" Fenoglio added that he is aware of only this one trial-court-plus-appellate line holding such games legal.

*(The two accounts differ in procedural posture — InComm describes Pace suing the sheriff's office to recover the machines, Fenoglio describes a case the defence tried and the state then appealed. Both are consistent with a seizure followed by litigation over return, and neither source says which. Left unresolved; see Open questions.)*

## The business model — Pace operates, it does not sell

Fenoglio drew the contrast for Val's benefit, and it lands on JVL: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Stephen_Fenoglio_Texas_Skill_Gaming_Counsel-June-10-2026.md]]

- **Banilla is bigger in Texas by unit count** — "Benella Gaines is big in Texas… has more games out than [Pace]" — but it **sells the game to the operator** and, in his words, "that's the end of the story."
- **Pace does not sell.** It "operate[s] it through a series of operators", and "**Pace on the other hand provides full legal defense**." *(The sentence naming who does not sell is elided in the transcript — "You may know does not sell their game;. They operate it through a series of operators" — and is recorded here as the derived notes read it, supported by Fenoglio's own full-legal-defence contrast in the same passage.)* [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Stephen_Fenoglio_Texas_Skill_Gaming_Counsel-June-10-2026.md]]
- **The implication for JVL, stated by Fenoglio:** "I am guessing your company would be the same — you sell the game to the operator, and that's the end of the story." JVL sits in Banilla's position, not Pace's. Whether JVL stands behind its Texas placements legally is not answered anywhere in the corpus.

## Pace as a shield other manufacturers hide behind

Fenoglio's account of how Texas ended up with so many "skill" games: "A number of the other skill game manufacturers have said, '**Well, our machines are virtually identical, and so they're legal too.**' And again, cops and prosecutors don't really have the ability to distinguish between them." His estimate that follows from it — **at least 200,000 games in Texas claiming to be skill, "maybe more"** — is on [[wiki/projects/texas-used-machine-distribution.md]] (Jun-10). [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Stephen_Fenoglio_Texas_Skill_Gaming_Counsel-June-10-2026.md]]
