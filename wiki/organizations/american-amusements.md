# American Amusements (Bank Shot)

The manufacturer behind **Bank Shot**, the game software that opened the Nebraska skill-gaming market by lawsuit and — on the evidence to **June 2026** — is now being taken out of it machine by machine. The company matters to JVL twice over: it is the precedent that made the Nebraska market legally possible, and its decline is the clearest case in the corpus of a gaming supplier dying because its owner stopped investing in the software.

*(Name forms: the corpus renders it **American Amusements** and **American Amusement** interchangeably, and once, in [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]]'s mouth, "American Amusement Facility" — a garble. The plural form is used here because it is the one the founder's own family uses. [[raw/nebraska.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Bank_Shot_and_Nebraska_Withholding-June-16-2026.md]])*

## Origins

- **Founded by the father of [[wiki/people/ryan-cruz.md|Ryan Cruz]] and his sister Sarah**, who run [[wiki/organizations/nts-nebraska-technical-services.md|NTS]], the largest Omaha operator. Ryan, on the 2025 NTS walkthrough: "the original **Bank Shot** game, which is the game that went to the **Nebraska Supreme Court** — and actually our father and the founder of **American Amusements** is the one who developed the original Bank Shot game. **That's the one that kind of broke this open.**" The original game was a pool-ball / tic-tac-toe design. [[raw/nebraska.md]]
- **Based out of Iowa** — [[wiki/people/rick-rees.md|Rick Rees]] of [[wiki/organizations/jj-operator.md|J&J]], naming the maker of Bank Shot: "That's the American Amusements. **He's out of Iowa.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]
- **The position was built on a legal win, not a product.** Rick, hedging the detail as hearsay: "[They] **brought a lawsuit against Nebraska and won. No other manufacturer would do it.** It's **my understanding** that they got their biggest push because of that. They filed a lawsuit against the **state of Nebraska** and won, and were **granted the ability to put them out**." For years they were the only thing available in the state: "They were the **only thing in town**. That's what made them as big as they were. They basically had a **monopoly**. They didn't have anybody else to fight against." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Bank_Shot_and_Nebraska_Withholding-June-16-2026.md]]
- **A second, later American Amusements lawsuit** cleared Nebraska's progressive/jackpot restrictions in June 2025 — the plaintiff won, and JVL was instructed by counsel to disregard regulation **sections 102C and 102D**. The same report notes American Amusement already runs a **WAP (wide-area progressive) and multi-site link**. See [[wiki/meetings/2025-06-17-nebraska-lawsuit-progressives-102cd.md]] and [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md]]. *(Whether this is the same action Rick described, or a separate one, is **not stated in any source** — see Open questions.)* [[raw/legislation_nebraska-june-17-2025.md]]

## How the business works (Rick Rees, June 16 2026)

The fullest description of the model in the corpus. All from [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Bank_Shot_and_Nebraska_Withholding-June-16-2026.md]] unless noted.

*(The derived notes for that segment summarise the same structure: American Amusement **owns the Bank Shot software and sells only the board and computer, not cabinets**, so an operator buys empty shells and builds the machines in its own shop.* [[raw/Meeting_Notes_JJ_Gaming_Bank_Shot_and_Nebraska_Withholding-June-16-2026.md]]*)*

- **They sell components, not cabinets.** "**Bank Shot is the software. American Amusement is the manufacturer**… they run a couple of different versions. A lot of **PC based**." And: "**They don't manufacture a machine; they only manufacture the component, the board, the computer.** So you purchase **empty cabinets, empty shell cabinets** and then install the computer components in there and made the machines… They **bought basically the rights to the software**." The operator builds the finished machine itself: "Then they would **build the cabinet in our office** and go put it out there."
- **Any cabinet can become a Bank Shot.** "Bank shot could be **any kind of cabinet**. If I had a **[[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md|Banilla]] cabinet** and I wanted to make it a bank shot, **I can do that just by adding** [the board]. That's what makes it. That's pretty much all they do. **They don't manufacture cabinets.**"
- **Revenue model: a per-machine fee scaled to play, plus a funded prize pool.** "**You pay a fee per machine based off of amount played**… That's a **very small amount per spin, per bet to the prize pool**. And then if somebody hits that prize pool, **we pay them and then Bankshot reimburses us** whatever that amount was."
- **The hand-pay mechanic, in Rick's own example.** "So if somebody hits a **fifteen thousand dollar** prize pool jackpot, **we hand pay them the money, they get to cash immediately**. And then Bankshot sends us [the money back]… because **we've already paid into that price [pool]**." *(The $15,000 figure is Rick's illustration, not a stated cap. NTS independently discusses paying out $15,000 jackpots in the 2025 walkthrough.* [[raw/nebraska.md]]*)*
- **The games are deliberately old-looking, and are not the original pool game.** "They look very **eighties nineties-ish** games. **Mister and Misses** is one of them." Val asked whether the theme was a pool table — Rick: "**Nope, it's like slot machines. Five reels.**" *(The original Bank Shot that went to the Nebraska Supreme Court was the pool-ball design;* [[raw/nebraska.md]] *the current line is not.)*
- **Placement is unrestricted in Nebraska.** "**Anywhere in Nebraska** you can have [them]… And then they're in the **gas stations**." In **Iowa** the position is the reverse of freedom — it is the *only* thing allowed in some venues: "Gas station, you can only run Bank Shot machines… If you are a **C store**, you can only run Bank Shot machines in there. **So we build those to go into the gas stations.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]

## The decline (as of June 16 2026)

- **Banilla broke the monopoly, and the rest of the field finished it.** "It's getting a lot smaller because [Bank Shot] wasn't the only market — the only thing available in Nebraska for a long time. **Banilla came out and started changing that market**… And now it's with the **introduction of other manufacturers**." Asked whether there is a market for rejuvenating the business: "**I don't think there is anymore.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Bank_Shot_and_Nebraska_Withholding-June-16-2026.md]]
- **The owner will not reinvest.** "**He doesn't want to update his software. He's basically moving himself out of the market because he doesn't want to improve the system.**" Asked who "he" is: "**The guy that owns American.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Bank_Shot_and_Nebraska_Withholding-June-16-2026.md]]
- **J&J's verdict: one of its worst performers, and being removed.** "Bank shot is **another one of our very low performing equipment**"; "for the most part, **I've probably taken more bank shots out this year**." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Bank_Shot_and_Nebraska_Withholding-June-16-2026.md]] Separately, reviewing his own floor data during the site visit, Rick read off a Bank Shot line as "performing a bank shot, which is **not surprising**." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]
- **What keeps it alive is a jackpot-chasing loyalist base.** "We have a few of them that are out there that are **diehard bank shot people**. They have an **app** that you can follow to see **where jackpots are at**. So they'll **go to play those games**." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Bank_Shot_and_Nebraska_Withholding-June-16-2026.md]]
- **NTS was already phasing it down a year earlier.** On the 2025 NTS walkthrough Bank Shot is described as historically central — "there's also a lot of **history** with bank shots… they were the **first game that we had out**" — but now a stopgap: "as long as they can have over four games, we'll get their three Banilas and we'll just **give them bank shots for the time being**… and then we'll eventually get to those places and probably **leave them with just one**." [[raw/nebraska.md]]

## Why this matters to JVL — the lesson Val drew

The same conversation produced a general read on progressives in Nebraska. Asked by Val whether progressives still matter there, Rick: "**I don't think it is as much anymore.** It was great when that was the only thing going on. But now, I think it's about **being able to know what the win is — on site.**" A progressive-pool product that is not visibly winnable at the machine no longer buys loyalty on its own. See [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md]] (Jun-16 2026). [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Bank_Shot_and_Nebraska_Withholding-June-16-2026.md]]

## Open questions

- **Is "the guy that owns American" the same man as the founder?** Rick describes a current owner who refuses to update the software; NTS's Ryan Cruz describes his own father as the founder who developed the original Bank Shot. No source says whether these are the same person, or whether the company changed hands. Not resolved. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Bank_Shot_and_Nebraska_Withholding-June-16-2026.md]] [[raw/nebraska.md]]
- **How many American Amusements lawsuits against Nebraska are there?** Rick describes a suit that won the company statewide placement rights; the June-2025 legal report describes a suit whose win struck regulation sections 102C and 102D. Whether these are one action or two is not stated in either source.
