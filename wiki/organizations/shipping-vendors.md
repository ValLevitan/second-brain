# Shipping vendors (ASIMEX, ATLAS, and others)

The freight companies JVL uses to move machines by ground between Mexico, the US, and Canada.

## Who does what

- **ASIMEX** (also "ACIMEX") — the Mexican freight/customs company that originally brought the machines into Mexico; it has the best connections at the border and carries loads from Mexico City to Laredo. **Alejandro** is JVL's contact there. [[raw/shipment_of_machines_from_mexico_january_8_2025.md]]
- **ATLAS** (also "ATAS") — carries loads from Laredo onward to Toronto/Georgia. JVL introduced ASIMEX and ATLAS to each other, and they tend to cooperate. [[raw/shipment_of_machines_from_mexico_january_8_2025.md]]
- The usual pattern: ASIMEX runs Mexico to Laredo (changing trailers), then ATLAS loads from Laredo to Georgia. [[raw/shipment_of_machines_from_mexico_january_8_2025.md]]
- **Boomer** and **Monoplus** were also to be asked for comparison quotes. [[raw/shipment_of_machines_from_mexico_january_8_2025.md]]

## Facts

- Shipments Canada-to-Mexico (and back) go by ground. [[raw/shipment_of_machines_from_mexico_january_8_2025.md]]
- ATLAS quoted $12,000 door-to-door for two 53-ft trucks (excluding export customs in Mexico and Canada). [[raw/shipment_of_machines_from_mexico_january_8_2025.md]]
- Val wants the two legs (ASIMEX Mexico City to Laredo; Laredo to Toronto) quoted separately, and wants ATLAS to know it is being quoted separately from ASIMEX. Alejandro at ASIMEX is the contact for a possible export tax rebate. [[raw/shipment_of_machines_from_mexico_january_8_2025.md]]

## Mexico's slot-machine import ban and the parts workaround (~Mar 19 2025 call, date inferred)

From the Val–Hector–Alejandro call about importing **Flex machines from Canada into Mexico** (see [[wiki/meetings/2025-03-19-asimex-flex-importation.md]]): [[raw/Asimex - Importation of Flex machines_2.md]]

- **Complete slot machines cannot be imported into Mexico.** A decree "publicated on past October" (Alejandro dates it **October 18**, i.e. Oct 2024) forbids it; the Secretary of Economy is not releasing import permits. Parts, however, are allowed — "video game machine parts," openly declarable even as parts for slot machines. [[raw/Asimex - Importation of Flex machines_2.md]]
- **The workaround:** ship a machine as **separate part shipments in separate trucks** (monitors + podium in one, the rest in another; Val suggested PC boards could fly by air) — if all parts cross in one truck, the authority may treat them as one slot machine and seize the merchandise. JVL can send everything to ASIMEX's **Laredo, Texas warehouse** and ASIMEX splits the import into different trucks, with a **separate invoice per part** crossing the border. [[raw/Asimex - Importation of Flex machines_2.md]]
- **Sample import cost:** on a hypothetical import of two machines valued **$7,000 USD total**, ASIMEX's all-in invoice Laredo→Mexico City (VAT + import duties + handling) is **$3,183**, of which **$1,138** is the VAT paid in the import *pedimento*; the Toronto→Laredo leg is an additional expense on top. [[raw/Asimex - Importation of Flex machines_2.md]]
- **Importer of record:** JVL's Mexico entity — rendered in the transcript as "Hotavial and Mobi in Mexico" / "**JVL Mobi in Mexico**" (garbled; JVL Mobi México is the group's Mexican entity per [[wiki/organizations/jvl.md]]) — would be the importer and could claim the VAT back **in Mexico**, via the annual SAT declaration; the exact reclaim mechanism (and whether it works if the machines stay under JVL's ownership rather than being sold) was left for Hector to check with "Alejandro, the accountant." [[raw/Asimex - Importation of Flex machines_2.md]]
- **Credit terms:** JVL's initial imports with ASIMEX were paid over **six monthly payments** with an interest percentage (rate not remembered on the call); Alejandro is to check whether the same terms can be offered again. [[raw/Asimex - Importation of Flex machines_2.md]]
- **NOM certificates (monitors):** monitors whose power source is 24 volts or more need a **NOM certificate** regardless of transport mode (truck, air, or sea) — Val's monitors run on 120 V, so it is required and is a prerequisite for the import. A certificate is valid **one year**, costs **$1,600**, and can cover **any number of monitor models on one certificate** provided they share the same brand and voltage (size — 21 or 27 inch — is indifferent). ASIMEX can apply itself; it needs only the technical manual and pictures, and estimates **~two weeks** to obtain it. (Val joked about sending "a picture of $1,600.") [[raw/Asimex - Importation of Flex machines_2.md]]
- **Mexico warehouse:** ASIMEX has a warehouse about **an hour from Mexico City** (transcribed variously "Teotihuacan" / "Tioloyucan," near "Cotitlan, Iscali" — spelling unclear; flagged), currently almost out of space but likely able to hold **~20–40 machines**. JVL may **partially assemble machines there** with its own people, ship out of it, and use ASIMEX's local Mexican transportation. [[raw/Asimex - Importation of Flex machines_2.md]]
