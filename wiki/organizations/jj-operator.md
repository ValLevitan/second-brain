# J&J (gaming route operator)

J&J is a privately-owned US gaming **route operator** (not a manufacturer) that JVL first spoke to on May 7 2025 as a prospective customer for its cabinets and FlexPay payment solution. [[raw/intro_jj_jvl-may-7-2025.md]]

## What they are

- A route operator that places gaming machines with locations; based in **Central Illinois** (Scott Todd said he is originally from Illinois). [[raw/intro_jj_jvl-may-7-2025.md]]
- **Over 25,000 machines "on the street"** as of the call, and roughly **1,400–1,500 employees** (they had just completed an acquisition). [[raw/intro_jj_jvl-may-7-2025.md]]
- Footprint: **Illinois, Georgia, Nebraska, Montana, Nevada**, and growing in **Pennsylvania**; expanding into **Nebraska**; eyeing a small route in **South Dakota** (a customer is asking) but not there yet. [[raw/intro_jj_jvl-may-7-2025.md]]
- Familiar with JVL's history — they had "run a lot of JVL countertops over the years" and later JVL's Cash Code validators. [[raw/intro_jj_jvl-may-7-2025.md]]

## J&J Nebraska cash operations and back-office systems (June 16 2026)

- **Cash operations.** A **14-person collection team** runs **7 two-man routes per day** ("we do about seven rounds a day"), covering "**upwards of seventy locations a day**." Teams carry pre-strapped cash from J&J's own **vault**, fill terminals, collect machine proceeds, and return cash for counting and recycling. **No armed security**; only one incident since Rick arrived — and "they didn't rob the collectors, they **broke into their vehicle**." Val's comment: "just a matter of time." Rick: "**It's always when, not if.**" [[raw/Meeting_Notes_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]
- **The route procedure, step by step (Rick).** Routes **change every day**, "so they don't know day to day where they're going." A back-office system tells J&J how much money is in each machine and how much needs to go back in. On arriving at a location with four machines and a terminal, "the first thing they'll do is **take the money that they brought with them and fill the terminal** — that way the **biggest bulk of the money is safe**. Then they go to the machines and they'll collect each machine and bring that money back." Bills arrive **already strapped** from J&J's own vault and are **not sorted on site**. "We have a vault that does all of our own counting… that's where it gets **recycled** back in. The ladies will count it in the vault, and then we **keep the amount that we need and deposit what we don't**." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]
- **Where they want cash handling to go.** "We're going to get to a system in Nebraska where we are **paying off locations using data from Interlock** and not relying on manual collection for cash counting after the fact… **no more cash exchanges on site; everything is done through ACH.**" *(The central-system vendor is rendered "Interlock"/"Intralock" in the transcript; the corpus elsewhere records Nebraska as running on **Intralot** — see [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md]] — but the garbled form is recorded as spoken and the identification is an **inference**, not asserted.)* Rick on that vendor's reporting: "They're getting better. We have a pretty big influence, so when we get into a state, we start to work very well with the states to try to gain more." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]
- **Cash stays in the machines.** Asked whether he would prefer cashless, Rick: "I don't think we get away from it… **I don't think the government will allow it**… It's **cash in, TITO**. I don't think we're ever going to get to a point where it's **cashless completely**" — citing industry/political pressure ("there are already concerns that we're being targeted"), while noting Illinois has begun relaxing credit-card use for mobile sports books. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]
- **Back-office systems.** All machines connect via the **Easy Link** system; Rick: "**You have to be dual SaaS in Nebraska** if you're going to run one" *(transcribed "dual SaaS"; recorded as spoken)*. Easy Link is **a third-party company's system, not J&J's** — "it's another company that we use that links the however-many machines to a **central server**… it has its own Easy Links server, it is its own device. All the machines and the terminals are linked to it, and that's where we're pulling the data from." Easy Link's interface boards are "**about a hundred dollars a piece**." **James Jansen** (in Illinois) handles all analytics and purchasing decisions; **Jeff Harwick** oversees technical. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]
- **The in-machine interface board.** Every machine at a location that has a payout terminal carries an interface module that connects the machine to the terminal **and** to the server. The transcript renders it **"SMID", "SMIP" and "MIBs"** in different places; the derived notes give it as **SMIB**. The expansion "Slot Machine Interface Board" is **not in either source** and is dropped here as an unsourced gloss. Rick did not know the price of the non-Easy-Link variant: "I'm not sure, because we haven't really bought any of these." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]

## Redemption terminals and cash dispensers at J&J (June 16 2026)

J&J runs **four different models** of redemption/payout terminal in Nebraska. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]

- **The hardware is cheap; the software is the cost.** "The machine itself is actually relatively cheap. We can get these for about **three to six thousand** depending on the model… but it's the **software**. The software is the biggest part of it." The terminal hardware is made by **Genmega** — "Genmega just makes the machine and provides a computer. **You have to provide your own software.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]
- **Express RT, and the $35/day.** The software J&J runs on them is **Express RT**, whose proprietor is **Clint Lerman** — "he built the software and then he installs it on the computers." The fee is **per site per day**: "I think we've gotten him down to about **thirty-five dollars a day**." Running the terminals on Easy Link's program instead is possible ("we just convert, make the changes that we need") and Clint still charges a fee, but "**theirs is a lot lower**." *(The transcript also contains the line "the software is made by **Point One**" a few turns earlier; whether that names a different component or is a garble is **unclear** and both are recorded as spoken.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]
- **Older German dispensers, acquired free.** J&J also runs **GBT** units ("there are three different models that we have") and **CountR**, which Rick called "another German." (Val, looking at the GBTs, volunteered a German maker's name, transcribed "**Goselman**"; the garble is left as-is and **no real-world manufacturer is substituted here** — though the corpus independently records GBT and CountR as German dispensers on the 2025 NTS walkthrough. [[raw/nebraska.md]]) The CountR units are **free**: "these are free — because we don't use them in **Illinois** anymore, so we get them all for nothing. These machines are probably **fifteen to twenty years old**. We just **refurbish and upgrade computers** as needed and put them back out." They go into "lower-end sites that don't go through a lot of money" and J&J's chain of gas stations. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]

## J&J's Iowa route and the Iowa market (June 16 2026)

- **What Iowa is.** "It's a mix — it's **bars** and **C-stores**, convenience stores." J&J has **~90–100 locations** there (the two figures Rick gave; see above). "**Iowa market's different. It's not as open as Nebraska is.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]
- **Bank Shot only, in C-stores.** "[In a] gas station, you can only run **Bank Shot** machines… If you are a C store, you can only run Bank Shot machines in there. So we build those to go into the gas stations." Bank Shot is made by **American Amusements** — "he's out of Iowa." *(American Amusements is already in the corpus as the plaintiff in the Nebraska progressive lawsuit and as the company founded by [[wiki/people/ryan-cruz.md|Ryan Cruz]]'s father — see [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md]].)* J&J has a **big current push** in Iowa driven by a large gas-station chain expanding there, with about **twenty locations** needing machines. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]
- **No cash payout — in-store redemption.** "There's no tax return [garbled]… so our gas stations and our C stores, our truck stops — it's **in-store purchase**… a **gas card** or whatever. And then at the bars, it's actually like **tickets**, little tickets that you get out of playing, like **skee ball**. You take [them] to the bar and redeem them for **in-store credit** — you can either buy a beer with it or buy a sandwich." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]
- **A licensing bottleneck, not a legal one.** "It's a harder process to get a machine **licensed** over there… **they have like one guy that does it.**" Val's reading, which Rick accepted: "So it's a **labor bottleneck**, it's not a legislative bottleneck." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]
- **Market size and game class.** Rick's guess for the **entire state of Iowa**: "**less than four hundred**" machines. Asked whether they are Class III or skill, he described the **"cherry bonus"**: "I guess I don't know that you classify it as a class three, but it **plays like** a class three. But it's **not considered skill** either. **Iowa is just such a weird market.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]

## J&J's regulatory posture in Nebraska (June 16 2026)

- **They are actively lobbying, and have already started.** On why J&J expects the location split to move in its favour: "**We'll push the state to eliminate** [regulations]… **we've already started that initiative.**" Rick's framing of the state: "They're building up their infrastructure because it's obviously pretty new to them… more regulated, and now they'll have more people to enforce it. So eventually we will get to the point where we're satisfied." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]
- **One ask already lost.** "We also did present that we would like to **get rid of the negotiated splits**. That **got vetoed** because we had some **backlash from the locations**." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]
- **The template is Illinois.** "We're trying to **flagship what Illinois is** and get it as similar as we can here." Rick on Nebraska: "probably [the most regulated state] — but it's also **one of the most profitable**." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JJ_Gaming_Nebraska_Site_Visit-June-16-2026.md]]
