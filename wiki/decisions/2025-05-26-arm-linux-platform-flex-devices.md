# Adopt a cheap ARM-based embedded-Linux board (Rockchip RK) for Flex Tower / Flex POS / TITO server

On the May 26 2025 hardware-selection call ([[wiki/meetings/2025-05-26-hardware-selection-arm-flex-tower-pos.md]]), [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/genadi-peari.md|Genadi Peari]] and [[wiki/people/pavel.md|Pavel Frolov]] resolved the tech-stack question that had been left open on May 20/21 ([[wiki/meetings/2025-05-21-tech-stack-flex-pos-tower-tito.md]]) — **embedded STM32 vs industrial-PC Linux** for the Flex payout devices. Part of the FlexPay evolution arc — see [[wiki/topics/flexpay-business-architecture-payout-devices.md]].

## The dilemma this resolves

The May-20/21 choice was between a cheap embedded **STM32** "sandwich" (~$30, but needing scarce low-level C programmers and hard to develop) and an **industrial-PC Linux board** (flexible/browser-friendly but "**в три раза дороже**" ≈ 3× the price, ~$130). [[raw/selection_tech_stack_hardware-flex-pos_flex-tower_flex-tito-may-21-2025.md]] A weekend investigation found a **third path** that gives Linux flexibility at nearly the STM price: Val — "**направление, мне кажется, для нас, по-моему, это то, что мы искали**" ("this is the direction we were looking for") and "**Очень хороший вариант. Очень. Я даже не думал в эту сторону**" (very good option, I hadn't even thought this way). [[raw/hardware_selection_flex-tower_flex-pos-may-26-2025.md]]

## What was decided

- **Platform: an ARM-based, fully embedded Linux board** (a **Rockchip RK-series** SoC — the "**линейка RK … 30, 31, 28, 33, 28, 35**"; a quad-core "**68-й, четырёхъядерный**" [≈ RK3568] cited), described as **~10× more powerful than STM** and a long-lived, widely-used product line (media players / TV-boxes / POS). It is **off-the-shelf**; samples available immediately. [[raw/hardware_selection_flex-tower_flex-pos-may-26-2025.md]]
- **Everything is onboard/embedded:** built-in **2 GB RAM + 16 GB storage (eMMC/flash)**, watchdog timer, battery, several USB, **two full RS-232 serial ports** (a dispenser can connect directly), **two I2C interfaces** (already provisioned for touchscreens / can drive two screens), Ethernet/network, **12 V** power, plus **4 GPIO** signals (and ~3 ADC pins) and extra **UARTs** reconfigurable to GPIO. **~$60 ±$1** — only "**чуть-чуть дороже**" than the ~$30 STM. [[raw/hardware_selection_flex-tower_flex-pos-may-26-2025.md]]
- **Software: Linux (Ubuntu) or Android**, programmable in any normal-computer language — **C, Java or JavaScript** — so **any JVL programmer** can work on it (not just low-level C specialists); [[wiki/people/evgeniy-alekseev.md|Evgeniy Alekseev]] and the other Linux developers can be harnessed, shipping the products faster. [[raw/hardware_selection_flex-tower_flex-pos-may-26-2025.md]]
- **One board for all three devices** — **Flex Tower, Flex POS, and the standalone (TITO) server** (the server would keep its logs on a cheap **swappable external SD card**, ~$6–8, so a dying card can just be replaced). [[raw/hardware_selection_flex-tower_flex-pos-may-26-2025.md]]
- **Firm sub-ruling — no extra I/O boards.** JVL will **not** add any additional I/O devices/boards (no "IO-борт"); the 2–3 sensors a POS/Tower needs (door sensor, dispenser sensor) are wired straight to the board's **GPIO pins or UART**. Val: this must be "**точно решить**" (definitely decided). [[raw/hardware_selection_flex-tower_flex-pos-may-26-2025.md]]

## The one piece of work that makes it cheap

- The cheap industrial touchscreens use an **LVDS** interface, so JVL builds a **simple adapter board** (like the one already in FlexPort) to connect the screen, and [[wiki/people/evgeniy-alekseev.md|Alekseev]] writes the **LVDS video interface** for Linux (a "довольно-таки небольшая работа," ~2 days). If that interface is **not** built, a controller adds **~$20**. JVL will also make providing the display driver a **condition of sale** with the board vendor (their software engineers can supply it). Net device penalty vs STM is therefore only **~$20 "all together,"** far below the May-21 industrial-Linux delta of ~$100–200. [[raw/hardware_selection_flex-tower_flex-pos-may-26-2025.md]]

## Execution / open items before final sign-off

- **Talk to [[wiki/people/evgeniy-alekseev.md|Alekseev]] first**, then **order samples** (they "cost pennies"); order the matching **LCD + touchscreen with the correct LVDS interface**. [[raw/hardware_selection_flex-tower_flex-pos-may-26-2025.md]]
- **[[wiki/people/pavel.md|Pavel]] to size memory** — 16 GB storage is already enough; check whether **2 GB or 4 GB RAM** is needed by reading current memory utilisation on the FlexBay device (custom 64 GB / 4 GB variants are available, e.g. on AliExpress, at ~the same price). [[raw/hardware_selection_flex-tower_flex-pos-may-26-2025.md]]
- **Power** still to be worked out (Tower needs 24 V for the dispenser + 12 V for the board; POS just 12 V). [[raw/hardware_selection_flex-tower_flex-pos-may-26-2025.md]]

## June 4 2025 confirmation — the formal STM-vs-Linux pros/cons, Linux chosen

A June-4 architecture call ([[wiki/meetings/2025-06-04-flex-tower-flex-pos-platform-decision.md]]) re-argued the platform choice as a formal, category-by-category pros/cons and **confirmed Linux**. [[raw/flex-tower_flex-POS_foundational-decision_architecture-june-4-2025.md]]

- **STM32's advantages (as weighed June-4):** cheapest bill-of-material (main board ~**$30 + $5** vs the ARM-Linux board ~**$60**); a single universal board can serve **all three devices** (Port, Tower, POS); highest **reliability** (little to fail; the ARM board is China-made and its **eMMC** is the weak point — mitigated by using eMMC read-only as the root filesystem, writing only on firmware update); and easiest **hardware testing** (the STM board can be bench-tested on a nail-bed/pin fixture). [[raw/flex-tower_flex-POS_foundational-decision_architecture-june-4-2025.md]]
- **Linux's advantages (the reasons it won):** far **more available developers** (browser-based graphics; backend in C++/Java/JavaScript — reuse of the Java modules [[wiki/people/dmitri-chudinov.md|Dima Chudinov]] built — vs STM needing scarce low-level firmware people); more **support hooks** (logs/state analysis in production); **flexibility** (adding a protocol is quick); can talk **directly to Incom over the internet / wireless**; **~7–10× more performance**; and — decisive — the **Passport, Lease and RIS (Remote Initial Setup)** features **already exist as Java modules** and port from x86 to ARM "almost for free," whereas on STM they would all have to be rewritten and a communication layer (via the TITO server or the CMS) built out. Val: on STM these features would all need rewriting, "я не вижу, как это можно сделать." [[raw/flex-tower_flex-POS_foundational-decision_architecture-june-4-2025.md]]
- **The ruling.** [[wiki/people/pavel.md|Pavel]]: "**Я за Linux. Я еще больше убедился в том, что надо Linux брать.**" Development time is roughly equal either way (~1.5–2 months per product once the board runs Linux), so the tie-breaker is developer availability + the existing modules, not price. Val's framing of price: the ~$30 BOM delta is "big" only because JVL applies a **5–10× markup to retail** — a ~$30 BOM difference becomes ~$600–1,000 at the customer — but it does not outweigh the resource argument. [[raw/flex-tower_flex-POS_foundational-decision_architecture-june-4-2025.md]]
- **Design frame — this is a financial system, not a gaming machine.** It must be **redundant, hot-swappable and modular**: one financial system per location, so if it fails every machine at that location stops until service. Service must take **minutes**, done by a non-technical **store owner / attendant** (no technician driving across Nebraska/Texas), by shipping a redundant module by courier and swapping it; a scanned new module must auto-rebind to the location in the CMS. Swappable modules is a top **design requirement**. [[raw/flex-tower_flex-POS_foundational-decision_architecture-june-4-2025.md]]
- **Next steps set.** Order **2–3 boards to Minsk and a couple to Toronto** immediately (Toronto arrives faster — "во вторник" vs a month to Minsk), with the FlexPort-integration engineer bringing up the vendor's **Ubuntu (18.04)** board-support image in Toronto; then write a **capabilities list** for all the products, build the overall **business architecture**, then per-device architecture, in parallel with **user-experience meetings** for each device (Val to facilitate; invite [[wiki/people/igor-nazarenka.md|Igor]] and possibly [[wiki/people/dmitri-chudinov.md|Dima Chudinov]] — if Dima is not needed the UX meeting can run in English). Target: both products **on-site testing by October**. [[raw/flex-tower_flex-POS_foundational-decision_architecture-june-4-2025.md]]

## Nov 5 2025 — production board is the Chinese Rockchip; software-update procedure defined

By November the platform is settled on the **Chinese Rockchip ARM board** in production, and the open question became **how to update the JVL software in the field**. On the Nov-5 requirements call ([[wiki/meetings/2025-11-05-arm-board-manufacturer-software-update-requirements.md]]; Val, [[wiki/people/genadi-peari.md|Genadi Peari]], [[wiki/people/pavel.md|Pavel Frolov]]):

- **Update Linux never; update only JVL software.** Rather than the board's full re-flash (USB cable + power-on button, or SD card), rework the **eMMC partitions** so a dedicated partition holds the JVL application, and add a **boot-time script** that pulls a firmware file off a USB stick and writes it at a fixed offset. [[wiki/people/evgeniy-alekseev.md|Evgeniy Alekseev]] to build it. [[raw/Requirements_ARM_board_manufacturer-november-5-2025.md]]
- **Demand full deliverables from the manufacturer** — documentation, the complete source/file-structure repository, and an initial two-monitor-capable boot image — so JVL can rebuild the image itself. [[raw/Requirements_ARM_board_manufacturer-november-5-2025.md]]
- **Own-board (STM) evolution deferred.** Building JVL's own board off an **STMicroelectronics** evaluation board is a **~3-month project at best** (and only if STM has a two-screen-capable chip, unknown), whereas JVL must ship a machine to a location soon — so it **stays with the Rockchip board** and defers any own-board evolution; STM's real **support** was noted as the reason it would be attractive later. [[raw/Requirements_ARM_board_manufacturer-november-5-2025.md]]

## Nov 11 2025 — update mechanism settled; supplier debate re-opened; STM/NXP re-weighed and dropped

On the Nov-11 POS + FlexPort schedule review ([[wiki/meetings/2025-11-11-pos-flexport-schedule-review-board-update.md]]; Val, [[wiki/people/pavel.md|Pavel Frolov]], [[wiki/people/evgeniy-alekseev.md|Evgeniy Alekseev]], [[wiki/people/eugine-yavorski.md|Eugine Yavorski]], [[wiki/people/genadi-peari.md|Genadi Peari]], 2h33m) the Nov-5 direction was pushed to a working conclusion, and the supplier question was re-argued at length.

- **The update model, settled.** Two requirements: (1) support **SD-card boot** so a fresh full image (Linux + applications + bootloader) can be laid over the board; (2) for day-to-day updates, a **higher-level boot-time script** checks whether an inserted USB stick carries a newer version and, if so, replaces **only the JVL application programs** (Java, browser, database, scripts) held on a **dedicated writable eMMC partition** — Linux is mounted **read-only** and never touched. [[raw/POS_and_PORT-schedule_review.md]]
- **Application update = the priority; USB and remote are the same manoeuvre.** [[wiki/people/pavel.md|Pavel]]'s framing: the highest-priority task for [[wiki/people/evgeniy-alekseev.md|Alekseev]] is to take the regular Linux image, insert the JVL software, and produce a writable image + a boot-time updater with a **backup/rollback** to the previous version if a flashed build fails to boot ("иначе… у нас кирпич" — otherwise a bricked terminal). Val insisted the product **must** support **remote/cloud update** — 1,000 locations, ~$30 per field shipment, gas-station clerks who cannot handle USB sticks make manual USB-per-location unrealistic — and was surprised to learn **FlexPay has no remote update today** (only **FlexPort** has remote update, still in progress, delivered onto FlexPay and pushed to Flex Sport over the **WSS** protocol). Resolution: same boot-time-script mechanism serves both USB and remote; remote-full-OS update stays out of scope (too risky). Val: keep it as an **MP/application** update built by Alekseev to avoid pulling **Dima or Sergei** off their FlexPos work. [[raw/POS_and_PORT-schedule_review.md]]
- **Supplier debate re-opened (Rockchip vs dev-board vs own board).** [[wiki/people/genadi-peari.md|Genadi]] is worn down dealing with ~50 Alibaba suppliers who variously won't support the screens/Ubuntu/Debian or push Android. Options weighed: buy a **finished board** and negotiate the deliverables (one supplier's answer was "order 1,000 boards, then we'll give you what you ask" — opening a quantity/price negotiation); buy from **development-kit** vendors (full sources + support + GitHub repos, but no finished product — you build your own hardware platform); or an **SoC-on-module + cheap interface board** approach (module carries processor/memory/wifi/SSD, a cheap carrier adds the connectors — needs only ~3 USB, 1 Ethernet, 2 display interfaces). Building JVL's **own board** = layout ~1 month + manufacture ~2 weeks ≈ **~3 months stuck**. [[raw/POS_and_PORT-schedule_review.md]]
- **Processor family comparison — Rockchip stays.** **Rockchip** on convenience + cost-efficiency is "hard to beat"; **STM (STMicroelectronics)** and **NXP i.MX** / **TI** Linux-capable families would run roughly **2× the cost** (the STM website was blocked for the team). Newer Rockchip parts noted: **RK3576** and **RK3588** (the latter AI-focused, with an NPU; the current part has a rudimentary NPU). Genadi's point that carried: once Alekseev builds the update structure once (~a week), it makes JVL **hardware-supplier-agnostic** — any Rockchip board can be adopted in "a couple of hours," so JVL is never locked to one manufacturer. The **own-board / STM evolution stays deferred** (as on Nov-5). [[raw/POS_and_PORT-schedule_review.md]]
- **Dima Pikulev, again ruled out.** Genadi mused that [[wiki/people/dima-pikulev.md|Dima Pikulev]] used to do exactly this board bring-up work; Val: "history proved we can't use Dima Pikulev… we don't need him," the requirements on such a specialist are minimal and Alekseev/Pavel can do it. [[raw/POS_and_PORT-schedule_review.md]]
