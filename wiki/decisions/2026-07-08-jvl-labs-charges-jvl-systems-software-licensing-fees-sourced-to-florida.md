# From 2026, JVL Labs charges JVL Systems software-licensing fees, sourced to Florida

Agreed on the [[wiki/meetings/2026-07-08-gilmore-bsp-intercompany-software-licensing-and-florida-nexus.md|July 8 2026 call]] with the external accountants. [[wiki/people/sean-riley.md|Sean Riley]] summed it up: "I think we have sort of a plan then. We're going to have the Canadian entity charging software licensing fees, Maybe even some head office fees down to and they'll be all allocated to the Florida state." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Gilmore_Cross_Border_Tax_Strategy-July-8-2026.md]]

## Why the old route ended

- **The old plan.** On an earlier call the accountants had said [[wiki/organizations/jvl-labs.md|JVL Labs]] should sell the equipment to [[wiki/organizations/jvl-systems.md|JVL Systems]] "at, you know, pretty high cost" to keep Systems' profit very low (Sean). [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Gilmore_Cross_Border_Tax_Strategy-July-8-2026.md]]
- **What changed.** "Canada is no longer making the machines shipping them down to the US." Systems "is now going to be generating some significant profits," while the losses and the head-office staff are in Canada (Sean). So the question became a management fee or office chargeback; Val added "Software licensing." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Gilmore_Cross_Border_Tax_Strategy-July-8-2026.md]]

## Why Florida, not Georgia

- **The Georgia risk (Mark).** Georgia sources sales to where the customer is, and treats even one dollar of sales as doing business there. Selling only tangible goods is protected by the federal law read out as "PL eighty two, seven, eighty, six, two, seven, two" (Public Law 86-272, by inference). Services do not get that protection, which is why the old plan was "Tangible goods, no services attached." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Gilmore_Cross_Border_Tax_Strategy-July-8-2026.md]]
- **The Florida rule (Mark).** Florida sources a service to where it is performed. If Labs performs the service in Canada, "Florida says it's not here." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Gilmore_Cross_Border_Tax_Strategy-July-8-2026.md]]
- **The facts that support it (Val).** "The product creation team resides physically in Florida." They "QA, they configure, they deploy and they manage uh service uh nationwide," and manage client relationships countrywide. "The development of the software is managed from Canada." Mark: so the software is used by the Florida team "exclusively" and developed from Canada; then Labs has no nexus in Florida and no more Georgia-sourced sales. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Gilmore_Cross_Border_Tax_Strategy-July-8-2026.md]]
- Val also stopped the room from agreeing too fast. When Inna said Florida gets the benefit "One hundred percent without a doubt", Val said: "Let's just ignore what Tina said for a second because I I like to doubt things… let the specialist talk." ("Tina" is a transcript rendering; by context he means Inna.) [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Gilmore_Cross_Border_Tax_Strategy-July-8-2026.md]]

## What the fee is worth — not settled

- **Arm's length is the test (Mark).** For any related-party charge "you are going to want to do... is to charge an arm's length price." Mark said he is not qualified to price a software licence. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Gilmore_Cross_Border_Tax_Strategy-July-8-2026.md]]
- **Val's two anchors.** (1) Cost: "my immediate cost for producing this software is two million dollars", and what counts is "what it costs me on a yearly basis to keep on producing and supporting the software." (2) A competitor: from a published New York Stock Exchange statement of a competitor transcribed as "Valley Gaming" (name as transcribed; not verified), Val said they charge "three thousand dollars per m achine" for what "they call it soft cost", on "let's call it ten thousand machines per year" — "that's thirty million dollars." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Gilmore_Cross_Border_Tax_Strategy-July-8-2026.md]]
- **Sean's caution.** Recovering the full development cost from one customer "might not necessarily be market rate"; the tax authorities would ask what the market charges. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Gilmore_Cross_Border_Tax_Strategy-July-8-2026.md]]
- **A transfer-pricing study.** Mark: the IRS says a formal study should be on file (not submitted), done by economists, not CPAs. At minimum keep documented arm's-length support, such as the competitor figure. Val: "I am in support of this suggestion and I will implement it." Sean: one study can serve both Canada and the US, and the CRA accepts other documentation more readily than the IRS. Mark named the firm he uses: "McGuire Sponsel", out of Indianapolis. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Gilmore_Cross_Border_Tax_Strategy-July-8-2026.md]]

## Side points agreed on the call

- **No withholding tax** on software or management fees, in Mark's view: under the treaty "Ze ro percent". [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Gilmore_Cross_Border_Tax_Strategy-July-8-2026.md]]
- **Filing for Labs in the US:** only the treaty-based federal return ("the eleven twenty F with a treaty"), which needs gross sales to US customers. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Gilmore_Cross_Border_Tax_Strategy-July-8-2026.md]]
- **Later, the profit may move back.** Val: as the losses get used up, "We could shift the profit then back to the US because there's definitely tax benefits." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Gilmore_Cross_Border_Tax_Strategy-July-8-2026.md]]
