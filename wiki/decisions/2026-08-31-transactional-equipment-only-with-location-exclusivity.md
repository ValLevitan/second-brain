# Transactional equipment only where JVL is exclusive in the location

[[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]]'s answer, worked out aloud at the [[wiki/meetings/2026-08-31-dixie-amusement-kiosk-wait-and-see.md|Aug-31 2026 Dixie Amusement visit]], to a problem [[wiki/people/salim-khambawala.md|Salim Khambawala]] had just dismissed as somebody else's: **what happens when a location does not pay its prize-card bill.** [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## The problem, as Val framed it

- **JVL already knew the non-payment was real.** "**I spoke to [InComm], and [InComm] is complaining that a lot of people are not paying them.** So let's say [a] location doesn't pay. **How do you deal with it? How do you enforce it?**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **Salim's position was that it is not his problem and not the distributor's** — the location contracts directly with [[wiki/organizations/incom.md|InComm]], InComm sets the credit limit, and non-payment kills the location's credit, not the master's. Detail on [[wiki/organizations/dixie-amusement.md]]. He offered JVL the same logic in reverse: "**It's like JVL. And as a master licensee, I buy something on credit from you, and I don't pay. It's between you and me.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **Val's counter is a size-of-claim argument, and it is the core of the decision.** "**the difference is that when you buy from me, each machine is let's say fifteen thousand dollars. You buy five machines. That's seventy-five thousand dollars**… **for seventy-five thousand dollars, I can go to court, and even if it takes me two years, seventy-five thousand dollars is worth it. It's worth my time.**" Against that: "**when [a] location defaults, let's say for validating four cards at five hundred dollars each. That's two thousand dollars. So a distributor makes for each card one dollar. So four cards. The dealer made four dollars, but he cannot collect two thousand.** So for two thousand, would he go to court? **It's going to cost you more to go to court. But plus you only made four dollars.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **And the exposure is automatic, not optional.** "**I have a contract with them. Automatically, they sen[d] me a bill two days later. Suck my money out… out of my bank account, automatically… The only thing that I get to keep is one dollar per card. Thousands, thousands of dollars**" — against a maximum of "**a thousand dollars**" of value per card. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## What was decided

**JVL will place transactional equipment only where it is the only way to validate a card or play a machine, and where it retains a remote shut-off.** Val's own statement of it: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

> "for us, the only way for us to do it, one way, is **if we are exclusive in location, and we can shut off the equipment**. So we can't shut off a machine. But **we can shut off the POS, the kiosk**. But we have to be exclusive in the location. **They cannot be another way of validating cards or playing.**"

The mechanics he spelled out, in order: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

1. **MudaGoose comes out.** "First of all, you remove. **You must remove.**"
2. **JVL rewires the location to itself.** "when you remove MudaGoose, **we rewire the system to ourselves**."
3. **A JVL point-of-sale replaces PAX.** "we have a point of sale, **a little point of sale device that replaces [PAX], but it's more elegant**."
4. **Flex ports go inside the machines.** "**we add the flex ports inside of the machine**."
5. **Both are remotely killable, which is the whole point.** "**this way we shut down the port and we shut down the point of sale. You cannot issue the card, and you cannot play the machine.** But even if you can play the machine, you cannot issue a payout. **So then it's pointless. Nobody's going to play the machine. Then [the] location will deposit money and pay the bill.**"

**And the reason it has to be this or nothing:** "**What do we make? One dollar per transaction. We don't want to be in that business.**" Salim did not argue: "**Nobody wants to be in that** [business]. The way others are doing, they are saying the payment and creditors let them deal directly." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## The commercial premise underneath it

Val stated, flatly and unprompted, why the kiosk itself is not the business: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

- "**But how do we make money? Selling kiosks. There's no money in selling kiosks. You cannot make money.**"
- "**first of all, we don't believe in kiosks. We're showing kiosks, but we don't think that people should be buying kiosks. There's no value in** [them]… **even that technology is changing now.**"
- "**The value is in recurring revenue. This is where the value is. Unless we can get recurring revenue, there's no point for us.**"

He also rejected the broker role on the way to the decision: "**We're not. We make equipment.** So for us, what do we do with the equipment? Do we sell it to you? **We can't sell it to [a] location. Do we operate? Do we become… broker… for [InComm]?**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

Salim's independent version of the same instinct — refuse the per-card cut, keep the risk with whoever takes the reward — is on [[wiki/people/salim-khambawala.md]]: "**You take the risk. You take the reward. You take everything. Just give me that API to process.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## How this sits against JVL's other rulings

- **The remote shut-off is not new — it hardened into a requirement two days later.** On Sept 2 2026 Val called it "a hard requirement" that JVL must be able to disconnect and freeze a non-paying location remotely; this Aug-31 conversation is the customer-facing reasoning that produced it. See [[wiki/topics/flexpay-business-architecture-payout-devices.md]] (Sept 2 2026).
- **Exclusivity is a harder condition than anything JVL had committed to publicly.** The transactional suite as pitched to [[wiki/organizations/narayani-amusements.md|Narayani]] (Aug 26) and [[wiki/organizations/halcyon-amusements.md|Halcyon]] (Aug 27) was offered to sit **alongside** MudaGoose — the Halcyon Flex-IQ pilot offer was explicitly "IQ working in [MudaGoose] for three months." Nothing in this source says the earlier offers are withdrawn, and the two are not reconciled here.

## Open questions

- **Will any Georgia location accept exclusivity?** Salim's own locations run PAX + MudaGoose + Fiery Pay in parallel precisely so a declined transaction has a fallback, and he described operators as unwilling to give up dual setups — "**the operators want to keep their dual setups.**" Nothing in this source tests whether the exclusivity condition is sellable. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **Does this supersede the alongside-MudaGoose pilot offers made Aug 26–27, or only govern the paid product?** Not addressed in either source. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

**§15.8:** clear — per-card distributor margins, machine prices and credit exposure only; no named-person compensation.
