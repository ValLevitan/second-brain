# Transactional equipment only where JVL is exclusive in the location

[[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]]'s answer, worked out aloud at the [[wiki/meetings/2026-08-31-dixie-amusement-kiosk-wait-and-see.md|Aug-31 2026 Dixie Amusement visit]], to a problem [[wiki/people/salim-khambawala.md|Salim Khambawala]] had just dismissed as somebody else's: **what happens when a location does not pay its prize-card bill.** [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## The problem, as Val framed it

- **JVL already knew the non-payment was real.** "**I spoke to [InComm], and [InComm] is complaining that a lot of people are not paying them.** So let's say [a] location doesn't pay. **How do you deal with it? How do you enforce it?**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **Salim's position was that it is not his problem and not the distributor's** — the location contracts directly with [[wiki/organizations/incom.md|InComm]], InComm sets the credit limit, and non-payment kills the location's credit, not the master's. Detail on [[wiki/organizations/dixie-amusement.md]]. He offered JVL the same logic in reverse: "**It's like JVL. And as a master licensee, I buy something on credit from you, and I don't pay. It's between you and me.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **Val's counter is a size-of-claim argument, and it is the core of the decision.** "**the difference is that when you buy from me, each machine is let's say fifteen thousand dollars. You buy five machines. That's seventy-five thousand dollars**… **for seventy-five thousand dollars, I can go to court, and even if it takes me two years, seventy-five thousand dollars is worth it. It's worth my time.**" Against that: "**when [a] location defaults, let's say for validating four cards at five hundred dollars each. That's two thousand dollars. So a distributor makes for each card one dollar. So four cards. The dealer made four dollars, but he cannot collect two thousand.** So for two thousand, would he go to court? **It's going to cost you more to go to court. But plus you only made four dollars.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **And the exposure is automatic, not optional.** "**I have a contract with them. Automatically, they sen[d] me a bill two days later. Suck my money out… out of my bank account, automatically… The only thing that I get to keep is one dollar per card. Thousands, thousands of dollars**" — against a maximum of "**a thousand dollars**" of value per card. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## What was decided

**JVL will place transactional equipment only where it is the only way to validate a card or play a machine, and where it retains a remote shut-off.** Val's own statement of it: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

> "for us, the only way for us to do it, one way, is **if we are exclusive in location, and we can shut off the equipment**. So we can't shut off a machine. But **we can shut off the POS, the kiosk**. But we have to be exclusive in the location. **They cannot be another way of validating cards or playing.**"

The mechanics he spelled out, in order: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

1. **MudaGoose comes out.** "First of all, you remove. **You must remove.**"
2. **JVL rewires the location to itself.** "when you remove MudaGoose, **we rewire the system to ourselves**."
3. **A JVL point-of-sale replaces PAX.** "we have a point of sale, **a little point of sale device that replaces [PAX], but it's more elegant**."
4. **Flex ports go inside the machines.** "**we add the flex ports inside of the machine**."
5. **Both are remotely killable, which is the whole point.** "**this way we shut down the port and we shut down the point of sale. You cannot issue the card, and you cannot play the machine.** But even if you can play the machine, you cannot issue a payout. **So then it's pointless. Nobody's going to play the machine. Then [the] location will deposit money and pay the bill.**"

**And the reason it has to be this or nothing:** "**What do we make? One dollar per transaction. We don't want to be in that business.**" Salim did not argue: "**Nobody wants to be in that** [business]. The way others are doing, they are saying the payment and creditors let them deal directly." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## The commercial premise underneath it

Val stated, flatly and unprompted, why the kiosk itself is not the business: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

- "**But how do we make money? Selling kiosks. There's no money in selling kiosks. You cannot make money.**"
- "**first of all, we don't believe in kiosks. We're showing kiosks, but we don't think that people should be buying kiosks. There's no value in** [them]… **even that technology is changing now.**"
- "**The value is in recurring revenue. This is where the value is. Unless we can get recurring revenue, there's no point for us.**"

He also rejected the broker role on the way to the decision: "**We're not. We make equipment.** So for us, what do we do with the equipment? Do we sell it to you? **We can't sell it to [a] location. Do we operate? Do we become… broker… for [InComm]?**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

Salim's independent version of the same instinct — refuse the per-card cut, keep the risk with whoever takes the reward — is on [[wiki/people/salim-khambawala.md]]: "**You take the risk. You take the reward. You take everything. Just give me that API to process.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## How this sits against JVL's other rulings

- **The remote shut-off is not new — it hardened into a requirement two days later.** On Sept 2 2026 Val called it "a hard requirement" that JVL must be able to disconnect and freeze a non-paying location remotely; this Aug-31 conversation is the customer-facing reasoning that produced it. See [[wiki/topics/flexpay-business-architecture-payout-devices.md]] (Sept 2 2026).
- **Exclusivity is a harder condition than anything JVL had committed to publicly.** The transactional suite as pitched to [[wiki/organizations/narayani-amusements.md|Narayani]] (Aug 26) and [[wiki/organizations/halcyon-amusements.md|Halcyon]] (Aug 27) was offered to sit **alongside** MudaGoose — the Halcyon Flex-IQ pilot offer was explicitly "IQ working in [MudaGoose] for three months." Nothing in this source says the earlier offers are withdrawn, and the two are not reconciled here.

## Sept 1 2026 — dated evidence, one day later, on both sides of the open question

At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-01-double-down-gaming-visit.md|Sept-1 Double Down Gaming visit]] Val pitched the same equipment to another Georgia master and said two things that pull in opposite directions. Recorded as evidence; **nothing here is treated as resolving either open question below.**

**(a) The threat this decision exists to answer was stated again, in fuller form, to a customer.** Val walked [[wiki/people/ryan-odonnell.md|Ryan O'Donnell]] through what happens when a location adds a second validation device in parallel with MudaGoose: the location holds each supplier in turn, "**Now they have three suppliers. None of them really get paid on time, and they manipulate each one against each other.**" He said it is already happening — "**We're in close touch with [InComm], and they're already complaining that people that have [PAX] are not paying**… Because they have [Fiery Pay], they have [Access], in addition to [PAX]." He introduced it as "**one important caveat, and I need you to start thinking about it.**" The full passage is on [[wiki/topics/georgia-prize-card-processor-economics.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]

**(b) The offer he actually made was explicitly non-exclusive — a drop-on-top of MudaGoose, with the operator choosing.** In the same meeting the positioning was "**Lego bricks**": [[wiki/people/andrei-klimovich.md|Andrei Klimovich]] presented the first step as "**a drop on top of a [MudaGoose]**… a very simple solution… **so it works with [MudaGoose]. Later, if you're ready to upgrade it, you upgrade it with ports and you get replayability. And later, card dispenser.**" Val made the same point as a principle: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]

> "**the only limitation of our system is the source of your imagination. It is how you wish to run your location**… You want to run your location with bill acceptors, then you cannot have a recycler… If you don't want the recycler, if you want more mechanical attendants… **You don't need to change anything. You just add that thing.** And you're in business."

- And, in the same breath: "**We're not saying that you should go with this solution or with that solution. We're just telling you that by coming with us, whatever solution you think you need, we will cater to that solution. We're absolutely decision agnostic.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]
- **The one hard removal Val did insist on is the validator, and the reason is mechanical, not commercial:** "**If you are going with this solution, you must remove your validator. Otherwise, there is no refill**" — i.e. the recycler cannot fund its own payouts if cash is also going into a separate bill acceptor. That is a different requirement from removing a competitor's POS. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]

So one day after this ruling, the customer-facing offer still runs **alongside** MudaGoose and PAX, exactly as the Aug-26/27 Narayani and Halcyon pitches did — while the reasoning behind the ruling was stated to the customer more fully than it had been to Salim. Neither source says the exclusivity condition has been withdrawn, relaxed, or applied. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]

## Sept 1 2026 (afternoon) — two more customers, two more non-exclusive offers, and the first evidence that removal IS sellable

Two further visits the same day ([[wiki/meetings/2026-09-01-narayani-akshar-mega-hit-demo-and-trade-in.md|Narayani/Akshar, ~1:05 PM]] and [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-01-air-gaming-group-transactional-walkthrough.md|Air Gaming Group, ~3:00 PM]]) add dated evidence on both open questions. **Nothing here resolves either one.**

**(a) The offer made to both customers was again explicitly non-exclusive.**

- To Air Gaming, Flex-IQ was positioned as the entry rung *for a MudaGoose location*, not as its replacement. The visitor asked directly, "**Is there any way to eliminate the MudaGoose?**" — Val: "**Yeah**" — and [[wiki/people/andrei-klimovich.md|Andrei]]'s framing of the device in the same breath was "**because it's a quick solution for locations with MudaGoose. We need just to comply with regulation.**" Removal is offered as a later rung, not a condition of entry. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Air_Gaming_Group_Transactional_Walkthrough-September-1-2026.md]]
- To Narayani/Akshar, the post-show bundle was offered for the customer's **existing mixed fleet**, whoever made it: "**We'll give you six of these units plus a terminal for whatever machines you have**," with JVL supplying brackets and the customer installing. Nothing about exclusivity, removal, or shut-off was raised in either meeting. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Narayani_Amusements_Mega_Hit_Demo_and_Trade_In-September-1-2026.md]]

**(b) But an operator described a competing vendor already enforcing full removal — and his locations accepted it.** This is the first evidence in the corpus that a Georgia route will tolerate having MudaGoose taken out. [[wiki/people/neel.md|Neel]] of [[wiki/organizations/akshar-gaming.md|Akshar Gaming]], on the card system already installed across his locations: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Narayani_Amusements_Mega_Hit_Demo_and_Trade_In-September-1-2026.md]]

> Val: "Do they remove MudaGoose?" — "**They completely remove.**" — Val: "They completely remove MudaGoose and they run their own wires." — "**Because if the MudaGoose is connected, then once the customer hits collect on it, the money goes to the card as well, and it prints a ticket at the goose too. So it confuses the location where to pay. So they remove the goose completely.**"

**The reason is mechanical, not commercial** — double payouts and a confused cashier, not credit enforcement or a shut-off lever. So it shows the *removal* is sellable; it does **not** show that Val's exclusivity-plus-remote-shut-off condition is sellable, and it is recorded on that basis. Note also the shape of the competing deal: about **$5,000 one-time for six machines** including POS, card readers and player cards, then **$13.30 per machine per month** — a recurring-revenue model of exactly the kind this decision says JVL needs. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Narayani_Amusements_Mega_Hit_Demo_and_Trade_In-September-1-2026.md]]

## Sept 2 2026 — the condition stated to the processor, a regulatory cost nobody had named, and a fourth non-exclusive customer offer

Two meetings on Sept 2 bear on this ruling from opposite ends. **Neither resolves either open question below.**

**(a) Val put the exclusivity-plus-shut-off design to [[wiki/organizations/incom.md|InComm]] itself, as the fix for InComm's own collection problem** ([[wiki/meetings/2026-09-02-incomm-machine-payments-and-recycler-standoff.md|the lunch]]). His argument: while a location keeps a parallel system, nobody has leverage — "as long as there is another system that allows for location to function, **you cannot really shut down anything**… right now you have [MudaGoose] in the location, so as long as that ticket gets printed, **you are out of power**." His proposal, unchanged from Aug 31: "**JVL would remove [MudaGoose] from a location. Run its own wires, so if location doesn't pay, we can shut down the terminal.**" And its point: "Doesn't that solve your problem?… your problem is that you're not getting paid. You process two thousand dollars worth of activation fees and you're not getting paid" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_InComm_Machine_Payments_and_ATM_Recycler_Standoff-September-2-2026.md]].

**The new fact is what it costs in regulatory terms — and it lands on the customer, not on JVL.** InComm's reply: "**you may get an angry letter from the lottery about that**. But yes, you would. That would be your decision, not mine… The lottery is weird. **You landed upon one of the very few things they will enforce, which is keeping the games on.** And ultimately, they probably wouldn't cite you. **They would probably cite the MLH** — they would cite that operator if the equipment was shut down." Val's refinement, which InComm accepted ("That's right"): "the interesting thing is that **the equipment is on. They just can't process the wins. Just the ticket out**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_InComm_Machine_Payments_and_ATM_Recycler_Standoff-September-2-2026.md]].

That is the first source in the corpus to say the shut-off lever exposes the **master licence holder** to citation. It does not say the lever is unusable; it says the penalty falls on the operator JVL is selling to — which is a harder thing to sell than the Aug-31 reasoning anticipated.

**(b) The same morning, the offer to a customer was again non-exclusive — but with removal built into the giveaway.** At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-02-lucky-coin-service-and-upgrade-offer.md|Lucky Coin visit]] Val offered six free ports and a terminal per location ([[wiki/decisions/2026-09-02-free-ports-and-pos-with-every-machine-bought.md]]) to an operator whose locations run **EasyLink + GBT kiosks and PAX terminals**, and said nothing about exclusivity, shut-off or non-payment. What he did say is that the free hardware displaces the incumbent as a by-product: "your location will become fully equipped. **It will make you remove your [MudaGoose]. It will replace [it] with this**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Lucky_Coin_Service_and_Upgrade_Offer-September-2-2026.md]].

So the pattern from Sept 1 holds for a fourth customer in two days: **removal is sold as a benefit; exclusivity is not asked for.** Recorded as evidence; the ruling is neither applied nor withdrawn in either source.

## Sept 2 2026 (afternoon) — a fifth non-exclusive offer, and a competitor already billing the location directly

At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-02-continental-bifurcated-operations-and-vendor-lock-in.md|Continental visit]] that afternoon the pattern held for a fifth customer in three days, and a rival's commercial model turned up that answers the same problem from the opposite direction. **Neither resolves either open question below.** [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Continental_Vendor_Lock_In_Dead_End-September-2-2026.md]]

**(a) Exclusivity, removal and shut-off were never raised.** [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] quoted the suite to a man running **Access on ~75% of his estate** and **MudaGoose on everything outside Georgia** — "our system is a thousand dollars per machine… **A thousand dollars for the POS**… **We charge ninety dollars per month**" per location — with no condition attached, no MudaGoose removal asked for, and no mention of non-payment or a remote kill. He positioned JVL *beside* the incumbent, not in place of it: "**We're exactly like Access, just a little more expensive.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Continental_Vendor_Lock_In_Dead_End-September-2-2026.md]]

**(b) Val gave a retail price on purpose, and said why.** This is the first source in the corpus where he names a two-tier posture out loud: "**Access gave you a wholesale price. I'm giving you a retail price. I'm giving you retail because you're not my customer.** But of course, when push comes to shove, and you would like to buy, we can sit down and talk." It bears on this ruling because the premise underneath it — the value is recurring revenue, not the box — assumes a customer relationship this one does not have. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Continental_Vendor_Lock_In_Dead_End-September-2-2026.md]]

**(c) The new fact is a competitor that solves the collection problem without exclusivity — by billing the location instead of the operator.** The ruling above exists because JVL earns a dollar a card while carrying the location's default risk. **Novomatic** avoids that shape entirely: **$210 per month per location for 36 months, then $150**, no equipment to buy except printers, and — the part the operator singled out — "**that two hundred and ten dollars doesn't come from my pocket. It comes directly from the sales point**… **Because they build** [bill] **directly to them.**" The location accepts because the replay function fixes its own Georgia Lottery reporting problem. Every location of his without Access is being moved onto it: "**all my locations that they don't have access to, I push them to Novomatics.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Continental_Vendor_Lock_In_Dead_End-September-2-2026.md]]

That is a **third possible answer** to the question this decision answers — alongside "be exclusive and keep a kill switch" (Val, Aug 31) and "let the location contract directly with the processor and keep the risk off the master" ([[wiki/people/salim-khambawala.md|Salim Khambawala]], the same day). **No source records JVL considering it**, so it is recorded as competitive evidence, not as an option under review. Pricing detail on [[wiki/topics/flexpay-business-architecture-payout-devices.md]] (Sept 2, Continental). [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Continental_Vendor_Lock_In_Dead_End-September-2-2026.md]]

## Sept 2 2026 (evening) — the mirror image: exclusivity demanded OF JVL, and Val called it "a big ask"

The same evening ([[wiki/meetings/2026-09-02-billy-liu-playpay-partnership-deep-dive.md|the PlayPay deep-dive]]) the exclusivity question arrived from the other direction for the first time in the corpus. **This is a different kind of exclusivity from the one this ruling is about** — a *supplier* asking JVL to carry only his product, not JVL asking a location to carry only JVL's — and it is recorded here because Val's own answer, given as the buyer rather than the seller, is the closest thing the corpus has to his view of what an exclusivity demand is worth. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Billy_Liu_PlayPay_Partnership_Deep_Dive-September-2-2026.md]]

[[wiki/people/billy-liu.md|Billy Liu]] of [[wiki/organizations/play-payments.md|Play Payments]], attaching his condition to Val's partnership proposal: "**You gonna think about it? It'll be an exclusive thing, right? You're not gonna offer [InComm] out of those things. You're not gonna dispense a physical card.**"

**Val's answer:** "**But I don't know. This is a big ask. Maybe even impossible.**"

- **His reason is the same one that makes locations resist him** — the need to be able to say yes to everything at the door: "**I want to be able to walk into a location and say, 'I do this, and I do this,' and it doesn't cost you anything**… Otherwise, **the location has a choice**" and will go elsewhere. Which is, from the other side of the table, precisely the objection [[wiki/people/salim-khambawala.md|Salim Khambawala]] raised against removing anything — "the operators want to keep their dual setups" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Billy_Liu_PlayPay_Partnership_Deep_Dive-September-2-2026.md]].
- **Billy's counter was that exclusivity costs JVL nothing if the cash problem is solved:** "**If you're dispensing cash, why do you need to dispense anything else?**"; "**Cash is what everybody wants.**" Val did not accept it and Billy conceded only "**I got it. There might be some pushback on it**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Billy_Liu_PlayPay_Partnership_Deep_Dive-September-2-2026.md]].
- **Nothing here withdraws, relaxes or applies the location-exclusivity condition this page records**, and it was not raised in that meeting at all. It is logged as evidence about how Val reasons about exclusivity, and the substance of the proposal it was attached to is on [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-02-val-proposes-fifty-percent-of-play-payments.md]].

## Sept 8 2026 — the shut-off lever put to a master licensee, and refused to Val's face

Eight days after this ruling was reasoned out with him, [[wiki/people/salim-khambawala.md|Salim Khambawala]] was asked the operative question directly at the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-08-dixie-amusement-bundle-offer-and-card-economics.md|Sept-8 Dixie visit]] — and said no. **This is the first source in the corpus in which an operator is asked to accept the remote shut-off and declines.** [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

**Val's question, put as a hypothetical about himself:** "let's say **I'm a broker, and it's my equipment and my location, and a location owes me two thousand eight hundred dollars. I cannot swipe their account** because they don't put enough cash in. They basically say I don't have that. **Can I shut down my terminal?** And what would an operator say?" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

**Salim's answer, twice:** "**I won't like it because it's not mine** [fault]"; and, when Val pressed that the money was really fronted on the operator's behalf — "It's still your operation, your money. The payout was made" — "**But as a master licensee, I won't like it.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

**And his reason is the one that makes it worse than a commercial objection:** shutting the device off is itself evidence in the live legal argument about whether the transactional device is part of the machine. "A, B, C. **C and A are connected, but the equipment is B's**… that's why we call these **lottery still in that gray area, muddy area. That is this part of the equipment or it's not part of it**… **if it's a part of the equipment, they are the regulator, so not only us not liking it, even lottery won't like it.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

**Val had no alternative to offer, and said so:** "Maybe nobody likes it, but **if the guy is not paying, somehow all of us should enforce it. So how do you enforce it?**… **Otherwise, I don't know how to enforce it.**" Salim's closing word on it was about who tries: "**If you, as a distributor, start enforcing it, they're not going to.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

**This compounds, rather than repeats, the Sept-2 cost.** On Sept 2 [[wiki/organizations/incom.md|InComm]] said the Lottery would cite the **master licence holder** if the equipment went dark. On Sept 8 the master licence holder, unprompted and without having heard that, gives the same reason for refusing. Two independent sources, six days apart, now put the cost of the lever on the customer JVL needs to sell it to. Neither resolves the open question below; both narrow it in the same direction. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_InComm_Machine_Payments_and_ATM_Recycler_Standoff-September-2-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

**And the offer made that same afternoon was, for a sixth customer, non-exclusive.** The [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-08-the-bundle-put-to-dixie-with-installation-explicitly-excluded.md|bundle put to Dixie]] carries no exclusivity condition, no removal requirement and no mention of a shut-off; **MudaGoose removal comes up only as an installation-cost question** ("You will remove your MudaGoose from the location, and you need to do the wiring. Who does the wiring?"), not as a term. The pattern from Sept 1–2 holds unbroken. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

## Sept 9 2026 — the clause named, and the reason restated as control rather than function

At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-09-october-rollout-staging-and-unit-ramp.md|Sept 9 rollout meeting]] a colleague asked the obvious engineering question — "[w]hy would we install pay and IQ in the same [location]?" — and [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]]'s answer was about lock-out, not utility [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_October_Rollout_Staging_and_Unit_Ramp-September-9-2026.md]]:

> "I believe that there will be a need for that, and **I don't want anybody else occupying with their equipment or offering their equipment to an attendant**. I don't want attendant to say, 'Well, you know what? I'm going to keep my [PAX], but I'm going to keep this.' I want to **sign exclusive agreement with each location. Exclusive, and I want to have a non-disturbance clause. A lot of legal mumbo-jumbo that neither you nor operator nor location will pay attention to. But when it comes to enforcement, I want to have all the aces in my deck.**"

Three things this adds to the Aug-31 ruling:

- **The instrument is named for the first time: an exclusive agreement per location plus a non-disturbance clause.** Earlier sources state the *requirement* of exclusivity; this is the first to name the contractual mechanism [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_October_Rollout_Staging_and_Unit_Ramp-September-9-2026.md]].
- **Val's own read of the paperwork's value:** nobody will read it, but it decides who wins at enforcement time [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_October_Rollout_Staging_and_Unit_Ramp-September-9-2026.md]].
- **The screens are reserved to JVL too, even for information.** "Even for informational screens, I want [this]" — the sentence is cut off mid-thought in the transcript and completed by another speaker ("[t]his to be available for the other"), so the wording is partial; the derived notes read it as Val wanting the screens reserved to JVL even for informational use [[raw/Meeting_Notes_October_Rollout_Staging_and_Unit_Ramp-September-9-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_October_Rollout_Staging_and_Unit_Ramp-September-9-2026.md]].

*(Note: the deployment that would put both devices in one location is **not available in the first iteration** — see [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-09-one-transactional-unit-per-location-until-the-wi-fi-iteration.md]]. Val is describing the end state he is contracting for, not October.)*

## Sept 9 2026 (customers) — the condition is finally ASKED for, twice in one day, in two different shapes

Nine days of customer meetings had gone by without the condition being put to anybody. On **Sept 9 2026** it was put to two counterparties three hours apart — **the first time in the corpus that exclusivity appears as a term of a live offer rather than as internal reasoning.** Neither meeting resolves either open question below; both add dated evidence, and they pull in slightly different directions because **they ask for two different things**.

**(a) 10:04 AM, to a distributor: exactly the Aug-31 condition, with the Aug-31 reason, and a three-year contract on top.** At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-09-rao-machine-performance-and-exclusivity-offer.md|Rao meeting]] [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] stated the reasoning almost word for word as he had worked it out with Salim: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Rao_Machine_Performance_and_Exclusivity_Offer-September-9-2026.md]]

> "we're definitely not going with [MudaGoose]. **Because if there is another system present in location, we will never get paid. We can't collect our money. We have to be able to be exclusive in the location.**"

And the instrument, for the second time after the Sept-9 8:43 AM internal statement above: "**we have to have three-year contract with the location or with the operator**" — refined to the location, "**and even they sign, otherwise we cannot collect. I mean, we give in so much away that somehow somehow we need to collect it back**." He also asked the distributor himself for **two years of undivided attention** and an end to his Access sales. Full terms on [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-09-rao-takes-the-bundle-with-jvl-exclusive-in-the-location.md]].

- **The first operator-side read of whether the contract is signable, and it is half negative.** [[wiki/people/narasimha-rao-namburi.md|Rao]]: "**Operator is not going to sign. Location may sign.**" That is the opposite split from what the Sept-2 InComm and Sept-8 Salim evidence implies — those two put the *regulatory* cost of the shut-off on the master licence holder, which is a reason for the **operator** to refuse; Rao says the **operator** refuses and the **location** might sign. **No source reconciles them.** [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Rao_Machine_Performance_and_Exclusivity_Offer-September-9-2026.md]]
- **And the same counterparty who said "I'm in" warned that removal cannot be forced.** "**I cannot force access to go away. It's not going to be that simple. It will happen organically.**" Val: "**Agree.**" So exclusivity was accepted as a *term* by a man who immediately said he cannot deliver it on a schedule — which is evidence about sellability, not a test of it. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Rao_Machine_Performance_and_Exclusivity_Offer-September-9-2026.md]]

**(b) 1:31 PM, to an operator: a different exclusivity — the whole account — with no shut-off and no removal mentioned.** At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-09-lucky-coin-service-burden-and-exclusive-partnership.md|Lucky Coin shop visit]] Val asked [[wiki/people/kyle-shepherd.md|Kyle Shepherd]] for "**an exclusive business with you, where I have your entire business**," with "**one machine on every location**" — and **nothing about being the only system in a location, nothing about removing a competitor, nothing about a remote shut-off, no non-payment discussion and no contract term.** [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Lucky_Coin_Service_Burden_and_Exclusive_Partnership-September-9-2026.md]]

- **It is account-level, not location-level**, and therefore does not test this ruling's condition at all — a route that buys only JVL machines is not the same as a location where no other card can be validated.
- **Kyle did not refuse it; he deferred it**, on his technicians' testing fatigue rather than on exclusivity: "if I tell these guys we're testing something else, **they're gonna be outside my office with pitchforks and lanterns**." Answer due **Tuesday Sept 15 2026**. Terms on [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-09-val-asks-lucky-coin-for-its-entire-business.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Lucky_Coin_Service_Burden_and_Exclusive_Partnership-September-9-2026.md]]
- **He also floated the exact thing exclusivity forbids, and Val let it stand:** "Maybe maybe I can talk with [them] at **one or two other places**." Val: "**You could try one. You could try zero. You could try all of them.**" *(The clause is garbled; the derived notes read it as testing alongside one or two other places.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Lucky_Coin_Service_Burden_and_Exclusive_Partnership-September-9-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Lucky_Coin_Service_Burden_and_Exclusive_Partnership-September-9-2026.md]]

**Net effect on the two open questions: narrowed, not answered.** The condition has now been *asked for* once (Rao) and *accepted as a term* by one counterparty who cannot promise delivery; a second, larger customer was asked for a different exclusivity entirely and has not yet answered. **Nothing withdraws, relaxes or applies the ruling.**

## Sept 10 2026 — the condition stated as a flat term to a tenth counterparty, and the third independent "the Lottery won't like it"

At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-10-diamond-amusements-machine-performance-and-bundle-pilot.md|Sept-10 Diamond Amusement visit]] Val stated the condition **as a bare term of the offer**, with no reasoning attached at all — the shortest form it has ever taken — and then, separately, put the non-payment question to the operator and got the fullest operator-side answer in the corpus. **Neither open question below is resolved.** [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Diamond_Amusements_Machine_Performance_and_Transactional_Bundle_Pilot-September-10-2026.md]]

**(a) The condition, as a term.** Tacked onto the end of the bundle terms, after the $90/month and the operator-intelligence caveat:

> "**and one more thing, we want exclusivity in the location. Nothing else can operate there. As far as transactional.**"

[[wiki/people/sean-fellows.md|Sean Fellows]]'s whole recorded response is "**Yeah, no**" — which the source does not disambiguate and **this page does not read either way.** No removal of a competitor was asked for, no shut-off was named at this point, and no contract term was mentioned. **This is the second time the in-location condition has been put to a master licensee as a live term** (after Rao the distributor on Sept 9), and the first time it has been stated with no justification offered. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Diamond_Amusements_Machine_Performance_and_Transactional_Bundle_Pilot-September-10-2026.md]]

**(b) The enforcement half came minutes later, and it is the Aug-31 reasoning repeated almost verbatim.** Val: "**we act as a broker. For card clearance, so we front our money… If the location doesn't pay us, we're out of money. So we swipe [the] location through an ACH** automatically, but the location could say, '**I don't have money**'… **What do you suggest we do?**" — and the per-card margin against it, again: "**let's say you issue ten cards a day. So I made ten dollars per day.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Diamond_Amusements_Machine_Performance_and_Transactional_Bundle_Pilot-September-10-2026.md]]

**(c) A second master licensee refuses the backstop — and gives the same regulatory reason as InComm and Salim, independently.** Full record on [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-10-diamond-will-not-guarantee-card-clearance-debt.md]]. The exchange that matters here: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Diamond_Amusements_Machine_Performance_and_Transactional_Bundle_Pilot-September-10-2026.md]]

> Sean: "**here's the problem: lotteries, lottery. We can't shut machines down.**" — Val: "**you're not shutting machines down. The machines are up and running.**" — Sean: "**They just can't take the money.**" — Val: "Right. **And cannot process the payment**… **And cannot award a winner.**"

**That refinement is word for word the one Val agreed with [[wiki/organizations/incom.md|InComm]] on Sept 2** ("the equipment is on. They just can't process the wins"). Sean concedes it is technically possible and keeps the objection anyway: "**we could. You could shut them off. I still think lottery['s] probably going to take [issue]**" *(the word breaks off and is not recoverable)*. Val's answer was unchanged: "**they could do whatever they want. I will shut the game off.**" Sean did not dispute the liability — "**No, I get it. Because you've got… liability. You've already pre-funded.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_InComm_Machine_Payments_and_ATM_Recycler_Standoff-September-2-2026.md]]

**Net effect: the regulatory cost of the lever is now asserted by three independent counterparties** — InComm (Sept 2), [[wiki/people/salim-khambawala.md|Salim Khambawala]] (Sept 8), Sean Fellows (Sept 10) — none of whom is recorded hearing the others, and two of whom are the master licensees who would carry the citation. **Nothing withdraws, relaxes or applies the ruling.**

**(d) And the one thing Val did get, which no earlier counterparty offered:** a call before the switch is thrown. "before we shut off the system, what do you want me to do? **Call you?**" — "**Absolutely, call me, because we have a different relationship with them.**" That is a softer version of the lever than the ruling contemplates — a notification duty rather than a right — and **no source says whether JVL accepts it as a term.** [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Diamond_Amusements_Machine_Performance_and_Transactional_Bundle_Pilot-September-10-2026.md]]

## Open questions

- **Will any Georgia location accept exclusivity?** Salim's own locations run PAX + MudaGoose + Fiery Pay in parallel precisely so a declined transaction has a fallback, and he described operators as unwilling to give up dual setups — "**the operators want to keep their dual setups.**" Nothing in that source tests whether the exclusivity condition is sellable. **Sept 1 supplies the nearest thing to a test so far and does not settle it:** a competing vendor removes MudaGoose completely across a ~150-location route and the operator accepts it — but for a mechanical double-payout reason, not for an enforcement lever, and that vendor is not described as demanding exclusivity of anything else. **Sept 2 adds a cost, not an answer:** InComm says the Lottery enforces "keeping the games on" and would cite the **master licence holder** if equipment were shut off — so the operator being asked to accept the lever carries the citation risk. **Sept 8 supplies the first direct refusal:** asked point-blank whether a broker could shut his terminal off over an unpaid $2,800, Salim Khambawala said "**as a master licensee, I won't like it**" — twice — and gave the regulatory reason, not a commercial one. Val had no alternative enforcement mechanism to offer. **Sept 9 supplies the first counterparty actually asked for it, and a contrary read of who would sign:** [[wiki/people/narasimha-rao-namburi.md|Rao]] accepted the deal — "**I'm in**" — with JVL exclusive in the location and a three-year location contract among its terms, then said "**Operator is not going to sign. Location may sign**" and separately warned that the incumbent can only leave "organically." **Sept 9 (evening) adds a fourth state and the first framing of the condition as a *technical* fact:** to ARC's CEO [[wiki/people/david-sloan.md|David Sloan]] the condition was stated not by Val but by a JVL colleague explaining the FlexPort, as a property of the hardware — "It's an **AFT device**… It connects to **SAS two port**. So we emulate an AFT device. **But we must be exclusive in the location. Otherwise, it doesn't work for us.**" Sloan's only response was "Exclusive for what?", answered "**The card.**" **He neither accepted nor objected in the recorded portion** — and the meeting continued unrecorded after the recorder's battery died (about 22 minutes captured of a 60-minute slot). **Sept 10 adds a fifth state — the condition put to a second master licensee as a bare term, and a second refusal of the backstop behind it:** at Diamond, "**we want exclusivity in the location. Nothing else can operate there. As far as transactional**", answered only with "**Yeah, no**" (not disambiguated by the source), while the same man refused any financial backstop — "**I'm not going to be a reseller**… **it's better for us to have an arms-length relationship**" — and gave the same regulatory objection to the shut-off that InComm and Salim gave: "**lotteries, lottery. We can't shut machines down**", conceding "**you could shut them off. I still think lottery['s] probably going to take [issue]**." He did offer one thing nobody else has: a call first. So the condition is now *agreed by a distributor in principle*, *unsigned by any location*, *refused twice by master licensees on the enforcement half*, *put to two master licensees as a product or contract term with no clear answer on tape*, and *never once tested in a live location* — five states across five counterparties, none of them a test. - **Does this supersede the alongside-MudaGoose pilot offers made Aug 26–27, or only govern the paid product?** Not addressed in either Aug-31 source — and **widened again on Sept 1**, three times in one day: to Double Down Gaming as a drop-on-top of MudaGoose, to Air Gaming as "a quick solution for locations with MudaGoose," and to Narayani/Akshar as free hardware "for whatever machines you have." **Widened again on Sept 8**, to a sixth customer — the Dixie bundle carries no exclusivity condition, no removal requirement and no shut-off, and MudaGoose removal arises only as an installation-cost question. **Widened on Sept 2**, twice — a fourth customer offered the free-hardware bundle with no exclusivity condition attached, removal pitched as a benefit rather than required, and that afternoon a **fifth** customer quoted the paid suite at retail with no condition, no removal asked for and no shut-off mentioned. **Sept 9 breaks the run in one direction only:** the condition was finally **stated as a term** to a seventh counterparty (the distributor Rao — exclusive in the location, plus a three-year location contract), while the **eighth** (Lucky Coin, the same afternoon) was asked for a different exclusivity altogether — the whole account — with no removal, no shut-off and no location condition mentioned. **That same evening a ninth** (ARC's CEO) heard the in-location condition again, this time as a hardware constraint rather than a commercial term, *and* was asked for his whole account — which he refused outright ("I don't think you can buy my entire business"); **no removal, no shut-off and no non-payment enforcement were mentioned to him either.** So on a single day the ruling was **applied, bypassed, and restated as a product fact**, and still nothing withdraws or relaxes it.
