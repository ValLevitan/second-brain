# Price the Georgia COAM software-upgrade kits at ~$2,900, finance them against kiosk orders

**Decision (Val, Apr 1 2025, on a commercial call with [[wiki/people/felipe-miranda.md|Felipe]], [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]], [[wiki/people/pavel.md|Pavel]] and an unnamed American commercial colleague who initiated it):** charge for the Georgia COAM **software-upgrade kits** (the "Peach"/PH1 enhanced software) — something **JVL had never charged for before** — at a **retail price of ~$2,900**, with a **floor of ~$2,500**. This is business economics (product pricing, not labour cost). [[raw/upgrade_ga-coam_kits-april-1-2025.md]]

## What the kit is and why it's worth charging for

- The upgrade delivers an **additional SAS port**, **printer integration**, and **all games reworked** with the current **nudge feature** — and makes the operator's existing machine **compliant with the new Georgia legislation (TITO)**, effectively "fully revamping their machines" so an old cabinet becomes current. Val: it partly cannibalises new-machine sales, "so I don't know why $2,500 is a shocking price." [[raw/upgrade_ga-coam_kits-april-1-2025.md]]
- **Cost basis:** an **Eclipse Lab** software submission runs ~**$10,000 one-time** (plus media/USB thumb-drives and COM harnesses); the initiator's spreadsheet used "just over $10,000" but Val corrected that the true cost is **"tens of thousands"** once weeks of many people's development work is counted. (The initiator noted that until JVL he had "only dealt with GLI," not Eclipse.) [[raw/upgrade_ga-coam_kits-april-1-2025.md]]
- **Market comparison:** other manufacturers charge an **average of $2,900 for software** these days (more for multi-level progressive) — so charging for software is "totally normal in the casino world." [[raw/upgrade_ga-coam_kits-april-1-2025.md]]
- **Machine price history (context):** the older **Altera** cabinets were bought for **under $5,000** (~200 machines, limited quantity); the bulk of the fleet were **Flex S at $13,000–$15,000**. [[raw/upgrade_ga-coam_kits-april-1-2025.md]]

## Financing strategy — kits have ~zero bill of material

- Val's core insight: the kit's **bill of material is ~zero** (the sunk development cost is large, but the marginal cost of shipping a kit is nil), so JVL can **finance kits very generously without a cash-flow hit** — unlike financing a physical machine, which carries real cost. [[raw/upgrade_ga-coam_kits-april-1-2025.md]]
- **Sell wholesale, not retail:** operators know how many machines they own and must convert them, so nearly all deals are wholesale. Set a retail price, a five-kit price, and a wholesale price; price each operator by machine count (remembering operators talk to each other). [[raw/upgrade_ga-coam_kits-april-1-2025.md]]
- **Tie long financing to kiosk orders:** don't discount the kits or the kiosks — instead **stretch payments** (12, 18, even 24 months) the more kiosks an operator commits to. "When they have to buy a kiosk from AXIS… or from JVL, why would they buy from AXIS? JVL is literally giving them 18 months payment on the kits." The kiosk is where JVL makes its money. [[raw/upgrade_ga-coam_kits-april-1-2025.md]]
- Other incentives floated: an in-house **machine refurbishment** offer at ~50% off parts/labour (customers bring machines to the office — but this risks stepping on the "technical ambassadors'" territory); extended payment plans. [[raw/upgrade_ga-coam_kits-april-1-2025.md]]

## The financing legal lesson (Val to Felipe)

- **JVL is not a finance company** — it offers **zero-interest installment terms** (baked into price), **12–18 months, monthly**, not multi-year. Third-party financing goes through **Elevate** (a Canadian financier, up to **5 years / 60 months**, quarterly payments). [[raw/upgrade_ga-coam_kits-april-1-2025.md]]
- Val warned Felipe that shutting off / repossessing a machine to collect on an unpaid balance is **illegal without a signed contract** stating those terms up front ("You're in America… I would sue you for loss of business"). The installment contract must spell out that the product **belongs to JVL until paid**, permit **repossession** and **service suspension on default**, and require the operator to disclose equipment location. "You cannot finance and discount at the same time." [[raw/upgrade_ga-coam_kits-april-1-2025.md]]

## Val's 2025–26 market-sizing bet (business context)

- Val's projection: **2026 nobody buys machines** — all operator cash gets "vacuumed" into legally-mandated **kiosks** (deadline **June 2026**, possibly pushed to Dec 2026). JVL should "forget selling machines next year" and fight for kiosks; machine buying maybe resumes 2027. [[raw/upgrade_ga-coam_kits-april-1-2025.md]]
- Rough numbers he cited: the market needs ~**6,000 kiosks** vs a maximum of ~**4,000 machines** sold; ~**60%** of the installed base are old machines; ~**30% (≈10,000 machines)** are **non-convertible** to TITO unless the operator buys **Port + a kiosk**; at ~**4 ports per location**, that's ~**2,500 locations**. JVL's edge is its large installed base. [[raw/upgrade_ga-coam_kits-april-1-2025.md]]
- Competitors named in the payment/kiosk space: **Axis**, **M3T** (kiosk with dual-SAS/pulse TITO printing), **Lucky Bucks** (doing it "through pulses"); Val's view is the main competitors "are all dedicated to TITO" and don't do pulses — but even with a competitor, JVL is "still entitled to 50% of the business." [[raw/upgrade_ga-coam_kits-april-1-2025.md]]

## May 29 2025 — the operator-pushback field test of this pricing ([[wiki/meetings/2025-05-29-felipe-georgia-market-update.md]])

Nearly two months later the pricing met its first real operator resistance, and Val turned it into a coaching argument with [[wiki/people/felipe-miranda.md|Felipe]]. Board member / operator **Selim Kwambuwala** (**Dixie Amusement**) told Felipe ~$2,800–$3,000 was "very dangerous" and that operators won't pay $3,000 "for software they already have," citing **Aristocrat** giving its Tito upgrade + printers **free** to recent customers. [[raw/felipe_georgia_market_update-may-29-2025.md]]

- **Val's reinforced framing:** stop calling it an **"upgrade"** — the machine is **rendered obsolete by the legislative change** (added second SAS port + skill-feature redesign), not merely upgraded, so it cannot qualify for a free upgrade the way a pre-committed sale would. Charging is existential ("I cannot exist without charging"); giving the dual-SAS away free would make JVL's games non-competitive and it would sell no new games. [[raw/felipe_georgia_market_update-may-29-2025.md]]
- **The economic argument he wants Felipe to make:** an operator earned ~**$55,000** over three years (~1,300 days × ~$50/day share) on that software; ~$2,800 to revive it for another ~$55,000 is obviously worth it. And JVL machines sell for ~**$13,000** vs ~**$16,000–$18,000** for Light & Wonder/Aristocrat — a ~$5,000 gap "almost two conversion kits" — so the kit price is small relative to what the operator already overpays competitors. [[raw/felipe_georgia_market_update-may-29-2025.md]]
- This is business/product pricing economics (not §15.8 labour cost). [[raw/felipe_georgia_market_update-may-29-2025.md]]
