# Georgia Class A machines

Georgia's second, lightly-regulated machine category — and, by August 2026, the fastest-moving thing in the state's amusement market. A **Class A licence** is trivial to obtain and unlimited in number; Class A machines pay a **maximum of $50 per spin** (worked around by a two-bucket win system), are **not taxed by the state**, sit on **no central system**, and can be placed in **unlimited numbers per location**, including beside the capped Class B COAM machines. The category went from a rumour JVL heard at a GAMOA board meeting in February 2026 to a market [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] committed out loud to enter within three months on **August 31 2026**. [[raw/To-Do_Georgia_meetings-February-18-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]

This page is the sequencer for that thread. The Class-B COAM business it sits beside is [[wiki/projects/georgia-market-rollout.md]].

## Feb 18 2026 — first seen as a threat

JVL's first sighting was defensive. [[wiki/people/felipe-miranda.md|Felipe]] reported back from a **GAMOA board-of-directors meeting** (JVL a sponsor) that **Primero** was bringing out Class A "amusement" machines that look like Class B COAMs but meter money differently, sell for about **$3,000**, need **no master licence**, and can be stacked next to Class B units. The board's fear was cannibalisation of Class B locations; JVL's answer at the time was to offer its own countertop / Echo units as Class A machines, explicitly not sold to Class B customers. Val's own position then was that he "doesn't fully understand the Class A mechanics/RTP yet." The full Feb-18 record is on [[wiki/projects/georgia-market-rollout.md]]. [[raw/To-Do_Georgia_meetings-February-18-2026.md]]

## Aug 31 2026 — the operator's account of how it actually works

Six months later [[wiki/people/georgios-zazanis.md|George Zazanis]] of [[wiki/organizations/arc-gaming.md|ARC Gaming]] described the same category from the inside, at the [[wiki/meetings/2026-08-31-arc-gaming-transactional-demo-class-a.md|Aug-31 demo]]. This is the corpus's first detailed operator-side account. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]

### The rules, as ARC operates them

- **$50 maximum per spin — and the two-bucket workaround.** "**You cannot win more than fifty dollars per spin.** That means if you win three hundred dollars, you have to press the button **six times** to win its time for fifty dollars. And some other weird [stuff] in between." Money in and winnings sit in separate buckets: "if you put money in, you have to play all of them… **if you put fifty bucks, you cannot take the fifty bucks out. You can only take out winnings.**" Once in the winnings meter the player collects the lot in one go — asked "So I can collect 150", the answer was "**All at once. 150 together. One voucher.**" And on the six presses: "In order to all the wins to go there, you have to press it six times to go in the winning bucket. Then, when I'm collecting, you can collect all of them." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]
- **The button-pushing is the tax the player pays** in the absence of the jackpot. Val put the arithmetic — "Five hundred dollars you win. So **for five hundred dollars you gotta cash out ten times**" — and the reply was an incomplete "As much as." Not contradicted, not confirmed. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]
- **Licences are effectively unlimited and near-free.** "to get the license, **everybody gets a license**. So a lot of locations never managed to get class B because they're crews. They get class A, so they get something… If you go to the GLC portal today… **there are thousands and thousands of locations** they have acquired class A licenses because everybody wants to put a Pacman… **The pool is unlimited.**" The price and process were given as "Five minutes in Serbia, twenty-five dollars per license" — **garbled; "in Serbia" is not interpretable** and neither the $25 figure nor the five minutes is corroborated elsewhere in this source. Recorded as spoken, not asserted. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]
- **Machines per location: unlimited.** Asked directly — "Are there any limitations for the amount of **paid loss** machines per location?" (transcribed as written; "paid loss" is garbled, read in context as the Class A / limited-payout machines) — the answer was "**Unlimited.**" Earlier in the same conversation: "this is unlimited. **You can have as many Class A as you want.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]
- **No state tax, no central system.** "The class A **don't have to give anything to the states of Georgia. There is no central system behind.**" Manufacturers may take revenue share: asked whether a manufacturer is allowed to participate in revenue, "**Sure. Why not?** We don't want to spoil the agreement with anybody." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]
- **Same redemption path as Class B, deliberately.** Asked "Can they use all the same redemption solution?" — "**Yes. You can take the money from the TITO from class A, put them in class B from class A.**" And the reason: "**We keep it the same. We don't want to cause buzz**… we keep the same rules… gift cards, kiosk, the same stuff, exactly the same… and **the reason we're doing this is so people then don't ask questions.** If you do something different, there's always going to be a question." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]
- **Class A and Class B share the room.** "**There is no visual separation needed**… we have locations that have Class B and Class A at the same time… **The colors are different colors**" — a distinction ARC makes for itself and its call centre, not for the player. On whether players can tell: "**We keep it secret**… The only thing they know that it's three machines, the same machine. **You think they know? We don't know.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]

### Where the demand comes from

Not new players — **capped locations**. "there are locations that have **maxed out their Class B license with nine machines**. They are top-tier locations. They have space, and people are waiting. Now we give them an alternative. To put three more machines, four more machines, five more machines, while people are waiting to play the Class B, they can sit down and play the Class A. That's all." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]

### The field numbers ARC gave

- **The top site is a 24-hour tire shop.** "It's a guy that fixes tires… It's a **twenty-four-hour tire shop**, and **he's the highest class eight location**" (the transcript writes "class eight" for Class A). "He put the machines on the back of the store, that it's a separate entrance. He's twenty-four hours open because he fixes tires, and he said there is a **line of people waiting** to play the machines, so we need to go and put more [machines] there." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]
- **What it takes: "He showed me his invoice. He was making ten thousand dollars per day. One location, ten thousand dollars per day, multiplied by thirty, it's three hundred thousand per month. It's a lot."** On the machine count he gave two figures in the same breath — "**he has forty machines. That's somewhere between thirty and forty machines**" — recorded with the spread visible, not averaged. Elsewhere: "he sells like ten thousand dollars a day." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]
- **Where:** "**Blue Ridge is the name of the place.** It's a top location… It's far away from here. Blue Ridge. **Two hours drive.**" He could not name the shop: "Do you remember the name of the tire shop? **No.**" He separately described it as "a tire shop outside of Georgia" — garbled and inconsistent with Blue Ridge being a Georgia town; not reconciled here. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]
- **Performance is uneven.** Asked "This makes money everywhere or spotty?" — "**Spotty.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]
- **ARC's rollout is supply-constrained, not demand-constrained.** "We started a couple of months ago. This is the second month we are installing it. **We don't stop. I have so much backlog**, I'm telling you, we don't stop. **There's no ceiling here.** I'm telling you, the locations that I have to install, I don't even know how." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]
- **Uniformity is the weakness.** "you go to the location. You will see three former machines, and you know how many they have. **Exactly the same machines. There is no difference. It's not appealing. It's not attractive to the players.**" ("three former machines" is transcribed as written.) ARC puts "anywhere from three to four cabinets in each location", all running the same six Primero games. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]

### The progressive jackpot — built, then pulled

The feature ARC believes will transform the category is not live.

- "They created a **progressive jackpot**. When we were ready to install this and go live together with the progressive jackpot, because it was so extreme, they pushed the limit to the limit. **They decided to shut down the progressive jackpot**, but they couldn't shut down this. So the main idea was to go with the progressive jackpot." And: "**They sat down the jackpot [GLC], and we went live with only the games**" — "sat down" transcribed as written, read as *shut down*. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]
- What it was: "The progressive jackpot was a big screen, a mounted… It was **very fancy**. As they play, it's very appealing, and **they could link from their backend the location, one location, [to another] location. You could do advertisement on the screen.** Lots of good stuff." Later: "**Screws could be 45 inch screens on top**, and you see the amount growing up. **This is what makes people crazy.**" ("Screws" transcribed as written; read as *screens*.) [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]
- What it is worth, in Zazanis's estimate: "**If the progressive jackpot goes through, it's gonna skyrocket, hundred percent, two hundred percent more revenue.** Imagine if you have two locations and you put your progressive jackpot connected to your two locations." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]
- Status: "there is a progressive jackpot that [the] GLC is fighting… **Until Primero fights it with the [GLC], they're supposed to… figure it out. We believe that it's going to happen sooner or later.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]
[[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]

### The regulatory risk, and why ARC discounts it

"we do not know what [the GLC] will do. So they say that they're gonna go after it extremely hard… They want to shut down, and I say why they want to shut down. **The worst case scenario: they're very regulated. They make more money. Why go and shut down?** There's no way to shut down. Worst case, they will do regulation, but it's just three years. It's gonna be forever." Val's read was the opposite of comfort: "**This business, you gotta act very, very quickly because the market fills up.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]

## The German AWP comparison — Val's frame for the whole category

Val's argument for taking Class A seriously was that Europe already ran this experiment — in the German AWP market split between **Gauselmann** and **Novomatic**. Quoted verbatim, garbles and all: "we know there is a German company called **Gazel Group**. Do you know the company? No. You know **Novomart**. Yes. Graf, right? Graf." … "The owner of the company is Professor Graff. **Dr. Graff** is actually a very smart guy, a very nice guy. I know him for many years. **He is Austrian.** So in Europe, he's got the largest competitor from Germany. **The last name of the owner is Goselmann.** Between Obermarchi and Goselmann, they **split the market in Germany fifty**. These are called, in some places, **Spielhouses, like playrooms**." … "And they have **20 to 40 machines**." … "Between two of them, they mastered the skill of amusement machines, and they **monopolized this market. 150,000** Class A, similarly limited payout. And they make crazy money now because of the licenses. **Licenses were unlimited. Nobody thought that it would ever make any money.**"

*(Reading: "Gazel Group"/"Goselmann" = **Gauselmann**, "Novomart"/"Obermarchi" = **Novomatic**, "Spielhouses" = Spielhallen. This is an **inference** from the roles described — an Austrian owner named Graf, a German competitor named Gauselmann — and both firms are already carried elsewhere in this wiki as the German AWP incumbents. The garbles are left in the quote, per §4; the canonical names are used in the surrounding prose.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]

## Sept 1 2026 — the operator case AGAINST it, from a board member

One day after ARC, [[wiki/people/ryan-odonnell.md|Ryan O'Donnell]] of [[wiki/organizations/double-down-gaming.md|Double Down Gaming]] — a [[wiki/organizations/gamoa.md|GAMOA]] board member — gave the opposite reading at the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-01-double-down-gaming-visit.md|Sept-1 visit]]. He has not seen the machines: "**I haven't seen them, but we've had a lot of discussions.**" The two accounts agree closely on the mechanics and disagree completely on whether the category should exist. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]

### The objection

- **It is a loophole, and that is the whole of the objection.** "Sure [it could add to the business], but **in our opinion, it's a waste of time. It's skirting the rules that are in place. It's trying to find a vulnerability and a loophole and exploit it.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]
- **The lottery gets nothing, and the Hope Scholarship is the stake.** "because there's **not a lot of regulation or rules around it**, [it] will be **very detrimental to our lottery. The lottery would not get paid a dime.** None of the money is made on the machines. **The hope scholarship won't be funded.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]
- **What Class B raises, as his reason the legislators will not let it slide.** "**We raised two hundred million dollars for them last year. They don't want them to go away.**" Asked why Class B would have to go away at all, he answered that the lottery "**wouldn't get any money from it unless their laws are rewritten**," and that "**it's only these that are regulated for that**" — Class B is the taxed category, Class A is not. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]
- **The gambling-hall end-state is what he thinks it leads to.** "**We could all do it tomorrow. At all of our locations**, we could say: You want ten more machines? Get rid of this stuff in here. A couple pool tables. **I'll put twenty of these machines in, and now you just have gambling halls.** And it's like **the whole point of this thing was to keep it far enough away at arm's length that it wasn't quite that.** Someone [is going] to blow up and get some terrible backlash." Asked whether the resistance is religious, he called it "**a cultural thing**," and distinguished casinos — localised to Atlanta, Savannah or Macon — from machines that can go anywhere. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]
- **It is his view, not a board position.** Asked "Is that a united view of all of the operators here?" — "**Not all. It's my view.**" Asked whether it is a board view — "**We don't have a specific thing.**" Earlier in the same exchange he had answered the question "where does the board sit on [it]?" with "**We're not in favor of them.**" Both are recorded; the source does not reconcile them. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]

### His account of the mechanics — independent corroboration of ARC's

- **Machine counts: Class B caps at nine, Class A has no cap.** "the idea of opening up a warehouse and putting a hundred of these machines in there because **there's nine machines. Nine of these machines can go in a location. There's no regulation over the number of machines.** There's bet amount, thing amount. **It's just a win amount.**" *(Transcribed as spoken; "thing amount" is garbled.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]
- **The per-hand caps, both classes.** "There is **no payout limitation. These are class B.** They can do what they do. **The class A's are fifty dollars per hand.**" And later, comparing: "for them, it's **five dollars a hand for us, but for them, it's fifty dollars** like a play." *(That last sentence is garbled and is recorded as spoken; read in context as $5 per hand on Class B against $50 on Class A, an inference.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]
- **The two buckets, described the same way ARC described them.** "they've created two buckets, **almost like your promo money**. So they create two buckets. **You put in the money here. That money cannot be redeemed. That money goes into your play bucket, and then your play bucket wins money.**" And the transfer: "it **cashes out into a redemption bucket**… it **takes the winnings from the unplayed bucket that you can't redeem to the played bucket that you can redeem. So there's no difference. It's confusing because there's an extra step. You have to have an extra step in order to redeem at that rate.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]
- **The $50 cap is defeated by an automatic, connected spin race.** "So they'll be **connected games**. You can hit spin, spin, spin. You win, and then this person also, because they're connected into the same thing, **it all happens at the same time**… So what it does is just a **built-in automatic spin**." He put the skill claim at zero: "essentially, it's the same thing. **You can win five grand on a machine that you're only supposed to win two. That's true skill. What if it's empty? You're the only person in there.**" Val: "**There's no skill.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]
- **The original legislative intent, as he understands it.** "The **original intent of the law was: this is a Pac-Man machine or some other arcade-type device, and you can get a prize for up to fifty.** But what they did is they're like, okay, **we're never going to give you a five hundred dollars jackpot… You're going to win a three thousand dollar jackpot, and fifty dollars at a time is going to go into this other bucket.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]

### Who decides, and when

- **He puts the call with the lottery, not the association.** Asked "Is the association also against this?" — "**It's going to be up to the lottery, really. Again, the legislators are not happy.** They sell it to their constituents as skill gaming." And on the regulator's posture: "**The lottery is trying to say it's not up to us.** The same thing with the ATM… **They're going to revisit that.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]
- **The Class A question is already live; the ATM question is the one he thinks a demonstration can move.** "**I think him seeing it in person will change his mind.** You mean the ATM? **The ATM piece, but the class A piece is already on the table.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]
- **His bottom line, and his exit from the argument:** "**Winds change, and this time next year, who knows? Maybe nobody cares, and if they don't care, I don't care.** At the end of the day. **The A's and the ATMs are kind of too big things. We need to get settled. Otherwise, it's a little murky.**" *("too" transcribed as written; read as "two.")* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]

### Val's German comparison, made a second time

Val ran the same AWP argument he had run at ARC the day before, in a shorter form and with the figures intact: "**in Germany, for example, that became successful, kind of an A class type of games. There's 150,000 of them, and it's well regulated, well administered**," and "**Novomatic has fifty percent of that market.**" He was explicit about the limit of his own knowledge — "I'm not in Germany. I don't sell any" and "**I don't know. We don't have class A games.**" He also stated the German distinction narrowly: "**There's no fifth form of difference. There's only payout limitation in Germany.**" *("fifth form" transcribed as written.)* This matches the Aug-31 version above (Gauselmann and Novomatic splitting the German market, 150,000 machines) without adding a new claim. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]

Val's only counter-proposal was to pay the state: "**Why not offer a cup to the lottery? I'm just asking. I don't know.**" *("cup" transcribed as written; read as *cut*, an inference.)* Ryan's answer — "Sure, we would need to… That would state that the lottery is simply enforcing the current laws" — was not developed. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]

## Sept 8 2026 — a fifth operator, and the first prediction of *how* the grey area closes

[[wiki/people/salim-khambawala.md|Salim Khambawala]], vice president of [[wiki/organizations/gamoa.md|GAMOA]], at the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-08-dixie-amusement-bundle-offer-and-card-economics.md|Sept-8 Dixie visit]]. Asked "what is your or the association's view on Class A machines?", he began by declining to speak for the association — "**Association is not like that**" — and then gave the fullest mechanical account in the corpus of why he thinks the category cannot hold. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

**The asymmetry, with numbers:** "**Class A is not as regulated as Class B. Class B, we pay the 13 percent plus licensing fees plus cost of doing business. On the other side, Class A doesn't have. I think their sticker is twenty-five dollars. They don't pay any taxes. They have zero regulations.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

**What Class A was for:** "**Class A was only formed to do jukeboxes or pool tables or those ball machines**… It was never to be in this category. But **somebody has created a class A title, but plays like class B and put it in class A.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

**The four-step collapse he forecasts** — this is the new content, and no other source in the corpus sets it out: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

1. **Class B pushes, and the Lottery is already unhappy.** "**it's not going to succeed because Class B is going to push so much**… **Lottery is not liking it. Forget about us**, because if lottery says if they want to do that, **we need thirteen percent from there too. We are not going to let them go.**"
2. **If the tax lands, Class B floods across.** "**if lottery approves that regulation and puts that thirteen percent, all these class B will jump in class A also. They're not going to sit around.**"
3. **But the genuine Class A world will not pay it, and out-lobbies Class B.** "those class A people who are **not running that gray area, who are running pinball machines or if you go to Chuck E. Cheese or Dave and Buster's**, those machines, **do they want to pay thirteen percent? No**… **They have a bigger lobby on that side with class A than what class B is.**"
4. **So the window is real but temporary.** "**Somebody is making a grey area, calling a class B machine a class A machine. Temporarily, it could work, but long term, it won't. Because there will be a lot of infighting.**"

**How this sits against the four positions already on this page.** It is the first operator view that predicts a *mechanism* and an *end state* rather than a stance — ARC (Aug 31) is commercially committed to Class A, the Sept-1 board member opposes it, the Sept-1-afternoon operator is exploring it, and the Sept-2 operator is warming to it. **Salim is the only one who says the category as currently used will not survive, and gives the reason.** Nothing here is reconciled with the ARC commitment on [[wiki/decisions/2026-08-31-class-a-deal-with-arc-within-three-months.md]] — no source addresses the other — and **Val said nothing about JVL's own Class A intentions in this meeting.** [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

One hundred percent.**" Yash — who sits on the board — did not dispute it, and gave the association's stated reason: "**their fear is this is gonna cut into the revenue of class B, and if it cuts into the class B revenue, then folks' scholarships aren't gonna get funded.**" His own objection to his own board: "we're all operators here… **they don't understand that you have to do what's best for the industry, and what's best for the industry is growth.**"

**Val named the fear he thinks Class B operators should have instead** — and it is the reverse of the scholarship argument: "**their fear should be that class B gets** [taken] **and they get stuck with already banned class A. So stuck with nothing. So now what do they have? Pinball machines. Pool tables. Loot boxes.**"

**This partly answers the standing question "Does GAMOA have a position on Class A at all?"** — a sitting board member accepts "one hundred percent against" without correction and supplies the reasoning. It is still **not** a recorded formal association position, and it sits against the Sept-1 board member who said "we don't have a specific thing." **Both stand.** [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Halcyon_Amusements_Georgia_Class_A_and_Texas_Route_Partnership-September-17-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]

## Sept 25 2026 — the first Class A screens, on Lions Riches, and party time as the thing players chase

- **The screen.** A "**reduced dual box**" that Val had liked; "**credits on the left, awards in the center**"; bet levels plus and minus "**in increments of 25 cents… all the way up to 5 bucks**". A VDEC layout with plus and minus buttons is still to be made. Michael: "**Graphically, not overwhelming, but mathematically it remains to be seen.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_GA_Progressives_and_Class_A_Lions_Riches-September-25-2026.md]]

Michael asked whether that means "**taking a class 3 lions riches and just throwing the dual box on top of it? That appears to be what our competitors are doing.**" Efim's challenges: the **$50 cap** ("$50 cup" in the transcript), "**What to do with free spins? - **Party time, as Michael now reads it.** "**when you go into party time, you're going to get repeated $50 wins. It's similar to their hot streak. Basically, I have concluded that this mechanism is a substitute for progressives. So, instead of winning 250 bucks, you win 50 bucks five times.**" The party-time message stays on top while it is active — "**It's basically a state**". Why it matters: in the competitor game he and Val played, "**Your base game wins were nothing to get excited about. Your free spins weren't even that exciting**"; "**this is the real chase here**", and the pay table is "**fuel to get to the oasis across the desert**". [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_GA_Progressives_and_Class_A_Lions_Riches-September-25-2026.md]]
- **The party-time math.** Efim's idea: take the part of the payout distribution above $50 and pay it through party time — "**maybe 10, 15, 20% of RTP**". Michael: the party-time wins they saw were "**always at least $100**"; target "**somewhere in that 150 to $250 range**", then "**somewhere between 100 and 250**"; "**50 is not enough**". Val had no view on the numbers: "**I have no opinion… I have no clue**"; his experience was "**I enjoyed the party time**", and "**turbo is not a bad thing**". [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_GA_Progressives_and_Class_A_Lions_Riches-September-25-2026.md]]

There is "**a minimum cashable award**", perhaps $5 — Michael was not sure. Then a continue / print ticket / reinsert dialog, "**quite similar to what [Primero is] doing**". When the ticket prints, "**your awards will go to zero**". [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_GA_Progressives_and_Class_A_Lions_Riches-September-25-2026.md]]
- **Val would rather not print.** "**I'm thinking how to avoid putting a printer inside**" — perhaps AFT with the optical ticket scanner, or scanning the e-wallet again: "**I just don't want to print another ticket.**" For now: "**Let's just leave it at this icon. We could always revise it.**" On the meters he noted "**the logic here is to have two pods**" (pots — credits and awards). Close: "**Let's leave it like this for now.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_GA_Progressives_and_Class_A_Lions_Riches-September-25-2026.md]]

## Oct 8 2026 — the requirements document, party time after free spins, and Lions Riches as the one target

At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-10-08-game-design-class-a-party-time-and-dual-box.md|Oct-8 12:00 PM game-design meeting]] (English) the Class A design moved from screens to rules. Full record: [[wiki/decisions/2026-10-08-class-a-party-time-may-start-on-the-paid-spin-after-free-spins-and-lions-riches-is-the-single-target.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Game_Design_Class_A_Party_Time_and_Dual_Box-October-8-2026.md]]

- **No rulebook, so Primero is copied with one change.** Michael found no legislative requirements; he "reverse engineered or deconstructed what Primero is doing". The requirements document is Jira IP-1220.
- **Party time after free spins.** Free spins are capped at $50, so they are weak. Michael and Efim's change: after free spins end, the next paid base-game spin may trigger party time.
- **The $50 is a cap, not a fixed amount.** Val: a payout "could be less than 50. It cannot be more than 50" — e.g. 125 as 50, 50 and 25. Michael has only seen $50 wins; the maths will check it.
- **Lions Riches (IP-1193) is the single target.** Refine Class A behaviour there, then carry it to the other games.
- **Storyboards.** Lena's 16-screen storyboard (GD-979) shows, for example, that changing the bet level disables party time and changing back enables it. Efim will brief the developers in Russian at the kickoff.
- **A new cabinet is noted.** The document says this release may have a new cabinet with a smaller V-deck showing bet up / bet down icons instead of the V43 bet levels.
- **The dual box sits lower** for Class A, right above the button panel — [[wiki/decisions/2026-10-08-dual-box-for-nebraska-class-a-and-georgia-five-reel-and-no-smaller-touch-area-for-the-wrong-coin.md]]
- **Schedule.** The requirements show "ready by October 16th"; Michael: "We'll see"; the maths will keep evolving.
