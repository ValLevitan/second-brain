# FlexPay business architecture and the payout-device catalog

In the week after the FlexSMIB architecture ruling ([[wiki/decisions/2025-04-07-flexsmib-modern-flexport-legacy.md]]), Val opened a deliberate "FlexPay Evolution" strategy thread: he now believes the kiosk-centred business model may be rejected by the Georgia market, and wants JVL to **obsolete its own model before someone else does**, offering a catalog of cheaper payout devices instead of one kiosk. Sessions: Apr 8 strategy ([[wiki/meetings/2025-04-08-flexpay-evolution-strategy.md]]), Apr 8 GAMOA demo recap ([[wiki/meetings/2025-04-08-flexport-gamoa-demo-recap.md]]), Apr 11 "FlexPay Evolution 1" ([[wiki/meetings/2025-04-11-flexpay-evolution-1.md]]). The engineering/dev thread stays on [[wiki/topics/flexpay-mobile-app-and-service-advocates.md]].

## Val's market math (Apr 8) — why the kiosk model may fail

- Georgia has "in access of 6,000 locations"; under the upcoming legislation each would need a payment kiosk "at $12,000 … it's an investment of close to $70 million," plus ~35,000 machines needing printer retrofits "at a cost of, I don't know, $500 per retrofit that's additional something like $17 million." [[raw/flex_pay_evolution-april-8-2025.md]] On Apr 11 Val rounds the market retrofit burden to "closer to a hundred million dollars" and calls a business architecture that forces a $12,000 kiosk on a five-machine location "flawed and possibly will fail." [[raw/evolution_flex_pay-april-11-2025.md]]
- Operators see no profit in complying — "they will try to do as little as possible to comply." Technically a cashier could pair a ticket number to a card manually with "a primitive application on a PC" using the Incom API — "a much, much, much … cheaper solution … and then our business model is completely ruined." [[raw/flex_pay_evolution-april-8-2025.md]]
- Constraint: the Incom agreement "puts the onus on the individual or the company who signed … to ensure that there is no fraud" — so tickets must be validated against a legitimate machine win (a mini TITO/SAS server), not typed in by hand. [[raw/flex_pay_evolution-april-8-2025.md]] For Mother-Goose-only operators, Val refuses to be the proxy signer: "I will not sign with them. I tell you one hundred percent now that I will not sign." [[raw/flex_pay_evolution-april-8-2025.md]]
- Strategic frame (Apr 11): the big competitors (Light and Wonder, IGT, Aristocrat) "are not making their own kiosks … we're not redesigning our machines"; Val wants JVL to capture the market as a technology leader with "three, four, five variations of the products, many levels of pricing" — "You cannot create a market with one style … A market is created by a catalog of products." Revenue thesis: "the bulk of our revenue will derive not from the sales of hardware, but from reoccurring revenue of processing transactions." [[raw/evolution_flex_pay-april-11-2025.md]] [[raw/flex_pay_evolution-april-8-2025.md]]

## The device variants on the table (Apr 8 + Apr 11)

1. **Cage POS (cheapest, half-automated):** attendant-facing touchscreen + scanner + magnetic card reader/loader in the cage, plus a customer-facing second display; ticket scanned, value moved to a card manually but validated against the mini SAS/TITO server. Rough component talk: "$250 altogether? Yes. Well, maybe even less" (Apr 8); Val's Apr-11 sizing: bill of material "probably … under $1,000 … a unit like this could be sold to the market at about $5,000." (The two figures describe different sketches of the same cage-POS idea — recorded as said, not reconciled.) [[raw/flex_pay_evolution-april-8-2025.md]] [[raw/evolution_flex_pay-april-11-2025.md]]
2. **Automated dispensing unit / "payout tower":** self-service card-dispensing unit next to the machines — scanner, small customer screen, large-capacity dispenser, mini TITO server inside; Val's Apr-8 cost vision "under 700 dollars, 600 dollars" (component talk: dispenser ~$350, FlexPort ~$100, enclosure ~$60 → "about 500 dollars"), vs FlexPay "considerably more." Marketing model: rent it "at a … dollar per day" on a three-year contract. On Apr 11 it becomes a slim **tower on a pedestal that fits between machines**; two towers per location give master/slave failover — "we already have this technology implemented in our embedded progressive" (Pavel). [[raw/flex_pay_evolution-april-8-2025.md]] [[raw/evolution_flex_pay-april-11-2025.md]]
3. **In-machine dispenser retrofit:** card dispenser inside each machine, routed through FlexPort — raised for completeness; Val: "Personally, I don't like it"; Juan adds many locations can't even open the master's machine door. [[raw/evolution_flex_pay-april-11-2025.md]]
4. **Cheap dual-dispenser kiosk:** Pavel's variant — strip bill acceptor and printer from the FlexPay kiosk and mount two large card dispensers ("that's how we planned the kiosk initially" — Val). [[raw/evolution_flex_pay-april-11-2025.md]]
- **Architecture dispute (open):** Val leaned on FlexPort as the embedded brain of the cheap devices ("it has all the input output … a dedicated team that could deliver the product in isolation"); Pavel dislikes the embedded route — "MBED is a difficult solution … not predictable in terms of development time" — preferring a cheap x86 platform with a 10-inch touchscreen at "the same money." Val: "whatever architectural solution … should really be dictated by you, not by me, Pavel." [[raw/flex_pay_evolution-april-8-2025.md]]
- **Direction picked (Apr 11, not a locked decision):** "I heard what Gennady said. Let's concentrate on the cheapest solution" (the cage POS), enabled by the TITO mini-server: "by the end of the month, we will have a Tito solution … The moment you have mini-server on a location, everything is open to you." New-device delivery talk: "we're talking about September of this year." Goal: "nail the business architecture by the end of this month," with a follow-up Wednesday 11 am ET and use cases written by everyone. [[raw/evolution_flex_pay-april-11-2025.md]]
- Applicability beyond Georgia (Juan/Val): the POS+TITO solution "is something we could also use in Puerto Rico … And in Nebraska? Yes … and I know for a fact that we could do it in Pennsylvania." [[raw/evolution_flex_pay-april-11-2025.md]]

## Temporary-wallet product rules (Apr 8 recap)

- The ticket is a **temporary e-wallet**, not a ticket — Val: "use it as a temporary wallet instead of a ticket. The ticket is just the wrong connotation." **No value is printed on the ticket** ("we do not print any value … It's hidden from the player") because the balance changes as the player moves between machines; the ticket carries **two counters** — playable credits and cashed/winnings value — and **only winnings can be redeemed to the prepaid MasterCard** ("by legislation, you can only convert credits won"). FlexPay shows both values; FlexPort shows only the machine-chargeable value. [[raw/flex_port_recap_felipe_juan_pavel-april-8-2025.md]]
- **Scope gap found — the no-value wallet:** Felipe's use case — a player walks in with "his last and only $20," feeds the machine directly, wins, and has no wallet to carry the winnings. Fix direction: let FlexPay "print a no-value temporary wallet"; Pavel's variant: a "generate a new ticket" button at the machine with a PIN, printing at FlexPay. Open problems: blank-ticket spam at the kiosk, and collisions when the FlexPay is occupied. "That's a scope [gap] that we identified right now … implement it for the beta version testing," not for the show. [[raw/flex_port_recap_felipe_juan_pavel-april-8-2025.md]]
- **Wallet validity period is unsettled:** Pavel mentioned tickets reusable "within 30 days"; Juan pushed back that a prior conversation had said as low as 24 hours (small operators' book-balancing; database size) — "we need feedback from the masters on that one." [[raw/flex_port_recap_felipe_juan_pavel-april-8-2025.md]]
- Player-anonymity stance (Apr 8 strategy): most route players are anonymous and won't give a phone number, so a phone/e-wallet-registration model "I don't see it being a business model, at least from the start" (Val); the missing ingredient is a card usable for betting inside the machines, which "Incom promised" later this year (see [[wiki/organizations/incom.md]]). [[raw/flex_pay_evolution-april-8-2025.md]]

## The Apr-16 Genadi one-on-one — tower/POS design & the priority pick

On Wednesday Apr 16, Val worked the device design directly with [[wiki/people/genadi-peari.md|Genadi Peari]] ([[wiki/meetings/2025-04-16-flexpay-evolution-dispensing-tower.md]]); the headline calls are on [[wiki/decisions/2025-04-16-dispensing-tower-first-magnetic-stripe.md]]. Design detail captured:

- **Two devices to design:** a **POS** (a cage unit + an attendant-facing device on the desk) and the **card-dispensing tower**. The tower can be a cage-internal customer-facing unit driven by the POS, or a **free-standing self-serve tower** — powered by mains on one side, CAT6 from the router on the other. It dispenses **cards only** (TITO format), reading the card's magnetic stripe; each machine already has SMIB or FlexPort to the router. [[raw/fkex_pay_evolution-apr-16-2025.md]]
- **Dispense flow:** read the TITO ticket (e.g. "$100"), dispense a blank card, read its number, pair number + value via InComm, get confirmation, re-read the stripe/QR, confirm the value, dispense. [[raw/fkex_pay_evolution-apr-16-2025.md]]
- **LED vs screen cost debate (resolved toward a small screen):** a FlexPortal touchscreen display "costs $20"; a larger 12–14-inch touchscreen "sandwich … about $60" plus a GUI to write; LED bulbs are "cents … maybe $2" but need a controller that erases the saving. Val settled on the small **7-inch FlexPort screen** in front, on a cheap embedded platform (one serial port + CAT5). Pavel had resisted embedded for the **POS** (many screens, heavy support) but the tower has only 3–5 screens, so embedded fits. [[raw/fkex_pay_evolution-apr-16-2025.md]]
- **Enclosure:** ~10–11 in wide, height of a computer tower, ~500-card capacity in one width (no parallel stacking), gray/milky plastic (no lighting), three-part shell (two cheeks + a front mold, no metal frame), front-loading extractable mechanism as on FlexPay. [[raw/fkex_pay_evolution-apr-16-2025.md]]
- **Optical-scanner question closed:** the TITO-ticket barcode can be read after the manufacturer reprograms a setting; Pavel tested a black/white inversion and "then he reads everything." Val: "I'm taking it off the agenda." (This is separate from the card reader, which stays magnetic-stripe-only.) [[raw/fkex_pay_evolution-apr-16-2025.md]]
- **Roadmap & market:** priority order = finish JVL business model → TITO architecture (SMIB + FlexPort + Local TITO Server) → dispensing tower → FlexPOS; tower work goes to [[wiki/people/evgeniy-alekseev.md|Alekseev]] + [[wiki/people/eugine-yavorski.md|Yavorski]]. Targeted at South Dakota, Nebraska, Puerto Rico; JVL follows InComm (Alabama, Texas). Legacy non-FlexPort machines (~3,000 of ~35,000) deprioritized. Rent-not-sell / recurring-revenue model (see decision). [[raw/fkex_pay_evolution-apr-16-2025.md]]
- Val floated handing the whole tower project to someone else (perhaps Evgeny's ex-cash-handling mechanics) to free Genadi for the Flex-S machines, but "отдать на сторону проект немножко скерри мне" — with control, not abandonment. [[raw/fkex_pay_evolution-apr-16-2025.md]]

## Apr 23 — architecture evolution (Juan/Pavel/Gennady/Val)

A long English architecture meeting ([[wiki/meetings/2025-04-23-flexport-smib-tito-architecture-evolution.md]]) worked the device architecture into a clearer shape; the decision-grade rulings (SMIB scope, the standalone TITO-server product) are on [[wiki/decisions/2025-04-07-flexsmib-modern-flexport-legacy.md]]. Design/strategy detail: [[raw/evolution_flex_port-apr-23-2025.md]]

- **Barcode readability dispute (action item, not resolved on the call):** Pavel thinks the optical scanner needs **~15–20 cm** reading distance for the long central barcode a third-party printer produces; Gennady measured **~10–12 cm** available and says 10 cm suffices. The scanner was chosen at "**above 50 degrees**" wide angle (the maximum). Undertakings: **Pavel** to verify the actual minimum distance and send Gennady the QR settings to reconfigure the reader; **Gennady** to check readability on **FlexPay** (its scanner-to-glass distance is a fixed 10 cm, sized for a card not a long ticket — may need a bigger opening). A lens/prism cap to widen the angle was floated as a fallback. [[raw/evolution_flex_port-apr-23-2025.md]]
- **One experience per location (reaffirmed, with a consequence):** a location runs **either** the digital/temporary-wallet model **or** TITO, never both — "you can't have digital wallet and Tito in the same building." The consequence Val drew out: in the **digital-wallet** model, since a modern IGT machine's cash-out prints only a plain central barcode (not JVL's scannable retain-and-reuse ticket), **every machine in the location needs a FlexPort with a scanner** so the player scans-and-keeps the wallet at each game — otherwise the wallet is lost. Which answered the standing SMIB question for that case: "do we actually need a SMIB or is it just a flex port that does it all? — **Just a flex port.**" [[raw/evolution_flex_port-apr-23-2025.md]]
- **Kiosk-not-first (evolution of thinking):** Val explicitly revised the original model — "one of the drawbacks… was, you needed to **start your play at the kiosk**. And that was a negative. We want to **start a player at the machine**," keeping the kiosk as an alternative, not the primary touchpoint. [[raw/evolution_flex_port-apr-23-2025.md]]
- **Two POS products:** **POS #1** = the **FlexPay top panel** driven by the regular FlexPay CPU (the CPU stays in the base unit; the top just becomes a point-of-sale surface). **POS #2** = a **FlexPort-based** unit — the 7-inch screen/touchscreen/board "sandwich" turned **vertical** in a plastic enclosure, mounted on a **$30 Amazon suction-cup arm** or an L-bracket. Val's worry: the FlexPay-derived POS is "the world's largest POS device" versus the **$350 tiny store POS** JVL competes with on a crowded gas-station counter; the interim fix is the L-bracket to stand it vertically while a smaller FlexPort POS is designed. The bill acceptor uses the **ID003** protocol (transcribed "ID03"); the FlexPort printer is driven over **RS232** (USB reserved for logs/firmware — "USB in… industrial application is not such a good interface"). [[raw/evolution_flex_port-apr-23-2025.md]]
- **Where the local TITO server lives (the long debate → a decision):** Val floated a standalone/cloud server; Pavel argued firmly for **Linux over the STM32/embedded route** (STM has "a lot of issues related to network communications… size of memory to write logs"; embedded code is "one monolithic software block file" vs modular, well-tested Linux; the Java/Linux code is reusable; more Linux specialists exist — Toronto, Minsk, Pavel himself). Reliability framing: the server is the **single point of failure for the whole location** ("when we have a CPU failure, one machine goes down… all nine machines go down" — max **9 FlexPort devices** per location), so a **99.5%-uptime + auto-recover** requirement, wired (not Wi-Fi) connection, minimal cooling (it only does "100 or 500 transactions per day"). The current cheap amusement board ("Kopini/Kardini", a "low-grade Chinese manufacturer") was judged unreliable; prefer a fanless industrial board (Gennady suggested **Tatang**) with NVMe + memory (~$120), "not… placing price tag as a criteria." **Resolution:** move the TITO server onto **its own standalone industrial-Linux hardware, presented as a separate product called "TITO server"** (see decision). [[raw/evolution_flex_port-apr-23-2025.md]]
- **Dispenser hardware:** the card-dispensing tower will use **embedded (STM32-based) hardware similar to FlexPort**; when a location has a dispenser but no FlexPay, the TITO-server CPU box can be placed inside the dispenser enclosure (a separate CPU from the dispenser's own). [[raw/evolution_flex_port-apr-23-2025.md]]

## Apr 24 2025 — server "in a box" locked; dispenser/POS design converges

The next-day continuation ([[wiki/meetings/2025-04-24-flexpay-server-dispenser-pos-evolution.md]]) turned the Apr-23 direction into concrete decisions. [[raw/evolution_flex-pay_pos_server-apr-24-2025.md]]

- **Standalone "server in a box" — Val's final verdict** (see [[wiki/decisions/2025-04-24-standalone-server-in-a-box.md]]): a Linux industrial board (3.5"×5", fanless, ~$120–130, ~10-yr life, "like our v1000 board, low performance"), mounted outside the machine like a router, **Ethernet-only** (never hardwired to FlexPay), in a deliberately oversized **Cisco-looking** enclosure. It runs **three** server apps — **JVL accounting host, Incom API, TITO server** (evolving the Apr-23 single-TITO-server framing). FlexPay stays on the machine CPU and just needs a **slave mode**. Samples: 4 to Toronto, 2 to Minsk. [[raw/evolution_flex-pay_pos_server-apr-24-2025.md]]
- **Dispensing tower:** plastic enclosure on a metal pedestal, driven by FlexPort hardware + an embedded **STM** solution; scans a presented ticket → local server → Incom validate → dispenses a card, shown on a player-facing screen at ~45°, ~8" wide × ~10–11" deep. **Stacker capacity raised to 450 cards** (from 300/320). A cheaper/better dispenser brand chosen (~**$320 vs $375**, more capacity). A **vertical magnetic swipe reader** is built in; the optical scanner reads only tickets, not cards. [[raw/evolution_flex-pay_pos_server-apr-24-2025.md]]
- **Detachable POS:** the top of the dispensing tower detaches to become the POS — a **mirror-image/symmetrical** design so a screen can face cashier or player (or the whole unit swivels 180°), mounted on an Amazon swivel/movable stand (legs ≥10–15 cm). A player-facing screen is required because the player must acknowledge the **$2 service fee**. [[raw/evolution_flex-pay_pos_server-apr-24-2025.md]]
- **FlexPort main-board revision for production:** one main board common to FlexPort / dispenser / POS — **three RS-232 COM ports** (dispenser, scanner, reader), Ethernet, USB, and a **wide-range adjustable-voltage power connector on the main board** (dispensers run 24V, FlexPort today 12V), dropping the bill-acceptor interface board. [[wiki/people/eugine-yavorski.md|Eugene Yavorski]] does the main-port production update. A future maximal FlexPort at the machine could scan/encode cards and act as its own dispenser; **reloadable cards** become allowed "a year from now." [[raw/evolution_flex-pay_pos_server-apr-24-2025.md]]
- Next meeting May 2. [[raw/evolution_flex-pay_pos_server-apr-24-2025.md]]

## May 1–2 2025 — tower/POS industrial design converges on "no detachable head"

Two [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] ↔ [[wiki/people/genadi-peari.md|Genadi]] design calls reviewing [[wiki/people/igor-starovoytov.md|Igor Starovoytov]]'s industrial-design proposal — the [[wiki/meetings/2025-05-01-flex-tower-spatial-design-review.md|May-1 spatial review]] and the [[wiki/meetings/2025-05-02-flex-tower-pos-form-fit-design.md|May-2 POS form/fit]] (the "next meeting May 2" from Apr-24). Both transcripts internally duplicate (§12) — facts taken once.

- **Tower anatomy (as proposed):** a **housing** containing only the dispenser (available in two sizes), a **removable head** as the main unit (holding the FlexPort electronics — the screen + touchscreen "sandwich" — plus an **optical scanner** to read any-shape barcodes), and an **optional pedestal** for the floor. Width discussion: currently **8 inches**, Val comfortable widening to **~10 inches** ("no strategic benefit" to saving width) with the credit-card swipe moved beside the screen inside the head; Genadi thought 10" felt like "a suitcase" (expected 6–7"). [[raw/flex_tower_spacial_design_review-may-1-2025.md]]
- **Unify FlexPort to drive both tower and POS.** Val wants the FlexPort brought to a state where the **identical** electronics/screen/insert serve both the tower head and a standalone POS — the extra cost to make FlexPort do both is minimal, and he doesn't want a separate hardware revision. FlexPort is "the main brains/engine." Only the **main board** goes in the tower head; the **third interface board** (bill-acceptor + extras) is left out there. [[raw/flex_tower_spacial_design_review-may-1-2025.md]]
- **The design resolution — drop the detachable head.** A head that detaches to become a convertible POS needs special **"floating" (self-mating) connectors**, guides and mounting — complex, costly, slow. Genadi warned that path means redesigning **three Flex-S machines + the tower dispenser + a separate POS**, which "we'd only come out with by the end of next year." Val's fix: **do not make a detachable head.** Build the **POS separately** from the same electronics/screen/insert, just wrapped in **"two hoods"** (top + bottom plastic covers), skipping the adapter boards and sliding connectors entirely — cheaper and faster. Mounting: the **back cover** carries the stand/leg; connectors (Ethernet, power, maybe USB for service) exit the side or back/bottom. [[raw/flex_tower_spacial_design_review-may-1-2025.md]]
- **Screen: rotate, don't flip (May 2).** For a POS that must serve both attendant and player, the screen should **rotate ~180° horizontally in the plane parallel to the table** (a "twist"), **not flip** — a flip would need an **accelerometer** and an ugly mid-redraw. Keep the device **thin** ("rotate a piece of paper, not a brick"). Orientation rule: **horizontal** reads better when the unit sits **on a stand**; **vertical** suits a unit held **in the hand**. Initial builds can ship with **one screen + a pivot**; full POS-by-rules needs two screens (attendant-facing + a player info screen). [[raw/flex_tower_pos_form_fit_design-may-2-2025.md]]
- **Next steps:** Val to hand Igor Starovoytov all the models so he draws over the weekend; Val had a **6-hour design session with Igor** that evening (May 2). A candidate off-the-shelf POS **bracket at $77** was noted as interesting. [[raw/flex_tower_spacial_design_review-may-1-2025.md]] [[raw/flex_tower_pos_form_fit_design-may-2-2025.md]]

## May 6 2025 — tower hardware "make it a product," speed over polish

A [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] ↔ [[wiki/people/genadi-peari.md|Genadi]] hardware working call ([[wiki/meetings/2025-05-06-flex-tower-hardware-design.md]]; transcript internally duplicates — facts taken once). Genadi is picking components (power supply, filter, **card reader still not received**) and finishing **speaker/acoustics** selection for the Flex (needs a rapid prototype so the acoustician can take a final volume measurement). [[wiki/people/igor-starovoytov.md|Igor Starovoytov]] did little this round at Val's request. Genadi's job now: "make it a product" — needs ~**4 days** to immerse and push it fast; **Tower first, POS second** (Tower can work standalone; the POS needs more work); **mass-production readiness** for ~six months of orders. [[raw/flex_port_hardware_design_evolution-may-6-2025.md]]

- **Enclosure/industrial-design calls:** body + back wall in **metal**, front **door in plastic** (cheaper, complex shape); a **removable head** holding the electronics that assembles separately then **slides in and fixes under the door** (two dispenser interface wires run down the back wall); make the machine **fully symmetric top/bottom** and slightly narrower/streamlined — **drop the reverse-angle undercut** that complicates the mold; the scanner becomes a **separate small plastic part** pointing down. **Speed is paramount** — "делайте самые простые формы… делайте это быстро"; dimensions unimportant (±2 cm "никого это не убьет"). [[raw/flex_port_hardware_design_evolution-may-6-2025.md]]
- **Lighting over labels:** replace the printed "swipe card" label with an **embossed icon**; **backlight the card-slot ribs** (green indicator) so it's usable in a dark location; light the card-exit bezel (self-lit by the dispensing device); a scan-sign area with an **embossed, blinking barcode** rather than a sticker. This requires adding **RGB support / an interface provision to the FlexPort electronics** — waiting on [[wiki/people/evgeniy-alekseev.md|Alekseev]] to make the change fit **all devices (port, tower, POS)**. [[raw/flex_port_hardware_design_evolution-may-6-2025.md]]
- **Card tray:** much thicker/robust with a semicircular grip. **Front (frontal) service** because the unit stands between machines. **Tilt angle is fixed** (scanner mounted parallel) — no vertical orientation in this first release; fine for now. [[raw/flex_port_hardware_design_evolution-may-6-2025.md]]
- **Pedestal/footing:** the pedestal is a plain "black box" (not fancy); a flat metal **footing plate** (like the Countertop pedestal's) usable under both the machine and the pedestal, or two perpendicular metal strips as a cross-foot; the foot extends slightly forward (and back if free-standing) for stability when the dispenser slides out. A **lazy-susan/carousel** with **stepped (clicking) ~100° rotation** was floated for the foot. [[raw/flex_port_hardware_design_evolution-may-6-2025.md]]

A **standalone server** still needs building separately, with electronics changes for it; the industrial boards were ordered but not shipped (vendor went on the **May holidays**) — expected this week.
