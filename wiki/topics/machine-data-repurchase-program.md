# Machine data repurchase program ($337 per year per machine)

JVL's commercial answer to not knowing how its own machines perform in the field: put an **LTE / cellular modem in every machine that ships from September 1 2026**, then **pay the operator an annual fee to buy that machine's data back**. The install-and-activate rule was decided on **Aug 21 2026** ([[wiki/decisions/2026-08-21-cell-modem-on-every-machine-from-september-1.md]]); the **price and the contract terms** first appear at the **Aug 25 2026** [[wiki/meetings/2026-08-25-epic-amusement-kenny-patel-visit.md|Epic Amusement visit]], then in the customer meetings of **Aug 26–27 2026**. *(Corrected on back-fill: this page earlier dated the first appearance to Aug 26–27.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Epic_Amusement_Kenny_Patel_Visit-August-25-2026.md]]

## The offer as Val states it to customers

- **Aug 25, to Epic Amusement — the earliest recorded offer.** Val: "we've added a cellular modem to the machine… because we would like to sign a contract with you for data sharing… we will keep it fully confidential… We need to know popularity reports because this is it will help us to develop better software… we will physically discount the machines for this data exchange contract." Epic's banker summed it up: "buy some games for me and I'll knock a thousand bucks off of it. If you'll keep my Opt Connect on the damn machines for three years." Kenny Patel then referred to "the contract you want to sign from me for three years where you are going to pay three hundred and thirty seven bucks" — so the **$337 figure was already on the table on Aug 25** (the passage where Val first said it falls in a gap in the recording). Kenny's condition: Epic's "lenders are okay with signing it". [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Epic_Amusement_Kenny_Patel_Visit-August-25-2026.md]]
- **$337 per machine per year, on a three-year contract.** To [[wiki/organizations/narayani-amusements.md|Narayani Amusements]] on Aug 26: "This contract I'm paying to you one thousand dollars over three years per machine. So I'm paying **three hundred thirty seven dollars per year per machine** for every machine that you will install it. So that's a thousand dollar discount right there." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Narayani_Amusements_Trade_In_Negotiation-August-26-2026.md]]
- **The same number, offered as standing terms, the next day.** To [[wiki/organizations/halcyon-amusements.md|Halcyon Amusements]] on Aug 27: "we are implementing in these machines starting **September first**… We're implementing an **LTE modem**, and when we're selling our machines, **we're offering to repurchase our own data**… we will give you an offer of three hundred and thirty-seven… **three hundred thirty-seven dollars a year** to purchase our data." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Halcyon_Amusements_Recycler_Pitch_and_Lottery_Notice-August-27-2026.md]]
- **Aug 27, at ARC Gaming — the operator raises the modem and says yes.** At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-08-27-arc-coffee-fleet-swap-and-transactional-pitch.md|Aug-27 coffee]] an ARC voice brought it up as part of the swap: "on top of that agreement that I asked you the other day that they want to put our modem inside in order to capture the data… That's okay right? We don't have an issue with that." No price or term was said in this recording. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Coffee_Fleet_Swap_and_Transactional_Pitch-August-27-2026.md]]
- **Aug 31, to ARC Gaming — offered as a discount lever.** At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-08-31-arc-gaming-transactional-demo-class-a.md|ARC demo]] the derived notes record that "Val explained JVL's data-buyback model (**$337/year/machine via the new LTE modems**)". In the transcript Val puts it in the language of pricing rather than of data rights: "Manufacture gives you discounts, or give me deals, or give me whatever else. **We found a way to give deals. For example, data discount.** Operators are happy. First of all, they get a deal. **Second, they get data.**" That last clause — the operator gets data back — matches the "make intelligent decisions" framing used at Narayani. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]
- **It is framed to the operator as revenue, not as a concession.** "we will pay you back for data sharing… for every machine that… we will buy from us, we will actually pay you on a yearly basis for data, which is **another revenue source**. We can also let you use this data to make intelligent decisions." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Narayani_Amusements_Trade_In_Negotiation-August-26-2026.md]]
- **In a deal it functions as price.** In the [[wiki/decisions/2026-08-26-narayani-three-machine-d27-trade-in-pilot.md|Narayani trade-in]] the $1,000 three-year contract value is the second half of a $5,000-per-cabinet trade-in credit ($4,000 + $1,000), and signing the contract is the **condition** of the higher credit. It is also one of the four stackable levers on the already-approved Georgia offer letter (discount + trade-in + pay-in-full + data-sharing). [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Narayani_Amusements_Trade_In_Negotiation-August-26-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Georgia_Machine_Sales_Strategy_Post_GOAM-August-21-2026.md]]

## The contract terms Val committed to in the room (Aug 26)

*Earlier, Aug 25 (Epic):* "We can never disclose anything. We can never. Export anything, we can never sell anything. It remains strictly anonymous"; if the operator changes its mind, "you owe us money"; for the three-year term, "if we need to fix machine, then we'll fix it" — "Yes, we will send a technician." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Epic_Amusement_Kenny_Patel_Visit-August-25-2026.md]]

- **Three years**, signed with the operator, covering "every machine that you will install it." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Narayani_Amusements_Trade_In_Negotiation-August-26-2026.md]]
- **Confidential and non-commercialisable.** "Fully confidential, the contract will spell out that we **cannot materialize on this data**… We cannot sell, we cannot import, we cannot export this data. We can not do anything except our engineers." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Narayani_Amusements_Trade_In_Negotiation-August-26-2026.md]]
- **Anonymised to JVL's own engineers.** "They're looking at the **serial number of the machine** and the data of the popularity. They don't even know who you are and what you do." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Narayani_Amusements_Trade_In_Negotiation-August-26-2026.md]]
- **JVL machines only.** Asked whether it covers other manufacturers' games: "of course, we're not hooked up to your other games. Of course, No, no, just just our data." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Narayani_Amusements_Trade_In_Negotiation-August-26-2026.md]]

## Why JVL wants the data: the third-party reports are not trusted

Val's Aug-27 pitch runs straight off his rejection of the industry performance reports. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Halcyon_Amusements_Recycler_Pitch_and_Lottery_Notice-August-27-2026.md]]

- "These reports are **incorrect**. I know there is a mathematical, gross mathematical error. I could show you reports where, for a period of time, **they claim that we paid out a million dollars**." The operator agreed: "It's absurd."
- "The **Eiler** report is a **self-proclaiming** report" — manufacturers supply their own numbers, and "there's no way of verifying it." Val qualified it: "there is way of verifying it, but they don't."
- **Advertising buys placement.** "they also charge you for advertising… So if I'm a good customer, which IGT Enlightened Wanderers, their prime customer, they will always put them in a prime spot. **For this reason alone**, we are implementing in these machines starting September first… an LTE modem." *("Enlightened Wanderers" is transcribed as written; read in context as Light & Wonder — an inference, not a corrected quote.)*
- *The report publisher is rendered "Eiler" here and "Ailer-type reports" in the derived notes; the corpus has carried this firm as **Eilers** since 2025 (and as "ILERS/ILAS" in one Aug-21 transcript). Treated as the same firm — an inference from role and context, not stated in this source.* [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Halcyon_Amusements_Recycler_Pitch_and_Lottery_Notice-August-27-2026.md]]

### Aug 31 2026 — the same argument, plus a mechanism, and an operator who got there first

The [[wiki/meetings/2026-08-31-arc-gaming-transactional-demo-class-a.md|ARC meeting]] opened on this subject before JVL raised anything else, and it is the first time in the corpus an **operator** states the case against the reports unprompted. [[wiki/people/georgios-zazanis.md|George Zazanis]]: "there is a flyer that goes around. I get my hands [on it at] the expo, and there was something very high. Guys, **I guarantee you, this is wrong. There is no way. There is absolutely no way**… I see it from our data." And on the source of the numbers, Val: "**the manufacturer gives their own data**… And if you advertise with [Eilers], you pay them an advertising fee." Zazanis: "Then the numbers are a little bit better." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]

Val added a **structural mechanism** he had not spelled out at Narayani or Halcyon — the numbers can be honest and still be wrong: "When a new manufacturer comes in the field, an operator puts that manufacturer **in the best location**. The best location average is higher, of course, simply because it's the best location among all other locations. So **a manufacturer with the smallest amount of machines always shows the best result.**" He contrasted it with casino practice, where floors are rated by area and a machine is bought at "average plus 25 percent, or average plus 5 percent" — "In locations, it's virtually impossible because locations don't do that." Zazanis's counter-measure is placement discipline: "That's why we place them here one, here two, here three. **So we don't put them all in the top location.**" And he warned it cuts the other way too: an operator can offer a manufacturer the top twenty locations "so by default they will do good. **I pay you less.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]

Val's conclusion in that room is the program in one line: "we got set up with [the] dealers and this report and [Eilers'] report and the other report. **We generate our own data.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]


### Sept 1 2026 — the modem pitched as a product capability, with no contract and no price

At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-01-double-down-gaming-visit.md|Sept-1 Double Down Gaming visit]] Val raised the modem a fourth time, to a fifth Georgia operator — and this time **the repurchase programme does not appear at all**. No $337, no three-year term, no data-sharing agreement, no mention of paying the operator. The modem is offered purely as the thing that lets JVL tune a machine to a specific location: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]

> "in the newest machines, **we have a modem**… Installed a modem, so **we can get the statistical data out of the machine in real time, and we can tweak that machine in real time as well.**"

- **The tuning claim is the point of it in this pitch**, not the data itself. It was the answer to "what makes you think that this game would outperform them?" — Val's argument being that JVL's settings range is so wide that generalised settings are the problem, and location-specific tuning is the fix. The full pitch is on [[wiki/topics/slot-game-design-principles.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]
- **Remote initial setup is the second half of it.** "**Most of these settings are flexible. Ninety-nine percent, and we set it through remote initial setup. So we program it in the back room and we send it to** [the machine]." What it cannot do: "You have to reset the machine. That's a hardware reset. You can set some data. You could change bad buttons. You could do certain things, but **you cannot download any software.**" *(The passage is garbled around which machines the limitation applies to; recorded as spoken. The remote-install thread is on [[wiki/topics/ris-remote-install-and-machine-states.md]].)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]
- **This does not bear on the open contradiction below.** Like the Aug-31 ARC statement, it describes **no data scope** — but unlike ARC it also describes no payment and no contract, so it is not a fourth statement of the offer at all. Recorded here so a later reader does not read its silence as the programme having changed.

### Sept 1 2026 (afternoon) — the modem pitched twice more, and the programme surfaces only inside a coaching session

Two further customer visits the same day ([[wiki/meetings/2026-09-01-narayani-akshar-mega-hit-demo-and-trade-in.md|Narayani/Akshar]] and [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-01-air-gaming-group-transactional-walkthrough.md|Air Gaming Group]]). **In neither meeting was the repurchase programme offered to the customer.** The modem was sold as a capability both times; the buy-back appears only afterwards, when Val is coaching his own people on how the pitch should sound.

- **To the Narayani/Akshar buyers, the modem is a remote-management feature, not a data deal.** "**we will be able to track how games are performing for your advantage and for our own investigation of what's hot in the market. We'll be able to tell — hey, I'll give you a call and say, listen, we see that this game is not performing. Would you like to change it for this one? You only need to do a hard reset.**" No fee, no term, no contract. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Narayani_Amusements_Mega_Hit_Demo_and_Trade_In-September-1-2026.md]]
- **To Air Gaming, the same — capability only, and this time with the scope named.** "**Our new machines come out with cell modems built in, and that provides you with additional information, not only money in and money out, but on the game popularity and on the betting trends.** For example, if your machine is constantly busy at six o'clock, it would be nice to remove twenty-five cents bet between six and eight o'clock. **Minimum bet, let's say seventy-five cents. So we can configure the machine this way. We have to have it on the cloud. There's no other option.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Air_Gaming_Group_Transactional_Walkthrough-September-1-2026.md]]
- **The programme itself appears once, in the coaching session after the Narayani customers left** — Val telling [[wiki/people/felipe-miranda.md|Felipe]] and [[wiki/people/andrei-klimovich.md|Andrei]] what the one-line pitch is: "**We're putting modems, for which we're paying. We're giving you discount for data sharing. We're buying your data for three years. This resonates with operators.**" The **three-year term** is restated; **no price is given**, and no data scope. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Narayani_Amusements_Mega_Hit_Demo_and_Trade_In-September-1-2026.md]]
- **The argument the modem is meant to win, stated more fully than anywhere else.** Val's case is that the standard report cannot tune a machine: "It has money in and money out. Average bet… total games played. **So which games are popular?** … **That's nowhere.** So isn't it the essence of tuning the machine? The **exit report**, the money in, money out, which I call exit report, is the end report… **But what makes the machine pay out X and take Y? A bet level. A game popularity. Maybe [another] game should be enabled? Maybe games should be priced differently at 6 p.m. versus 9 a.m.** … **I'm not saying the report's wrong. I'm actually accepting that the report is correct**… I'm just saying that **this information doesn't exist. It's unavailable. You cannot get this information** — unless you have **our machine with a modem.**" The one-line version he wants sold: "**This is the only machine where you are in control of how to fine tune your games.**" The reporting-distrust half of this argument, and Felipe's pushback on it, are on [[wiki/topics/jvl-operating-principles.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Narayani_Amusements_Mega_Hit_Demo_and_Trade_In-September-1-2026.md]]
- **Effect on the open contradiction below: none, and deliberately so.** The Air Gaming statement names a scope ("game popularity… betting trends," alongside money in / money out) but is describing **what the modem can report**, not **what the contract entitles JVL to read** — the question the contradiction is actually about. It is recorded so a later reader does not mistake it for the Aug-26 side of that argument. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Air_Gaming_Group_Transactional_Walkthrough-September-1-2026.md]]

### Sept 2 2026 — a sixth customer, still no programme, and bill-validator acceptance rate offered as a favour

At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-02-lucky-coin-service-and-upgrade-offer.md|Sept-2 Lucky Coin visit]] the modem was pitched for the sixth time. **The repurchase programme again does not appear** — no $337, no three-year term, no contract, no payment to the operator. Two things here are new [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Lucky_Coin_Service_and_Upgrade_Offer-September-2-2026.md]]:

- **The modem is sold as one of three Georgia firsts in the machine itself.** "This machine will come with a **built-in modem**, and this modem has never been done in Georgia. So what we will be able to tell you is **how your machine operates, what it does, which game is popular, which is not**, and we have a bunch of settings here that **we can adjust remotely**. Not all of them, but many." Same capability framing as Sept 1, now attached to a specific machine on offer.
- **Bill-validator acceptance rate, offered as a free demonstration.** *(Corrected on back-fill: this was not the first mention — on Aug 25 Val already told Epic "we also want. Service data… let's say a bill acceptor is downgrading. The acceptance rate is falling" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Epic_Amusement_Kenny_Patel_Visit-August-25-2026.md]].)* Val offered it unprompted, as a free demonstration rather than as part of any contract — "**I will just report your acceptance rate to you on your validators. You'll be surprised**" — having argued that a swapped validator shows up as a collapse in acceptance: "through a modem, I could tell you that **your acceptance rate on the bill acceptor went from 96 percent to 75 percent**." His authority for it: "the reason I know [is] because I had a company who used to manufacture bill acceptors, and I'm telling you that that acceptance rate **deteriorates very quickly**."
- **A third use, and the only one with an immediate operational payoff:** the modem removes the QR-scan step that had caused a service complaint at this very customer — "we've addressed that issue. We're now **installing modems into the machine, and you never have to go and scan it**." See [[wiki/organizations/lucky-coin.md]].
- **Effect on the open contradiction below: none, and it is not treated as a third side.** Validator acceptance rate is neither the "popularity, service and gameplay reports" of Aug 26 nor the "net win, gross, payout" of Aug 27; but it was offered as a **favour outside any contract**, not as a description of what the data agreement covers — which is the question the contradiction is about. Recorded so a later reader does not mistake it for either side, or for a widening of the scope. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Lucky_Coin_Service_and_Upgrade_Offer-September-2-2026.md]]

## What the data is meant to tell JVL

Val's Aug-27 description of the reporting he wants back: "Here's your **geo positioning** of that machine. We don't even know where it is… This **net win**. This **gross**. This **payout**. This how much a machine made. These games are popular. These games are not popular. You gotta replace the games. **This is your setting. You're being killed because 25 cents is being overused. 80 percent of your games is played in 25 cents mode.**" The derived notes summarise it as "real geo-positioned net win, popularity, and settings data." This makes the program the evidence base for the peak-hour / minimum-bet argument on [[wiki/topics/slot-game-design-principles.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Halcyon_Amusements_Recycler_Pitch_and_Lottery_Notice-August-27-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Halcyon_Amusements_Recycler_Pitch_and_Lottery_Notice-August-27-2026.md]]

## Open contradictions

**What the contract actually entitles JVL to look at (Aug 26 vs Aug 27 2026).** Two statements by Val one day apart, about the same program, describe different data scopes. Recorded, not resolved.

- **Aug 25, to Epic (earlier dated evidence, added on back-fill):** "Game popularity and and statistics without cash", plus "Service data" such as a falling bill-acceptor acceptance rate. This sits with the Aug-26 side, but it is a third statement, not a resolution. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Epic_Amusement_Kenny_Patel_Visit-August-25-2026.md]]

- **Aug 26, to Narayani:** "**We're not looking at your financials.** We are looking at **popularity reports, service reports and gameplay reports.** That's important for us." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Narayani_Amusements_Trade_In_Negotiation-August-26-2026.md]]
- **Aug 27, to Halcyon:** "We want the real numbers… This **net win**. This **gross**. This **payout**. This how much a machine made." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Halcyon_Amusements_Recycler_Pitch_and_Lottery_Notice-August-27-2026.md]]
- Neither source reconciles the two, and neither describes the written contract's actual clauses. The Aug-26 statement is the one made to a customer who was **signing**; the Aug-27 statement was made to a prospect.
- **Aug 31, to ARC Gaming — no help either way.** The program was raised again four days later, and Val described **no data scope at all**: the offer was put purely as pricing ("data discount") plus the operator getting data back ("Second, they get data"). The derived notes summarise the same passage as the buy-back model at $337/year/machine and likewise say nothing about what JVL reads. So the third dated statement **does not narrow the contradiction and does not widen it** — it is recorded here so a later reader does not mistake its silence for agreement with either side. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_ARC_Gaming_Transactional_Demo_and_Class_A-August-31-2026.md]]

### Sept 8 2026 — the data comes back to the operator as a priced product: "operator intelligence"

At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-08-dixie-amusement-bundle-offer-and-card-economics.md|Sept-8 Dixie visit]] the reporting appears for the first time as a **named product with a stated commercial future**, inside the $90/month bundle: "the only thing that we're giving away for free now is the **operator intelligence, which is optional, but we eventually will start charging for it**… but eventually we'll offer it as an option." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

**What it contains, as itemised to a customer** — the fullest list in the corpus of what JVL's system actually sees: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

> "We will give you **the entire intelligence of your operation, from collection to service to popularity reports.** We will tell you that **machine number one has not played for three days. Send the technician.** Most likely something is wrong. We'll tell you that **the cash door was open at such and such time.** We'll tell you that your **bill acceptor's acceptance rate on the first insertion is falling to sixty five percent. Time to clean your bill acceptor** … **Delivered directly to your cell phone.**"

**Why this is dated evidence on the contradiction below, and not a resolution of it.** The list is of what JVL **reports out to the operator** — collection, service, popularity, door events, acceptance rates. It is much closer to the Aug-26 "popularity reports, service reports and gameplay reports" scope than to the Aug-27 "net win, gross, payout" scope, and it contains **no financial-performance category at all** except "collection," which is not defined in the source. But **the buy-back programme itself is never mentioned** — no $337, no three-year contract, no data-licence language — so the passage describes the *output* side, not the *contract* side, and cannot be read as either party stating what JVL is entitled to read. **Recorded; the contradiction stays open.** [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Georgia_Transactional_Equipment_and_Bundle_Economics-September-8-2026.md]]

### Sept 10 2026 — the programme inverts: the operator collects the data, for free

At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-10-diamond-amusements-machine-performance-and-bundle-pilot.md|Sept-10 Diamond visit]] the modem and the reporting were pitched again **without the repurchase programme** — no $337, no three-year term, no contract, no payment, continuing the run of customer meetings recorded above. **But this meeting reverses the direction of the deal:** rather than offering to buy data off the operator, Val asked the operator's own compliance staff to **collect it for him by hand, for nothing**, and got it. Full record on [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-10-diamond-field-auditor-captures-jvl-settings-data.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Diamond_Amusements_Machine_Performance_and_Transactional_Bundle_Pilot-September-10-2026.md]]

- **What he says he needs, in the language of this page:** "what helps me is the **popularity report, setting reports**. This [is] what I need… for my machines, it's either **QR code on the older machines or the modems on the newer machines.**" And the reason the operator should care too: "**you and I [are] in the same boat. If you want good performance of machines, you need to understand how all machines are set, and… which games are popular and which are not. Not machines, but games.**"
- **The admission underneath it:** "**we're operating blind, so that would be a fantastic help.**"
- **And the limit he stated himself, which is the programme's structural ceiling:** "I need exactly what you need, **but you cannot get it. None of the manufacturers will give you.**" The arrangement therefore covers **JVL cabinets only** — Diamond's own response: "**within your own, but you're not seeing comparison within the location.**"
- **The existing practice it extends:** Diamond already sends a QR capture **every two weeks** for its two new machines — "That's right. That's what we're doing now with the two new ones" — with the complaint that "**nobody else does that, so we have no other way to pull the other data.**"
- **The reports it is meant to replace, rejected to the customer's face:** "**the financial data that we pull off the [Intralot]… is not reliable**… **[Intralot] generates erroneous reports**… some of the machines will have **ridiculous data, like payouts of a million dollars**", which he attributes to "**somebody['s] changed the configuration… and didn't commission**". Same rejection as Aug 27 and Aug 31, with a named mechanism. Recorded on [[wiki/organizations/diamond-amusement.md]].
- **Effect on the open contradiction below: none.** No data scope, no contract language and no payment appear in the source at all, so it is not a fourth statement of the offer — the same status as Sept 1's and Sept 2's silences. Recorded so a later reader does not read it as the programme having changed.

**The "operator intelligence" side, restated for a second customer.** The free-now-chargeable-later caveat first recorded at Dixie on Sept 8 was repeated here — "**Ninety dollars does not include charges for the intelligence, the operator's intelligence. Right now we're giving it for free, but eventually we'll start charging for it**" — with one genuinely new term: **the API is free.** "we'll give you an **API for free**, but if you want us to provide you with intelligence, then it will cost." Val's suggested consumption path — via an **MCP** using Anthropic or Grok rather than the raw API — is on [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-02-free-ports-and-pos-with-every-machine-bought.md]].
