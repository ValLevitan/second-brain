# JVL machine pricing and commercial strategy

How JVL prices its new gaming machines (the [[wiki/topics/jvl-machine-lineup.md|Flex]] lineup) into the US class-3 / skill markets — the standard retail number, the per-market premium/discount, the discount-and-quoting rules, and the cost pressures pushing the price. Machine retail and market economics only; no named-person compensation (§15.8). Set on the **Sept-23 2025 pricing meeting** ([[wiki/meetings/2025-09-23-pricing-strategy.md]], [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] + [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]]) and captured in a written "Pricing Strategy" policy summary of that meeting.

## The standard retail number

- JVL's **bill of material** for a machine lands around **$15,000-$18,000**; retail is set **between $16,000 and $18,000**, with deals "cut from there." [[raw/pricing_strategy_meeting-september-23-2025.md]]
- Val wants a **single number that everyone talks about** — a standard price JVL publishes on a "digital medium" and quotes consistently. [[raw/pricing_strategy_meeting-september-23-2025.md]]
- The written policy summary settles the machines as **"premium gaming machines," retail $16,000-$18,000**, all now shipping **with printers**. [[raw/Pricing_strategy-september-23-2025.md]]

## Market-specific pricing

Val worked the per-market numbers through live; the written policy summary codified them: [[raw/Pricing_strategy-september-23-2025.md]]

| Market | Price | vs baseline | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nebraska** | $16,000 | baseline | standard reference [[raw/pricing_strategy_meeting-september-23-2025.md]] |
| **Texas** | $16,500 | premium, no SD add-on | premium-brand positioning; the SD regulatory premium does not apply [[raw/pricing_strategy_meeting-september-23-2025.md]] |
| **South Dakota** | $17,200 | +regulatory premium | GLI soft-cost burden (below) [[raw/pricing_strategy_meeting-september-23-2025.md]] |

- **South Dakota GLI premium math:** the SD market is very small and the **GLI (lab-certification) burden very high** — Val expects to spend **~$15,000-$20,000/year extra on GLI just for South Dakota**. Normalized over ~100 machines/year that is **~$200/machine of burden**; on a **×4 market-entry multiplier** that is a **~$800 premium per machine**. So a machine sold to Nebraska at $16,000 becomes **~$16,800** in South Dakota. Val then rounded the standard SD/TX pair to **$17,200 / $16,500**. Stable SD sales are expected to top out at **~100-150 machines/year** (Val: can't raise the price once machines are stable). [[raw/pricing_strategy_meeting-september-23-2025.md]]
- Earlier in the meeting Val floated a flat **$500** GLI premium before computing the $200×4 = $800 figure — recorded as him reasoning toward the $800 number, not a separate price. [[raw/pricing_strategy_meeting-september-23-2025.md]]

## Quoting and discount rules (Val's Sept-23 policy)

- **"Dennis" (sales) may quote ONE number only** — no other number, no matrix. Val: "I don't like to have matrix circulating because somehow it escapes my digital box." Any actual deal is cut by **Val or Juan directly with the operator, bypassing Dennis**; Dennis holds only the single list number in his head. [[raw/pricing_strategy_meeting-september-23-2025.md]]
- Dennis had reportedly been quoting **$15,000 with a printer / $14,500 without**; the plan is to raise the standard number, then bring specific deals back down (e.g. "you opted for the topper, an $800 item, so it's $15,000"). [[raw/pricing_strategy_meeting-september-23-2025.md]]
- **CFO-to-CFO credit mechanism:** for a key account like [[wiki/organizations/mg-oil.md|MG Oil]], the **invoice states the full retail price ($18,000)** but the company receives a **credit applied separately, routed directly from JVL's CFO to the buyer's CFO, "bypassing everything else"** — preserving list-price integrity while still discounting. [[raw/pricing_strategy_meeting-september-23-2025.md]] [[raw/Pricing_strategy-september-23-2025.md]]
- **Volume + terms:** discounts key off units bought and terms; "people that can afford always want terms." [[raw/pricing_strategy_meeting-september-23-2025.md]]

## Competitive positioning

- At **$16,000-$17,000, JVL is lower than almost everybody in the class-3 casino space.** The **IGT** casino box for sale "has to be $18,000 or higher." **Ainsworth's** electronics sit inside an old **Grand Vision** cabinet ("at most a $5,000 cabinet, old as hell"); **Spilo** machines are old; **VLCs** are 20-year-old. Val's read: the new-machine suppliers in this space reduce to **JVL, IGT, and a new "Epic."** In Texas, Val sees "no issue positioning ourselves as a premium brand." [[raw/pricing_strategy_meeting-september-23-2025.md]]

### The Texas price reality (Juan↔Jay call, same day)

On the separate Sept-23 Texas market call ([[wiki/meetings/2025-09-23-texas-skill-game-strategy-jay-call.md]]), Texas consultant **Jay** told Juan that JVL's **~$17,000 list price is too high to enter Texas** and "you're going to have to really rethink what your price is." The Texas competitive floor: [[raw/Texas_skill_game_strategy-september-23-2025.md]]

- **Vanilla** big steel-cabinet skill game runs **~$8,000-$10,000** all-in (shipping + tax); its **boards** run **~$5,000-$9,000, generally ~$5,000** (a customer supplies the cabinet). JVL could self-build from a board + cabinet + printer + bill acceptor for **~$7,500**. [[raw/Texas_skill_game_strategy-september-23-2025.md]]
- **Pace** (distribution rights held by **Matt Paschal / E2**) **gives the game away free or ~$900** on a **30-35% revenue-share** model where "Matt's group" also takes an estimated **~5% override** on all action running through their games; Pace's **TRT terminal** was **$9,500** and is now given away too. [[raw/Texas_skill_game_strategy-september-23-2025.md]]
- Market share order per Jay: **Vanilla #1, Pace #2, Aurora #3** (plus "Paramount"). A $17k premium machine faces a hard entry against ~$8-10k incumbents on rev-share terms. [[raw/Texas_skill_game_strategy-september-23-2025.md]]

## Cost pressures pushing the price up

- **Tariffs:** a known **10-15% uplift** on JVL's bill of material is coming; the biggest driver is **South Korea (all of JVL's panels come from there)**, not China. "We cannot really avoid this." (Broader tariff context: [[wiki/topics/tariffs-and-customs-compliance.md]].) [[raw/pricing_strategy_meeting-september-23-2025.md]]
- **Printers:** every machine now ships **with a printer**, adding **~$1,000+** of retail uplift; printer cost is roughly **~$500** ("a brand-new Transact was ~$800 retail"). Refurbished JCMs may or may not still be usable — **unclear**. [[raw/pricing_strategy_meeting-september-23-2025.md]] [[raw/Pricing_strategy-september-23-2025.md]]

## Aug 21 2026 — "dollars, not discounts": the JVL-Dollars alternative to discounting

A year on from the Sept-2025 policy above, the Georgia price point in play is **$13,000** and the discount question has been reframed. On the [[wiki/meetings/2026-08-21-post-goam-show-recap.md|Aug-21 post-GAMOA recap]] [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] set out why he prefers giving **JVL Dollars** over cutting price. Full ruling: [[wiki/decisions/2026-08-21-keep-jvl-dollars-printed-serialized-cards.md]].

- **A discount is one-way; a discount cannot be undone.** "if I sell ports for $1000, that's expensive, but if I give $1000 in JVL money… then it's smart. Then I'm creating good revenue and I'm driving people back to JVL. The moment I give discounts, I could never bring the price back. I'm done." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Flex_IQ_Georgia_Recap_and_JVL_Dollars-August-21-2026.md]]
- **The dollars pull a second product into the market instead of shrinking the first.** Val's worked example, with figures he flagged as hypothetical: a customer who buys 4 Flex ports and earns, say, **$2,000** of JVL cash then looks at a **$13,000** machine and reasons "13,000, I have 2000 in cash. Why don't I buy JVL for 11,000?" — "instead of giving machine for free and discounting it. This way we're getting two things in the market." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Georgia_Machine_Sales_Strategy_Post_GOAM-August-21-2026.md]]
- **The Georgia machine price in the same conversation.** Val used **$13,000** as the reference machine price when arguing JVL should be able to sell 400–500 machines a year out of a 4,000–5,000-machine market. Felipe stated the price to **Raul at Texas Play** as "12-5" — $12,500 — because "I sell to him cheap" and Raul routes sales to about 10 masters. Juan's hypothetical show-special framing was "the machine, order now, it's $12,000 for equal payments. Buy 4 machines, get one free," and Val's answer on whether such packages exist was "Yes, we do. Yes, we do. I gave Felipe a range of discounts. He can do trade-ins." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Georgia_Machine_Sales_Strategy_Post_GOAM-August-21-2026.md]]
- **The stackable Georgia offer letter** already approved for Felipe: a base **discount**, a further discount for a **trade-in**, another for **paying in full**, and another for **sharing data**. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Georgia_Machine_Sales_Strategy_Post_GOAM-August-21-2026.md]]
- **The zero-cost alternative to a price cut** is a free software conversion of a customer's existing vertical machines, conditioned on installing JVL's cell modem — see [[wiki/decisions/2026-08-21-convert-vertical-machines-instead-of-new-trials.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Georgia_Machine_Sales_Strategy_Post_GOAM-August-21-2026.md]]
- **§15.8:** clear — machine, market and programme economics only; no named-person compensation.

## Sept 4 2026 — Val's own account of how the $15,750 and the giveaway were built, on the fly, at the show

Two days after quoting it to Lucky Coin, Val told his own product team at the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-04-naming-convention-standards-and-nova-conversion.md|Sept-4 game-development meeting]] how the number came about. It was improvised, in sequence, in front of an operator [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Naming_Convention_Standards_and_Nova_Conversion-September-4-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Naming_Convention_Standards_and_Nova_Conversion-September-4-2026.md]]:

- **Step one — the package.** "I am making a play. With operators, by giving away… **five additional 5 flex ports. One IQ and one… Flex P[ay]**… for with each machine that they buy."
- **Step two — the price rise at the show.** "I increased the price from get-go, **even at the show, to 14.5**."
- **Step three — the 15-game up-sell, improvised while Felipe was mid-pitch.** "as Felipe was talking to an operator and I was listening, **Felipe was promoting 10 game library**. And I needed to increase the price even further. So I said in the same conversation, the kind of flew, kind of flew out of my mouth too, in a way… **but with 15 games[,] we are selling at 15,750, which represents $250 per game more**, which is exactly what you want." *(The $250-per-game figure matches the Sept-1 up-sell rate recorded above.)*
- **Step four — fencing the giveaway to the higher price.** "then I didn't really offer that package deal. But then when I added the package deal, then I said **this package deal is only valid for the $15,750 machine**."
- **It landed.** "**The operator actually took very well to 15 [games]. It actually went very well.**"
- **The ceiling he is aiming at.** "I need to get closer to **18 plus 1000**[;] then I can discount it. I could provide more. And grab the market"; and for the years after, "**in 2027, 28**… the transactional equipment should be something more expensive that they want[,] that we discount or package together. And so **I need to get to that 18, $19,000 level**." Val's own name for the strategy: "that's the strategy."
- **The bet on the customer's psychology:** "Operator should say[,] I'm getting the stuff for free I want. I don't even care… I'm getting all this stuff for free"; "that free stuff, that big value should… have an enormous effect." Michael: "**Because they all love a deal.**" Val: "And they squeeze the living daylights out of me. And there is… nothing more… joyful than squeezing the living daylights out of JVL."

**The constraint the price rests on.** The machine can only *load* about ten games today. Val's workaround is remote enablement: "the operator understands that we can only load 10 games, but **they buy 15 games. And because we have a modem, we'll be able to set it through the modem. Enable it through the modem**, and they're… ecstatic about it; they're not even thinking about showing all 15 games." Lena put the display limit at twelve ("12, 12, we never show more than 12"); Juan had first heard 14 then 10. The limit is **memory, not maths** — Efim: "it's what's in the box is graphical assets… We want almost everything build ready when you [press the button], otherwise you have loading time," Val: "it's a memory." A RAM upgrade would fix it but "RAM… right now, it's crazy expensive" (Val); "RAM was not cheap now, right?" (Efim). Val settled on designing for **14** games on the selection screen: "It doesn't have to be 15. Could be 14." Michael: "14 is a great number." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Naming_Convention_Standards_and_Nova_Conversion-September-4-2026.md]]

**One hardware cost stated in passing:** the V-deck costs JVL "probably **120** our cost" against a plain panel with one button — Michael: "that's a lot of money." See [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-04-georgia-v4-mechanical-button-deck.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Naming_Convention_Standards_and_Nova_Conversion-September-4-2026.md]]

## Sept 4 2026 (midday) — the giveaway costed from the inside: $12,820 of product against a $2,930 increase

Hours before telling the game team how the $15,750 came about (section above), Val walked [[wiki/people/genadi-peari.md|Genadi Peari]] through the same offer as arithmetic, on the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-04-flex-slim-first-ten-and-flex-port-sas.md|midday production call]]. ## Sept 16 2026 — the doctrine behind all of the above, stated as a full rule

*(Heading corrected: an earlier, shorter form of the no-discount rule was said on Jan 8 2026 — see that section above.)*

On [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-16-jvl-transactional-ecosystem-and-class-a-market-strategy.md|the afternoon strategy session]] [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] argued the whole pricing position from first principles and against a direct objection from JVL's games lead: **operators buy on cash flow, not price, so extend terms rather than cut the number; if a discount is forced, discount from a high price or add value instead; make the Class B machine expensive and make it look expensive; fight for every ten dollars on Class A.** The full argument, the objection, and the $16,000 / $30,000 bundle framing said in the same conversation are on [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-16-do-not-discount-hold-price-extend-terms-add-value.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JVL_Transactional_Ecosystem_and_Class_A_Market_Strategy-September-16-2026.md]].

**It is not reconciled with the field practice catalogued above** — the $15,750 machine with ~$7,000 of free hardware attached is exactly the "add value instead of cutting the price" move, but no source connects the two, and the doctrine is stated without reference to any of the quotes on this page.

## Sept 29 2026 — the transactional fee goes from $90 to $150, "100 months" to the equipment price, and the pitch becomes a lease

On the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-29-larry-sharon-lease-pricing-problem-b-and-the-cash-plus-revenue-share-pivot.md|Sept-29 6:21 PM call]] with his investor partner [[wiki/people/larry-sharon.md|Larry Sharon]], [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] set out the pricing problem of the transactional equipment in his own numbers [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Larry_Sharon_Lease_Pricing_and_Profit_Repatriation-September-29-2026.md]]:

- **$90 told, $150 needed.** "Я всем сказал… если ты покупаешь машину, то мы тебе это даем за девяносто долларов сервис-чардж. Но сейчас мне нужно было взять сто пятьдесят долларов" — everyone was told $90 a month with a machine purchase; now it has to be $150.
- **The customer's arithmetic.** At $15,000 for the equipment bought outright ("Все equipment, которое я тебе перечислил, я попрошу пятнадцать тысяч"), $150 a month is "сто месяцев, это не шестьдесят месяцев" — 100 months, not the 60 a five-year contract implies; "где еще сорок месяцев они возьмут".
- **$200 would fix the math; Val will not go there.** "We should charge closer to two hundred dollars. I wouldn't want to charge it two hundred dollars because I would want to penetrate the market." Larry: $175 or $200 only pays the investor note off faster ("from nine years to… five or six").
- **Why $150 is "palatable".** "competitors are approximately the highest competitors at the same price. Plus… he doesn't have the same equipment. Plus… limited capabilities." The competitor "is charging upfront" for equipment; "Он берет четырнадцать долларов с машины в месяц" ($14 per machine per month); "сто пятьдесят долларов он берет" is also said — which charge is which is **unclear**, and no competitor is named. *(Compare the Sept-15 "ten dollars per machine" and the Sept-10 "card or gift card processing" exclusion above; nothing reconciles them.)*
- **The operating-cost baseline behind the investor model:** about 10% of revenue, "fifteen dollars", on a ten-year machine life (a 49-second exchange at 5:01 PM the same day) [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Machine_Life_and_Operating_Cost_Baseline-September-29-2026.md]].
- **The framing changes to a lease.** "Мы делаем за 150 долларов минимум" — nothing is free; $150 a month is the floor; terms of 3, 4, 5, 7 and 10 years to be modelled, with a buy-out or return at the end; Val floated "$199" for a three-year term. Ruling on [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-29-pitch-the-transactional-equipment-as-a-lease-with-set-terms-and-a-buy-out-or-return-not-as-free.md]]. **Owner ruling 2026-10-05: still under consideration — no decision made yet on the pitch.**

**This is the first source that puts a number on the gap between the customer-facing "$90 per location" of Sept 2–17 and the "$150" of the Sept-28 investor model** — and Val's own account of why the two cannot both stand. The $15,750 machine price is not mentioned on this call; "$15,000" here is the price of the transactional set bought outright.

## Oct 7 2026 — cash, three-year financing, or rent; $199 a month for three years; nothing shorter than three years (a proposal)

On the [[wiki/meetings/2026-10-07-larry-sharon-hardware-rental-pricing-and-november-pilot.md|Oct-7 4:19 PM call]] with [[wiki/people/larry-sharon.md|Larry Sharon]], [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] priced the transactional equipment again [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Larry_Hardware_Rental_Pricing_and_November_Pilot-October-7-2026.md]]:

- **Three paths.** Cash; financing "Say three years for them to buy it out… roughly fifteen thousand dollars divided by thirty-six months"; and rent, "the most attractive way".
- **The cost under the price.** Larry: "the machine costing four K, not fifteen." Val: "four thousand dollars divided by thirty six months is hundred eleven dollars per month… You want to recapture more."
- **The customer's arithmetic, again.** "150 times 60 months, 9 тысяч долларов… is not четырнадцать" — the same gap Val named on Sept 29 above.
- **The numbers on the table.** Rent: three years at $199 a month ("Just short of seventy-two hundred") and a five-year option, all costs included (management, updates, upgrades); nothing shorter than three years except a cash sale. Buy: cash, or three years of financing on $14,000 plus interest (Val's rough example, about 11% a year, "one thousand five hundred forty"; the monthly "three sixty-two" does not follow from the stated numbers — **inference**). Larry's earlier model: $150–180 for five-to-nine-year terms, $205–225 for three years.
- **Status.** Both men said they can live with it. Under the owner's 2026-10-05 ruling, prices worked out on these calls are **proposals until the owner decides** — see [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-29-pitch-the-transactional-equipment-as-a-lease-with-set-terms-and-a-buy-out-or-return-not-as-free.md]] (Oct-7 update).

Against Sept 29: the $150 floor stays as the five-year example; the three-year price is now a number ($199); and the equipment's outright price is spoken as $14,000–15,000 ($14,000 in the financing example) [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Larry_Hardware_Rental_Pricing_and_November_Pilot-October-7-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Larry_Hardware_Rental_Pricing_and_November_Pilot-October-7-2026.md]].
