# Low-cost street machine (sub-$2,000 street cabinet program)

A durable, multi-meeting JVL hardware program (June 2026) to build a stripped-down, very cheap "street" slot machine — a self-contained single-screen cabinet — aimed at **price-sensitive markets like Texas where JVL's Flex cabinet is too expensive to sell** and where street prices have dropped a lot. The guiding rule Val set is: **strip the cabinet, do not cheapen the games** — the machine loses hardware, not picture quality. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Low_Cost_Street_Cabinet_Platform-June-9-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Low_Cost_Street_Machine_Design-June-8-2026.md]]

The thread runs across the [[wiki/meetings/2026-06-08-low-cost-street-machine-design.md|June-8 design opener]] (cost levers + business model), a [[wiki/meetings/2026-06-09-low-cost-street-cabinet-platform.md|June-9 cabinet-platform feasibility read]] (the compute board), and a [[wiki/meetings/2026-06-09-street-machine-industrial-design.md|June-9 industrial-design walkthrough]] (the cabinet itself); the [[wiki/meetings/2026-06-16-street-cabinet-design-direction.md|June-16 design-direction session]] adds the functional-requirements pass (plastic back cavity, VIDEK-lip peripherals, local assembly). The cabinet aesthetic/design direction is also carried on [[wiki/decisions/2025-02-27-flex-street-cabinet-design-direction.md]], and the sell-cheap-plus-recurring-fee business model on [[wiki/topics/flexpay-business-architecture-payout-devices.md]].

## The build target and economics

- **Whole device under $2,000, including the bill acceptor.** Val counts the bill acceptor at ~$800, so the machine itself must land around **$1,200**. Cost is cut in many small places because "it adds up." [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Low_Cost_Street_Machine_Design-June-8-2026.md]]
- **Cut components, not visuals.** Savings come from removing hardware, not from downgrading the picture. Switching the bill acceptor saves about **$600**; dropping the amplifier, light board, light controller, counters board and VDEC off the current Flex saves roughly **$500–600 in parts**; the bare metal cabinet is only ~$300, so little is left to save there. Parts prices are rising (the compute board doubled, memory up ~4×). [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Low_Cost_Street_Machine_Design-June-8-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Low_Cost_Street_Cabinet_Platform-June-9-2026.md]]
- **Not a low-quality product.** Games keep normal resolution and color; Val explicitly rejected dropping to the animation-only level of Biratko's online games. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Low_Cost_Street_Cabinet_Platform-June-9-2026.md]]
- **Business model.** Sell the machine cheap (~$5–6k, rough) and earn a **recurring connection fee** (~$3/day, or ~10%); the recurring remote services are what make selling cheap machines a viable business (details on [[wiki/topics/flexpay-business-architecture-payout-devices.md]]). [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Low_Cost_Street_Machine_Design-June-8-2026.md]]

## The compute platform (RK3568 → RK3588)

Because the topper and video board are dropped, the machine can move **off x86 to a cheaper ARM board** — NOVA already runs on ARM, so it is not tied to x86. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Low_Cost_Street_Cabinet_Platform-June-9-2026.md]]

- **Step up from RK3568 to RK3588.** The team has Rockchip **RK3568** boards but only at **2 GB RAM**, which is too little. Pavel and Genadi favor the **RK3588** — about twice the cores, 4× video decode, handles 8K — for only **~$10 more**; the **RK3576** is an in-between option. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Low_Cost_Street_Cabinet_Platform-June-9-2026.md]]
- **Memory sizing is the main open question.** Each game is ~**500–700 MB** and must load fully into RAM to switch quickly (that is why Flex uses 16 GB). Capping the machine at **~6 games** — which fits the planned subscription/ELT model — means **4 GB might work**, but 6–8 GB gives headroom, especially with progressives. Decision: **order 8 GB sample boards, measure real use, then size down** rather than guessing. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Low_Cost_Street_Cabinet_Platform-June-9-2026.md]]
- **Order proper samples from China, standardize on one maker.** Val rejected buying an **Orange Pi 5** (RK3588, 16 GB) locally in Minsk; he wants **two sample boards ordered from a more reliable / cost-effective manufacturer in China**, and that manufacturer standardized on for support across all products. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Low_Cost_Street_Cabinet_Platform-June-9-2026.md]]
- **Test path — port Calle Ocho.** Port **Calle Ocho** (the only game currently on NOVA) to the RK board, strip the topper and video-decoder components, run it, then load **5–6 copies at once** to measure memory use and performance. This can start now on the existing RK3568 boards while the RK3588 samples are ordered. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Low_Cost_Street_Cabinet_Platform-June-9-2026.md]]
- **Rising board price.** The RK3568 supplier now wants **$100** (up from $50 in December), driven by memory cost. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Low_Cost_Street_Cabinet_Platform-June-9-2026.md]]

## The cabinet / industrial design

- **Single-screen, vertical 43" HD display** with a good black level; **no topper and no static top** — the whole design must be self-contained on the one screen. The **touchscreen covers the full screen** (not half), because skill features sit at the top and need to be touchable. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Street_Machine_Industrial_Design-June-9-2026.md]]
- **Screen built into the cabinet** — a shallow panel, glass over a single metal cover/channel — not hung outside like Flex. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Street_Machine_Industrial_Design-June-9-2026.md]]
- **Monolithic sheet-metal body** (cheapest), not split into a face plus extra parts; a cast/molded face would need a mold. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Street_Machine_Industrial_Design-June-9-2026.md]]
- **A single door** with the bill acceptor and cassette (nightstand-style, no separate front access in the strip-to-the-bone framing). [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Low_Cost_Street_Machine_Design-June-8-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Low_Cost_Street_Cabinet_Platform-June-9-2026.md]]
- **Placement in formations.** Machines set up in clean shapes — a straight line, back-to-back, triangle or square; backs need handles and wheels for moving; floors will not be level (these are not casinos) so the legs need a big adjustment range, levelable **from the top** without reaching underneath. Player seating is unresolved. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Street_Machine_Industrial_Design-June-9-2026.md]]
- **Peripherals.** Leaning to a **TITO ticket printer** (cheapest), hidden in the door/bezel to keep the machine slim, with paper pre-sized for the US region; the bill validator must also support TITO/rating tickets (JCM has options, model TBD). Genadi proposed a **back-load payload bill validator** that drops down and opens together with the front-service door — Val agreed and said "make it a provision." **About 8 buttons**, one large spin button, with the panel flipping from the back for easy button swaps. (The June-9 platform meeting had pushed a harder strip of **≤2 buttons** as the extreme target; the industrial-design meeting settled the fuller cabinet on ~8.) [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Street_Machine_Industrial_Design-June-9-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Low_Cost_Street_Cabinet_Platform-June-9-2026.md]]
- **Lights.** Val's lean is **one perimeter light line** — proven, industry-standard, not expensive, "won't cut $10 to make it worse"; Genadi pushed two vertical strips or reusing the Flex light assembly, and a halo light shows poorly in bright street locations. Not fully locked. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Street_Machine_Industrial_Design-June-9-2026.md]]
- **Audio.** No amplifier and no subwoofer — use the Rockchip board's built-in **3 W/channel amp** with two small fixed front-facing speakers (~$20 saved). [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Street_Machine_Industrial_Design-June-9-2026.md]]
- **Self-built bill-path "nose"/bezel with an integrated QR-code reader** (the June-8 idea) replaces the $35 plastic nose plus the complex player-tracking port and its screen. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Low_Cost_Street_Machine_Design-June-8-2026.md]]

## Service and recurring revenue as the core

Val stressed repeatedly that **service is the core of the business**. Front service access is mandatory, and every peripheral — bill validator, printer, buttons, screen — must have **two-way feedback**, so operators can see status remotely (is the screen working, is a button dead), push software updates over the air, and pull service and accounting data. Without those recurring services, selling cheap machines alone is a hard business; the feedback hooks are what make the services sellable. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Street_Machine_Industrial_Design-June-9-2026.md]]

## Shared progressive

Because street machines stand close together in a bank, link them through a **cloud "progressive" control** into a shared jackpot/bonus displayed across the row of screens — half-screen, third-screen, or the whole row for a big bonus, like a scoreboard. Val considers this a strong feature. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Street_Machine_Industrial_Design-June-9-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Low_Cost_Street_Cabinet_Platform-June-9-2026.md]]

## Jun 16 2026 — the low-cost street cabinet: functional requirements, plastic back cavity, and local assembly

On the [[wiki/meetings/2026-06-16-street-cabinet-design-direction.md|June-16 4:53pm ET street-cabinet design-direction session]] ([[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/genadi-peari.md|Genadi Peari]]) Val walked Genadi through **functional requirements** for the new low-cost street cabinet aimed at **non-casino locations** (bars, convenience stores, route locations). Genadi pushed early to keep functional requirements separate from design decisions; Val agreed he was crossing into design territory but said his intent was to describe the machine functionally. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Street_Cabinet_Design_Direction-June-16-2026.md]]

- **Commercial target.** A **vertical single-screen** machine selling at **$5,000**, produced at **~1,000 units/month** (12,000/year). (Decision.) [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Street_Cabinet_Design_Direction-June-16-2026.md]]
- **Physical concept — a plastic back-cavity shell (new proposal, unresolved).** Val proposed a **monolithic plastic back cavity (shell)** as the structural frame, with a **43–49 inch HD vertical touchscreen (Gorilla Glass) bonded to the front** — not field-serviceable, "like an iPhone." This shifts from the [[#the-cabinet--industrial-design|June-9 monolithic sheet-metal body]]; it was **not resolved**. Genadi's concerns: mold tooling ~**$150K** (needs 5,000+ units to break even), **ABS softens in heat** (would need carbon-fiber or nylon reinforcement, eliminating the savings), and it may cost more than metal. Val's counter: because the screen mounts monolithically and nothing disassembles, the plastic depth is only ~**4 inches** with ~2-inch ribs — not truly load-bearing — so ~**$100 vs the current ~$300** metal cabinet. Genadi remained unconvinced but said he'd think about it. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Street_Cabinet_Design_Direction-June-16-2026.md]]
- **Peripheral layout — kill the dashboard, everything under the VIDEK lip.** Eliminate the traditional dashboard panel between screen and the lower VIDEK area; **all peripherals (bill validator, printer, QR scanner) move underneath the VIDEK lip** — a shallow tray/shelf with slots for bill insertion and receipt output. Everything sits on a **slide-out metal-frame drawer** that pulls out the back for servicing, with a **single cable connection** to the display; speakers integrate into the front plastic panel below the VIDEK. Removing player tracking frees substantial space in the old dashboard area, which is what makes the bill validator fit under the lip. (Both agreed after Val clarified the lip/shelf concept.) [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Street_Cabinet_Design_Direction-June-16-2026.md]]
- **Lighting — three zones on one API.** A perimeter light around the screen edge, a transition radius, and "washing" lights on the front panel — all controlled by **game designers through the API as a unified perimeter**. (Decision.) [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Street_Cabinet_Design_Direction-June-16-2026.md]]
- **Other settled points.** **No player tracking or flag support** (never needed on the street model); **support for two displays** (vertical main + deck); a **slam button on the right side** of the VIDEK panel; a **single rear connection** to an external controller. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Street_Cabinet_Design_Direction-June-16-2026.md]]
- **Local assembly only — no Chinese semi-finished imports.** Assemble locally from **individual component suppliers** rather than importing Chinese semi-finished units, to (a) maintain credit terms from each supplier separately, (b) avoid shipping dependencies, and (c) potentially qualify for local tax benefits; assembly can distribute to **sub-shops (Georgia, Texas)**. (Val's reasoning, accepted by Genadi.) [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Street_Cabinet_Design_Direction-June-16-2026.md]]
- **Market context.** Val was in **Omaha** (Texas the day before), visiting route locations in both states; **Texas alone has ~200,000 machines** in the grey/illegal market, where operators care about **cost and revenue share, not brand** — JVL needs a price point even Chinese competitors can't match. Val cited the **ECO cabinet** (designed for a 3–5 year life, still running after 25 years) as proof that simple construction lasts. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Street_Cabinet_Design_Direction-June-16-2026.md]]
- **Open items.** Plastic vs metal back cavity (needs a concrete cost comparison); bill-validator type and back-load vs front-load placement within the drawer; printer service access; exact screen size (43–49 inch range set, final pick depends on cost sourcing). Once the Transaction Management work wraps, the street cabinet becomes top priority. §15.8: device/BOM/build-cost economics only (the $5,000 sell target and mold/cabinet costs are product economics); no named-person compensation. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Street_Cabinet_Design_Direction-June-16-2026.md]]

## Jul 29 2026 — RK3588 memory findings: the sizing question answered

The [[wiki/meetings/2026-07-29-rk3588-game-performance-findings.md|Jul-29 7:00am ET findings meeting]] ([[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/pavel.md|Pavel Frolov]], [[wiki/people/alexander-biratka.md|Alexander Biratko]], [[wiki/people/irina-shishko.md|Irina Shishko]]) reported measured results of running a Nova game on the RK3588, answering the [[#the-compute-platform-rk3568--rk3588|memory-sizing question]] above. The board plays the base game smoothly at ~60 FPS; the tested **4 GB board** causes short stutters on bonus/free-spin transitions because it runs out of RAM and swaps textures. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_RK3588_Game_Performance_Findings-July-29-2026.md]]

- **Memory sizing — 16 GB for 8 games.** One game plus the system plus LGS uses roughly **5 GB**; each additional game adds at least **1 GB**. So 8 games is about 5 + 7 = **12 GB**, and a **16 GB board leaves ~4 GB headroom**. An 8 GB board only covers about 2 games. Boards come in 4, 8 and 16 GB; onboard storage (32–64 GB) is more than enough for years of games. Textures could be hand-optimized to trim roughly **200 MB per game** (from ~1 GB toward ~800 MB) if needed. **Decision: order an RK3588 with 16 GB RAM** (RAM is soldered, so a different board is required — not an upgrade). [[raw/Meeting_Notes_RK3588_Game_Performance_Findings-July-29-2026.md]]
- **Multi-progressive / multi-product.** Irina asked whether running multi-progressives that mix games from different products (Caliente + Peaches + Jackpot Rush) would change the picture once ported to Nova; Alexander said it would add some resources per game but likely isn't critical, and the 16 GB estimate still holds. Irina noted the current multi image has grown ~30% versus the old one, approaching ~8 GB. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_RK3588_Game_Performance_Findings-July-29-2026.md]]
- **Board and display decisions.** RK3588 confirmed as the evaluation platform (RK3576 comparable, RK3399 older and no faster). The board supports at most 2 monitors, so no horizontal layout — only a compact one-main-plus-topper. One video interface is LVDS (impractical); the team prefers a board variant with **2–3 uniform HDMI outputs**. A progressive controller would add a database + controller app (a bit more load). Becoming a **Rockchip developer costs ~$3,000**, likely per platform (the earlier application was for the RK3568; a fresh request for the RK3588 is likely needed — Pavel to check). §15.8: board/memory/BOM economics only; no named-person compensation. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_RK3588_Game_Performance_Findings-July-29-2026.md]]

## Aug 14 2026 — machine-design direction from the NOVA wishlist: capability over cost, buttons over VDEC, removable toppers

On the [[wiki/meetings/2026-08-14-nova-capability-wishlist.md|Aug-14 NOVA wishlist call]] ([[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/michael-gottlieb.md|Michael Gottlieb]], [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan Martinez]], [[wiki/people/lena.md|Lena Zhukova]], [[wiki/people/efim-podvoiski.md|Efim Podvoiski]]) Val polled the group through a machine-design run-through for the new FP7 cabinet. The focus is explicitly the **street market** — Val sees little Class-3 business in the next five years. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_NOVA_Capability_Wishlist-August-14-2026-Part_2.md]]

- **Go with the higher-capability CPU/graphics package and scrap the cheaper board.** Given a choice, Val would "rather overpay and choose the very capable one and keep pushing towards very capable all the time"; Juan and Michael agreed on Moore's-Law logic (don't ship an 8-year-old CPU; components only get cheaper). Not every game must use every feature — capability is headroom. Two hardware tiers ("expensive and inexpensive") was rejected — one high-capability platform. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_NOVA_Capability_Wishlist-August-14-2026-Part_2.md]]
- **Mechanical buttons over a VDEC.** Michael (controversially) would spend the money on the CPU/motherboard rather than the VDEC (~$1,500 part vs ~$5/button); competitors (Light & Wonder, Aristocrat, older IGT) and the street markets do fine with physical buttons. Juan and Val agreed. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_NOVA_Capability_Wishlist-August-14-2026-Part_2.md]]
- **Toppers: removable/optional, never a permanent fixture** — seamlessly joined to the top monitor so it looks like one continuous screen, but removable so extra height never costs a sale (a great "come-on" for progressive signage). [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_NOVA_Capability_Wishlist-August-14-2026-Part_2.md]]
- **Perimeter lights: yes — a definitive-line perimeter light tied to game activity** (celebratory "carnival" lighting). Avoid garish two-inch Light-&-Wonder-style strips, and avoid distracting lighting around the VDEC (fatiguing, pulls the eye off the reels). No two different light types (extra mechanical failure point). [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_NOVA_Capability_Wishlist-August-14-2026-Part_2.md]]
- **No wedge machines / LCD-screen wedges** — a waste of money and space in street rows of 4–7 machines (no room for pie/circle formats). [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_NOVA_Capability_Wishlist-August-14-2026-Part_2.md]]
- **Parked or rejected ideas.** 3D no-glasses toppers — parked (not needed for street; 3D historically makes people sick). Promo credits in street locations — parked (needs player tracking, raises jurisdictional issues; "not all business is good business" — the Treasure Island $200-free-play and Hard Rock free-bus examples show free play draws non-customers who crowd out regulars). An at-home machine as a dumb terminal to a legal offshore server for real money — parked (huge project, needs state-by-state approval and player tracking, plus social/psychological risk). Camera / player identification — interesting but parked over regulatory/privacy concerns; the anonymous alternative is posting a screenshot of the machine's own jackpot-lockup screen (no human in frame). Social-media win sharing — rejected (players are private about money). [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_NOVA_Capability_Wishlist-August-14-2026-Part_2.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_NOVA_Capability_Wishlist-August-14-2026-Part_2.md]]

## Sept 16 2026 — the electronics get specified, and the cabinet gets a shape

**Val's own description of the machine, in one pass:** "**очень, очень простая машина за тысячу долларов**", running on the compute board (transcript "**рк 38 76**"), with a bill validator "**как об[ыч]но**" and **JVL's own FlexPort**. **Five things were actually fixed, each on its own page:**

- **The electronics layout** — two boards (a new I/O board plus a button/counter board), the I/O board carrying a USB hub, 2 SAS, a printer port and a bill-acceptor port (**ID003 + ccTalk**, pulse ruled out of gaming without discussion), plus lighting support; the amplifier dropped for the TV's own; no electromechanical counters; **cell modem and WiFi both mandatory**. → [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-16-the-street-machine-carries-two-boards-and-a-new-io-board.md]]
- **The memory cut** — the **six 256 FRAM chips go to one**, the per-game statistics (last 10 games and last 50 free spins, for each of six games) move out to an SD card or the eMMC, and low-power mode is **laid into the design but not populated**. → [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-16-one-fram-chip-instead-of-six-and-low-power-mode-laid-in-but-not-populated.md]]
- **Serviceability** — electronics at **eye level** in a metal box screwed to the back of the TV; the monitor **hinged like a cupboard door**, opening no more than **90°**; no podium, no base, no two-screen-to-horizontal conversion. Driven by a recording Val holds of technicians refusing to buy JVL machines, not by preference. → [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-16-street-machine-electronics-move-to-eye-level-and-the-monitor-opens-like-a-cupboard-door.md]]
- **Machine identity** — the cabinet-bound encrypted passport component is removed and identity binds to **JVL's own I/O board**, because JVL does not change its own board but may change processor suppliers. → [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-16-machine-identity-moves-off-the-cabinet-passport-onto-jvls-own-io-board.md]]
