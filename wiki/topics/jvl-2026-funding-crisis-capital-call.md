# JVL 2026 funding crisis and capital call

In August 2026 JVL ran out of operating cash and [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] moved to raise roughly **$1 million** through a **capital call** on the company's partners. This page tracks that funding arc: why the cash ran out, the numbers Val put to partners, the ownership/debt picture, and the alternative (short-term "pref debt" secured by an approved tax refund) that emerged before any cash-call letter went out. Val made the calls one partner at a time, starting with the phone rather than "circulating paper." [[raw/Meeting_Notes_JVL_Funding_Crisis_Cash_Call_Doug_Skrepnek-August-7-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_JVL_Liquidity_Crisis_Cash_Call_Louis_Glenn-August-8-2026.md]]

The planning started earlier. In July 2026 Val was already saying "JVL is running out of cash" and worked out with his lawyer [[wiki/people/alan.md|Alan Litwack]] how to structure a raise offered to the warrant holders (see the July 2026 History section below). [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Alan_Litwack_Convertible_Debenture_Design-July-10-2026.md]]

It "could come next month or six months from now." Together with Val's ~$1M, that is about **$2.8M**, roughly a year of runway to turn the company around. - **No $100M exit.** Val was explicit he sees **no hundred-million-dollar exit strategy** — the engineering is in Belarus, which makes the company hard to sell, and there are **no company-owned profit-share machines** left to use as security (Mexico closed, Peru closed, Colombia being milked). ## Why the cash ran out — the Georgia freeze

Val traced the crisis to the **Linux-kernel + AMD-processor freeze** that silently locked machines with the game picture still on screen, so they quietly stopped earning; machines **lost revenue for about half a year** before anyone understood why (Val suspected games and content first), and it "eradicated" the accumulated cash. The fix took about two weeks once found, and machines now perform well, but sales must be won back ("take our product for free" is the uphill pitch), with no recourse against the vendor. The full technical investigation and fix are on [[wiki/topics/egm-freezing-investigation.md]]. On the Aug-7 call Val gave the kernel loosely as **"Linux 4.7"**; the established figure from the investigation is **kernel 4.19** (upgraded to 5.15 LTS) — treat "4.7" as a retrospective misstatement, not a new fact. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JVL_Funding_Crisis_Cash_Call_Doug_Skrepnek-August-7-2026.md]]

Contributing factors Val named: the **COVID collapse of the casino market** (casinos stopped buying), forcing the switch to the **street market** (variety stores, gas stations) and the Georgia expansion; **competitors squeezing Georgia** last year; and **tariffs forcing the factory move to Georgia** and closure of the local factory (which did cut the expense line). [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JVL_Liquidity_Crisis_Cash_Call_Louis_Glenn-August-8-2026.md]]

## Doug's alternative — short-term "pref debt" secured by the CRA refund

Rather than send a bare cash-call letter (which some partners might answer badly "after five years of silence"), Doug urged Val to **borrow short-term money** — which needs **no partner approval** — and repay it from the SR&ED/media refund when it lands. His structure: **pref (preferred) debt** that sits in front of everyone, effectively **secured by the approved CRA refund** with a **pay-direction** so the lender is repaid automatically when the money comes in. Doug offered to **co-fund** it (illustratively ~$300K from him and ~$300K from Val to bridge ~90 days) and, asked directly, confirmed he would participate in the form of pref debt. Both also recommended the **CRA complaints-department** route to speed the refund (each had used it before and been paid within days/weeks). Sizing came down over the call from ~$1M to **maybe ~$600K to get through ninety days** if trade-show orders come. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JVL_Funding_Crisis_Cash_Call_Doug_Skrepnek-August-7-2026.md]]

## History — July 2026: the Litwack design for the raise

Three short calls with [[wiki/people/alan.md|Alan Litwack]] came before the August cash calls. The Aug-14 warrant-holder notice (same terms for every holder) follows the principle set here.

- **Jul 7 — ask the warrant holders, same terms for all.** Val wanted ~$1M from the warrant holders; Litwack said they cannot be forced, the terms must be attractive, and the warrant holders should get the same terms Val takes. Detail on [[wiki/topics/jvl-corporate-loans-and-shareholder-financing.md]] (Jul 7). [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_warrant_holder_1M_cash_injection_Litwack-July-7-2026.txt]]
- **Jul 10 — Val's picture.** "JVL is running out of cash. But actually, prospect looks quite good." Val named the transactional product "about to launch", the Nebraska licence, and good South Dakota location tests. He thought the transactional product could "recreate what cash code did, and maybe even more." He expects the warrant holders most likely will not contribute. He also wants to dilute [[wiki/people/boris.md|Boris]] and [[wiki/people/peter.md|Peter]] — "I don't mean to forcefully take take them out… but in a way diluting their interest." Litwack: "you are going to dilute everybody." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Alan_Litwack_Convertible_Debenture_Design-July-10-2026.md]]
- **Jul 10 — the instrument: a secured convertible debenture.** Litwack's design: new money comes in as **secured debt** with a yield "at mezz rates" (mezzanine finance). It either carries warrants or converts into equity "based on today's value, which is in the toilet." If the company does not turn around, the lender gets its money out first. If it does, the debt converts on a very dilutive basis — "you do it like at a penny, a share type thing" (Litwack: "we have to think it through a little more"). [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Alan_Litwack_Convertible_Debenture_Design-July-10-2026.md]]
- **Jul 10 — why the same offer goes to everyone.** If Val puts money in and the company later does very well, holders could say with "twenty twenty hindsight" that he gave himself too good a deal. Offering everyone the same terms makes that complaint "very difficult". Litwack also suggested a short story to go with the offer: how the company got here, that it kept working "so that we could realize the value for our shareholders", that "we're on the doorstep", and that the terms must be favourable but everyone may take part. Val: "Yep, agreed." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Alan_Litwack_Convertible_Debenture_Design-July-10-2026.md]]
- **Jul 10 — illustrative dilution math.** Litwack: if the company has no value today, "maybe" $1M "should get you fifty percent of the company." Val worked it through: at about half the company now, a $1M round for 50% with no other takers would leave him about 75%; Litwack: "not quite but a lot more." Val put his current stake at "fifty eight percent right now", then "about fifty four or fifty eight percent; I can't remember"; counting all the warrants, "my dilution amounts to about forty six percent." (Consistent with the Aug-8 "56–58%" above.) [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Alan_Litwack_Convertible_Debenture_Design-July-10-2026.md]]
- **Jul 10 — Litwack's comparable.** A client company raised money for years at a dollar a share, "always… turning the corner", then brought in venture capital as convertible preferred shares; the original common shares are now valued at one cent, a 99% fall. "That's what happens sometimes." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Alan_Litwack_Convertible_Debenture_Design-July-10-2026.md]]
- **Jul 10 — next step.** Val: "I'll put a draft together. I'll run it by you, depending on your comments, we'll do final adjustments." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Alan_Litwack_Convertible_Debenture_Design-July-10-2026.md]]
- **Jul 15 — draft letter exists; new money is paid back first.** By Jul 15 Val had a letter. Litwack: the new raise "has security. It just means the new money gets paid back ahead of the existing debt" — e.g. if the new raise is $5M, "the five million gets paid back first, then the eleven million". Val: "Which is exactly… what my letter describes." Litwack repeated the same-offer protection: if the company turns around and "most of everything else goes to you", holders may call it "self dealing"; the answer is "I made the same offer available to everybody, so now you can't complain." Holders who decline "don't have to." (The "$5M" is Litwack's example, not a set size.) [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Alan_Litwack_Warrant_History_and_New_Raise_Priority-July-15-2026.md]]
- **Jul 15 — Doug's likely condition.** Val expects [[wiki/people/doug-skrepnek.md|Doug]], if he puts money in, to say JVL must keep paying against "the… fifty thousand dollars a month commitment." Val's answer: JVL has several obligations and will keep managing them "depending on our cash flow." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Alan_Litwack_Warrant_History_and_New_Raise_Priority-July-15-2026.md]]

*(Evolution, not contradiction: the July plan was a secured convertible debenture; by Aug 12–14 the working instrument was a short bridge loan (pref debt, 4% fee + 10% interest) secured by the CRA refund, offered to all warrant holders on the same terms. The "same terms for everyone" principle carried through.)*
