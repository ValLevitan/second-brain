# Georgia prize-card processor economics

Where the money goes in the Georgia COAM gift-card chain, worked out by [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] in real time at the [[wiki/meetings/2026-08-31-dixie-amusement-kiosk-wait-and-see.md|Aug-31 2026 Dixie Amusement visit]] from a **Fiery Pay fee schedule** that [[wiki/people/salim-khambawala.md|Salim Khambawala]] had attached to one of his cards and handed over. It is the commercial premise behind [[wiki/decisions/2026-08-31-transactional-equipment-only-with-location-exclusivity.md]] — JVL's $1-per-card margin is the smallest number on this page. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## The fee sheet

Val asked for the document and kept it: "Can I look at the chart that you have?… **I need this. Where did you get this? This is on the card. Attached to the card.** I didn't open mine. **Can I have this?**" Salim: "This is from the [Fiery Pay] stuff." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

| Fee | Amount | Source line |
|---|---|---|
| Activation | **$2** | "**activation fees is two dollars**" |
| Dormancy | **$2.95 per month** | "**You will be charged two ninety five each month after you have not completed the transaction**" |
| Lost / replacement card | **$6** | "**they charge six bucks for a lost card**" |
| Per transaction | **3–5%** | "**It's about three to five percent. That's what I heard. Per transaction.**" |

[[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

- **The sheet is silent on the transaction fee.** Val, reading it: "**They're silent on the transaction itself. I don't know how much they charge for the transaction.**" The 3–5% is what he had heard elsewhere, not what the document says.
- **Who pays it was disputed in the room.** Salim believed the merchant absorbs it — "if they go to Walmart, **Walmart pays it. The card holder doesn't pay**" — while the JVL side said "**They do pay. They do pay. On [Fiery Pay].**" Unresolved in the source.
- **Val's total:** "**it's nine point six percent per year of just the transaction fees, dormancy fee, activation fee, replacement card fee. This is where the money is.**" The derived notes gloss it as "roughly **9.6% per year** on a **$1,000** card." *(His spoken derivation is partly garbled — "At a hundred dollars, that's three percent… at a thousand dollars, it's point three percent times twelve… It's nine hundred and sixty" — so the stated conclusion is recorded, not the arithmetic.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **His reaction:** "**I'm in the wrong business. I need to get key operators as my partners and get that thing going.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## The float and the stranded balance

Val's worked example of why the processor's real income is not the fee line: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

- "When a customer puts his cash, let's say **five hundred dollars** on the card, and he uses **four hundred and ninety-three dollars and seven dollars remaining**, and he lost the card. **That seven dollars stays and eventually the card gets lost and gets out of commission. That seven dollars goes to [InComm].**"
- **The lost-card fee makes the stranded balance unrecoverable in practice.** Salim raised the $6 replacement fee; Val's answer: "**But let's say there's seven dollars left on the card, and nobody uses it. That's lost money. Would you pay six dollars if you have seven dollars on the card?**"
- **The principal itself sits with the processor, twice over.** "**The five hundred dollars that initially [was] put on the card, if it's redeemed somewhere, [InComm] charges money again for the redemption**… **The entire five hundred dollars goes to them, minus the commission. So they hold the money and the commission.**"
- **Where he thinks it ends:** "**They're slowly growing the locations. They will start allowing from two thousand dollars to two and a half to three to five, and eventually, they'll own the entire state. They'll be managing that cash.**"

## The scale of it

- **Val's estimate:** "**It's five billion in payouts**… The total goes in six billion. Five billion. Six point something. **So on five billion, how much money do they make? They probably make close to fifteen percent overall**… **Seven hundred fifty million dollars per year.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **He gave a second, higher figure minutes later and did not reconcile the two:** "**They'll probably get close to twenty percent per year. So five billion dollars, twenty percent that's one billion dollars.**" Both are recorded; the derived notes carry only the 15% / **$750M** version. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **Salim's split of the same $5 billion:** "**From that five billion, at least four billion will go into this cash, because another billion just take it out as lottery.**" Val disagreed on where it settles: "**It will all die. Nobody will buy lottery. People want cash.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## Why the terminals are free — the giveaway is rounding error

- **InComm gave away 5,000 devices.** "**They told me that they gave away five thousand devices. Five thousand**… let's say they gave away five thousand devices at **three hundred dollars**. That's **one and a half million dollars**, and they're making seven hundred fifty million dollars." Val's conclusion: "**I would give away my [PAX].**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **They were not always free.** Salim: "**But before they were charging. Only May June July**," and of the promotion: "**[InComm] did a program in May June, it was free, and they marketed it. It's free access, and before it was like 100 bucks.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## What the intermediaries actually earn

- **JVL, as a distributor, keeps $1 per card** — against card balances of up to $1,000 each and an automatic ACH pull two days after issue. Detail on [[wiki/decisions/2026-08-31-transactional-equipment-only-with-location-exclusivity.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **Fiery Pay offered a master licensee 20–30 cents per card issued, and Salim declined it** so as to carry no risk: "**When they were offering me twenty cents [or] thirty cents [per] card issued, I said I don't want it**… **That dollar also, what they are offering you, I would say don't take it.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **The credit limits that bound the exposure:** InComm "approves them with a limit of **twenty five hundred, thirty five hundred, or two thousand**, whatever their credit limit allows," and non-payment is settled between the processor and the location. See [[wiki/organizations/incom.md]] and [[wiki/organizations/dixie-amusement.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## The cash-arbitrage Val expects the locations to invent

An inference Val drew aloud, framed as a prediction rather than an observation: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

- "**I think that the locations will wake up and say, 'Hey… here's your card. I'm issuing legally'**… **Can they buy that five hundred dollars from you for four hundred ninety dollars? I don't know of any player that will say no.** Every player will say, '**Here's my five hundred dollars. Give me four hundred ninety dollars in cash.**' And [the] location will find another legitimate way to turn it back into profit."
- His reasoning about why: "**locations are smart. They're street people. They're mom and pop, and they value ten dollars. They're not going to give it away. And nobody will charge a location any misdemeanor or any crime.**" *(The last clause is Val's assertion, not a legal finding, and no source in the corpus tests it.)*
- **The card in question is not the restricted pilot card.** Salim recalled the pilot card's sticker — "**This card cannot be redeemed for tobacco, alcohol, firearms, or cash**" — and Val drew the distinction: "**that was a prepaid MasterCard, and that was a stipulation from MasterCard. We're now talking about that blue card… That's a [Skill Pay] card, which is acceptable everywhere… ATM machines anywhere.**" The Skill Pay card is the InComm product tracked on [[wiki/organizations/incom.md]].

## Why this matters to JVL's product strategy

The whole page is the answer to a question Val put to himself in the same meeting — "**how do we make money? Selling kiosks. There's no money in selling kiosks**" — and the reason he concluded "**the value is in recurring revenue.**" The device catalogue built against that conclusion is on [[wiki/topics/flexpay-business-architecture-payout-devices.md]]. ## Sept 1 2026 — the policy rationale, and the multi-provider non-payment game

Three additions from the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-01-double-down-gaming-visit.md|Sept-1 Double Down Gaming visit]], one day later. None of them touches the fee sheet; all three sit around it.

### Why gift cards exist at all, from a GAMOA board member

[[wiki/people/ryan-odonnell.md|Ryan O'Donnell]] gave the legislative intent in one paragraph — the first time the corpus records the policy argument from the operators' own political side: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]

> "the legislators are not happy. **They sell it to their constituents as skill gaming. It's very siloed. They're not paying cash. That was the whole point of it.** You don't want these places to give out cash. **We'll give them a gift card. That gift card will be used somewhere locally. You'll get sales tax from it. Local merchants will be selling more product, whether it's that store or the gas station on the street or Home Depot, whatever it is. It's going back into the market, not going back into somebody's mattress. That's the idea.**"

That is the intent the cash-arbitrage prediction above cuts against, and it is why he expects the regulator to revisit the ATM question — "**I think him seeing it in person will change his mind**… **the ATM piece**," referring to the regulator having watched at the show how quickly a win becomes cash: "**Whether it's one step, two steps, three steps, it doesn't matter. You're walking out with the cash pretty quickly.**" A JVL voice added the detail he found strange — the same official "**didn't have any argument against people buying a money order with a gift card. He said that's totally legal.**" Ryan's version: "you win five hundred dollars, and you can get a **money order for some of it, but not all of it. Who's going to do that?**" *(The official is never named in this source and is not identified here.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]

### Locations play the processors against each other — and it is already happening

[[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] laid out the mechanism as a warning to an operator considering adding a second validation device: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]

> "you brought this to a location… and they plug it into parallel with [MudaGoose], and **the location becomes very smart**, and they say, **I can validate two thousand dollars worth of cards per day. And I have to pay right away. What if I just use another device like this from another company? When I have to pay this one, I'll put them on hold and use the other device. When I have to pay that one, I'll put that on hold and have a third device. Now they have three suppliers. None of them really get paid on time, and they manipulate each one against each other.**"

- **He says it is not hypothetical.** "**We're in close touch with [InComm], and they're already complaining that people that have [PAX] are not paying. Why aren't they paying? Because they have [Fiery Pay], they have [Access], in addition to [PAX].** So yeah, it will happen. It's going to happen." *(The transcript renders these "PACs", "fireplay" and "access control"; **Fiery Pay** and **Access** are read from the Georgia competitor set already on [[wiki/projects/georgia-market-rollout.md]] and [[wiki/people/salim-khambawala.md]] — an inference, not a corrected quote. The InComm non-payment complaint itself is the same one Val cited on Aug 31 — see [[wiki/decisions/2026-08-31-transactional-equipment-only-with-location-exclusivity.md]].)*
- **JVL's stated position on the processing margin is that it does not want it.** "**Clearly, we also commission agnostics… We're not really after that dollar, that processing dollar. It's not really where we're going with it.**" What it does want instead: "We also process in the background. **We also have a cloud application that processes all the payments and puts the money directly into the bank of an operator the same day, automatically.** We also give you full reporting on everything." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]

### Reloadable cards do not exist yet, and Mastercard — not technology — is the limit

- **Nobody offers reloadables, including Fiery Pay.** [[wiki/people/andrei-klimovich.md|Andrei Klimovich]]: "It depends on the provider. As far as I know, **there is still no provider in the market who supports reloadable cards. Even [Fiery Pay]; they do not provide reloadables. Do you know any?**" *(Rendered "Affray pays"; read as **Fiery Pay** from context — an inference.)* Ryan's counter was that the rules are moving: "**I know that was a rule change in July, that was the big thing**… I feel like **the law is kind of opening itself up to that reloadable option.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]
- **The cards belong to Mastercard, and that governs what can be done with them.** Val, refusing to treat a redemption question as a technical one: "**These cards are not yours. They are not [the] location['s]. They are not [the] player['s]. They belong to Mastercard. You deposited your cash onto a Mastercard**… **so Mastercard rules on what could and could not be happening to these cards, and who accepts the card.** [A JVL person] cannot accept that card. You have to have [a] proper… **ABA number** or whatever it is that is licensed by Mastercard." And the conclusion: "if you want this device to accept it, **it's not a technology issue. It's a MasterCard issue. These devices have to be licensed by MasterCard. Will that ever happen or not? I have no idea.**" This is the same restriction that produced the [[wiki/decisions/2026-08-26-no-cash-from-coam-redemption-device-for-skill-pay.md|Aug-26 no-cash ruling]], stated here from the card-network side rather than the regulator's. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]
- **A player can redeem at an ATM from September 15, still non-reloadable.** Andrei, walking through the upgrade path: "as a player, I go back to exactly the same kiosk as I did **starting with September fifteenth**… **It is an ATM. It's still not reloadable, but it's an ATM.** So let's say that they get their card. **Now they can go to an ATM to redeem their cash.**" Anything beyond that "**all depends on legislation and how it will go.**" *(The passage is garbled around the date; the September-15 date and the not-reloadable qualifier are both stated plainly and are recorded as such.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Double_Down_Gaming_Operations_Review-September-1-2026.md]]

## Sept 1 2026 (afternoon) — the credit ceiling that drives the multi-provider game, and the fee sheet recited to a customer

Two further visits the same day ([[wiki/meetings/2026-09-01-narayani-akshar-mega-hit-demo-and-trade-in.md|Narayani/Akshar]] and [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-01-air-gaming-group-transactional-walkthrough.md|Air Gaming Group]]) supply the missing lower half of the mechanism above: **why** a location goes looking for a second provider in the first place.

### The InComm location credit ceiling, from an operator running ~150 locations

[[wiki/people/neel.md|Neel]] of [[wiki/organizations/akshar-gaming.md|Akshar Gaming]] raised it unprompted. The derived notes read the figures as locations getting only **$3,000–$6,000 per week** of card credit while some locations **pay out $50,000**, so "**they need something with a higher payout**" and go looking elsewhere. The underlying transcript is garbled on the numbers — "[InComm] gives them a bare minimum of three thousand or six hundred dollars per week. And some location payouts, like fifty thousand dollars just once a year… They need something with a higher payout" — and the figures are recorded here as the **notes'** reading, with the garble left visible rather than smoothed. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Narayani_Amusements_Mega_Hit_Demo_and_Trade_In-September-1-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Narayani_Amusements_Mega_Hit_Demo_and_Trade_In-September-1-2026.md]]

This is the same pressure InComm's own account manager described on Aug 12 (a location may activate ~$5,000 of cards before paying; some do $10,000/day and want more; InComm's answer is a deposit, so some locations sign up a second provider — see [[wiki/organizations/incom.md]]) and the same pressure Val warned Double Down about that morning. **Three independent sources now describe the ceiling; none of them states the actual credit-limit policy**, and the figures quoted differ. Recorded, not reconciled.

### Their locations' actual cash-out stack

The same route runs a **third-party card system** — about **$5,000 one-time for six machines**, then **$13.30 per machine per month** — which holds the player's balance but **cannot issue gift cards**: "**The X's does not issue gift card themselves. You still need to have [InComm] or some other form of gift card.**" So the prize-card processor sits downstream of the in-location wallet, not in place of it, and the gift cards themselves are **not reloadable** and get reordered by the location every few months. Full account on [[wiki/organizations/akshar-gaming.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Narayani_Amusements_Mega_Hit_Demo_and_Trade_In-September-1-2026.md]]

### The fee sheet, recited to a customer

Val used the card's own charges as a selling point against it, in front of the Air Gaming visitor: "**Two dollars, and they also pay when they put it into an ATM.** They also put, and **there is a bunch of other charges on the card. Did you read this?** … **Plus, there is a charge for inactivity. There is a charge for forgetting your password.**" [[wiki/people/andrei-klimovich.md|Andrei]] added the second hop *(read as Andrei from role — the inference is flagged on the meeting stub)*: "to get this cash right away, this [kiosk] comes… [otherwise he has] to go to another ATM, where **he will be charged 2.5 plus ATM charge**." Val's conclusion about whose money it is by then: "**You see, you think it's your money, but it's not your money.**" These are the same categories as the Aug-31 fee sheet above, stated from memory rather than from the document, and **no figure here should be read as a quoted rate** — the passage is fragmentary. ## Sept 2 2026 — the processor's own fee sheet, from the processor's side of the table

At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-02-incomm-machine-payments-and-recycler-standoff.md|Sept-2 InComm lunch]] the numbers on this page were finally given by [[wiki/organizations/incom.md|InComm]] rather than reconstructed from an operator's copy of a competitor's card wrap. Three of them are new to the corpus.

### Putting a card back into a machine will cost the player 5%, capped at $50

The full terms are on [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-02-card-to-machine-launches-with-the-location-as-merchant-of-record.md]]. The economics in one line: **5% of the load, capped at $50 per transaction, paid by the player, no discounts at launch**, on typical purchases of **$10–$20** even from a **$300** card; InComm's experience of similar programmes is that **20–60% of gift-card activations go back into the game** [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_InComm_Machine_Payments_and_ATM_Recycler_Standoff-September-2-2026.md]].

- **Val's objection is pure arithmetic, and it went unrebutted:** why pay 5% to put money back in the machine when "he could turn around, go into [an] ATM, pay five dollars or three dollars, whatever that ATM charge, **get everything in cash, and go back and play at the machine**?" The reply conceded the comparison and fell back on behaviour — "this is probably not how you would behave… **a significant portion of those players spend 20 to 60 percent of the card in 10 and 20 dollar purchases**" — and on locations that have no suitable ATM [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_InComm_Machine_Payments_and_ATM_Recycler_Standoff-September-2-2026.md]].
- **The hardware economics behind it:** a **$40 barcode scanner** in place of a **$300** card reader, across **50,000** Georgia machines that are not PCI compliant. That is the real reason the product exists [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_InComm_Machine_Payments_and_ATM_Recycler_Standoff-September-2-2026.md]].
- **JVL's slice is unknown even to InComm's own people:** "**somewhere to start would be like fifty basis points**" out of the 5%, with the rest going to Nuvei and Mastercard [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_InComm_Machine_Payments_and_ATM_Recycler_Standoff-September-2-2026.md]].

### The ATM side of the same card, priced

- **$2.50 per transaction and up to $600 a time on the MoneyPass network, with no surcharge** — InComm contracted for that; off-network it controls nothing and the store's own ATM "may have a six dollar service fee on top." Full detail on [[wiki/organizations/incom.md]] (Sept 2) [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_InComm_Machine_Payments_and_ATM_Recycler_Standoff-September-2-2026.md]].
- This is the same **$2.50** the Aug-27 Halcyon account calls the *floor* of the retailer's cash-back stack (see [[wiki/organizations/incom.md]], Aug 26–27) — now sourced to the processor and attached to a named network.

### The $900 a day nobody accounts for, asked of the processor directly

Val turned InComm's own **1.5 cards per day per location** figure into the accusation: in "the 70 percent of the locations… **you are issuing one and a half cards a day**." Then the arithmetic: "if an average machine gives a hundred dollars, it needs to take in a thousand. So **what happens to nine hundred dollars on a daily basis per machine? The lottery paid out five billion dollars. Do you have five billion dollars on your cards?**" **The transcript records no answer** [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_InComm_Machine_Payments_and_ATM_Recycler_Standoff-September-2-2026.md]].

This is the third time the corpus asks where the rest of the money goes — Juan on Aug 21, Val's cash-arbitrage prediction on Aug 31 (above), and now to InComm's face. **No source has ever answered it**, and the suspicion of off-book cash payouts remains a suspicion.

### And the free terminal, priced by the people who give it away

The PAX **A80** "retails for about **two hundred**" and has been free to locations "**since March**" — against Val's guess of "about 400 bucks." InComm declined to buy JVL hardware in its place and offered a **bounty weighted to net-new locations** instead, on the reasoning that it holds **~70% of the market** and "**that last 30% is going to be a lot harder for us to reach**." Full exchange on [[wiki/organizations/incom.md]] (Sept 2) [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_InComm_Machine_Payments_and_ATM_Recycler_Standoff-September-2-2026.md]].

*(The $200 retail figure sits against the **$300** per device Val used on Aug 31 to compute the giveaway as "one and a half million dollars." Both are recorded; the Aug-31 figure was Val's own estimate and the Sept-2 figure is InComm's. Not reconciled.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_InComm_Machine_Payments_and_ATM_Recycler_Standoff-September-2-2026.md]]

### The operator side of the same day

At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-02-lucky-coin-service-and-upgrade-offer.md|Lucky Coin visit]] that morning, a 50-location master described the stack he actually bought: **EasyLink plus GBT kiosks** in **30–40%** of locations ("that's been the best combo that we found"), **PAX handhelds** elsewhere, all of it purchased and installed by the route itself. He was moving further down that path "until this ATM debit card" came up — and on cash dispensing, "**If it could spit out cash, I'd throw that in every one of my locations**." ## Sept 2 2026 (Continental) — why operators actually want replay, and JVL's own read that it will not sell more cards

The [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-02-continental-bifurcated-operations-and-vendor-lock-in.md|Continental visit]] that afternoon produced the clearest account in the corpus of **what the replay demand is really for** — and then, in the car afterwards, JVL's own conclusion about what it means for card volumes. Both belong on this page because both are about reported money versus real money. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Continental_Vendor_Lock_In_Dead_End-September-2-2026.md]]

### The double-count: how the Lottery's in/out report inflates

[[wiki/people/salim-punjani.md|Salim Punjani]] gave it twice, unprompted, in both halves of the visit. The point is that **replay is a reporting fix, not a player feature**. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Continental_Bifurcated_Operations_Texas_and_Virginia-September-2-2026.md]]

> "**It's like hundred dollars, play ten, cash out ninety. Play ten, cash out eighty.** So the machine, **Georgia Lottery reports this as… hundred seventy or hundred ninety in and twenty out, which is wrong**… **So that's why people are asking for replay.**"

Restated in the second half with the store's exposure named: "currently **Georgia Lottery says hundred dollars in, ninety dollars out**… The customer takes same ninety bucks and puts it in machine number two… **so Georgia Lottery says you had one hundred and ninety dollars in**… **So 170 out is the one that's bothering the stores a lot.** Because now he is saying he gave a guitar. **Technically, that's not the case. ## Sept 16 2026 — what the 8% actually *is*, and the endgame said plainly: be a partner at a bank

On [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-16-jvl-transactional-ecosystem-and-class-a-market-strategy.md|the 2:53 PM strategy session]], talking to a JVL colleague rather than a counterparty, Val stated the whole economic thesis in about ninety seconds. **It is the first time in the corpus he says what the 8% is made of** [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JVL_Transactional_Ecosystem_and_Class_A_Market_Strategy-September-16-2026.md]].

- **The fee line, restated:** "**It is approximately one percent to be made on the amount of money that you transact. You transact five billion dollars. That's what I'm up**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JVL_Transactional_Ecosystem_and_Class_A_Market_Strategy-September-16-2026.md]].
- **And then the part that matters, named for the first time:** "**It's not about the charge. It's about money that you leave behind and don't process. That amounts to approximately eight percent.**" **On Sept 11 the same 8% was labelled only "back end"** ([[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Digital_Cash_Processing_Card_Solution_and_Cash_Float_Partnership-September-11-2026.md]]); here he identifies it as **unspent, unprocessed balances** — breakage, not investment yield. *(That is a clarification of his own earlier figure, not a new number, and it still does not reconcile the 8 / 15 / 20 % spread in the open question below.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_JVL_Transactional_Ecosystem_and_Class_A_Market_Strategy-September-16-2026.md]]
- **The transaction fee itself he is willing to give away entirely:** "**Two dollar fee. We don't really care. We're willing to give it up. ## Sept 28 2026 — the thesis told twice to investors: the two cards' fee sheets, "whoever owns the equipment clears the transactions", and a bank as the end

Prepaid card" (afternoon) [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Equipment_Depreciation_Investor_Pitch-September-28-2026.md]].
- **The fee range stays 1–8%, and he says he cannot pin it down.** "I don't know how to fully research this, but depending which way I research, the fee, the combined interest fee, is somewhere between one percent and eight percent on all transactions" (lunch); "according to my research, we can make approximately between one and eight percent on the money, depending on the number of factors, on five billion dollars" (afternoon).
