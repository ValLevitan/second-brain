# Georgia prize-card processor economics

Where the money goes in the Georgia COAM gift-card chain, worked out by [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] in real time at the [[wiki/meetings/2026-08-31-dixie-amusement-kiosk-wait-and-see.md|Aug-31 2026 Dixie Amusement visit]] from a **Fiery Pay fee schedule** that [[wiki/people/salim-khambawala.md|Salim Khambawala]] had attached to one of his cards and handed over. It is the commercial premise behind [[wiki/decisions/2026-08-31-transactional-equipment-only-with-location-exclusivity.md]] — JVL's $1-per-card margin is the smallest number on this page. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## The fee sheet

Val asked for the document and kept it: "Can I look at the chart that you have?… **I need this. Where did you get this? This is on the card. Attached to the card.** I didn't open mine. **Can I have this?**" Salim: "This is from the [Fiery Pay] stuff." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

| Fee | Amount | Source line |
|---|---|---|
| Activation | **$2** | "**activation fees is two dollars**" |
| Dormancy | **$2.95 per month** | "**You will be charged two ninety five each month after you have not completed the transaction**" |
| Lost / replacement card | **$6** | "**they charge six bucks for a lost card**" |
| Per transaction | **3–5%** | "**It's about three to five percent. That's what I heard. Per transaction.**" |

[[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

- **The sheet is silent on the transaction fee.** Val, reading it: "**They're silent on the transaction itself. I don't know how much they charge for the transaction.**" The 3–5% is what he had heard elsewhere, not what the document says.
- **Who pays it was disputed in the room.** Salim believed the merchant absorbs it — "if they go to Walmart, **Walmart pays it. The card holder doesn't pay**" — while the JVL side said "**They do pay. They do pay. On [Fiery Pay].**" Unresolved in the source.
- **Val's total:** "**it's nine point six percent per year of just the transaction fees, dormancy fee, activation fee, replacement card fee. This is where the money is.**" The derived notes gloss it as "roughly **9.6% per year** on a **$1,000** card." *(His spoken derivation is partly garbled — "At a hundred dollars, that's three percent… at a thousand dollars, it's point three percent times twelve… It's nine hundred and sixty" — so the stated conclusion is recorded, not the arithmetic.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **His reaction:** "**I'm in the wrong business. I need to get key operators as my partners and get that thing going.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## The float and the stranded balance

Val's worked example of why the processor's real income is not the fee line: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

- "When a customer puts his cash, let's say **five hundred dollars** on the card, and he uses **four hundred and ninety-three dollars and seven dollars remaining**, and he lost the card. **That seven dollars stays and eventually the card gets lost and gets out of commission. That seven dollars goes to [InComm].**"
- **The lost-card fee makes the stranded balance unrecoverable in practice.** Salim raised the $6 replacement fee; Val's answer: "**But let's say there's seven dollars left on the card, and nobody uses it. That's lost money. Would you pay six dollars if you have seven dollars on the card?**"
- **The principal itself sits with the processor, twice over.** "**The five hundred dollars that initially [was] put on the card, if it's redeemed somewhere, [InComm] charges money again for the redemption**… **The entire five hundred dollars goes to them, minus the commission. So they hold the money and the commission.**"
- **Where he thinks it ends:** "**They're slowly growing the locations. They will start allowing from two thousand dollars to two and a half to three to five, and eventually, they'll own the entire state. They'll be managing that cash.**"

## The scale of it

- **Val's estimate:** "**It's five billion in payouts**… The total goes in six billion. Five billion. Six point something. **So on five billion, how much money do they make? They probably make close to fifteen percent overall**… **Seven hundred fifty million dollars per year.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **He gave a second, higher figure minutes later and did not reconcile the two:** "**They'll probably get close to twenty percent per year. So five billion dollars, twenty percent that's one billion dollars.**" Both are recorded; the derived notes carry only the 15% / **$750M** version. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **Salim's split of the same $5 billion:** "**From that five billion, at least four billion will go into this cash, because another billion just take it out as lottery.**" Val disagreed on where it settles: "**It will all die. Nobody will buy lottery. People want cash.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## Why the terminals are free — the giveaway is rounding error

- **InComm gave away 5,000 devices.** "**They told me that they gave away five thousand devices. Five thousand**… let's say they gave away five thousand devices at **three hundred dollars**. That's **one and a half million dollars**, and they're making seven hundred fifty million dollars." Val's conclusion: "**I would give away my [PAX].**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **They were not always free.** Salim: "**But before they were charging. Only May June July**," and of the promotion: "**[InComm] did a program in May June, it was free, and they marketed it. It's free access, and before it was like 100 bucks.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## What the intermediaries actually earn

- **JVL, as a distributor, keeps $1 per card** — against card balances of up to $1,000 each and an automatic ACH pull two days after issue. Detail on [[wiki/decisions/2026-08-31-transactional-equipment-only-with-location-exclusivity.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **Fiery Pay offered a master licensee 20–30 cents per card issued, and Salim declined it** so as to carry no risk: "**When they were offering me twenty cents [or] thirty cents [per] card issued, I said I don't want it**… **That dollar also, what they are offering you, I would say don't take it.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]
- **The credit limits that bound the exposure:** InComm "approves them with a limit of **twenty five hundred, thirty five hundred, or two thousand**, whatever their credit limit allows," and non-payment is settled between the processor and the location. See [[wiki/organizations/incom.md]] and [[wiki/organizations/dixie-amusement.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

## The cash-arbitrage Val expects the locations to invent

An inference Val drew aloud, framed as a prediction rather than an observation: [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dixie_Amusement_Kiosk_Wait_and_See_and_Market_Economics-August-31-2026.md]]

- "**I think that the locations will wake up and say, 'Hey… here's your card. I'm issuing legally'**… **Can they buy that five hundred dollars from you for four hundred ninety dollars? I don't know of any player that will say no.** Every player will say, '**Here's my five hundred dollars. Give me four hundred ninety dollars in cash.**' And [the] location will find another legitimate way to turn it back into profit."
- His reasoning about why: "**locations are smart. They're street people. They're mom and pop, and they value ten dollars. They're not going to give it away. And nobody will charge a location any misdemeanor or any crime.**" *(The last clause is Val's assertion, not a legal finding, and no source in the corpus tests it.)*
- **The card in question is not the restricted pilot card.** Salim recalled the pilot card's sticker — "**This card cannot be redeemed for tobacco, alcohol, firearms, or cash**" — and Val drew the distinction: "**that was a prepaid MasterCard, and that was a stipulation from MasterCard. We're now talking about that blue card… That's a [Skill Pay] card, which is acceptable everywhere… ATM machines anywhere.**" The Skill Pay card is the InComm product tracked on [[wiki/organizations/incom.md]].
