# Online games / RGS distribution business

JVL's online casino games business: its games run on JVL's RGS (remote game server) and are distributed to operators through aggregators. This is a separate line from the physical EGM/route business, run on the commercial side by [[wiki/people/andrei-klimovich.md|Andrei Klimovich]], who recaps it to Val weekly. Synthesised from the Jan 30 2025 recap. [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]

## How distribution works

- JVL's games reach operators through **EveryMatrix** (referred to as "RGS Matrix" / "EveryMatrix"), an aggregator platform. JVL is a game supplier plugged into that aggregator rather than dealing with every operator directly. [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]
- JVL's take on online play was described as about **5%**; the strategy discussed is to re-negotiate this upward (toward **8–10%**) in newly regulated markets, possibly on a stepped schedule (higher first to recover certification cost, then tapering). [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]

## Brazil shut-off (Jan 1 2025)

- As of Jan 1 2025 JVL's games were switched off at all the operators it was live with in **Brazil**, because of the new **licensing** regime — Brazil revenue effectively went to zero (only some "grey" operators remain). [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]
- The biggest loss was the operator **Bitano** (transcribed "BitAnon"/"Bitana"), which had been about **30% of JVL's revenue** through the EveryMatrix/RGS channel. [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]
- The opening this creates: because JVL's games are now off the Brazilian market, JVL can offer Bitano an **exclusive** on those proven games for a period (e.g. ~3 months after re-certification) as leverage in a re-negotiation, since the market is now regulated. [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]

## Certification economics (Val's payback rule)

- Val's principle: a per-game **certification spend (~$2,500/game)** must pay back in roughly **3–5 months**, never a year ("I wouldn't even go into such a business… too much uncertainty over a year"). [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]
- Unlike other investments, certification cost can't be spread across other markets — it is specific to the one game in the one market — so it must be recovered from that game's incremental earnings there. Hence the push to raise the revenue share. [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]
- Val's reasoning about the wider market: certification labs and rivals only stay in business if certification pays; if others can profit at current rates and JVL can't, the likely difference is that others **re-negotiate their platform/operator deals** and JVL has not. [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]
- **RiskCherry** — an alternative certification lab. Its cost came out about the same as GLI (slightly cheaper) but it claims to be faster: ~2 weeks for a pack of 10 games versus GLI's ~3. See [[wiki/topics/gli-certification-bottleneck.md]]. [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]

## Game configuration

- **MaxBet limit standardised at 250,000** across all games in the category. Only ~10 of the ~40 games currently exceed 250k; the rest are already under it, so only those ~10 need tuning. 250k was judged a good, mid-market limit. To be applied with EPUM. [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]

## What online platforms sell operators (Feb 1 analysis)

Andrei Klimovich mapped the full stack of turnkey services an online-casino platform provides an operator, to frame the marketplace idea. The layers: a **website** (front/back-end + CMS, optionally managed for you); **multi-tenant architecture** (one account → many brands); **player account management** (customer support, player **segmentation**, CRM/VIP); **player verification** (ID check, facial recognition, address verification, global watch-list); **anti-fraud** (AML, fraud detection, multi-account prevention); **payments** (multi-currency + crypto, regional **wallets**); **data/analytics** (real-time dashboards, reporting); **content** (aggregation, curation, regional restriction/certification enforcement); **engagement & retention** (promos, jackpots, tournaments, free-spins, loyalty, social features); **affiliates**; and **licensing/certification** cover. Andrei flagged three under-served niches platforms don't yet package: news aggregation, streamers/influencers, and slot-review sites. [[raw/recap_with_andrei_klimovichonlineecho212025_2025-02-01.md]]

## The JVL Marketplace concept (Feb 1)

- Andrei's idea: a **game-provider marketplace** where operators integrate **directly** and pay JVL a **membership** for unlimited access to JVL's games, cutting out the platform's markup. Platforms typically take ~10% of GGR, but layered with content-provider and other cuts an operator loses ~30% on top of ~50% marketing spend — the marketplace targets that markup. [[raw/recap_with_andrei_klimovichonlineecho212025_2025-02-01.md]]
- **How operators unbundle:** an established operator first takes back the **website + affiliates** (most control/cost), then the **wallet** (a separate third-party business); once it has a site and a wallet it is the "perfect target" — JVL plugs into its wallet and supplies only game content + RGS. [[raw/recap_with_andrei_klimovichonlineecho212025_2025-02-01.md]]
- **MVP (Val's framing):** not the full marketplace — just a JVL **website** with well-polished digital steps to embed JVL's RGS and take JVL's games (a "pump" pushing games out), i.e. one convenient entry point for **direct integration**. Andrei worries the end-state marketplace makes JVL "just another platform on a different business model," and that platforms could **replicate** the membership model quickly and out-scale JVL — his main concern, for which he wanted Val's feedback (Val declined to argue either way). [[raw/recap_with_andrei_klimovichonlineecho212025_2025-02-01.md]]

## Michael Gottlieb's "online is unwinnable" assessment (Mar 12)

On the Mar 12 2025 "developing games faster" call, [[wiki/people/michael-gottlieb.md|Michael Gottlieb]] gave [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] a blunt argument that JVL cannot make money in the online space, drawing on his time working for a top provider. [[raw/developing_games_faster_michael_gottlieb_march_12-2025.md]]

- **It's a flooded, no-barrier market:** the top-tier players release around **20 games a month**, of which maybe **one sticks** — "they throw a lot of crap against the wall." A newcomer coming from outside "ends up on the bottom of the list" no matter how good the game is. [[raw/developing_games_faster_michael_gottlieb_march_12-2025.md]]
- **Providers secretly own the casinos:** many online game providers **also own the online casinos** (he named **Bet88**, **Casino Stars**, **Jackpot Winners** as re-skinned front ends run off the same servers in **Malta**); they promote their own products, share player data across their casinos, and target incentives to retain players — "the game is rigged." He said he saw this firsthand at the **Isle of Man**. **Microgaming** allegedly owns **17 studios** and **40 or 50 online casinos** (with **"for the win"** among the studios) without acknowledging it; the company was privately owned by "**Martin Moshal**." **SlotCatalog.com** is the tool that reveals top products and who's behind them. [[raw/developing_games_faster_michael_gottlieb_march_12-2025.md]]
- **Margins don't work:** online **RTPs run ~96%**, and the number of spins needed to grind out profit is enormous (he cited a **Village People** game he built); land-based margins are far better. He likened it to sports betting, which needs ~10x the volume for the same money. [[raw/developing_games_faster_michael_gottlieb_march_12-2025.md]]
- **Val's counter (30%):** Val agreed ~70% but kept alive a **"Netflix for games" digital-distribution** idea — a JVL platform where operators **rent** or hold a **membership** for seamless RGS integration instead of buying games — while conceding 70% might be enough to "step away and stop burning money." He framed JVL's real edge as its **licenses and relationships** in physical territories. This aligns with the earlier ruling that land-based is the priority: [[wiki/decisions/2025-02-01-online-games-derive-from-land-based-mechanics.md]]. [[raw/developing_games_faster_michael_gottlieb_march_12-2025.md]]

## The "JVL Netflix" stealth-launch platform vision (Mar 28)

A short two-person conversation fleshed out Val's **"Netflix for games"** idea (first raised Mar 12) into a concrete build plan for an **independent games-distribution platform**. Speaker other than Val is unidentified — see [[wiki/meetings/2025-03-28-jvl-netflix-platform-vision.md]]. [[raw/jvl_netflix-march-28-2025.md]]

- **Independent, not first-party (the Steam lesson).** The other speaker noted **Electronic Arts, Activision, Epic** all built their own Steam-equivalents on *better* terms than Steam, yet players stayed with Steam **because it is independent** and already had the inertia/variety of many publishers — likened to **Disney+** being all-Disney when players "want non-Disney things too." The catch that makes JVL's platform work: **open it to other developers** and make it "uber easy" for them to place their games. [[raw/jvl_netflix-march-28-2025.md]]
- **Stealth-seed then launch.** Val's method: **before launch, quietly seed the platform with JVL's own games** so it isn't empty, keep it secret (if it leaks, competitors will jump in and dilute the market), **build and test** the system, then **raise a lot of money and advertise heavily** so no competitor can pivot in. Then open it to **other publishing houses** and to **online casinos**, with an easy publishing mechanism. [[raw/jvl_netflix-march-28-2025.md]]
- **Business model = arbitrage.** Anyone can operate their games **"at cost"** on JVL's platform; JVL's profit comes from the **arbitrage** — it is cheaper for small operators to run on JVL's platform than to build/run their own on Amazon/AWS. JVL funds it early for developers and counts on the arbitrage over the longer term. [[raw/jvl_netflix-march-28-2025.md]]
- **Team (12-month objective):** an **art director, a full-stack engineer, a QA engineer, a game producer, and a business developer**. [[raw/jvl_netflix-march-28-2025.md]]
- **Physical-floor tie-in turns the CMS into an asset.** The platform's **top-performing games** (e.g. a top-10 that could rotate monthly) from *other* publishers get hand-picked and packaged onto **JVL's physical casino machines** (which JVL bills to operate in casinos under existing contracts) — giving those publishers real-floor exposure plus a **cut of the machine-operating profits**. In exchange JVL **shares play data** (who played what, cross-market insight) with publishers and operators. This makes the back-office **CMS** "a revenue-producing thing" rather than a liability. Val's framing: with "the old JVL… the food is emptying out of the fridge," JVL must put its own games on the platform because "that's where we're putting our bets." [[raw/jvl_netflix-march-28-2025.md]]

This extends the Feb-1 JVL Marketplace concept (Andrei Klimovich) and Val's Mar-12 "Netflix for games" counter above; it remains a **vision — nothing was decided or resourced** in this call.

## History — 2023–2024 origins (batch 16)

Together they show the online-distribution business, the marketplace/"Netflix" idea, and the ECHO/Amazon push all began well before 2025. Meeting stubs: [[wiki/meetings/2023-08-08-online-platforms-aggregators-status.md]], [[wiki/meetings/2024-11-06-website-architecture-and-design.md]], [[wiki/meetings/2024-11-12-online-vision-and-rgs-scaling.md]], [[wiki/meetings/2024-11-24-andrei-klimovich-online-strategy-update.md]], [[wiki/meetings/2024-12-online-strategy-storefront-brainstorm.md]].

**Aug 8 2023 — aggregator pipeline & economics (earliest record).** [[wiki/people/andrei-klimovich.md|Andrei Klimovich]] was already running JVL's online distribution and reported a full aggregator/operator pipeline to Val and [[wiki/people/monica.md|Monica]]. [[raw/jvl_online_sync_2025-03-29 1.md]]
- **Relax Gaming** was the lead aggregator prospect (contact **Matthew Williams**, partner commercial manager): 67 game providers integrated, 120 in line; they want fewer, higher-quality providers; they reviewed and liked JVL's collection. Relax charges JVL **2% of GGR** (not the operator). Val's math: 2% on an 8% operator deal = **25% of JVL's take**; on a 6% deal = 33% — so quotes must be watched. [[raw/jvl_online_sync_2025-03-29 1.md]]
- **Rev-share benchmarks (Val's knowledge):** Novomatic ~12%, Light & Wonder 12–15%, GreenTube 11%. JVL competes with Novomatic, so pricing ~10% would be 20–50% cheaper and get JVL's content prioritised. [[raw/jvl_online_sync_2025-03-29 1.md]]
- **Licensing strategy:** Relax needs an **MGA** licence for MGA operators (an Isle of Man licence is not enough there); the cheap/quick route is a **Romanian** licence → an MGA "letter of recognition" → then Greece/Sweden. [[raw/jvl_online_sync_2025-03-29 1.md]]
- **JVL land-based gaming licences (Val, for credibility):** Chile, Florida, Georgia, Peru, South Dakota, and Oklahoma. **Raul Mondragon** obtained the Chilean licence for JVL long ago. [[raw/jvl_online_sync_2025-03-29 1.md]]
- **Crypto payments:** Monica researched it — no legal barrier to JVL accepting crypto, provided it is converted to fiat immediately and declared. [[raw/jvl_online_sync_2025-03-29 1.md]]
- Other named partners/prospects (mostly single-mention pipeline entries, no pages): **Pariplay/Pinnacle** (Ashley Blur, ex-EveryMatrix; 7% GGR + progressive discount; Latin America/Peru), **Spearhead** (games shown under the brand "gimmicks"; 6-month per-game exclusivity), **Hollywood Bets** (big in Africa; wants South Africa/Kenya/Ghana exclusivity), **Baden/Merkur** (Gauselmann family, Graz, Austria — a competitor Val warned to avoid; "they will truncate your content"), **Apuesta Total** (Peru; contact David Escalante; JVL's **Juan Jose Castillo** connection), **Bally** (Steven Acrow, on hold), **Solbet/Sorbet** (9% flat), **BetConstruct** and **Hub88**. [[raw/jvl_online_sync_2025-03-29 1.md]]

**Nov 6 2024 — the self-service vision written into the website brief.** On the website-alignment call ([[wiki/meetings/2024-11-06-website-architecture-and-design.md]]) Val dictated to [[wiki/people/andrei-klimovich.md|Andrei Klimovich]] the "final frontier" the new site must be architected for: a new operator finds JVL via SEO/LinkedIn/expo, plays any game free **without registration** ("нажатим на одной кнопке… погонять бесплатные спины"), reads the **template Business + Technical Agreement** openly published on the site, executes it via **DocuSign**, then **self-deploys**: requests/installs JVL's RGS, one-click "activates" any new game straight to his deployment server under a framework agreement, gets **JIRA service access** to post tickets, and can always press pause for a live operator. [[raw/business_architecture_and_the_website_design_for_online_2025-03-29.md]]
- Val's supporting assumptions, stated on the call: JVL releases **at least one game per week**; scale from ~200 accounts toward **1,000 casinos**; even a "modest assumption" of a game earning **$10k to JVL over its lifetime across 1,000 locations** makes the system "хайпер интересный" (a $50k-lifetime-GGR figure was also floated — the passage is garbled). He also framed the milestone logic: reaching one game/month proves the idea-to-production deployment process, after which scalability is "в принципе гарантированно." [[raw/business_architecture_and_the_website_design_for_online_2025-03-29.md]]
- The openly-published **7% template revenue share** was debated: Andrei balked at publishing sensitive percentages (and noted regional norms — Asia ~4%, LatAm ~7–8%); Val's answer: publish a "ритейл прайс" of e.g. 7% and explain regional pricing honestly (no GLI approvals or certified RGS needed in unregulated markets); some existing clients pay 5. Andrei's risk flags: many operators are contractually locked to their aggregator's content and can't do direct integrations; early-days liability caps (~€250k–500k) had already cost JVL integrations with big operators. Andrei framed JVL as replacing the aggregator in the standard weekly-release/notification flow, with the final "галочка" (per-operator game activation) self-served instead of account-manager mediated. [[raw/business_architecture_and_the_website_design_for_online_2025-03-29.md]]
- The website vehicle for all this is [[wiki/projects/jvl-corporate-website.md]] (release ladder + the online-first ruling [[wiki/decisions/2024-11-06-website-online-first-brand-registry-for-echo.md]]).

**Nov 12 2024 — Val's scaling vision (origin of the marketplace/Netflix idea).** Val told the RGS engineers he wants to **release one online game per week**, **integrate directly with ~1,000 casinos** (from ~150 accounts then), and build an **Amazon-style self-serve storefront** where a casino signs the contract online via **DocuSign**, downloads the RGS API + samples/videos, and self-integrates with minimal JVL help — the same idea that resurfaces as the Feb-2025 "JVL Marketplace" and Mar-2025 "JVL Netflix." He wanted it broken into monthly milestones. The RGS-scaling architecture is on [[wiki/topics/rgs-architecture-and-integration.md]]. [[raw/online_vision_efim_2025-03-29.md]]

**Nov 24 2024 — game-outsourcing / RGS economics.** Andrei walked Val through the model of a Ukrainian outsourcing studio (owner [[wiki/people/pavel-studio-owner.md|Pavel]]): sell games **flat-fee ($15k–$25k)** + charge **RGS maintenance**, no revenue share, then upsell its own titles into operator collections for a **direct ~7%**. [[raw/andrei_klimovich_update_report_online_strategy1_2025-03-29.md]]
- Central point (Val and Andrei agreed): **earnings come from distribution/exposure, not game quality or mechanics.** Evidence Andrei pulled from the studio's RGS: its **5 top games earned $92k GGR in 2 weeks on one Turkish operator** (~$18k each); one game did **$35k GGR in 2 weeks**. [[raw/andrei_klimovich_update_report_online_strategy1_2025-03-29.md]]
- **JVL's own share:** ~**5%** via an aggregator, up to **~7%** on a direct integration (Val corrected the earlier "always 5%" framing to "5–7% on average"). [[raw/andrei_klimovich_update_report_online_strategy1_2025-03-29.md]]
- **Hypothesis to test:** buy a game for ~$15k, run it on JVL's existing operators for 2–3 months, and measure earnings; at a 5% share a $15k game must generate ~**20× ($300k GGR)** to break even. Also reverse-engineer the two sample game estimates in-house to learn JVL's own build cost. [[raw/andrei_klimovich_update_report_online_strategy1_2025-03-29.md]]

**Dec 2024 — fixed-price sales model & digital storefront (brainstorm).** Val and Andrei brainstormed selling games **outright at a fixed price** as an alternative to revenue-share, in a **tiered digital storefront** with discounts. [[raw/online_strategy_brainstorm_with_andrei_klimovich_decembe_2025-03-29.md]]
- Offer both in parallel — e.g. a game at **$25k outright or 7% rev-share**, a cheaper game at **$8k–$12k or 4%**; a perpetual **"God license"** floated at ~$2k–$5k; non-exclusive (buyer can't stop others buying the same game). **Try-and-buy:** a ~2-week trial, then credit **50–100%** of trial earnings against the purchase price. [[raw/online_strategy_brainstorm_with_andrei_klimovich_decembe_2025-03-29.md]]
- **Distributor variant:** a platform buys at a ~**20% discount** (e.g. $2,000 → $1,600) and pays JVL per copy it resells. [[raw/online_strategy_brainstorm_with_andrei_klimovich_decembe_2025-03-29.md]]
- **Market-research task:** assign a partnership manager to survey custom online-slot developers in the CIS (Ukraine, Belarus, Russia) and cheaper Europe (Poland, Bulgaria) — pricing and RGS-billing models. [[raw/online_strategy_brainstorm_with_andrei_klimovich_decembe_2025-03-29.md]]
- **Risks (Val):** most operators sit on third-party platforms and lack tech teams, so only large operators can integrate directly; platforms may block/impede a direct JVL integration. JVL's first two direct integrations were **Golden Palace** and **Golden Matrix**. [[raw/online_strategy_brainstorm_with_andrei_klimovich_decembe_2025-03-29.md]]
- **Regulated-market edge:** JVL's advantage is its licences + history; Ukrainian studios can't get licensed or sheltered. **EveryMatrix sheltering was shrinking in Europe**, so JVL planned to move to **Pariplay ("Parry Play") sheltering**; Brazil was becoming regulated, opening space as uncertified providers exit. Val was skeptical of Andrei's claim of ~100 established competitor studios. [[raw/online_strategy_brainstorm_with_andrei_klimovich_decembe_2025-03-29.md]]
- **Market shift (via Pavel):** operators had, over the prior ~6 months, stopped paying separate RGS fees, so Pavel now bundles RGS into the game price and deploys it on the operator's server — detail on [[wiki/people/pavel-studio-owner.md|Pavel's page]]. [[raw/online_strategy_brainstorm_with_andrei_klimovich_decembe_2025-03-29.md]]
