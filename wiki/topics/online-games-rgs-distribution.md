# Online games / RGS distribution business

JVL's online casino games business: its games run on JVL's RGS (remote game server) and are distributed to operators through aggregators. This is a separate line from the physical EGM/route business, run on the commercial side by [[wiki/people/andrei-klimovich.md|Andrei Klimovich]], who recaps it to Val weekly. Synthesised from the Jan 30 2025 recap. [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]

## How distribution works

- JVL's games reach operators through **EveryMatrix** (referred to as "RGS Matrix" / "EveryMatrix"), an aggregator platform. JVL is a game supplier plugged into that aggregator rather than dealing with every operator directly. [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]
- JVL's take on online play was described as about **5%**; the strategy discussed is to re-negotiate this upward (toward **8–10%**) in newly regulated markets, possibly on a stepped schedule (higher first to recover certification cost, then tapering). [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]

## Brazil shut-off (Jan 1 2025)

- As of Jan 1 2025 JVL's games were switched off at all the operators it was live with in **Brazil**, because of the new **licensing** regime — Brazil revenue effectively went to zero (only some "grey" operators remain). [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]
- The biggest loss was the operator **Bitano** (transcribed "BitAnon"/"Bitana"), which had been about **30% of JVL's revenue** through the EveryMatrix/RGS channel. [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]
- The opening this creates: because JVL's games are now off the Brazilian market, JVL can offer Bitano an **exclusive** on those proven games for a period (e.g. ~3 months after re-certification) as leverage in a re-negotiation, since the market is now regulated. [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]

## Certification economics (Val's payback rule)

- Val's principle: a per-game **certification spend (~$2,500/game)** must pay back in roughly **3–5 months**, never a year ("I wouldn't even go into such a business… too much uncertainty over a year"). [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]
- Unlike other investments, certification cost can't be spread across other markets — it is specific to the one game in the one market — so it must be recovered from that game's incremental earnings there. Hence the push to raise the revenue share. [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]
- Val's reasoning about the wider market: certification labs and rivals only stay in business if certification pays; if others can profit at current rates and JVL can't, the likely difference is that others **re-negotiate their platform/operator deals** and JVL has not. [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]
- **RiskCherry** — an alternative certification lab. Its cost came out about the same as GLI (slightly cheaper) but it claims to be faster: ~2 weeks for a pack of 10 games versus GLI's ~3. See [[wiki/topics/gli-certification-bottleneck.md]]. [[raw/Recap with Andrei Klimovich - January 30, 2025.md]]

## Game configuration

- **MaxBet limit standardised at 250,000** across all games in the category. Only ~10 of the ~40 games currently exceed 250k; the rest are already under it, so only those ~10 need tuning. 250k was judged a good, mid-market limit. To be applied with EPUM. ## What online platforms sell operators (Feb 1 analysis)

Andrei Klimovich mapped the full stack of turnkey services an online-casino platform provides an operator, to frame the marketplace idea. The layers: a **website** (front/back-end + CMS, optionally managed for you); **multi-tenant architecture** (one account → many brands); **player account management** (customer support, player **segmentation**, CRM/VIP); **player verification** (ID check, facial recognition, address verification, global watch-list); **anti-fraud** (AML, fraud detection, multi-account prevention); **payments** (multi-currency + crypto, regional **wallets**); **data/analytics** (real-time dashboards, reporting); **content** (aggregation, curation, regional restriction/certification enforcement); **engagement & retention** (promos, jackpots, tournaments, free-spins, loyalty, social features); **affiliates**; and **licensing/certification** cover. Andrei flagged three under-served niches platforms don't yet package: news aggregation, streamers/influencers, and slot-review sites. [[raw/recap_with_andrei_klimovichonlineecho212025_2025-02-01.md]]

## The JVL Marketplace concept (Feb 1)

- Andrei's idea: a **game-provider marketplace** where operators integrate **directly** and pay JVL a **membership** for unlimited access to JVL's games, cutting out the platform's markup. Platforms typically take ~10% of GGR, but layered with content-provider and other cuts an operator loses ~30% on top of ~50% marketing spend — the marketplace targets that markup. [[raw/recap_with_andrei_klimovichonlineecho212025_2025-02-01.md]]
- **How operators unbundle:** an established operator first takes back the **website + affiliates** (most control/cost), then the **wallet** (a separate third-party business); once it has a site and a wallet it is the "perfect target" — JVL plugs into its wallet and supplies only game content + RGS. [[raw/recap_with_andrei_klimovichonlineecho212025_2025-02-01.md]]
- **MVP (Val's framing):** not the full marketplace — just a JVL **website** with well-polished digital steps to embed JVL's RGS and take JVL's games (a "pump" pushing games out), i.e. one convenient entry point for **direct integration**. Andrei worries the end-state marketplace makes JVL "just another platform on a different business model," and that platforms could **replicate** the membership model quickly and out-scale JVL — his main concern, for which he wanted Val's feedback (Val declined to argue either way). [[raw/recap_with_andrei_klimovichonlineecho212025_2025-02-01.md]]

## Account-manager economics (Feb 27)

- The online business's **top-20 accounts** generate roughly **$733,000 turnover per month**, which resolves to about **$38,000 GGR** and about **$2,000 of revenue to JVL** monthly. [[raw/meeting_summary_andrei_klimovich-Feb-27-2025.md]]
- To justify the more expensive account-manager candidate's (Egor's) salary, revenue from these accounts would need roughly a **150% increase**. A proposed baseline performance metric / bonus structure is about **$200 of revenue per account per month**, with bonuses tied to turnover growth or account-revenue performance. The candidate review itself is on [[wiki/people/andrei-klimovich.md]]. [[raw/meeting_summary_andrei_klimovich-Feb-27-2025.md]]
