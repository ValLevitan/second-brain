# ECHO home gaming device and Amazon go-to-market

**ECHO** is a JVL consumer **home gaming device** — a stand-alone machine that ships with **149 games**, works **offline** (no internet needed), sold direct to consumers through **Amazon**. It is run on the commercial side by [[wiki/people/andrei-klimovich.md|Andrei Klimovich]] and reported to Val in the weekly recap. Val flagged it as **priority #1** among Andrei's ventures (see below). [[raw/recap_with_andrei_klimovichonlineecho212025_2025-02-01.md]]

> **Name-overlap flag:** an "Echo" also appears in [[wiki/topics/jvl-machine-lineup.md]] as a *countertop cabinet being converted back to a gaming cabinet with HG6 games for Colombia*. Whether the consumer home ECHO and that Echo cabinet are the same product line or just share a name is **unclear** — recorded in Owner Decisions, not resolved. [[raw/recap_with_andrei_klimovichonlineecho212025_2025-02-01.md]]

## Amazon advertising test (Feb 1 update)

- A buyer named **Jeff** wrote in directly asking for a discount; JVL offered a personal **15%-off coupon code** (Amazon sales having just started). Amazon does not reveal how a buyer arrived (organic vs. ad), so attribution is a black box. [[raw/recap_with_andrei_klimovichonlineecho212025_2025-02-01.md]]

## June 11 2025 — the Amazon FBA finance/process walk-through

A finance-process call ([[wiki/meetings/2025-06-11-amazon-echo-sales-finance.md]]; [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/andrei-klimovich.md|Andrei Klimovich]], [[wiki/people/hector.md|Hector]], with [[wiki/people/arkady-batson.md|Arkady]] shipping) to map the order-to-cash and record-keeping for ECHO sold through Amazon. Only **ECHO** is sold on Amazon so far (**Echo Home + Echo Amusement**; for Georgia it's Echo Amusement only). [[raw/finance_amazon_sales-june-11-2025.md]]

- **The model = Fulfilled By Amazon (FBA), consignment.** JVL ships machines to an Amazon **fulfillment center** on consignment; when a customer buys, Amazon reports the sale and deducts fees. **Retail price ~$4,000/machine**; Amazon takes an **8% referral fee** plus **fulfillment-center storage + subscription + minor fees**. Amazon assigns a **different US warehouse per shipment** by demand (prior shipments went to **Oklahoma**, **Illinois**; the current one to ~Arkansas). This is a **holding warehouse, not a sale** point — "extremely important for our internal taxation." [[raw/finance_amazon_sales-june-11-2025.md]]
- **Intercompany flow.** **JVL Labs sells to JVL Systems**, and **JVL Systems does the commerce**, shipping **directly from JVL Canada (Labs) to the Amazon US warehouse**. Labs invoices Systems instantly on shipment (a sale to Systems); Systems places it on consignment at Amazon; when the end customer buys, JVL books both the **sale and the cost of sale** on the Systems side. Statements/disbursements come **bi-weekly**; the first disbursement was initiated **Feb 19**, hitting the bank **Feb 21 2025**. [[raw/finance_amazon_sales-june-11-2025.md]] **(Open contradiction — see below: the June-4 update put the Amazon account under [[wiki/organizations/jvl-gameworks.md|JVL Gameworks]], not Systems.)**
- **Sales to date: 9 total**, first on **January 29 2025**. [[raw/finance_amazon_sales-june-11-2025.md]]
- **The serial-number / warranty gap.** JVL **can't track which serial number Amazon ships to which buyer**, gets only a buyer **nickname** (no real name) and **state/zip/city** (no full address). Warranty is **1 year** — but honouring it needs a serial number. **Fix adopted:** a **self-registration** flow (QR code / website link) offering free 1-year warranty coverage so buyers voluntarily register name + serial (validating that Amazon actually sold what it reports), plus JVL **monitoring the serials it ships monthly** (5 at a time) as a second control. A one-machine warranty-case **experiment** was also floated. [[raw/finance_amazon_sales-june-11-2025.md]]
- **Returns policy.** Amazon offers sellers three options for returned units: (1) Amazon **refurbishes and resells at a discount** (a black box to JVL); (2) **donate**; (3) **return to seller**. JVL chose **option 3 (return to us)**. [[raw/finance_amazon_sales-june-11-2025.md]]
- **Finance actions.** Set up a **separate revenue category** for Amazon sales; get JVL its **own Amazon portal access** (Hector, later Laura, or whoever does bank recs) to **download the bi-weekly statements** and validate the **gross sale less fees** against the bank (Amazon only remits a **net** amount) — Val insisted JVL pull its own statements rather than have Andrei send them. **API integration into Business Central** for auto-reconciliation was **deferred**. [[raw/finance_amazon_sales-june-11-2025.md]]

## Feb 11 2026 — return-packaging architecture: dual-box vs single-box (written doc)

A short **written architecture doc** (title "Echo Packaging Architecture: Navigating Dual-Box vs. Single-Box Design Trade-offs for Returns and User Experience") — **not a meeting transcript** — captures a packaging-design debate between [[wiki/people/andrei-klimovich.md|Andrei Klimovich]] and [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] about how the ECHO ships and how customers return it. It is about the **physical packaging**, not the commerce-entity question below. [[raw/Echo_packaging_Andrei-February-11-2026.md]]

- **Andrei's dual-box proposal.** An **outer shipping box** in premium **"wet crepe"** corrugated (a smoother, more tactile alternative to standard "dry crepe" cardboard) with **branded logo tape/printing** for the unboxing experience, plus an **inner structural box** with foam inserts to protect the machine in transit and serve as the return container — mirroring FedEx/Amazon premium-logistics patterns. [[raw/Echo_packaging_Andrei-February-11-2026.md]]
- **Val's operational objection.** Customers will break and discard the outer box on unpacking, keep only the attractive inner one, then grab the wrong box (or find they've thrown the required one away) when a return arises — leaving **JVL to absorb replacement-packaging cost** while the customer faces no friction. [[raw/Echo_packaging_Andrei-February-11-2026.md]]
- **The single-box "Plan B" fallback.** One shipping box: the user removes a **top retention bolt**, extracts the machine from foam blocks, and places it directly into the box for storage/return. Simpler, but the **reduced box height** puts the handle below shoulder level (awkward ergonomics, "like our old boxes"), and the **load shifts from the machine's rear axle to the front frame and plastic housing** — a configuration Andrei calls "our coffin" (untested structural risk). [[raw/Echo_packaging_Andrei-February-11-2026.md]]
- **Unresolved variables.** Box dimensions proposed but not validated; a size reference for the **10-inch prototype** is lacking; and Val notes the available supplier contacts "are not engineering companies which will consult you." Andrei expects "a lot of unpleasant discoveries ahead" but frames readiness as psychological — "at least now I am ready to continue." Action items recorded: Andrei to provide a **wet-crepe supplier contact** and finalize the **Plan B single-box spec** (dimensions, handle placement, load-distribution validation); **Val to vote** dual-box vs single-box. [[raw/Echo_packaging_Andrei-February-11-2026.md]]

## Jul 30 2026 — the assembly-capacity model: labor-hours per machine and six-person capacity vs demand

On the [[wiki/meetings/2026-07-30-georgia-production-ramp-forecast.md|Jul-30 2:14 PM ET manufacturing review]] ([[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/mikhail-perelman.md|Mikhail Perelman]], [[wiki/people/genadi-peari.md|Genadi Peari]], [[wiki/people/elizabeth.md|Elizabeth Palomino]], [[wiki/people/felipe-miranda.md|Felipe Miranda Giorgi]] translating) Elizabeth presented a conservative Excel capacity model for the Georgia build (Echo, Flex, Flex IQ, countertops). The forecast/parts side is on [[wiki/projects/georgia-market-rollout.md]] (Jul-30). [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Georgia_Production_Ramp_Forecast-July-30-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Georgia_Production_Ramp_Forecast-July-30-2026.md]]

- **The model.** ~**9.5 hours per Echo** (including ~2h software configuration + testing), ~**14.5 hours per Flex**, ~**2.5 hours per Flex IQ**; **six people × 35 hours/week × 4.2 weeks ≈ 882 hours** of monthly capacity (varying 882 / 903 / 924 by month) against ~**850 hours** of demand (a representative month ≈ 12–15 Echo + ~30 Flex). Genadi thinks the true per-machine times are roughly half — the numbers are conservative and should drop as the learning curve improves. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Georgia_Production_Ramp_Forecast-July-30-2026.md]]
- **Val's "belt and suspenders" acceptance.** Val does not accept that a Flex really takes 14 hours or that a week is only 35 hours (a normal week is **44 hours**, and overtime + half-day Saturdays can be added), and did not want to be held to those figures half a year later — but even on the deliberately conservative numbers the model leaves surplus capacity, so he accepted it. Conclusion: enough people and parts, no capacity issue for the ramp. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Georgia_Production_Ramp_Forecast-July-30-2026.md]]
- **The mix and why the first month is hardest.** Elizabeth is assembling countertops plus the first **Flex Slim / base-slim-station** prototypes and **Flex Sports** starts, so the first month is the most critical (prototypes take longest). Genadi added that small tasks — soldering the vision connector, fan crimping — add real time, and buying pre-made parts saves it. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Georgia_Production_Ramp_Forecast-July-30-2026.md]]

**§15.8:** clear — per-machine labor-hour / capacity economics only; no named-person compensation.
