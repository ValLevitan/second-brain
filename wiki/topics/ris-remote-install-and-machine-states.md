# RIS (remote install/setup system) and machine states

**RIS** is JVL's system for **installing and configuring the settings on a gaming machine** — pushing the correct screen values (progressive settings, RTP/bet buttons, payout order, devices, SAS/system, denomination, game selection) onto a machine from a **USB stick or a remote connection**, instead of a technician entering 30–40 pages of settings by hand. Each issued RIS carries its own **unique number**, distinct from the software version [[raw/ris_business_central_integration-april-3-2025.md]]. This page synthesises what RIS is, how JVL uses it to keep control of machines it sells, and the **Feb-6-2026 direction** to modernise it for sold machines.

*(Naming note: some staging labels call RIS a "reporting/integration system"; the sources consistently describe it as the machine **install/setup** system — that is what is recorded here.)*

## Why RIS matters to the business

- RIS lets JVL install several machines very fast — Juan cited **four to six machines in as little as 30 minutes** in South Dakota / Nebraska, versus competitors who deliver a machine and leave technicians to enter data "page after page." [[raw/RIS Modernization & Operator Flexibility Alignment.md]]
- RIS is also JVL's **control point**. JVL wanted to control the firmware and what goes inside its machines to prevent field abuses Val has seen over 45 years of operating — e.g. Latin-American technicians swapping a brand-new bill acceptor out of a JVL machine for a used one, or a crew that "doesn't care which product goes where" as long as the machine stays on the floor. [[raw/RIS Modernization & Operator Flexibility Alignment.md]]
- Licensing/enforcement sits on top of RIS: JVL licenses **software to an operator by a count of installations** (per-seat / concurrent-install), not to a specific machine, enforced through the **RIS manager fed by Business Central invoice data** — see [[wiki/decisions/2025-04-03-license-software-per-operator-not-machine.md]]. [[raw/ris_business_central_integration-april-3-2025.md]]

## The problem Feb-6-2026 opens

The current RIS was "designed with the requirements of the past" — mainly **profit-sharing (participation) machines in locations**. That is no longer the only case: JVL now **sells** machines. The existing 80/20-rule participation logic is fine and Val does not want to touch it; what needs new requirements is the **sold-machine** case, where the new owner ("the new ideologist / operator") may run the machine however they like and JVL's forced restrictions may be resented, rejected, or actively worked around. [[raw/RIS Modernization & Operator Flexibility Alignment.md]]

## Proposed direction (Val's "ideology," not yet a locked decision)

- **Three machine states** instead of the current participation-vs-sold flag: **participation**, **financed** (sold but still owed money — Val also floated "lease"), and **debt-free purchase** (sold and fully paid). Val wants a clean taxonomy rather than "faking the intent" the way Nebraska is handled today (a machine put in participation and later moved to sold once paid). The **participation RIS stays as-is** — a good product he does not want to dilute. [[raw/RIS Modernization & Operator Flexibility Alignment.md]]
- **Manual *override*, not manual *entry*.** Pavel argued a full manual mode (entering every screen by hand) would be a very large project; the cheap path is to keep using the **same RIS screens** but **enable editing on specific fields per case** (e.g. progressive value, RTP per game, which devices/games are installed). Val and Pavel converged on a **flexible flag marking which screens are editable and which are locked** — quite a bit more achievable than full manual mode. [[raw/RIS Modernization & Operator Flexibility Alignment.md]]
- **RIS as a paid subscription (business idea Val floated).** When a machine is fully paid, JVL could **disable RIS and turn the machine to manual-only**, then **charge the operator a recurring fee** to keep RIS/server access as a positive feature; if the operator stops paying, RIS becomes unenforceable and every allowable screen is manually editable. A legitimate operator may also simply decline RIS and enter fields manually. [[raw/RIS Modernization & Operator Flexibility Alignment.md]]
- **Lock/unlock feature (onus on the operator).** Once a machine is sold and fully paid, allowable screens open for editing; but the operator can choose to **lock screens from editing for a set period (up to ~180 days)** and, staying registered on JVL's server, **scan the machine to see whether any screens were overridden** — a control service the operator opts into on a recurring calendar. [[raw/RIS Modernization & Operator Flexibility Alignment.md]]

## Controls that cannot be sacrificed (Val, explicit)

- **Roll-back to restrictions must remain possible** — engineering must not treat "sold" as "no more restrictions ever," because JVL may need to re-assert jurisdiction control (example: a machine sold into loose-jurisdiction Puerto Rico must not come back to the US mainland). [[raw/RIS Modernization & Operator Flexibility Alignment.md]]
- **Finance control is absolute.** JVL frequently finances the machines; **until it is paid, JVL keeps every restriction it needs** ("please don't mix freedom with owing us money"). Val wants **tighter, less-manual control over the owing-money case** — check Business Central: if the balance for that serial number is **zero**, then (and only then) allow the move to a long lease, possibly automatically — rather than relying on someone (his example: Mery messaging Dima) manually granting a 99-year lease as a favour, which "could be a critical mistake." [[raw/RIS Modernization & Operator Flexibility Alignment.md]]
- **Approved software only.** In every US jurisdiction there is a regulator; only approved software can be installed. Software selection stays RIS-pushed and is **out of scope for phase one** — no messing with software/game-theme selection yet. [[raw/RIS Modernization & Operator Flexibility Alignment.md]]

## Juan's field constraints (why flexibility is needed now)

- **Regulated-jurisdiction timing (Florida game conversion).** For a linked progressive, Juan cannot burn the RIS USB two days ahead (the Gaming Commission wants the software 24–48h early for a GLI/Kobetron certification check) because the casino only turns the machines off the night before / morning of; today Sebastian waits in the parking lot to burn the RIS the morning of. Juan wants the ability to **push defaults down and manually override certain fields on site**, with **secure fields (progressive contribution) settable once, then locked** — changing them again forces a full **factory reset** that alerts supervisors and the Commission. [[raw/RIS Modernization & Operator Flexibility Alignment.md]]
- **Grey-market resale reality.** Used machines come off the floor, sit in a container/warehouse for months untouched, and are bought to be set up manually wherever they land (the IGT model — no manufacturer help). JVL wants its machines to be resaleable the same way, so a new owner who is **not a JVL customer** can set the machine up **manually** without JVL giving them server access (which Juan warned would make JVL **legally liable** for enabling a machine in an invalid jurisdiction). [[raw/RIS Modernization & Operator Flexibility Alignment.md]]

## Reinitialisation anchor (engineering)

- Pavel: if a sold machine's **RAM is cleared**, it needs the RIS previously used on it re-applied to keep operating. Val pressed for an **irrevocable reference number that survives a RAM wipe**; Pavel confirmed today that ID relates to the **Dallas one-wire "cube" that holds the passport information** on the CPU board. The restore can only ever return the machine to **the last state JVL officially sold it at**. [[raw/RIS Modernization & Operator Flexibility Alignment.md]]

## July 8 2026 — RIS personas and a field-editable RIS

By July 2026 the RIS **code had been reverse-engineered into human-readable requirements**, which surfaced five "personas" (role archetypes, not real people) — the actors the RIS redesign must serve. Two same-day working sessions worked through them: [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] with [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]] first ([[wiki/meetings/2026-07-08-ris-persona-clarity.md|2:45 PM]]), then Val with [[wiki/people/pavel.md|Pavel]] and [[wiki/people/irina-shishko.md|Irina]] ([[wiki/meetings/2026-07-08-ris-persona-roles-engineering-review.md|3:46 PM]]). The full persona framework (the five roles, their renames, and the operator-side additions) lives on [[wiki/topics/persona-driven-design-and-orchestration-platform.md]] (July-8 section); the RIS-specific design points are here.

- **RIS stays, but becomes field-editable.** A key design intent: keep RIS because it makes installation fast, but allow the configuration to be **tweaked in the field after install** — which cannot be done today. This is the same "manual *override*, not manual *entry*" direction opened Feb-6-2026 (editable-per-screen flags), now attached to the persona work. (Juan, endorsed by Val.) [[raw/Meeting_Notes_RIS_Persona_Clarity-July-8-2026.md]]
- **The reverse-engineering produced only 22, too-coarse requirements.** Because the RIS code ties whole pages to one person, the 22 requirements bundle too much; Val wants each split into five or more finer, singular requirements. The redesign method: first document how RIS works today, then use that gap analysis to design new personas → requirements → features → code. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_RIS_Persona_Clarity-July-8-2026.md]]
- **The field workflow the redesign enables.** Persona 5 builds the **immovable frame** (the absolute limits no one can cross); Persona 1 — a JVL role renamed **"Production Personnel"** — prepares the machine and sets default modes before the operator takes it; if the master operator does not want those modes, someone under him reconfigures the machine as the operator wishes and releases it that way; location personnel have only **limited capabilities at the site**. Market-specific limits (e.g. different values for Georgia, Nebraska, Texas) are set by Persona 4 but only within Persona 5's frame. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_RIS_Persona_Roles_Engineering_Review-July-8-2026.md]]
- **New operator-side roles mean new front-end work.** Three of the personas — **master operator, operator administrator (renamed "Operator Production Supervisor"), and location personnel** — sit on the operator's side, outside JVL. These are roles JVL never had, so the RIS rebuild needs new capabilities and front-end screens written for them; Pavel and Irina are to write those once the personas are settled. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_RIS_Persona_Roles_Engineering_Review-July-8-2026.md]]
- **An undecided control point.** Val flagged it as still open whether **location personnel (and tips) can influence a machine's configuration**, or whether selling a machine hands full control to the operator. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_RIS_Persona_Roles_Engineering_Review-July-8-2026.md]]

## Jun 18 2026 — the earliest operator conversation about the Passport, and the first offer to remove it

**This is the earliest source in the corpus in which Val explains the component lock to an operator, offers to remove it, and uses the name "passport" for it** — two and a half months before the Lucky Coin and Diamond conversations below. It is also the only one where the operator answers with a *narrower* request instead of accepting the offer. From the [[wiki/meetings/2026-06-18-mg-oil-dana-giesey-rapid-city-visit.md|June-18 2026 MG Oil visit]] in Rapid City ([[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] + [[wiki/people/dana-giesey.md|Dana Giesey]]). [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_MG_Oil_Dana_Giesey_South_Dakota_Visit-June-18-2026.md]]

**The complaint, from the operator.** "some of our technicians are kind of struggling a little bit on the machines because they're just **so different than what we were used to operating** … with the new machines that have the QR code and certain components on the machine that need to be replaced. Like, we had a **backplane board** or something we replaced a couple days ago here, and then to get it out — and certain components — you got to **reload the software after your change and scan the QR code.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_MG_Oil_Dana_Giesey_South_Dakota_Visit-June-18-2026.md]]

**Val's answer is the same validator-swap origin story he later tells Lucky Coin and Diamond — told first here, and told twice in this one meeting.** He names the markets ("a technician, let's say in **Peru or Mexico or Colombia** — this is where we used to operate"), the mechanism ("they would take our brand new validators, sell it and replace it with some **used validators**"), and the commercial consequence ("our machines **acceptance rate falls down** … your machine makes less money"). Second telling, later in the same meeting, with the customer's own frustration acted out: "they'd take our validators out, put **some crap validator** inside of our machine, put the good stuff in theirs … the customer will get frustrated and say '**I don't want this shit**,' and move on to a machine that takes bills." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_MG_Oil_Dana_Giesey_South_Dakota_Visit-June-18-2026.md]]

**And he names the mechanism "passport", with the birth-certificate metaphor, in June.** "we have a **passport for machine**. So when machine is manufactured, we scan passport. The passport scans every **product number, serial number** installed at the date of … the machine" — and the second telling: "**When a machine is born on the manufacturing floor, whatever we put in at that time is issued a passport — like a birth certificate. 'I was born with this.'**" The inventory half: sanctioned spares go "**to a remote warehouse for pre-scanning**", so "if it's a sanctioned device, we say go ahead… but if they bring some piece of shit that we don't know about, **we wouldn't issue a QR code**." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_MG_Oil_Dana_Giesey_South_Dakota_Visit-June-18-2026.md]]

**The second reason, which the later sources do not give: settings discipline.** "we wanted machines to be set the way we wanted to set them… a tech would set the machines for them, and they had **a little piece of paper** … And every tech would make a little mistake here, a little mistake there. So… **limits would be set up differently on every machine**… it would drive us nuts having different sheets of papers." The payoff he sells from it is fleet-wide change: "**We will change it globally on every machine**, and then it's much easier to control." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_MG_Oil_Dana_Giesey_South_Dakota_Visit-June-18-2026.md]]

**The offer — free, in the next release.** "We designed it for ourselves; **it's not for everyone** … **We will remove it** … you establish a lease — basically, you say for the next ninety-nine years, don't worry about this machine … and then your hands are free; go do whatever you want, operate it as you were before." And: "**We will put it in the next release of software totally free** … **you tell us what you want to be removed and we'll remove it. If you don't want the QR code scan, no problem.**" (He also told Dana **Keno games are coming in the same release**.) [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_MG_Oil_Dana_Giesey_South_Dakota_Visit-June-18-2026.md]]

**The operator's counter, which is the interesting part.** Dana did **not** take the removal. He wanted the **initial-setup scan kept** — "As far as the initial setup of the machine, that's probably something we want to set **because we don't want people changing anything in there**" — and only the **reload-on-component-change** removed: "**That part I think we would want to remove.**" Val's framing had invited exactly this: "don't forget, then **you don't have control over what, how the machines are set**"; Dana: "**No, they definitely not** [want that control]." Val closed it: "**Done. We'll do it in the next series.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_MG_Oil_Dana_Giesey_South_Dakota_Visit-June-18-2026.md]]

**And then the better answer: give the operator the portal.** Dana's real problem was the wait, not the lock — "you have to **take a picture and call somebody and send them that picture, then wait for them to get something back to you**"; parts arrived fast, "**the only holdup was just waiting on the codes**." What he asked for: "**Portal** — so I don't have to send it in and wait for somebody to send me a number to put in it. I can just **log in**… **I can hurry up, get done, get out of the location, get the player back.**" Val's read, on the spot: "**For us, it's probably easier to give you access to the portal. I assume I have to speak to our guys**" — and "maybe a portal is going to resolve everything for you. **We'll teach you once and you guys do whatever you need to do.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_MG_Oil_Dana_Giesey_South_Dakota_Visit-June-18-2026.md]]

**Why it matters to this page.** The Sept-11 internal ruling below — keep the Passport, change what JVL does with it, and let the operator nominate who may update it — is what this customer asked for in June: **keep the lock, move the key.** Nothing in the corpus connects the two, and this is noted as a resemblance, not a causal link.

**Open commitments from this exchange.** Val undertook to confirm portal access with his engineers, and to deliver the agreed South Dakota QR/settings changes free in the next release; Dana undertook to canvass his team and say what to keep — listed on [[wiki/people/dana-giesey.md]] and [[wiki/organizations/mg-oil.md]]. **§15 #8:** clear — product control design only; no named-person compensation. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_MG_Oil_Dana_Giesey_South_Dakota_Visit-June-18-2026.md]]

## Sept 2 2026 — the customer-facing rationale for component locking, and a second offer to switch it off

The [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-02-lucky-coin-service-and-upgrade-offer.md|Sept-2 Lucky Coin visit]] is where Val explains **to a Georgia operator** why JVL's machines require a reset when hardware changes, and offers to remove the control on request [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Lucky_Coin_Service_and_Upgrade_Offer-September-2-2026.md]]. *(This was recorded as the first such conversation in the corpus until the June-18 2026 MG Oil source above was ingested; the June-18 exchange predates it by two and a half months and makes the same offer.)*

- **The reason is validator-swap fraud, learned as an operator.** "**I'll tell you why we require [a] reset**, and you tell me if you don't need this feature. **We require [a] reset when we restrict certain things.** For example, we don't want certain bill acceptors installed on our machines. We don't want certain bill acceptors to be replaced on our machines, **and that comes from my experience being an operator**." The mechanism: "if I install a brand new validator on the machine and I put it on my route, and I have a **disgruntled technician**… he will take my validator, **sell it on the side, and replace it with the used validator**. That was **prevalent in Latin America**."
- **So the machine is trained, not just configured.** "we **allow and disallow certain changes, allow and disallow certain components**. For that reason, we build [a] **remote initial setup structure that guides the machine, trains the machine a certain way** — how we want to build it."
- **He named the cost of it in the same breath.** "then, if I want to quickly swap a validator, and you, as the business owner, set it up not to allow it — **well, it's inconvenient for the technician**."
- **The question he says it really poses is the operator's, not JVL's.** "**How should a business be run? Should I have serialized inventory control, or should I not? Should I have inventory control by brand, or should I not? Should I have a twenty-five cents bet on the machine, or should I not?** So certain locations do, certain locations [do] not. **All of this could be set through remote initial setup, and now downloaded through a mo[dem]**."
- **And his own account of why the fight happens at all:** "we just sometimes [are] not getting the decision maker, the operator who really makes a decision in the business, to listen to us. **That creates a lot of inconveniences. We can remove all of this, but in the long run, I'm not sure that we are servicing the business correctly.**" He put it as an open dilemma: "**On one hand, we're pushing it forward. On [the] other hand, we're saying, should we not do that and just be like everybody else? I don't have an answer.**"
- **The offer, made on the spot, when the customer said he had never suffered the fraud:** "**Then you don't need that feature, and we should remove it.** Then we should remove it." He attached a counter-offer rather than an argument: "**I will just report your acceptance rate to you on your validators. You'll be surprised.**" (That data offer is on [[wiki/topics/machine-data-repurchase-program.md]].)

**Why it matters to this page:** RIS is documented here mostly as an internal control problem — jurisdictions, finance, resale. This source shows the control being **negotiated with the customer as a product option**, per location, which none of the earlier sessions contemplates. It is also the operator-side cost of the same lock-down: the **decommission / re-flash / recommission** cycle that made this customer's service complaint (see [[wiki/organizations/lucky-coin.md]]), and which Val committed to reduce without a date.

## Sept 10 2026 — the lock is named ("the Passport") in Georgia too, and given a date on which it dies

Eight days after offering to switch component locking off for one customer, [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] explained the same mechanism to another — [[wiki/organizations/diamond-amusement.md|Diamond Amusement]], at the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-10-diamond-amusements-machine-performance-and-bundle-pilot.md|Sept-10 visit]] — and this time he **placed it in the architecture and gave a date for its removal.** *(This was recorded as the first source in the corpus to use the name "passport"; the **June-18 2026** MG Oil source above uses it three months earlier, so the name is not new here — the architectural claim and the removal date are.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Diamond_Amusements_Machine_Performance_and_Transactional_Bundle_Pilot-September-10-2026.md]]

**What the operator experiences.** A Diamond technician, unprompted: "**When you reset the software on any other game, I can go back in and manually put something in. I may have to wait all day to get the settings back from Felipe**, and… going back and forth **to get a passcode key. It's just so much more to do with a JVL.**" The workflow they described: call JVL, wait for the settings file **and a passcode key**, "**download the settings onto your thumb drive**", fetch a laptop, drive it back out. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Diamond_Amusements_Machine_Performance_and_Transactional_Bundle_Pilot-September-10-2026.md]]

**Val's account of where it came from — the same validator-swap story as Sept 2, with numbers.** "**We had a very, very large operation in Latin America, and we would send brand new machines there. And our bill acceptors would get swapped with a used bill acceptor**… So we said, 'Okay, well, how do we combat it?' **We created a passport, which is the thing that you're dealing with.** And we will prohibit **any exchange of anything at all**… **And if you have to change something, we will give you [a] security code.** Why? Because… **in exactly 30 days we would end up with the used printers, validators that had [an] acceptance rate of 60 percent.**" The setting he describes is a casino with a mixed floor: "we would have a bank of machines, and **the owner of the casino would have his own machines, and it's his technicians that would service the machines.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Diamond_Amusements_Machine_Performance_and_Transactional_Bundle_Pilot-September-10-2026.md]]

- He repeated that RIS is not a bad idea and is not designed wrongly: "The current RIS… is designed based on the requirements of JVL being an operator in Latin America. ### Felipe's analysis — what a hard reset demands

- **The reframe:** "the passport is a symptom, not a root cause. The underlying issue is how many different conditions have forced hard reset on a JVL machine, and what a hard reset obligates our customers to do every single time it happens." Val agreed: "all difficulties come from… The necessity of a hard reset, if there was a point of recovery… Without hard reset… then none of it would be an issue." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_RIS_and_Passport_Hard_Reset_Burden-September-23-2026.md]]
- **Three things the technician must carry:** the correct software version on a USB stick (JVL has "approximately 15 to 17 different software versions across the history of JVL"), the RIS file on the stick, and the passport code, because "any peripheral change, any change, bill acceptor, whatever, it will require new passport update". Missing any one, "the technician skill is irrelevant". The software cannot be emailed: about 7 GB for the older software and more than 9 for the new. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_RIS_and_Passport_Hard_Reset_Burden-September-23-2026.md]]
- **Felipe's standing advice** is to leave the RIS file on a USB stick inside the machine, but JVL cannot control that — a technician can "put it in their pocket, leave, and the RIS file is gone". Pavel confirmed settings can still be downloaded over a LAN cable; Felipe said that can mean running a 100-foot cable across the store. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_RIS_and_Passport_Hard_Reset_Burden-September-23-2026.md]]
- **The commissioning cost (Georgia).** Before the repair the technician calls the central-system hotline (transcript "Interlock"; read as **Intralot** — inference), gives location ID, address and port number, and waits; decommissioning "Sometimes it takes 8 to 15 minutes". After the reinstall he calls again to commission the port; "This could take 10 to 20 minutes if it all goes well. If something is not matching, it could take an hour, two hours". If the hard reset was done before the call, a forced decommission is needed and "the supervisor consent is required". Felipe noted the documented order is to call and decommission first, then reset. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_RIS_and_Passport_Hard_Reset_Burden-September-23-2026.md]]

### The cases Felipe listed ("Of the 8 cases that I listed here, these are the most common")

1. **Battery changed with the machine powered off.** The machine boots into a critical error that only a hard reset clears; the on-screen QR warning is "routinely" ignored. Workaround found: change the battery with the machine powered on. "A five-minute maintenance task becomes a full decommission and recommission cycle."
2. **Software upgrade and reinstall.** JVL creates a new RIS file per machine, one at a time; the customer "physically drives to JVL to collect RIS and USB". Error code 2 or error code 3 appears during install "across all different software versions", more often since the new install flow that asks the technician to confirm by pressing spin; the USB stick and hardware have been ruled out; "no clear response has been received to date".
3. **Software crash, freeze or error tilt** that survives a power cycle — fixed only by a clean reload of the exact version, "only possible if the technicians happens to be carrying it". Felipe said freezes are not happening "right now" with the new software; he was "speaking historically".
4. **Adding or removing a printer** (TITO being introduced for transactional products). JVL builds a new RIS for that serial number; after the reinstall "The passport screen appears, guaranteed"; and "A JVL employee must be next to his computer or with internet access at the moment he sends the code because the lease is not active."
5. **Boot loop** after an electrical problem at the location — "unexplainable"; only a hard reset clears it (see [[wiki/organizations/diamond-amusement.md]]).
6. **Critical error from a hardware fault** — an IO board failure, the IO board not reading the counter board, a failing USB connection, or "a data chip improperly connected". Repairing the board "does not clear the error".
7. **RIS and lease mismatch at renewal** — when one or two of a batch of machines were not updated, the lease scan fails and the only fix is a hard reset with the correct software or RIS.
8. **The critical-error screen says nothing** — "a yellow screen, a large triangle, critical error"; scanning it only says the information was sent to JVL customer support, "and the word information is misspelled, by the way".

All from [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_RIS_and_Passport_Hard_Reset_Burden-September-23-2026.md]].
