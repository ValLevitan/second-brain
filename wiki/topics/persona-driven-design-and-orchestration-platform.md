# Persona-driven design and the new orchestration platform

A long teaching session (March 10 2025) in which [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] and [[wiki/people/aaron.md|Aaron]] pushed the Minsk engineering team to design a **new "orchestration platform"** for JVL's machine operations by first understanding the **people (personas)** who will use it — rather than starting from the software JVL already has. Aaron facilitated in English; a "Dima" translated to Russian (this embedded "Dima" is **not attributed** — see Owner Decisions). Other Minsk participants named: Andrei and Sergei. [[raw/persona_development_journey_meeting_with_minsk-2025-03-10.md]]

> The transcript is heavily garbled (Whisper artefacts, mixed English/Russian, no punctuation). Only claims that are literally readable in the source are recorded below; the rest is left out rather than guessed.

## The core disagreement: persona vs. job description

- Val had earlier written a **persona** for an assembly worker, "**Alex**." The Minsk lead ("Dima"/"Dmitry") reformatted it into GitHub as a **job description** — cutting the "irrelevant" personal details (marital status, where he lives) and keeping only the technical facts of what Alex does, because those seemed to have nothing to do with designing the product. [[raw/persona_development_journey_meeting_with_minsk-2025-03-10.md]]
- Val's counter-argument: the personal details **are** the point. Knowing where someone lives, whether they're married, whether they drive kids to school builds **empathy** for who the customer is, which reveals their skill level and the value they bring — and therefore what the software must do for them. A job description tells you the technical facts; a persona gets you "into the mind" of a potential customer. [[raw/persona_development_journey_meeting_with_minsk-2025-03-10.md]]
- "Alex" is deliberately a **temporary, low-skill assembly worker with no technical background** — someone you could swap with an automotive mechanic, who just follows instructions. The software must be designed for that person, not for the engineer. [[raw/persona_development_journey_meeting_with_minsk-2025-03-10.md]]

## The teaching frame

- Val cited the book **"The Design of Everyday Things"** (the teapot on the cover) as his inspiration for this thinking, and shared it with the team (English PDF plus a Russian translation). The point: design the everyday thing the **best way for the user (Alex)**, which reduces cost and increases efficiency — not the best way for the builder. [[raw/persona_development_journey_meeting_with_minsk-2025-03-10.md]]
- The value of a written persona is a **shared reference document**: instead of Sergei, Dmitri, Andre and Pasha each carrying their own private idea of the customer, everyone agrees on who the customer is and can then apply their own minds to how to build for that customer. [[raw/persona_development_journey_meeting_with_minsk-2025-03-10.md]]
- The "wheelchair door" story (an architect whose mother used a wheelchair) was used to show that understanding the end user changes the product — and that, for Val, understanding the customer is ultimately **about making more money**, not sentiment. [[raw/persona_development_journey_meeting_with_minsk-2025-03-10.md]]

## Who the system's customers are

- Val insisted **he is not the only customer**. Having one source of truth (himself) introduces bias — which is why he brought in [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]] and [[wiki/people/michael-gottlieb.md|Michael]] as strong voices to counteract his own opinionated one. [[raw/persona_development_journey_meeting_with_minsk-2025-03-10.md]]
- Customers of the **system** include Val (owner), Juan, the technician, the assembly manager, "Marta," "Alex" the assembler, "Andre," and **Sergei Novitsky** (see below). **Players are not** the system's customer — Val is designing an operations *system*, not a game. [[raw/persona_development_journey_meeting_with_minsk-2025-03-10.md]]
- Requirements should be gathered by **interviewing** these people with the question **"what do you actually do?"** — without preconceived notions, and without letting the software JVL already has colour the answer. From those interviews Val will derive the requirements for the new **orchestration platform**, and only then move to features. [[raw/persona_development_journey_meeting_with_minsk-2025-03-10.md]]

## Why it matters for JVL's operations

- JVL's machines today are set up through a **CMS** whose procedures are "not easy." **Alex** (the general assembler) is **not trusted** with anything technical; the most advanced machine setups are done by **Sergei Novitsky** (transcribed "Serge Navitsky") or **Arkady**. Val's stated goal is to **automate that advanced-setup role away** in the new platform ("I'm driving for an up-solution of this function"), reducing manual processes and requiring fewer people. [[raw/persona_development_journey_meeting_with_minsk-2025-03-10.md]]
- Val framed the business objective as **scalability both ways (up and down, on demand)**, fewer manual processes, and large **free cash / profit margin** (not revenue). He noted this style of thinking makes **monolithic software** impossible — "you can't write monolithic software that way anymore." [[raw/persona_development_journey_meeting_with_minsk-2025-03-10.md]]
- Related to the "service advocate" persona work on [[wiki/topics/flexpay-mobile-app-and-service-advocates.md]] — the term "sales advocates" recurs here for the people who champion machines. [[raw/persona_development_journey_meeting_with_minsk-2025-03-10.md]]

## The review workflow instruction (~Mar 25 2025, date inferred)

Two days on, Val gave Dima ([[wiki/people/dmitri-chudinov.md|Chudinov]]) and [[wiki/people/sergey-tsymanov.md|Sergey]] (with "Andrei" — [[wiki/people/andrey-bosik.md|Bosik]] by context — present) the concrete review workflow for the AI-generated material now sitting in GitHub (stub: [[wiki/meetings/2025-03-25-cms-requirements-review-workflow.md]]): [[raw/cms_requirements_development-dzima-sergei-andrei.md]]

- **Sequence:** talk through the legacy-CMS **overview** first; if ~80% right, don't dig further ("не пытайтесь сделать это perfect") — then the **use cases** per persona, then the **requirements**. Corrections go in as **pull requests only** — "Коммит, пожалуйста, сами не делайте… Я хочу прочитать, что вы написали"; nobody pulls until the group discusses. [[raw/cms_requirements_development-dzima-sergei-andrei.md]]
- **Provenance disclaimer Val gave:** the requirements were written by AI from AI-written use cases, from an AI interpretation of Dima's block diagram — "здесь возможно, что ошибки compile"; he lacked the engineering knowledge and time to polish each layer, and he hasn't read all of it himself. The goal is to **conserve** the legacy system, touch it minimally, and move new work to the future platform. [[raw/cms_requirements_development-dzima-sergei-andrei.md]]
- **After approval:** derive **features** for the legacy product, link requirements↔features (Val believes an Atlassian application can link them graphically "с нитками"), open the features in the GitHub repo, place the code under each feature ("если у вас есть код, который нельзя ни к чему прилипить… вы неправильно продумали фичерс"), then build with **actions**, deploy, and conserve it. Long-unused functionality (e.g. the **Italy** legacy code) can be dropped — historically nothing was thrown out "потому что формально он же нам не принадлежит." [[raw/cms_requirements_development-dzima-sergei-andrei.md]]
- **For the new platform afterwards:** new use cases per persona (example: **Alex** the production persona has none yet, but they want Alex able to "оживить машину" — revive a machine — anywhere, regardless of location, without being able to make a mistake), then requirements (discussed, approved, with **votings** — "За каждый requirement we're gonna vote"), then features, then architecture, then applications, grooming, MVPs by priority — and no code before requirements: "если нет requirements, то никому не надо писать код." [[raw/cms_requirements_development-dzima-sergei-andrei.md]]

## The product = repository doctrine argued onto the CMS (Apr 14–15 2025)

Two more sessions ([[wiki/meetings/2025-04-14-cms-svn-github-product-repos.md]], [[wiki/meetings/2025-04-15-cms-migration-staged-decision.md]]) carried the methodology into the CMS migration; the resulting staged plan is [[wiki/decisions/2025-04-15-cms-staged-migration-plan.md]]. Durable methodology points:

- **A product exists in exactly two forms** — "Мой продукт может быть в двух формах. Или это ISO image … Или он должен лежать на … production server. Других продуктов у меня быть не может" — and it leaves its repository via **GitHub Actions**. One product = one repository; if pushing a product to production needs Actions written across two or three repos, "наверное, у нас продукт неправильно описан." [[raw/cms_svn_github-april-14-2025.md]]
- **The Biratka framework-repo lesson** (Val's cautionary tale): after ~a year on the new framework, when "Бератко" ([[wiki/people/alexander-biratka.md|Sasha Biratka]]) had to write Actions and assemble the product from two-three repositories, "тут зажглась лампочка … у нас продукт неправильно описан" — repo structure exposes wrong product definitions. [[raw/cms_svn_github-april-14-2025.md]]
- **The shared vocabulary chain Val is instilling:** taxonomies (таксономии) → personas → requirements (требования) → product → architecture diagram → capabilities (возможности) → features (функции) → feature branch → code — "Если мы не научимся говорить правильно про таксонами … то как компания мы ничего не застегнем." Tester culture is part of it: tests are designed with the feature ("тестировка начнется во время проекта, а не в конце"; unit + integration tests; "Тестировщик это … инженер, который пишет тесты"). [[raw/cms_svn_github-april-14-2025.md]]
- **The contracts precedent** (Val on requirements he wrote himself): "я лично писал эти контракты … я ошибся … Я понял, что я от вас требую того, что не нужно требовать" — which forced removing the contracts; his argument for developer–executive dialogue over isolated work. The Marta persona uses both the current flows and contracts, with "функционал у них просто пропасть между ними, а суть примерно одна и то же — пропустит машину в продакшн." [[raw/cms_svn_github-april-14-2025.md]]
- **No status tables:** Val refuses table/деливерable-style reporting ("Вам дали таблицу, и что вы с ней делаете потом? … Вы нагрузите людей … вы ничего с этим не сделаете") — agreement is reached by recorded conversation + AI transcript with action items, not written artifacts. [[raw/cms_svn_github-april-14-2025.md]]
- **Fault-isolation rationale for the future modular CMS** (Chudinov, Apr 15): in the monolith "если один работает нестабильно на сервере … перестают работать все," while machine-licence issuance "должны продолжать работать всегда"; manifests (game settings, progressives, bet buttons) are filled by hand today by "либо я, либо Паша, либо Ира" and must be automated in the new platform. [[raw/cms_github_svn-apr-15-2025.md]]

## Aug 14 2025 — CMS modernization kickoff; the observability & orchestration platform

On the [[wiki/meetings/2025-08-14-backend-architecture-cms-transactional-hhr.md|backend-architecture kickoff]] ([[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]], [[wiki/people/aaron.md|Aaron]], [[wiki/people/will-jones.md|Will Jones]], [[wiki/people/pavel.md|Pavel]], [[wiki/people/nathan-carver.md|Nathan]], [[wiki/people/dmitri-chudinov.md|Dmitri Chudinov]]) the CMS-replacement thread advanced. The cross-cutting ruling (block-diagram-first, phased MVPs) is on [[wiki/decisions/2025-08-14-backend-architecture-block-diagram-first-mvp.md]]. [[raw/backend_architecture-august-14-2025.md]]

- **The client portal is now the "observability and orchestration platform."** Pavel: the old CMS design assumed the only users are **internal (JVL employees)**; the future platform (the former "client portal," now renamed the **observability and orchestration platform**) needs **different user types** — operators who own their own **Flex POS / Flex Tower / other devices** and set up their locations, assign devices, and operate their flex transactions; and customers who own some machines and change settings. This is the future replacing the legacy CMS. [[raw/backend_architecture-august-14-2025.md]]
- **Aaron synthesized ~2 years of Val's recordings into a CMS knowledge-base repo.** Two GitHub repositories were created — one holding the **CMS code**, and a "**CMSNG / NgCMS**" space where Aaron distilled the recorded CMS discussions into an **architectural design doc + GitHub issues** of "inferred," "speculative" concerns and challenges — explicitly "not correct, not wrong," an **inspirational starting point** ("what could/should we be doing"), not ground truth. It does **not** yet cover HHR or transactional products. [[raw/backend_architecture-august-14-2025.md]]
- **The CMS as it stands.** A **decade-plus-old monolithic Microsoft SQL Server** codebase "on the iron," with strong **tribal knowledge** but poor organizational state — documentation exists but is scattered across obsolete Confluence pages, not in repos or a README "storyboard." Aaron's SQL caveat: many scripts are single-thread-locking-transaction-specific, so the DB backend can't be swapped piecemeal — it's a holistic change. Val's framing added "**architectural debt**" alongside "technological debt" (some CMS applications are no longer available or updatable). [[raw/backend_architecture-august-14-2025.md]]
- **Common modules for both legacy and future (Pavel).** Both systems reuse the same building blocks — **passport management, remote initial setup (RIS), lease management, user/operator/location management, analytics/reporting** — usable for machine operation and payment-device operation alike. Val noted Pavel had "skillfully" identified them as modules/features/capabilities that "are not identified" and must be extracted from the monolith into a form executives and developers can discuss. [[raw/backend_architecture-august-14-2025.md]]
- **The "lease" taxonomy is erroneous.** Val recounted that the term **"lease"** was coined between [[wiki/people/efim-podvoiski.md|Efim]] and **Boris** (JVL's then-VP of engineering), both of whom "hardly spoke English" — it was the closest single word and "got stuck at JVL." Aaron reframed it: they are really describing **machine-state management** and **separation of concerns**, not leasing. The design gap: the lease should turn **off / to indefinite** when a machine is fully paid (invoice balance = 0) and control pass to the operator. JVL has **no desire to control machines it doesn't own** (a legacy of operating machines in Latin America, where everything hinged on an operational contract). Resolution direction: on **Omni**, setup screens **emulate RIS** so passport/lease/setup can be done manually on the machine (no remote access needed once sold); keep RIS/passport/lease **mandatory for transactional products** but **optional on owned-out EGMs**. [[raw/backend_architecture-august-14-2025.md]]
- **Operator self-service + identity.** Operators would log into JVL's **web** system and see only their own locations/machines/devices, changing settings centrally instead of via on-device setup screens; customer separation via **Microsoft Entra** (Aaron corrected the group's "Active Directory" → it's **Entra**, an identity-management system). Juan flagged the concern that no other manufacturer lets third parties log directly into the master/Omni system at this level of access; Aaron pushed the group to decide the **system scale** (POS-management-at-scale patterns, e.g. Square) before diving to modules. [[raw/backend_architecture-august-14-2025.md]]
- **Aaron's method warnings.** "**Speculative feature development is very expensive**" — make seemingly-valuable operator features concrete before building. **Language/terminology confusion** is "the biggest obstacle to success" at JVL (e.g. don't casually say "database" when you mean "data lake"). Work **capabilities-driven**, "**don't create the solution while writing the specification**," treat the migration as **ETL for the software stack** (inventory what still serves vs what doesn't), and bring documentation **into the code repo** (Mermaid in-repo, IEEE-style architecture docs "as a framework, not a mandate"). [[raw/backend_architecture-august-14-2025.md]]
- **Phased place in the plan (derived undertakings doc).** Minimal CMS support is a **Phase-1 MVP** owned by Pavel (device authentication, location/terminal-ID management, daily email reports; defer legacy migration); full **CMS modernization + Omni migration** is the **Oct-15 2025** deep-dive (Phase 2). [[raw/backend_architecture_meeting_undertakings_august_14_2025.md]]

## Aug 20 2025 — CMS/PayFlex block-diagram review (arc session 1 of 3)
