# Game art & animation production pipeline (comp art → Miranda)

How JVL turns a game concept into finished visual art and animation. Synthesised from [[wiki/people/lena.md|Lena]]'s Mar 12 2025 step-by-step walkthrough for [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]]. This is the *art production* side; the design/mechanics side is on [[wiki/topics/slot-game-design-principles.md]] and the throughput goals Val wants to impose on this pipeline are on [[wiki/topics/game-production-throughput-strategy.md]]. [[raw/Game Development How animation are made with Lena - March 12-2025.md]]

## The stages, in order

1. **Concept → brief.** After the market requirements are understood and the game mechanics and math are ready, [[wiki/people/michael-gottlieb.md|Michael]] (in discussion with [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]]) settles a game concept, then **sends Lena an email describing his vision** and asks her to develop **comp art**. [[raw/Game Development How animation are made with Lena - March 12-2025.md]]
2. **Fundamentals comp art.** Lena, as art director, first produces the **fundamentals** — how the main screen, bonus, logo and symbols will look — before any animation work. [[raw/Game Development How animation are made with Lena - March 12-2025.md]]
3. **Animation design.** Only once the fundamentals are set does she produce more elaborated comp art for the **animations of each symbol and the transitions**. A **complex** animation gets a **storyboard** (comp art showing the milestones of the animation); a **simple** one (e.g. a rooster lifting its head and opening its beak, or a symbol just rotating/enlarging) gets a **verbal description** instead. Storyboards are posted "in conference" and kept with the game-design doc / story description; Lena said she focuses on the creation, not where it is stored. [[raw/Game Development How animation are made with Lena - March 12-2025.md]]

Lena stressed that **animation is a production-stage activity, not a design-stage one** — it can only be worked on **after the final comp art is developed** — which is why she personally cannot focus on animation until the comp art is done. She defines what needs to be animated and how (approving it with Michael, and sometimes sending it to Juan, e.g. for signage). A Photoshop storyboard takes **a couple of hours to about a day**, depending on how complex the scenario is. [[raw/Game Development How animation are made with Lena - March 12-2025.md]]

## How many animations

The number of animations is roughly **predetermined by the game's mechanics/structure**. Each game has a **basic set**: animations for all symbols, the game background, the free-spins background (if the game has free spins), a logo animation, and a bonus-background animation — plus extras if a feature (e.g. a recognition or transition) is decided to involve the main character. [[raw/Game Development How animation are made with Lena - March 12-2025.md]]

## Who does the work

- **Animations** are done by the game artists — named as **Julia** ([[wiki/people/yulia.md|Yulia]]), **Anastasia**, **Sergey**, **Natasha**, or **Dima** ([[wiki/people/dmitriy-khandov.md|Dmitriy Khandov]]) — though Dima lately does little game graphics, being focused mostly on **Miranda**. (Sergey and Natasha are named artists with no page yet.) [[raw/Game Development How animation are made with Lena - March 12-2025.md]]
- There is **no automation** that produces the animation automatically yet ("not yet, maybe in a pretty close future"). A rooster opening/closing its beak and twisting its head still has to be animated by hand. [[raw/Game Development How animation are made with Lena - March 12-2025.md]]
- **Placeholder cutting** can be given to any game artist, usually a junior (Natasha, Vitaly, "anybody"). [[raw/Game Development How animation are made with Lena - March 12-2025.md]]

## Tools — 3D vs illustration

For each symbol the team discusses the most optimal/fastest route: [[raw/Game Development How animation are made with Lena - March 12-2025.md]]

- **3D model** when it is faster or when the symbol needs a different camera view in different parts of the game (e.g. the cats for **Fortune Football**). The 3D model is **post-produced in Adobe After Effects**; the animation is done in **Blender** — JVL is **moving off 3D Max** in the new framework because **Blender is free and not tied to 3D Max versions**. [[raw/Game Development How animation are made with Lena - March 12-2025.md]]
- **Illustration + Spine** when a character/object is used only once — draw it, cut it into special pieces, animate in **Spine**. [[raw/Game Development How animation are made with Lena - March 12-2025.md]]
- JVL **almost never uses the raw output** of a 3D program directly — there is always a **post-production pass in Adobe After Effects**. [[raw/Game Development How animation are made with Lena - March 12-2025.md]]

## Assembly in Miranda

- The **final output** (a frame sequence from Spine or After Effects) is applied to an object in a **Miranda scene** that represents a symbol on the reels; on export, that object plays the animation. [[raw/Game Development How animation are made with Lena - March 12-2025.md]]
- **Placeholder build (fast play-testing):** an entire game can be built for Michael with **just comp-art placeholders and no animations** — textures cut from the Photoshop file — so the reels spin with static/proxy art. This takes about **one day per game**. Michael and Efim asked for this specifically, which is why **Dima worked on integrating the math into Miranda**, so the game can run as soon as possible for them to test gameplay. It isn't produced automatically because each game has different symbol sizes, reel matrices and reel positions, so it would need a special script. [[raw/Game Development How animation are made with Lena - March 12-2025.md]]
- **History → Miranda:** animations were previously applied in **Rage Studio** (or earlier in "Realtor"); now they are assembled in **Miranda**. **Fortune 4** / Fortune Foo Paws (transcribed "Fortune Food Pause") is the **first game** having its main scene assembled in Miranda (they had done it before for the online games, but Miranda's process is now improved). Running the game live in the browser depends on **Dima and Andrey** finishing the integration of the math/mass server into the game so it runs proper gameplay inside **Miranda 9** without programming. [[raw/Game Development How animation are made with Lena - March 12-2025.md]]

## Iteration example

For the **Queen game** recognition feature, Lena showed Michael one scenario, he asked to try something else, she did, he liked it, then had second thoughts (it looked like "a whip") and asked her to go back to the earlier **silver-horseshoe** version — an iterative creative loop that is why she prefers to storyboard quickly in Photoshop before involving real game animators. ## Porting a game's assets to Omni — the Calle Ocho method (Apr 17 2025)

Moving the released **Calle Ocho** game's assets onto Omni forced a choice between three approaches, worked through by [[wiki/people/lena.md|Lena]], [[wiki/people/dmitriy-khandov.md|Dima]], [[wiki/people/alexander-biratka.md|Sasha]] and Val ([[wiki/meetings/2025-04-17-calle-ocho-asset-redesign-omni.md]]): [[raw/graphical_assets_redesign_omni_calle_ocho-apr-17-2025.md]]

- **Plan A (Lena's, to hit a May-1 date):** take the videos made for Calle Ocho in **Rage Studio** and just re-export them through **Spine** and **Miranda**, only separating the particles that need **additive** blending (Omni can't do alpha + additive in one video today). Lena's own objection: this reuses nothing for the *next* game. [[raw/graphical_assets_redesign_omni_calle_ocho-apr-17-2025.md]]
- **Plan B (Sasha's):** rebuild assets "properly" so the control code is reusable in future games — but then no firm time estimate, and Sasha argued ~2 months (Val: 2 months is "a non-starter"). [[raw/graphical_assets_redesign_omni_calle_ocho-apr-17-2025.md]]
- **The middle approach (Dima's, adopted):** port as-is the many things that can be (e.g. symbols exported into **Spine containers** with minimal designer effort, without redrawing the internal animation), and **properly rework only what recurs across future games** — the "pigwin"/penguin effect "we'll use in thousands of games," a **flexible BigWin** (swap diamonds↔Chinese coins by scheme, reused straight into Fortune Fu Paws / "HotShenFuPo"), and a shared **Dashboard**. Val's framing: throwing away some Calle Ocho-only work is a small sacrifice if it launches the engine. [[raw/graphical_assets_redesign_omni_calle_ocho-apr-17-2025.md]]
- **"Spikes" (unsolved reworks) identified:** a **soft mask** for the negative transition (Omni's mask is a hard geometry mask; the fix is a two-layer video where the second layer is the soft mask); **video-in-button** complex buttons for the **Pick Your Fortune** mini-game (buttons that change skin per state and embed video — never built before); and **sorting** (per-symbol individual sort order, "very important… needed in all games," ~90% on Sasha). [[raw/graphical_assets_redesign_omni_calle_ocho-apr-17-2025.md]]
- **Concrete asset rulings for Calle Ocho:** the new **Dashboard** pushes the reels up (Lena to shrink the rooster + logo slightly, remove the "Lions," reposition — **not resize** — the reel matrix); **Royals** redone once in **Spine** as recolourable **skins** (one font, per-game colour sets); **anticipation** and **precognition** kept as video (rework only if performance drops); the **V-Deck** gets the new last-three-buttons flame feature (as on Fortune Fu Paws / Diamond Link Georgia). [[wiki/people/sergey-tsymanov.md|Sergey]] to rebuild the FireEyes progressive Sign for Omni; **Vitalik** cuts resources; **Dima** owns templates + the hardest fixes while others parallelize. [[raw/graphical_assets_redesign_omni_calle_ocho-apr-17-2025.md]]

## Production-time estimation on the Miro board (Apr 18 2025)

Val and Lena walked the **game-production task list in Miro** item by item to estimate **production time per item**, setting creative reiterations aside (Lena's rule of thumb: ~2.5 reiterations on average, not every task) ([[wiki/meetings/2025-04-18-games-production-time-estimation.md]]). [[raw/games_production-apr-18-2025.md]]

- **Unit convention:** a "day" = **8 hours**; e.g. "5 days" = **40 hours** of actual labor time (not thinking time). [[raw/games_production-apr-18-2025.md]]
- **Structure separated for estimating:** the game part vs the **Fiery Ice Link** progressive part. Progressive transitions/bonus are **common to all Fiery-Ice games** and copy-paste between games (the Diamond Link / Jackpot Rush lineage — "developers have the technology to put code inside another game"), so they're pulled out of per-game work; **game math differs per game** and Lena only cares about which symbols are special (visual hierarchy), not the math detail. **Kaio-Ocho** has fewer tasks than **Dragons of Rain** because its bonus/progressive-sign were already done in Dragons. [[raw/games_production-apr-18-2025.md]]
- **The production stages (Lena's map):** CompArt for main scene + free spins → per-symbol **"detailing"** (each symbol's parameters: oversized?, animation length, animation description, anticipation animation) → per-symbol production. **Precog** = an artistic-approach illustration Lena shows Michael for approval; **Anton** draws the objects (illustration, since Anton doesn't do 3D), then **Nastya/Natasha** animate (AI used to combine/cut illustration pieces, then hand-animated). [[raw/games_production-apr-18-2025.md]]
- **Critical resources:** Lena priced only the **critical, non-substitutable** resources (**Dima, Nastya, Yulia**) who run **fully in parallel** — Dima's V-Deck project is completely self-contained (depends on no one). **Anton** shares his time across games (Jet Patrasche / George), so she hadn't priced his tasks and would fill them in the next day with him. Val pushed to estimate **time-per-person first**, then lay out a parallel schedule to expose the resource bottleneck (the "second Dima" thought experiment). [[raw/games_production-apr-18-2025.md]]

## Nov 11 2025 — a graphics specification for automated testing (the Miranda scene manifest)

On a Val ↔ [[wiki/people/pavel.md|Pavel Frolov]] ↔ [[wiki/people/irina-shishko.md|Irina Shishko]] working call ([[wiki/meetings/2025-11-11-graphics-specification-for-testing.md]], 37m, Russian), the group worked out **what layout information to get from the art team ([[wiki/people/lena.md|Lena]]) and in what form** so QA can test a game's on-screen graphics automatically. [[raw/Specs_graphics.md]]

- **The problem Val named.** When JVL asks Lena for "the information," she happily agrees but delivers it in *her* preferred form (gigabytes of files) — and JVL then finds it is not what they can work with. The risk is not Lena's willingness but that **neither side has agreed the taxonomy/form of the specification** JVL actually needs. [[raw/Specs_graphics.md]]
- **What QA needs per component:** for each dashboard/game element (e.g. the four dashboard **meters** like *user balance*, the frame/ramka, each symbol) — its **coordinates, size, label**, plus a **reference ("эталон") screenshot**. QA's test then locates the component at those coordinates, screenshots it, and **pixel-compares** to the reference; a 1-pixel shift is tolerated, ~3 pixels fails. A cheaper first check they already run: verify **meter labels change correctly on language switch**. [[wiki/people/pavel.md|Pavel]] pushed back that testing *line thickness* pixel-by-pixel is "shooting sparrows with a cannon" — the useful target is component **position/label**, not every pixel. [[raw/Specs_graphics.md]]
- **The source of the spec = a Miranda scene export.** [[wiki/people/pavel.md|Pavel]]: when **[[wiki/people/dmitriy-khandov.md|Dima Khandov]] is back from vacation** he will extract from the **Miranda** project the full **scene hierarchy** — every component's coordinates, size, nesting (parent/child) — and generate it in a usable form. In a finished game QA can then **hide all components except one** (frame, then a symbol, etc.), screenshot, and compare against that deterministic reference. Caveat Pavel raised: the live game overlays effects/transitions so no full-frame ever matches the "ideal" comp art; only **isolated, static components** at a fixed instant are comparable. A second risk: Dima's export can itself carry an error (a wrongly-built scene), so **each manifest must be verified once against a known-correct game before it becomes the reference** for future regression. [[raw/Specs_graphics.md]]
- **Manifests as the per-game reference.** Irina relayed (attributing it to a "**Sasha**" — likely [[wiki/people/alexander-biratka.md|Alexander Biratka]] by templating context, but left by-context) that the team had started building **scene manifests** which, once written, become the **reference for all subsequent games** — describe where each element sits once, and artists follow the manifest thereafter. [[raw/Specs_graphics.md]]
- **Tools (as transcribed).** Lena draws comp art from reference images ("compart"), works in **Photoshop** (layers of pixels) and a **3D application** (transcribed "3 дима акции" — name unclear, not asserted) whose output file is "always repeatable/explainable" and re-importable into **Adobe**. Val speculated about giving an AI the comp image + the structured scene and asking it to generate elements or re-theme them — explicitly a "wild idea," not a plan. [[raw/Specs_graphics.md]]

Val's larger why — generating/cloning games from this specification — is on [[wiki/topics/game-production-throughput-strategy.md]] (Nov-11 section); he told Pavel and Irina to keep that ambition to themselves and not raise it with Lena's team.

## Apr 2 2026 — deliverable vocabulary settled (master file / storyboard / comp art) + Val's pre-build-shell vision

On the [[wiki/meetings/2026-04-02-game-development-review.md|Apr-2 game-development review]] ([[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/michael-gottlieb.md|Michael Gottlieb]], [[wiki/people/lena.md|Lena Zhukova]], [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]], [[wiki/people/efim-podvoiski.md|Efim]]) Val spent much of the meeting nailing down exactly what the design stage delivers and how it flows downstream, arriving at a shared vocabulary (his "shopping bag," Efim's "collage"). [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Game_Development-1-April-2-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Game_Dev_Pipeline_Review-April-2-2026.md]]

- **Master file** (the "shopping bag"): a large Photoshop (PSD) file containing all composition-art assets layered together — the central deliverable showing the whole screen (symbols, background, dashboard, logo, pay table, reel frame, effects). A game may have several master files by complexity (main screen, free spins, bonus). All downstream artists reference it for proportion, positioning, and context. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Game_Development-1-April-2-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Game_Dev_Pipeline_Review-April-2-2026.md]]
- **Storyboard**: a visual description of transitions, messages, and animation sequences (game flow, e.g. base → free spins). Always required — even for an update you copy-paste and modify a prior release's storyboard, but the full storyboard must exist; there can be several for multiple bonuses. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Game_Development-1-April-2-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Game_Dev_Pipeline_Review-April-2-2026.md]]
- **Comp art** (composition art): specifically the rolled-up cumulative image of *all* assets, not any single element; individual items in it are "symbols," "assets," or "elements." The pipeline flows concept (initial comp art) → design (animation references, transitions, storyboards, additional comp art) → production (final assets). Downstream, game artists Julia (layouts/templates) and Anton (illustrations) extract individual layers from the master file but need the whole file for context. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Game_Development-1-April-2-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Game_Dev_Pipeline_Review-April-2-2026.md]]
- **Val's efficiency vision (Michael endorsed).** Pre-build the game shell before final art is ready so comp art can be dropped in and previewed running within minutes, and (aspirationally) **automate animation generation** from a description (e.g. "30 frames, closed to open mouth"). The team noted this needs the **Omni 2 architecture** — it cannot be done within the current Rage Studio framework; Efim tied it to the math-playtest automation direction. This extends the Nov-11-2025 "generate games from a specification" endgame on [[wiki/topics/game-production-throughput-strategy.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Game_Development-1-April-2-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Game_Dev_Pipeline_Review-April-2-2026.md]]

## Sept 14 2026 — what is baked into a background video, and why horizontal art will not become vertical art

The [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-14-texas-vertical-conversion-and-georgia-game-base.md|Sept-14 6:38 AM ET session]] (Russian) is the clearest statement in the corpus of what a JVL game background actually *is*, and of the limits that puts on reusing art across cabinet orientations. [[wiki/people/igor-metelski.md|Igor Metelski]] was demonstrating attempts to turn horizontal Texas games into vertical ones; the product ruling that came out of it is on [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-14-vertical-texas-starts-from-the-georgia-coam-vertical-games.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Texas_Vertical_Conversion_and_Georgia_Game_Base-September-14-2026.md]]

- **The geometry.** A horizontal machine's main scene is **two Full HD monitors** (1920 × 1080 each); a vertical machine is **one Full HD monitor turned to portrait**. Stacking the two horizontal scenes — for a base game, the main reels scene over the pay-table scene — without distorting anything leaves a large empty block: by Igor's worked figure, roughly **another 1253 pixels** are needed above the two 1080-high areas for the composition to carry the portrait monitor's aspect ratio (he first estimated the gap at "1200 пикселей там где-то так"). Stretching each monitor to half the vertical screen instead distorts everything by about **57%** in height — "вся сцена и каждый символ они примерно на 57% выше". [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Texas_Vertical_Conversion_and_Georgia_Game_Base-September-14-2026.md]]
- **The scene conversion is not the problem; the art is.** Building the converted scene "вполне себе возможен[о] и не очень сложн[о]" — find the videos, download and join them — and animations run on the result. What fails is composition and art: on a street/canal-themed 3×3 game the two halves "на точке стык… плавно не свозится" (do not blend at the join), and "с существующим ар[то]м эта задача не решим[а]". Igor did not think AI closes the gap either, short of generating new art, which he called doubtful. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Texas_Vertical_Conversion_and_Georgia_Game_Base-September-14-2026.md]]
- **Replacing a background means replacing the whole scene.** The durable production fact: **the reel frame and the logo are baked into the background video** — "рамка [р]елов она тоже запечена в фон. Вот [и] лого запечено [в] фон." A developer can freely place the reels and style the symbols, but cannot produce the background video; that needs a person who can assemble it, after which Igor can fit it to the element positions with Claude. **No such person was named.** [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Texas_Vertical_Conversion_and_Georgia_Game_Base-September-14-2026.md]]
- **Progressive frames are baked in too — and the designers hold the un-baked source.** Every Georgia COAM game carries a symbol-driven progressive whose symbol frames were baked into the background video. The fix Igor gave: designers still hold the un-baked scene (rendered "автосэмакс[е]" — 3ds Max by context, an inference), so they can switch off the visibility of those objects and re-bake a clean video — "запечь на новое видео, где их не будет… вполне себе рабочий вариант." [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Texas_Vertical_Conversion_and_Georgia_Game_Base-September-14-2026.md]]
- **Re-skinning an existing game is the normal path, and cheaper than building from scratch.** [[wiki/people/irina-shishko.md|Irina Shishko]]: "переделать фон или… одну игру доработать, это всё-таки меньше, чем с нуля сделать там все игры." Igor confirmed the team does this routinely — take existing games with old art and put more modern art on them (the passage is garbled; read as an inference) — noting the development side usually adds no new bonuses in such a pass. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Texas_Vertical_Conversion_and_Georgia_Game_Base-September-14-2026.md]]
- **One layout does not give you the other, even on NOVA.** NOVA was designed so a single game could be shown vertical or horizontal through configured layouts, but each layout was always to have **its own background video** — "для вертикальной и для горизонтальной раскладки это были разные видео". [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Texas_Vertical_Conversion_and_Georgia_Game_Base-September-14-2026.md]]

The three games used as test material were **Spicy Fruits**, **Midnight Money** and **Dutch Mill** (a 3×3 title), all rendered in garbled form in the transcript and identified from the existing lineup; the Georgia COAM game whose background video Igor opened is rendered "куриного флюксора" and is **not recoverable** — it is left unidentified. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Texas_Vertical_Conversion_and_Georgia_Game_Base-September-14-2026.md]] [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Texas_Vertical_Conversion_and_Georgia_Game_Base-September-14-2026.md]]

## Sept 17 2026 — what a scene actually is on disk, and the first generated background video inside a running game

Two `Game_Conversion` sessions the same day — [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-17-game-conversion-vertical-scene-progress.md|a four-minute stand-up at 10:14 AM ET]] and [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-17-vertical-game-skeletons-and-the-art-pipeline.md|a 24-minute working session at 12:19 PM ET]], both in Russian — add the missing structural layer under the Sept-14 material above: not what a background video contains, but **how a game's art is stored and addressed**. The direction Val set out of the second session is on [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-17-hard-geometry-from-the-scene-files-and-a-vertical-skeleton-per-game-format.md]].

**A scene is a folder in the game directory.** [[wiki/people/igor-metelski.md|Igor Metelski]] walked Val through it on screen: "**у меня в директории игры … вот эти вот папки. По большому счету, в каждой из этих папок лежит … то, что я называю сценой, и все эти сцены они выгружаются в игру. [А] игра уже занимается менеджментом, когда какая сцена отображается.**" The main scene is one of them and it is where the background video sits — "**мейн — это отдельная сцена, в мейне есть фоновое видео**". Val restated it back ("**в каждой папке … папка — это сцена**", each holding a set of artifacts the scene is assembled and transformed from) and Igor confirmed it holds for **every game JVL has ever made** — Val: "**Под каждую игру, которую мы вообще делали когда-то**", Igor: "**Да, да, да, всё верно.**" [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Vertical_Game_Skeletons_and_Art_Pipeline-September-17-2026.md]]

**Where the hard geometry lives, and how it is read.** The geometry of a scene is written in a file inside the scene ("**в сцене написано в файле**"), and JVL's tooling already carries **parsers that read existing geometry out of it** — "**у нас в рамках инструментов написаны парсеры, которые вот эту самую существующую [геометрию] разбирают**". This is the enabling fact behind Val's whole demand that the artists' intent be replaced by numbers. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Vertical_Game_Skeletons_and_Art_Pipeline-September-17-2026.md]]

**Which scenes need a vertical version.** Igor's list: the main game scenes, free spins, transitions, messages, and any separate bonus that a game has — his example being the plate in **Dutch Mill** ("**бонусы, если есть какие-то отдельные бонусы, как, например, в этом [Dutch Mill] … есть этот плейт**"). *(The derived notes render this as a "touch-wheel plate"; the transcript's "тачмиле / точмиле" is the same 3×3 title the Sept-14 source renders "дачмил" — **Dutch Mill**, not a wheel. The notes' reading is not followed* [[raw/Meeting_Notes_Vertical_Game_Skeletons_and_Art_Pipeline-September-17-2026.md]]*.)* [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Vertical_Game_Skeletons_and_Art_Pipeline-September-17-2026.md]]

**The demo: a generated video became a playing game.** Igor took an existing game and put the video [[wiki/people/anton-leshkevich.md|Anton Leshkevich]] had generated the previous day into it as the scene; symbol positions are computed from the video's proportions so each symbol lands in its own cell, and the game plays. Anton's video was itself produced in part by parsing an existing **vertical 3×3** game JVL already owns — "**оно сгенерировано в том числе на основании того, что мы разобрали какую-то вертикальную 3 на 3 игру, которые у нас уже есть**". Some vertical resources existed before this: Igor named the **game-selection screen** as one already available for vertical machines. He worked in the existing branch rather than cutting a new one, and said the changes can be moved to a fresh branch later if the product goes ahead. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Vertical_Game_Skeletons_and_Art_Pipeline-September-17-2026.md]]

**The pay table was cut by hand, around the mechanism.** For the demo the vertical pay-table screen was made out of the **horizontal** pay table and deliberately bypassed the mechanism the team had built earlier — "**он сделан … в обход того механизма, который мы уже ранее разработали**" — because the horizontal source was a whole scene with its own background and did not fit; it had to be sliced and recomposed ("**всё это пришлось как-то нарезать, скомпоновать**"). It displays in the correct position. What Igor said would have made it clean is the thing the Sept-14 material also identified as missing: a **comp art** to aim at — "**если бы у нас был какой-то [компарт], на который можно было ориентироваться**". [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Vertical_Game_Skeletons_and_Art_Pipeline-September-17-2026.md]]

**Comp art *is* the skeleton.** [[wiki/people/pavel.md|Pavel Frolov]], answering Anton's grey-squares template idea: "**компарт — это по сути то и есть вот этот вот скелет, да, с положением чего-то**" — which ties Val's new skeleton directly to the comp-art vocabulary settled on Apr 2 2026 above. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Vertical_Game_Skeletons_and_Art_Pipeline-September-17-2026.md]]

**The squashed picture was not a defect.** Val asked why everything looked flattened; Igor said it was his monitor layout or the screen share, not the scene — "**это … не артефакты самой сцены, это артефакты того, что я ввожу это … на [W]indows машине**". Pavel had him squeeze the shared window until the sides were roughly **9:16**, after which the proportions read correctly. No work is being spent on it. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Vertical_Game_Skeletons_and_Art_Pipeline-September-17-2026.md]]

**From the 10:14 AM stand-up — the state of the same scene a couple of hours earlier** [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Game_Conversion_Vertical_Scene_Progress-September-17-2026.md]]:

- **The stretched symbols are a placeholder.** The symbols in the vertical cells come from a horizontal layout and had to be pulled a little to fit — "**символов с горизонтальной раскладки, поэтому их пришлось чуть-чуть растянуть**" — and Igor said this stops mattering once the real vertical symbols exist. No work is spent on them.
- **The pay table on its own camera squashes the main scene.** "**вот вот этот мейн он так вот сплющен, потому что здесь есть вот этот вот [pay table]**" — a separate camera for the pay table compresses the main view. Igor was generating a scene that puts the pay table on the **upper part of the screen** instead, after which "**не будет вот этого вот искажения … оно будет выглядеть как … игра**". He put it at half an hour to an hour of work.
- **Launching in vertical mode was itself not the problem.** What broke were small things: **font names that do not match in some scenes**, and **elements missing from the dashboard**.
- **The steal / nudge scene may only need a camera change**, not a rebuild — "**даже не переделывать. Его можно … просто там камеру … изменить … он по масштабу как раз [низ] перекроет**". *(The scene name is garbled — "стилдейм" / "стил день" / "ненаджев стиль" — and whether this is one scene or two is not recoverable.)*
- **Deliberate ugliness is a requirement, not a defect.** [[wiki/people/irina-shishko.md|Irina Shishko]] interrupted to remind the call that the **additional games inside the Texas product were meant to be unattractive on purpose**: "**чем страшнее, тем лучше. Эти игры, которые дополнительные … у Техаса … они должны быть не привлекательные**", so that players would not want to play them. Igor accepted it ("**ну хорошо**"). Art effort on those games is therefore not a quality target.

## Sept 20 2026 — the vertical geometry standard is written, and liquid glass moves out of the video

At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-09-20-vertical-geometry-standard-and-adr-consolidation.md|Sunday Sept-20 7:04 AM ET session]] (Russian), [[wiki/people/igor-metelski.md|Igor Metelski]] showed the first written standard for the geometry of a vertical screen. Val's ruling on where it lives is on [[wiki/decisions/2026-09-20-the-vertical-geometry-standard-glossary-and-adrs-go-to-github-outside-any-framework.md]]. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Vertical_Geometry_Standard_and_ADR_Consolidation-September-20-2026.md]]

**The base and the count.** Igor used the product rendered "г к м 3" (read as Georgia COAM 3, an inference) because its art is the newest. By Claude's review, **66 scenes** must be made in vertical format, plus **60 symbol scenes** if the symbols are redone. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Vertical_Geometry_Standard_and_ADR_Consolidation-September-20-2026.md]]

**The zones of a vertical screen.** On a main screen Igor split the picture into: a large **background video**, "**1920 на 1080 вертикальное**"; the **frame for the reels**; the **logo**; one bright **accent element** on a fairly neutral background — "**как, например, в случае игры [Crescent City Carnival]? Вот эта вот девушка**"; a **frame for progressives** where the game has them; and a separate **dashboard** area. Each element has a written position and size, set out "**словесное [о]писание, табличное описание**" and in drawings. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Vertical_Geometry_Standard_and_ADR_Consolidation-September-20-2026.md]]

- **Layouts shown:** a **3×5** game (Igor first called it 3×4; Val and Pavel pointed out it has five reels, and Igor corrected himself) and a **3×3** game.
- **Screens covered:** main screens of the base game and of free spins, messages, transitions, precognition, anticipation, pay table and help.

**Bonuses had nothing to copy.** In the other product (rendered "над" / "н а т", unresolved) the bonuses have no match in "г к м 3" — the **Dutch Mill** plate-picking bonus, a **Clover Fortune** bonus, and a bonus rendered "джембриллианте" (title not verified). Their originals are horizontal, over two screens. Igor asked Claude to propose vertical layouts from them. The documents describe the bonuses **without naming the games** — "**в документах обезличены**" — so the standard is not tied to one title. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Vertical_Geometry_Standard_and_ADR_Consolidation-September-20-2026.md]]

**Transition timing.** A transition starts as a clear layer over the base game; the base screen dissolves; the transition art shows; then that art dissolves into the bonus screen, so the move is seamless. Igor also wrote where alpha must rise. **Pavel** wanted the base-screen and bonus-screen curves drawn under the alpha curve. **Igor agreed the chart is wrong**: "**это иишка как бы [генерило] это все надо перепроверять ещё руками**"; an earlier draft had drawn it more clearly. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Vertical_Geometry_Standard_and_ADR_Consolidation-September-20-2026.md]]

**Liquid glass, drawn by code.** Today the semi-transparent frames — dashboard, progressive frames, the backing under symbols — are baked into the background video by the designers. Igor built the same effect as a **post-process shader** so the framework draws it over a clean video. Over a moving background (the swaying woman in the game) his frame "**корректным образом преломляет картинку**". Claude wrote the shader and tested it "**через VGL**", not in RAGE. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Vertical_Geometry_Standard_and_ADR_Consolidation-September-20-2026.md]]

- **Why:** a clean video means a new style later needs no new video. [[wiki/people/pavel.md|Pavel]]: "**это правильный подход, потому что очень легко поменять игру с трёх прогрессив левел на 2**".
- **[[wiki/people/irina-shishko.md|Irina]] had not known the two were fused:** "**Я даже не думала, что они вместе соединены.**"
- **The old objection.** Pavel recalled that when this came up before, "**Елена говорила, ой, там перепрограммировать, это очень все сложно. Мы не уверены, хватит ли нам перформанса**" — read as [[wiki/people/lena.md|Lena]] by context. Igor's answer: performance-manager statistics show the GPU is not heavily loaded, and the effect is a shader, so it should not hurt much.
- **The limit.** The effect is baked into the **material of the background video**. An element from another scene placed in that area does not get it. Engine work to lift this was started but not finished — a "**place hole[der]**" sits where working code should be.

[[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Vertical_Geometry_Standard_and_ADR_Consolidation-September-20-2026.md]]

**Video rules.** Each video is its own decoder, so a running game should use as few videos as possible, with elements baked together. But before baking, the pieces should stay separate, so a revised standard (say, the logo moves) means only a new bake. The **alpha channel is optional**: where nothing happens it is left out, to save a Full-HD decoder. Igor also noted the layered approach is not new — designers' tools are layered too; only the output arrives flat. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Vertical_Geometry_Standard_and_ADR_Consolidation-September-20-2026.md]]

**Re-cutting the Dutch Mill background.** Val thinks Claude Code can move the horizon point of a flat horizontal picture into a vertical one. Igor said maybe, but with the existing art he cannot split it; for a new game the art could be built for both layouts from the start. He offered to try it as cheap background work. Pavel suggested using only the **raw houses video without frames** — "**лучше иметь голое сырое видео только домиков**" — since the frames would pollute any AI description. Igor noted Claude has no video or image generator of its own; third-party services are needed, and [[wiki/people/anton-leshkevich.md|Anton]] (by context, Anton Leshkevich) had found a good method: break the animation down frame by frame and write a prompt for such a service from it. [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Vertical_Geometry_Standard_and_ADR_Consolidation-September-20-2026.md]]

## Oct 8 2026 (10:02 AM) — a 20% stretch is accepted, and static toppers become looping AI videos

## Oct 18 2026 — a vertical Dutch Mill in a week: master frame, ChatGPT and Gemini, then Claude; AI video loops badly, programmatic animation loops cleanly

At the [[wiki/meetings/2026-10-18-dutch-mill-rage-to-nova-conversion-and-reskin-plan.md|Oct-18 8:04 AM Dutch Mill session]] (Russian) [[wiki/people/igor-metelski.md|Igor]] showed the vertical Dutch Mill [[raw/Meeting_Transcript_Dutch_Mill_Rage_to_NOVA_Conversion_and_Reskin_Plan-October-18-2026.md]]:

- **Steps.** A master frame assembled from the existing horizontal resources; ChatGPT and Gemini to bring it to an acceptable level; then Claude to animate. Older games' art is more primitive than Dragon Pays', so more work was needed. 4 symbols were redrawn to match the new background and animated; the help screen is new; the whole job took a week.
- **Looping.** All these are idle animations that play in a loop, so there must be no visible jump at the seam. AI video tools loop poorly; the "first frame and last frame" option does not always work. The girl is a video, and a small part of her loop had to be finished with Claude. The mill blades and the sky were made programmatically with Claude, and "когда я делаю чтото программное, я… могу корректно… зациклить". Pavel added that the motion must also carry through the joining frame, not just match first and last frames. The sky had to be slowed — fast, it made viewers dizzy.
- **Next.** Igor expects [[wiki/people/sergey-kleimenychev.md|Sergey Kleimenychev]] to find the right mix of prompts and references with more tries; Sergey's AI access was set up that day and he is sending new bonus videos.
- **A re-skin from a theme.** Val wants the art for a re-skinned Dutch Mill made from scratch, from a theme abstract by [[wiki/people/michael-gottlieb.md|Michael]] on Igor's spec of scenes and placeholders — [[wiki/decisions/2026-10-18-rage-is-closed-dutch-mill-reaches-nova-by-wednesday-and-a-reskin-from-a-new-theme-tests-the-tools.md]].
