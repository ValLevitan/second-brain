# Slot game-design principles and the progressive-bet-incentive debate

Durable design principles and one live, unresolved product debate from JVL's game-design reviews. Synthesised from the Feb 21 2025 Fortune Foo Paws review with [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/lena.md|Lena]], [[wiki/people/efim-podvoiski.md|Efim]] (transcribed "Athim"/"Athene"/"Athena") and [[wiki/people/michael-gottlieb.md|Michael]]. Game-by-game details live in [[wiki/topics/jvl-machine-lineup.md]]; this page holds the cross-game design thinking. [[raw/game_design_meeting_february_21_2025.md]]

## The progressive-bet-incentive debate (RESOLVED Feb 28 — do it on the V-Deck, not the math)

**Update (Feb 28, with Juan present):** the debate landed on Val's/Juan's side for the *presentation*, not the math. The group agreed to use the **"jackpot rush" flame approach** on bet buttons **3, 4 and 5** — a flame that grows with the bet (small on the 180 button, medium on 300, large on 600; **nothing** on the 60/120 buttons) plus a **pronounced/unique sound** when buttons 3/4/5 are pressed — to nudge players to bet up **without** any textual claim. Michael, who had argued the opposite on Feb 21, proposed this specific mechanism himself, while restating his "bankroll philosophy" caution (many players stay at min-bet no matter what, and over-glamorising high bets risks making the min-bet experience feel "less than"). The math is **not** changed for this. Alongside it, JVL will also A/B a **second math pack** with higher (Chinese) bet levels — see [[wiki/decisions/2025-02-28-fortune-foo-paws-second-math-pack.md]]. [[raw/Game design part 1 - Feb-28-2025.md]] [[raw/Game design part 2 - Feb-28-2025.md]]

The central argument of the Feb 21 call, which this resolves:

- **How JVL's progressive works today (Efim):** the displayed progressive value is the **same at every bet level**; what changes with bet size is the **probability** of winning it (probability is proportional to the bet). So a min-bet player is eligible for the same $25,000 major, just far less likely to hit it. There are two ways to build it — scale the progressive *value* to the bet, or scale the *probability* to the bet — and JVL (like the competitor "Cointreau") uses the probability method. [[raw/game_design_meeting_february_21_2025.md]]
- **Val's objection:** a player **cannot read the math** — the only thing a player perceives is the digits on the meter, which don't change with bet. Passing on [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]]'s point that a player needs a *visible* incentive to bet more, Val argued JVL may be "making the wrong games" if there is no perceptible reason to bet up. He stressed it was an opinion, not an order, and asked to **park it and revisit with Juan**. [[raw/game_design_meeting_february_21_2025.md]]
- **Why the industry does it this way (Michael):** scaling the value to the bet would drop the min-bet jackpot to a non-"life-changing" number (e.g. $5,000), discouraging the many min-bet players; hanging the big $25,000 as reachable "at any bet" (à la IGT Mega Millions/Mega Bucks) gives every player something to chase even though the odds are tiny. Michael conceded it is "kind of deceptive" but is the direction the industry has gone. [[raw/game_design_meeting_february_21_2025.md]]
- **Val's alternative floated and rejected:** award **no progressive at minimum bet** (only at higher bets). Michael/Efim pushed back hard — it would **cripple the game**, because the top-10 performing games all offer **full progressive eligibility at any bet**; only the "quick-hit tower" genre requires max bet, and that is its own category with its own player base. A player would skip a JVL game that isn't eligible at 60 credits when every competitor is. [[raw/game_design_meeting_february_21_2025.md]]
- **Where it landed:** use the **V-Deck (live secondary screen)** and the bottom promo text as the surface to *sell* betting up — sparklier/hotter high-bet buttons, a slightly larger progressive meter, "bet more for a better chance at progressives" copy — rather than changing the math. Val wants Lena to present a **visual proposal on Friday** and the incentive question revisited **with Juan on Tuesday**. [[raw/game_design_meeting_february_21_2025.md]]

## Durable design principles

- **Signage vs game-screen detail:** the ornamental frame is kept on the **game screen** but removed from the **signage/progressive sign**. At 10–50 ft, granular detail "becomes noise" and distracts from what matters on signage — the numbers. Signage exists to be read at distance. [[raw/game_design_meeting_february_21_2025.md]]
- **Help-screen icon versions:** when a symbol is shrunk for the help screens it can lose the detail that distinguishes it (e.g. the subtle red outline that makes a **"golden red" Caban coin** different from a plain **gold** Caban coin). The fix is to make a dedicated **iconographized version** of the symbol for help screens / sticker lines so the player, Ivan, and GLI can tell symbols apart. Help screens should fit on as few pages as possible (competitors' norm; players don't read them) rather than being broken into 25 tiny-font pages. [[raw/game_design_meeting_february_21_2025.md]]
- **Perceived persistence ("fat cat"):** cats grow and shrink on-screen based on fake/real hits; the player chases whichever cat is "fattest / closest to triggering" (a horse-race feel), not a specific colour. Gold/white/blue Caban coins and cats have close-but-not-identical EVs; gold is centred and slightly larger only because gold *connotes* wealth, not because it pays more. In a bank of machines each machine can show a different cat as the largest. [[raw/game_design_meeting_february_21_2025.md]]
- **Topper modes:** "idle mode" shows only meters; "attraction mode" cycles character animation / fireworks / logo. A four-machine "cross" configuration has a different flow than a row. Toppers sit physically higher than players, so character eye-lines are set accordingly. [[raw/game_design_meeting_february_21_2025.md]]
- **Bet levels for Fortune Foo Paws** were set to **60 / 120 / 180 / 300 / 600 credits** (1-2-3-5-10), with Val correcting a placeholder of 240/500 to 300/600. [[raw/game_design_meeting_february_21_2025.md]]
- **Design against the market, not the field:** Val's rule — "only games that make money are the ones we look at… we don't waste time on games that don't make money"; the competitive reference here is **Cointreau** (Juan asked for a game close to it). [[raw/game_design_meeting_february_21_2025.md]]

## Further design principles (Feb 28)

- **Attract mode / no-credits vs credits state:** the **FireEyes Wink** build introduced full-screen demo videos of all games in attract mode, which behave **differently with credits on the machine versus zero credits**. Design goal: the attract video **shows off** the visually interesting free-spins mechanic to look winnable, but deliberately **doesn't give away** the deeper "fiery ice" bonus — a tease/"come-on," not a full reveal. [[raw/Game design part 1 - Feb-28-2025.md]]
- **Dimming the non-choice reels (subtle):** when a game dims reels to steer the player to a choice (the sparkly-arrow reel), the dimming must be **very subtle** — the player must still clearly read the symbols on the dimmed reels. This is *not* the heavy overlay some competitors use; it just draws the eye. (Explicitly the opposite intensity from the progressive gray-out on HG-6, which was minimised.) [[raw/Game design part 1 - Feb-28-2025.md]]
- **Denomination buttons must always have one selected:** the selected denom is shown brighter **plus an outline** (brightness alone isn't obviously "selected"); the machine must **default to one selection** and never sit in a state with no denom selected (e.g. 1 cent highlighted at zero credits). Idle/zero-credit state shows an **"insert credits"** message. [[raw/Game design part 1 - Feb-28-2025.md]]
- **Progressive meter layout:** show the two current progressive levels (the majors) on top in place of the old white/blue section; on the V-Deck, keep the two progressive values the **same size** for clean symmetry (Val's push to enlarge/reposition "mega" was dropped). [[raw/Game design part 1 - Feb-28-2025.md]]
- **Minimum-bet direction:** current Fortune Foo Paws min bet is **60 credits**; Michael recommends raising the minimum to at least **70**, noting 70 and 88 credits are "the new norms" among premium competitor boxes. Val prefers to keep the lower minimum on this cabinet and **reserve higher bets for the next cabinet** and the data learned from this game. Framing: in South Dakota/Georgia/Puerto Rico JVL is "a Porsche, not a Toyota"; Florida is a **practice ground** where JVL won't take real floor money until ~mid-2026. [[raw/Game design part 1 - Feb-28-2025.md]]
- **"Maneki-neko"/"Manikineka Cats"** is a Florida-specific product with different math from the South Dakota/Georgia versions. [[raw/Game design part 1 - Feb-28-2025.md]]
- **Taxonomy discipline:** Val asked everyone to use consistent, proper taxonomy in meeting invites/descriptions (Michael links conversations and documentation to specific meetings), and asked Michael to state each meeting's objective in the invite so reviews aren't open-ended standbys. [[raw/Game design part 2 - Feb-28-2025.md]]

## Bet-up flame buttons — visual spec (Mar 7)

- The "hot / hotter / hottest" flame treatment on the three higher bet buttons was made concrete: **flames on the bottom** of the 180 button, **top and bottom** on the 300 button, and **all around** the 600 button; the **60 and 120** buttons carry no flame. The **selected** bet button is shown **white**. When credits are too low to afford the higher bets, those buttons show in a **deactivated (grayed) state** rather than disappearing. At zero credits the message must read **"insert money."** [[raw/game_design_meeting_march_7_2025.md]]
- **Sound effect** on buttons 3/4/5 only, and **only the first time** the player steps up to a higher level (a swoosh/ignition, not a repeated bell/gong) — the cue signals the higher level without "banging the player over the head." Michael cautioned that a congratulatory message on the highest bet ("you're on fire") could risk a **GLI** violation and over-set player expectations; keep celebratory messaging to **outcomes** (big win / mega win). [[raw/game_design_meeting_march_7_2025.md]]

## Per-denomination RTP and how to signal it (Mar 7)

- JVL **can** set a different RTP **per denomination** (not per bet). Michael's standard configuration (shown on screen, used for **Jackpot Rush** and **Diamond Link**): **1¢ and 2¢ at 89%, 5¢ and 10¢ at 90%, 50¢ and $1 at 92%** — averaging **90.3%** for the box, excluding progressive contributions. He always rewards the higher-denomination player; this is **not done in South America** (Colombia confirmed, likely Peru). [[raw/game_design_meeting_march_7_2025.md]]
- **Do not expose the actual RTP** to players or attendants (operators like Hialeah don't want employees seeing hold settings; publishing per-button percentages can be used against a property by competitors). Instead Val wants a **visual conveyance** of "this denom is looser" without stating a number — e.g. a **three-tier colour code** (green → yellow → red for low → mid → high RTP, mapped to the three RTP tiers), or **larger icons** for higher denominations. Copy like "win more at higher denom" is allowed because it's non-specific. [[raw/game_design_meeting_march_7_2025.md]]
- **Historical caution (Michael):** manufacturers (Aruze, Konami) experimented with per-denomination RTP ~10–15 years ago (e.g. 85% at the lowest denom, 92% at the highest); players **couldn't feel** the difference, casinos pushed RTPs **lower** (Caesars pushing 85%), and it "went the way of the dodo bird" because no one delivered materially better financial results with it. [[raw/game_design_meeting_march_7_2025.md]]

## The progressive-per-denomination debate (Mar 7 — unresolved, Val's opinion recorded)

- Val argued it is **illogical ("stupid")** that JVL's progressive value is the **same across denominations** — he believes a higher denomination should carry a proportionally higher progressive (e.g. $500 at 1¢, $5,000 at 10¢) as an incentive to bet up. He stressed it is his opinion, and that "for the first time in JVL's history" he has enough engineering horsepower to develop his own game-design philosophy. [[raw/game_design_meeting_march_7_2025.md]]
- **Michael/Efim's rationale for the current design:** players like **big, life-changing numbers** and want to see them **even at the lowest bet**. Dynamically scaling the progressive to the bet was tried historically and **discouraged** players (e.g. a $25,000 mega at 600 credits dropping to $2,500 at 60 credits feels like something taken away). The **"jackpot rush" genre** keeps the big number visible at all bets and has held roughly **25% of the casino floor for ~30 years** across all manufacturers — evidence the design works. Fortune Foo Paws' brief was to get **as close as possible to "Coin Trio"** (a proven competitor), which does **not** scale the progressive to denomination — so the current implementation is faithful to Coin Trio. Val conceded he would love a visual conveyance **only** when a machine is set to different RTPs, but the base math stays as-is. [[raw/game_design_meeting_march_7_2025.md]]

- Val and Juan want the **Kai-Ocho** ("Kaiocho"/"gaiocho"/"Caglioccio") **Hold-and-Respin bonus music** changed — the 80s electronic techno clashes with the gentle salsa main-game music. Michael chose it intentionally (low-energy background so the player focuses on the diamonds being hit) but agreed to source **three or four audio samples**; the music must be **neutral** across the whole "Firelink" family (Roosters, Kai-Ocho, Dragons of Rain, Radio Queen). Changing an audio file changes the master checksum, but for online games the rules are relaxed and JVL can tell GLI/the lab to re-check **only** the audio for a quick turnaround. See [[wiki/decisions/2025-03-07-final-signoff-on-assembled-machines.md]]. An "Anton" (transcribed **"Anton Mishkevich"**) is credited with resolving earlier performance issues. ## Skill-market "nudge" presentation and the "hands" feature (Mar 14)

- JVL's skill games have **two presentations of the nudge feature**: a **conservative** one (the current/older presentation) and an **enhanced/advanced** one (matching what competitors are introducing). The two can be swapped in software — the Nebraska build reverts to the conservative nudge while keeping the other feature enhancements. See [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md]]. [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]
- The **"hands"** display (a Georgia skill-market requirement) is **part of a common UI used across all games**, so it can be **removed cleanly** for markets like Nebraska that don't require it. [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]
- The **VDEC flame-button** treatment recurs as the skill-market bet-up cue: flame only the **highest three** bet buttons, not the first two "big bad buttons" — consistent with the Mar 7 flame spec above. [[raw/Application_Nudge_Skill_for_Nebraska_2025-03-14.md]]

## Nudge-event animation freeze (Mar 28)

- On Peach 1 games the nudge arrows sit on a nearly-static frame, so nothing distracts. But **Peach 2** games run **particle animations across the top and bottom frame border**, which clash with the nudge event: the incorrect choice's arrow is greyed out, yet animated effects still play all around it — a "mixed message" that could hint the player toward the wrong choice. Michael's fix, adopted for design: when the nudge event initiates, **freeze all animation** except the correct arrow's sparkle ("tinkly-winklies"), then resume all animation once the player picks. Still to be seen implemented to confirm no odd behaviour. [[raw/games_design_meeting-march-28-2025.md]]
- Schedule impact: because of this discovery, **Peach 2 takes longer** (original estimate was ~two days per game theme → the two-week figure); **Peach 3** should be back on track once the animation-freeze method is known. [[raw/games_design_meeting-march-28-2025.md]]
- **Attract/bonus videos:** attraction videos are being updated for **FireEyes only** (not the Georgia sequence), and attraction mode is being added to **single-game mode** too. Juan changed his earlier ask — he now wants a **bonus-reveal** video (not just the free-spin video), so a new game reads as "a new game with a new feature," not "just another JBL [JVL] game," to maximize floor performance. [[raw/games_design_meeting-march-28-2025.md]]

## Recycle math and mechanics, not look-and-feel (Mar 28)

A design-philosophy debate that recurs across La Rosa and the Nebraska bonus (see [[wiki/topics/jvl-machine-lineup.md]] and [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md]]):

- Michael's default is to **amortize investment by reskinning** existing games — take what JVL already has and reimagine it "with the least amount of people hours." [[raw/games_design_meeting-march-28-2025.md]]
- Val drew a **sharp line**: he is **fully for recycling the math and game mechanics** (they sit in the background, are accepted, and are cheap to reuse), but is **~70% against recycling old graphics / look-and-feel / audio** — reusing the old visuals "ages" a game, ties it to the era/style/intent it was made for, and makes it "a non-transportable item" when moving to a new market. [[raw/games_design_meeting-march-28-2025.md]]
- **Conceptual continuity in naming.** Michael's rule that a bonus's **name should be evocative of the visuals on screen** decided the Nebraska bonus: with **corn on the coin and corn stalks**, "Golden Husk" fits, whereas "Buffalo Bounty / Bison Bucks" would read as strange (no buffalo on screen). "Buffalo" was also rejected because it evokes **Aristocrat's** top-five game **Buffalo**, and a bison isn't worth building/animating. See the Golden Husk naming on [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md]]. [[raw/games_design_meeting-march-28-2025.md]]

## Aspirational, ethnically representative themes (Mar 26, Gottlieb one-on-one)

- [[wiki/people/michael-gottlieb.md|Gottlieb]] framed a principle from his Bally history: Bally would not let him put **people of color on slot machines** ("they were afraid of ethnic people… it's kind of a racist world"), which he sees JVL correcting — **Kai-Ocho** shows "the beauty of people of different ethnicities," and players want to see themselves ("it's a black Barbie… People want to see them. Aspirational"). **La Rosa** applies the same logic for Juan's Mexican audience ("she's the Mexican princess" — the car, mansion, diamonds); a **Quinceañera** concept was explicitly dropped because the girls are 14–15 ("we're not going there") in favor of "the Jennifer Lopez approach." His rule: "I want to create games for the player," not for himself ("an old white Jewish guy") — citing **Wonder Woman**'s success over internal doubts. [[raw/michael_gottlieb_val_meeting-26-2025.md]]
- **Advertising-vs-game decoupling (open disagreement):** Val argued the marketing "wedges" are **advertising, not game continuation** — unconventional attention-grabbers are the point (his 1970s pasta-ad example; a rooster holding sparkling diamonds) — while Gottlieb held that elements a player never sees in the game (a rooster with a diamond; an orange diamond that doesn't exist; vertical text — "something I was taught you never do") make it "completely decoupled from the game." Tried as an experiment; Gottlieb warned against making *everything* unconventional. [[raw/michael_gottlieb_val_meeting-26-2025.md]]

## "Reimagined" legacy titles as the concept engine (Apr 1)

The Apr 1 theme brainstorm ([[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/michael-gottlieb.md|Michael]], [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]] — [[wiki/meetings/2025-04-01-game-design-theme-brainstorm.md]]) extended the Mar-28 "recycle math/mechanics, not look" philosophy into a concrete brand strategy: [[raw/game_design-april-1-2025.md]]

- **"Reimagine," don't "tweak" or "recycle."** Take a proven old title and rebuild its **graphics/audio from scratch** while keeping the proven **math/map** — the analogy used repeatedly was the original vs. 2000s **Thomas Crown Affair** (and **The Fugitive** TV show → film). This "moves our assets forward," amortizes cost, and builds a **stable IP library** — Val cited **Aristocrat**'s brand-focus discipline ("they don't make games, they create brands"; **Buffalo** in every flavour) versus **WMS/Bally's Fireball** having no sequel. Val banned parentheses/slashes in working titles because "the machine language reserves special symbols" for AI tools — use underscores/dashes. [[raw/game_design-april-1-2025.md]]
- **Data-driven selection:** three months of **Intralot Georgia** reports drove the shortlist — top game **Crazy Spins**, with **Aztec Star** and **Midnight Money** also high. [[raw/game_design-april-1-2025.md]]
- **Audience read:** Georgia's COAM clientele is "majorly Latino men," Puerto Rico similar; machines are in bars (~60% of what was seen). This killed a **German/Oktoberfest "Heidi's Beer House"** direction — both because the audience wouldn't resonate and because of a strong **IP caution** (Michael's Bally-era experience: Oktoberfest/beer-maiden iconography is so distinctive it's near-impossible to clone without confusion; they'd used "St. Pauli Girl" and been told no). The pivot: a **Latina cantina/tequila** theme (a Salma-Hayek-type lead) usable in both markets. The specific greenlit concepts are on [[wiki/topics/jvl-machine-lineup.md]]. [[raw/game_design-april-1-2025.md]]
- **Aspirational-character principle reaffirmed:** feature an attractive woman the target player fantasizes about (Michael: "we look to tap into fantasy… of wealth, power, success"); layout caution — two female symbols shrink the game logo, and on a modest billboard screen "bigger is always better" for a human character facing the player. [[raw/game_design-april-1-2025.md]]
- **Process:** concepts go into a **concept-development stage** with working titles TBD; Val warned the team to reach clarity **before** engaging [[wiki/people/lena.md|Lena]] ("the second we talk to Lena we start taking her cycles… she'll go into overwhelm"), treating concept as its own cycle. [[raw/game_design-april-1-2025.md]]

## Diamond Link V-Deck and Jackpot Rush background (Apr 3)

On the Apr 3 review ([[wiki/meetings/2025-04-03-game-design-vdeck-review.md]]) the team confirmed the **nudge/animation-freeze** solution (the Mar-28 fix) is working "exactly as intended," Peach 3 art issues closed, and debated the **Diamond Link (DLK) Georgia** V-Deck: [[raw/game_design-april-3-2025.md]]

- **Keep the crystal background, adopt the new rectangular buttons.** Val liked the newer Jackpot Rush deck "day and night" better, but wanted DLK to retain the **classy crystal edging/background** while moving to JVL's **new rectangular button standard**. DLK for Georgia deploys with **7 buttons** ($5/$10 max bet) laid out like Peach (2 small + 5 big), with the **top three buttons "on fire"** (progressive flame 3→7) — and **no textual incentive** ("the fire should sell that without any text… to not be misleading," as done for Fortune Football). Redesign estimated at ≥2 weeks; DLK has only ~50 units deployed in Georgia. [[raw/game_design-april-3-2025.md]]
- **Progressive eligibility / bet-incentive** recurs: Val again pressed that the flame/incentive is justified because probability of winning scales with the bet even though eligibility is at any bet — and again cast his as "one voice out of five," leaving the decision to Juan and Michael (consistent with the Feb-21/Mar-7 debate above). [[raw/game_design-april-3-2025.md]]
- **Jackpot Rush** background: Val dislikes the **purple/space background** (says it clashes and reads "oriental," while Jackpot Rush is "extremely American"), and asked Lena to try **desaturating** it for Georgia. Design principle restated: on a **multi-game** product (Jackpot Rush = 5 games, different themes/colour schemes) the V-Deck background is a **universal "wrapper,"** not tied to any one game — whereas a **single-title** V-Deck should be reflective of its game (an earlier agreed rule Lena cited). [[raw/game_design-april-3-2025.md]]
- **Major-in-the-bang-up (Efim):** Val wants the **major** progressive value included in the **white-coin "bang-up"** choreography — it must be **pulled and converted to credits dynamically in real time** at the moment of the event (only visible at high bet), sorted into the proper place in the sequence. A durable "we absolutely have to have it… we're going to use it going forward" requirement. [[raw/game_design-april-3-2025.md]]

## Math emulator first playable + CoinTrio bonus philosophy (May 1)

On the [[wiki/meetings/2025-05-01-game-design-review.md|May-1 review]] the **math emulator / test tool** (the Miranda→math tool above) reached a first milestone, and the team articulated the Fortune-Foo-Paws bonus-math philosophy: [[raw/game_design_review-may-1-2025.md]]

- **First playable build this week.** A "preliminary" math emulator now lets [[wiki/people/michael-gottlieb.md|Michael]] and [[wiki/people/efim-podvoiski.md|Efim]] play the bonuses and see how they behave — "buggy, but expected to be buggy." Val's one hard note: reduce the **delay between pressing spin and the reels starting** so it "plays like a slot machine" (animations/polish not the concern yet). [[raw/game_design_review-may-1-2025.md]]
- **It is separate from the shipping product.** The emulator is built **in Unity, in C#** (not Pixi, not Node.js), and is "**one monolithic piece**" wedged into the **Fortune Foo Paws** game — so it **cannot be reused** as a generic math emulator for another game. The team split on framing: Michael read fast emulator progress as "Fortune Full Pause is moving faster," which Efim/others corrected — a math-emulator framework is not the actual-product framework. Some solutions/ideas (reels rotation, math-server connection, 15-independent-reel bonus rotation) will transfer to other games' math, but the emulator itself won't. [[raw/game_design_review-may-1-2025.md]]
- **Bonus-math philosophy (CoinTrio-aligned).** The design mirrors the competitor **CoinTrio**: trigger **one coin** in the bonus → a decent-but-maybe-underwhelming experience; **two coins** → elevated expectation, a pretty good bonus; **three coins** → a shot at "knocking it out of the park." The **base game is deliberately "worthless"** — a ~**38% base / bonus** split (Michael noted ~91% RTP context) vs a conventional ~**70/30** base/bonus — because the value lives in the bonus (matching CoinTrio "and all these damn games now"). Building the exact feel is hard because competitor **parse sheets** give only surface-level behavioural data (e.g. no false-hit-to-real-hit ratio for the "Kaban" coin). ## South Dakota vertical screen, seated-player ergonomics, and the JPRE any-bet origin (May 5)

A game-design/priorities review ([[wiki/meetings/2025-05-05-game-design-south-dakota-jpre.md]]) with [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]], [[wiki/people/lena.md|Lena]] ("Selena"/"Elena"), [[wiki/people/efim-podvoiski.md|Efim]], [[wiki/people/michael-gottlieb.md|Michael]] referenced, and a "Landon" (unclear, Tier B). **Dated 2025-05-05 by content (a misfiled May file staged in the June folder — §11); back-filled here in chronological place, not overwriting later June current-state.** [[raw/games_design-may-5-2025.md]]

- **Seated-player reach — a durable vertical-cabinet ergonomics rule.** Juan flagged that on the tall **South Dakota vertical (D27)** more-games screen, the top game icon sits "more than half the screen up" — could a seated 60–70-year-old reach it without standing? Val's principle: **"bigger is better"** for icon legibility (aging vision/cataracts), **but** you must respect the **ergonomic reach**, because players will unconsciously pick the **bottom, easier-to-touch games** — "polluting performance data" (games picked for reach, not preference). Fix options: keep game selection to the **bottom ~50%** of the screen (moving the touch-select icon above and the games down), or ship 4 enabled games and keep 2 "in the back pocket." Verify the real reach on a physical machine before reworking art. [[raw/games_design-may-5-2025.md]]
- **South Dakota more-games screen specifics.** The vertical product has **no progressive umbrella logo** (unlike Georgia's Peach Jackpot / Diamond Link), so the screen is "just games" — make the icons bigger. A neutral **blue textured animated V-Deck background** (no common theme) complements the bright icons; during **attraction mode** (no credits) games highlight sequentially with logo + promo text, and touching an icon goes straight to the game. **Six games** picked (Bayou/Bio Riches, Crazy Cold, Liberty Spin, Rodeo Spins, plus others), but the screen supports 2/3/4/5/6 via existing templates; Michael's aesthetic rule: prefer an **even number** of games (2/4/6) for visual balance. **No Spanish** needed for South Dakota. [[raw/games_design-may-5-2025.md]]
- **Classic "Ainsworth-style" games are fine for this market (recycle, don't over-redesign).** The South Dakota vertical games are an **amalgamation of the Class-3 and Georgia work** with changed rules; several (Crazy Cold, designed >10 years ago) are "antiquated" but deliberately kept classic — IGT still ships Egyptian-themed classics; "he who ships wins… we need to ship." Only the gems/royals were shrunk for space. Extends the Mar-28 "recycle math/mechanics, not look" line to a whole-market call. [[raw/games_design-may-5-2025.md]]
- **The JPRE any-bet origin (later locked May 30).** This is the **earliest** discussion of the Jackpot Rush / **JPRE** "any-bet jackpot" change later decided on [[#jackpot-rush-any-bet--the-denomination-scheduler-may-30--jpre-review|May 30]]: convert Jack Patrasch / Jackpot Rush to award the progressive **at any bet** (remove the max-bet requirement), **revert to the $600-credit max bet** it was originally designed for, and have **Efim retune the jackpot hit frequency** (it "has never hit on Jackpot Rush") — three changes at once. Put on the **Class-3 Florida** priority board; this revised version "might go to Colombia before it even hits a casino stateside." Val floated then rejected a $3 three-button max-bet-jackpot deck (clamps turnover). **JPRE is $25,000** (stated as its value/price — unclear whether the product's price point or the progressive; recorded as stated). [[raw/games_design-may-5-2025.md]]
- **Priority sequencing (Nebraska first, JPRE fills a gap).** **Nebraska is the priority**, but JVL is "a week to 10 days" from being able to start it (open regulatory/design questions), so Val's plan: dedicate **Efim's ~1 week** to JPRE now, then switch to Nebraska — "if you simply ask me the binary question, what's the priority? I'm not going to say Nebraska," but there's a real gap to fill. **Nebraska work:** introduce the **Husk bonus** into previous Class-3 games (find RTP room, agree the allocation) — ~**5 games in a bit over 2 weeks** — plus the still-undiscussed **skill element** (needs Efim + framework). Resources named for JPRE: mainly **Efim** and, in the framework, **Igor** ([[wiki/people/igor-metelski.md|Metelski]]) **and Anton** — "the functionality is there" from Georgia, just needs porting to the Class-3 branch cleanly. ## Reading the Nebraska market — the "Vanilla" competitor and math-vs-choreography (May 8)

On the [[wiki/meetings/2025-05-08-game-design-review.md|May-8 review]] the team drew design lessons from [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]]'s Nebraska trip (the market-entry detail is on [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md]]). [[raw/game_design-may-8-2025.md]]

- **The Vanilla competitor as a cautionary reference.** **Vanilla** is the dominant Nebraska box (~3-year-old market; either pure Vanilla games or their CPU dropped into others' cabinets, vertical). Michael and Juan panned it as **"WMS Dribbler math"** — bet $1 win $0.25/$1 repeatedly (Juan's best win across 8 locations / 4 hours was $8) — the same trap that "got [WMS] their ass handed to them" in the Bluebird era: over-investing in the entertainment/precog show (e.g. a **"Lucky Penny"** with a 6-second penguin precog banner that only paid a 1× wild) while the game "drops like a rock." Vanilla machines had **no hard drives**, 5–7-second load screens, and boring gameplay. Design rule reaffirmed: don't confuse sizzle with substance — better to have zeros then **5–10× wins** for variety than a steady dribble. [[raw/game_design-may-8-2025.md]]
- **Nudge presentation vs Vanilla's slider.** Vanilla's nudge is a green sliding-bar ("touch when it's green"); JVL's sparkly-arrow nudge is judged "more direct and communicative… we do it better." Players in the market are already accustomed to nudge, easing JVL's entry. [[raw/game_design-may-8-2025.md]]
- **Math vs choreography (durable principle).** Efim's **Mass Fortune Football** ladder revealed the rule Val wants held: keep the **mathematical behavior of the ladder** in its own space, separate from the **choreography** (cadence/tempo/pacing of anticipation → hit → the end "wiggle" tease). The math tool just goes "boom-boom-boom"; the shipped game adds anticipation. Sweet spot ≈ **4–6 hits** (maybe 5–7) — "Goldilocks": too few isn't compelling, too many is bad only if you're still not at an exciting credit value (e.g. 5 hits to reach just 600 credits feels like a lot for little). Efim to aim for ~5–6 hits given the ~sub-600 average win. Val: "keep that in the Athene bank" for when they work on choreography. [[raw/game_design-may-8-2025.md]]
- **Why no multi-denomination (design rationale).** JVL games "don't show anything in glyphs… everything in cash, so there is no place for denomination" — with ~7 bet levels ($1–$5) there is "no room" for a denomination selector; games have internal denominations (1¢–25¢) but the player only ever sees cash bets. [[raw/game_design-may-8-2025.md]]

## The nudge is killed; designing a Vanilla-style "power slider" (May 15)

On the [[wiki/meetings/2025-05-15-game-design-nebraska-south-dakota.md|May-15 review]], the Nebraska regulator's attorney ruled **nudge does not qualify as skill** (a wrong nudge can lose a winnable outcome), so the team designed a replacement post-spin skill mechanic modelled on the incumbent competitors. Market-entry consequences are on [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md]]. [[raw/game_design_nebraska_south_dakota-may-15-2025.md]]

- **Why the competitors' mechanic is compliant.** Nebraska's #1 supplier **Bonilla Games** (~60% of the market) and the dominant box **Vanilla** use a **power slider**: on a win, dexterity sets the payout (press dead-center = 100%; off-center = 90/80/50%), so **every winning combination stays winnable** — that is what makes it "skill." JVL's nudge failed exactly because a wrong choice could zero a win. [[raw/game_design_nebraska_south_dakota-may-15-2025.md]]
- **Two candidate designs (mathematically identical).** (1) **Lucky Rainbow** — a rainbow arc, four colour segments red/orange/yellow/green, e.g. red 10% of the arc / 50% of the win, orange 10%/70%, yellow 10%/90%, green ~70%/100%; (2) **Bullseye** — four concentric archery rings with the same colour/percentage mapping. Both **start at 100% (green)**, like Vanilla's slider, so a player who acts immediately gets 100%. Michael's view: pick and **brand one** mechanic; doing both is "expensive insurance." [[raw/game_design_nebraska_south_dakota-may-15-2025.md]]
- **Design toward familiarity, kept simple.** Vanilla uses **four** percentage scales and the red/orange/yellow/green hierarchy; JVL matches that so players (trained on Vanilla) don't perceive JVL as paying less. Keep it "geometrically straightforward… bigger, easier to read" than Vanilla's small/ugly slider — a **functional tool**, not ornate. Likely a **bar** that desaturates from green over a few seconds, sweeping faster (~10–15%) through the junk colours back to green. [[raw/game_design_nebraska_south_dakota-may-15-2025.md]]
- **Where the meter lives.** Val floated putting it on the **V-Deck** (a slam-button-selectable meter) to save rework; consensus was to keep it on the **main screen** (players are trained by Vanilla to look there) and **optionally duplicate on the V-Deck** — because interrupting play is "verboten" in slot design, and covering both screens is the "quickest path of least resistance." Nebraska machines are mostly **mechanical (2-button)** with no VTEC, so players are trained to touch the screen. [[raw/game_design_nebraska_south_dakota-may-15-2025.md]]
- **Regulator's framing.** These are **amusement machines, not casino machines**; the regulator wants them to look/behave the **opposite** of a casino machine, so if the skill effect is "annoying, so be it"; there is **no state-level rule** on how many segments or what percentages (Vanilla's fixed intervals are just its own implementation). [[raw/game_design_nebraska_south_dakota-may-15-2025.md]]
- **Hit-rate concern (Efim).** Because the skill event fires after every winning spin, prefer games with **hit rate ~4/5/6, not 2/3**, so the slider isn't used almost every spin. [[raw/game_design_nebraska_south_dakota-may-15-2025.md]]
- **Flatten-and-feed the math.** Val's rule for converting Georgia games: keep a **randomly-hitting "Swift"/Golden-Fast bonus**, flatten the pay table, take the top-win allocation (est. ~1–2% of total) and feed it into that bonus so the largest wins "never show up" — controlling volatility without an unfair machine (Efim to confirm the top wins are safely allocatable). This extends the Mar-28 "recycle math/mechanics" line. [[raw/game_design_nebraska_south_dakota-may-15-2025.md]]

## Bonus economics for a low-win market — Nebraska Golden Husk (May 29)

On the [[wiki/meetings/2025-05-29-games-design-recap.md|May-29 games recap]] the team applied the "recycle math/mechanics" line to tuning a bonus for a **low-win-per-unit** market (durable knobs, not just Nebraska-specific — market detail on [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md]]). [[raw/games_design_recap-may-29-2025.md]]

- **The three knobs.** A jackpot/bonus has (1) a **progressive base value**, (2) the **RTP allocated** to it, and (3) its **hit frequency** — separable but related. Georgia Peach's reference settings: base progressive **$2,400**; **5% of RTP** to the bonus; hits **1 in ~600 games**; bonus EV ~15 bets; base **progressive RTP ~1%** (so the base value barely affects the bonus RTP). [[raw/games_design_recap-may-29-2025.md]]
- **Tuning for a small market.** Where win-per-unit is ~half of Georgia's, drop the base far (to **$500 / $300–$400 / even $200**) so wins stay under the **$599 lock-up** and the bonus **hits more often** (option to raise allocation to **6–7%** and frequency toward 1-in-300). Design rule Michael restated: **marry math to presentation** — the player should feel a triggered bonus *could* have paid big (a juicy value among the pick bins), even when the actual win is modest. [[raw/games_design_recap-may-29-2025.md]]
- **Fortune FooPaws min bet (recurs):** the additional bet set should start at **70–75 credits** (not the 88/880 "Cointreau" design that excludes low-wallet players, nor JVL's 60), chosen so the games also reuse in **Colombia** (bet levels are hard-coded; denominations just **scale** by a currency multiplier — you can't insert arbitrary values because the progressive means is in money). [[raw/games_design_recap-may-29-2025.md]]

## Jackpot Rush any-bet + the denomination scheduler (May 30 — JPRE review)

On the [[wiki/meetings/2025-05-30-game-design-review-jpre.md|May-30 Jackpot Rush review]] ([[wiki/people/michael-gottlieb.md|Michael]], [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]]) — decision on [[wiki/decisions/2025-05-30-jackpot-rush-any-bet-and-denomination-scheduler.md]]: [[raw/game_design_review_jpre-may-30-2025.md]]

- **Max-bet-required is a trap when players don't bet max.** Jackpot Rush ("JPRE") requires ~$3 / 480-credit max bet for progressive eligibility, but average bets run ~$1.20–$1.87, so most players get a **static credit tower** instead of running progressive **meters** — "a tower of credit values is shit… they play these games to chase the progressives." Fix: make progressives **eligible at any bet** (the Georgia version already is; the Florida/Class-3 version needs a math redesign). Michael: "the definition of insanity is doing the same thing… and expecting a different outcome." [[raw/game_design_review_jpre-may-30-2025.md]]
- **First-button / minimum-bet philosophy (durable).** Val: most players only ever press the **first button**, so a 40¢-vs-60¢ (or 50¢-vs-70¢) minimum is a **20–50% swing** in turnover — "statistically I don't see how we make money being cheaper." Juan's nuance: players do dabble up to button 2/3 (hence $1.20–$1.80 averages), and the market floor min bet is now $0.50–$0.60. Michael's "Ferrari vs Toyota" counter: perceived value can justify a higher entry. [[raw/game_design_review_jpre-may-30-2025.md]]
- **Denomination scheduler (the "chameleon").** Change bet levels/denoms by **time of day / day of week** — cheaper for the weekday value crowd, higher on weekends. Precedent: **Interblock** built Juan a "Denom scheduler" (dropped 25¢/50¢ after 5 pm). Mechanically = two GLI part-sheets, one enabled by schedule. Ruled to design **across the board**; possible patent worked around via a random trigger. [[raw/game_design_review_jpre-may-30-2025.md]]
- **Progressive-frequency retune (June 11).** On the June-11 review Michael noted the current **Jackpot Rush** progressive takes ~**2 million games** to hit — at ~1,500 games/day that's **over three years**, so a frequent player never sees it fire; the retune underway (for "Jackpot Rush 2"/"Gaucho 2") aims to bring it down to **~twice a year**, frequent enough for a regular player to witness a hit. Michael also wants to track **progressive payouts (even sub-IRS amounts)** with Pavel to be sure the game isn't over-paying outside the pay table. [[raw/games_design-june-11-2025.md]]

## Nebraska compliance mechanics — power meter, the Simon secondary skill, and "no-loss math" (Jun 4–5)

The [[wiki/organizations/eclipse-gaming-lab.md|Eclipse]] lab call ([[wiki/meetings/2025-06-04-eclipse-nebraska-testing-requirements.md]]) and the next-day design session ([[wiki/meetings/2025-06-05-nebraska-regulation-game-design.md]]) turned the [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md|Nebraska]] "every play must be winnable" rule into concrete game-design mechanics — the durable design knobs (market-entry consequences on the Nebraska project page). [[raw/eclipse_nebraska_testing_requirement_review-june-4-2025.md]] [[raw/nebraska_regulation_game_design-june-5-2025.md]]

- **Two skill layers.** On a **winning** spin, the **power meter** (press dead-center = 100%, off-center 90/80/70/50%) is a sufficient skill element — you can win **up to 100%** of the bet, never more, on the meter. On a **losing** spin (zero on the reels) the player must be offered a **secondary skill** to recoup **up to 104%** of the bet — most commonly a **Simon-says memory game**. You **cannot award anything without a skill interaction** (Nebraska's attorney: a nudge alone does not qualify; a wrong nudge can zero a winnable outcome). [[raw/eclipse_nebraska_testing_requirement_review-june-4-2025.md]] [[raw/nebraska_regulation_game_design-june-5-2025.md]]
- **The Simon game as an anti-farming device.** A 3×3 grid, escalating rounds (shortest ~9, up to 25–30 positions), all-or-none, identical pattern each time plus one new light, with machine-controlled **intermissions** to force a memory "brain dump" — deliberately built so recouping 104% is a tedious, low-wage "job" (~$5–10/hour) most players abandon. Players "cheat" it with a dry-erase marker on the button deck; the design counter is escalating difficulty/speed and multiple RNG-selected variants. Val's design instinct: something "counterintuitive to every cell in my body" — designing a bonus meant to be unpleasant. [[raw/nebraska_regulation_game_design-june-5-2025.md]] [[raw/eclipse_nebraska_testing_requirement_review-june-4-2025.md]]
- **The "design out the loss" alternative (Efim/Val's preferred).** Rather than ship the Simon bonus, build the **math so every spin wins something** — a guaranteed **scatter pay** (e.g. a special symbol on every spin paying ≥5 credits, or even 1¢ as a competitor "Thistle" reportedly does) — so, by the letter of the reg, every play delivers a winnable outcome and the secondary feature is unnecessary. Cost: a **power meter on every spin** (acceptable since players learn to hit spin instantly). Val/Efim judged this easier and cleaner than the Simon path, pending Eclipse/attorney sign-off. The full-game **RTP with perfect play could reach ~150%+** because natural base-game wins stack on the recouped bonus, so the bonus must stay a hard grind. [[raw/nebraska_regulation_game_design-june-5-2025.md]]
- **Presentation rule.** If the Simon path is used, hide its prompt as a **vague V-deck/player-information message** the player overrides by pressing spin — never interrupt play; the credit-back is modest (104% of bet). The secondary game may live on the **main screen or the digital button deck**. [[raw/nebraska_regulation_game_design-june-5-2025.md]] [[raw/eclipse_nebraska_testing_requirement_review-june-4-2025.md]]
- **Disable-the-feature legality (open).** The incumbent **Banila/Bonilla** reportedly lets the **operator** disable the secondary feature; Val holds that operator-selectable disabling "breaks the law" while **player-selectable** is a legitimate choice — an unresolved compliance line (Juan to verify the actual game-setting screen). [[raw/nebraska_regulation_game_design-june-5-2025.md]]
- **Multiple light-pattern variations (June 11 anti-farming extension).** Players defeat the Simon memory game by writing the light pattern down with a **dry-erase marker**; JVL's counter is to run **two or three RNG-selected light-pattern variations** so a player must learn several — *unless* a reg requires the bonus to **behave identically every time** (unknown — Juan to confirm with Eclipse; competitors observed using "four times the light pattern," others up to nine, inconsistently, and **not documented in the regs or Eclipse/GLI materials**). Val judged the **primary power meter "nailed down"**; only the **secondary bonus** needs full design understanding. [[raw/games_design-june-11-2025.md]]

## Jackpot Rush "any-bet" presentation + the meters-on-buttons debate (June 12)

On the [[wiki/meetings/2025-06-12-games-design-priorities-review.md|June-12 games-design review]] the team designed how to *sell* the [[wiki/decisions/2025-05-30-jackpot-rush-any-bet-and-denomination-scheduler.md|Jackpot Rush "progressives eligible at any bet"]] change to the Florida player, and re-litigated the bet-up display: [[raw/games_design-june-12-2025.md]]

- **Sell the USP loudly.** "Progressives eligible at any bet" is a **unique selling point** and must "hit the customer over the head" — two mechanisms: (1) a **tracked/attract mode** with the biggest possible text ("take up the whole blank in the screen"); (2) on the **main screen**, transition the **logo → "progressives eligible at any bet" → back**, looping on top of the meter block. Rationale: don't let a min-bet player *discover* eligibility by accident — sell it up front as a better proposition than competing machines. [[raw/games_design-june-12-2025.md]]

Ruling: **do not** put meters on the buttons (scroll them above the buttons instead, an option left open). Val ceded — "there has to be only one driver… so Michael, it's you." [[raw/games_design-june-12-2025.md]]
- **Simplicity as a design guidepost (durable).** Michael invoked designing the **V-Deck at Bally** ("hundreds of iterations"); Val extended it into a principle — drawing on the Tesla minimalist interior and a critique of Apple's latest "layers of buttons with translucency and noise" — that **simplicity of design "should be one of our guideposts,"** because the world is "too complicated, too many choices, too much noise." ## Nebraska secondary-skill mechanics refined — power meter vs Simon, and why 104% (June 12)

The [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md|Nebraska]] compliance design (see the June-4/5 section above) was sharpened on the June-12 review: [[raw/games_design-june-12-2025.md]]

- **Two layers, cleanly split.** On a **winning** spin the **power meter** is presented and returns **50–100%** of the win (dead-center = 100%); it should appear **only on a win**. On a **losing** spin the player may touch the screen for the **secondary Simon-says memory game** to recoup **up to 104%** — deliberately built to be "**incredibly annoying and not user-friendly**" so most players skip it. The **primary game must offer real losses** (not a guaranteed win every spin). [[raw/games_design-june-12-2025.md]]
- **Why 104% (not 100%).** The regulator allows the secondary game to return up to **104%** because at a **25-cent bet** a sub-penny win (e.g. $0.003) can't be shown with only two decimal places — you need 104% to display at least a **one-cent** win; at 50¢ you can show a penny at 102%, and at higher bets 101%. Whether the theoretical return should be **tied to the bet** was raised (Efim: "it doesn't matter for us" — perfect play already pushes total RTP well over 100%, ~150%, so the exact figure is immaterial once the bonus is a hard grind). [[raw/games_design-june-12-2025.md]]
- **Copy Vanilla, not Thistle.** A competitor (**Thistle**) awards a small win on **every** spin to bypass the Simon game entirely — but their operator partner said that is Thistle's **worst-performing game**, so a guaranteed-win-every-spin is a poor player experience. JVL should copy **Vanilla** (the better performer). Juan to gather **much more video** of the competitor mechanic before finalizing. A separate confirmation (video from "Dennis") settled that **any base-game win — even one cent — counts as a "win"** per the casino rule, and JVL should **not sacrifice the game** to manufacture small wins. [[raw/games_design-june-12-2025.md]]

## Nebraska firsthand market research + Georgia-titles-to-class-3 plan (June 19)

On the [[wiki/meetings/2025-06-19-games-design-nebraska-market.md|June-19 games review]] ([[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/michael-gottlieb.md|Michael]], [[wiki/people/lena.md|Lena]], [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]] reporting from the Nebraska floor, [[wiki/people/efim-podvoiski.md|Efim]]) Juan's firsthand observations sharpened the [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md|Nebraska]] design (market/payout detail on the project page). (§12: transcript internally duplicates — facts once.) [[raw/Games_design-june-19-2025.md]]

- **The competitor "Banilla" (Vanilla) has two versions — with and without nudge.** The **nudge** version adds a secondary **"banana bonus"** — a **glorified Simon-Says** (bananas instead of colored circles), **25 rounds** of pattern-matching, **all-or-none** (one wrong = bonged out), with **intermissions after every 5** to force a memory dump — to win back barely a penny (help screen: complete all 25 rounds; ~104% on a $0.25 bet). Operators hate it (the game earns nothing during it), locations running it were empty — so **JVL will not do nudge** (the regulator also warns against it). This is firsthand confirmation of the "make the secondary skill deliberately annoying" design already on this page. [[raw/Games_design-june-19-2025.md]]
- **50–60% of the install base = Banilla "Nebraska 1" and "Nebraska 2" skill games with the power meter** — reaffirming JVL's chosen power-meter primary skill. **Autoplay bypasses the secondary skill entirely** on Banilla (autoplay just re-presses spin on a loss) — so JVL can legitimately do the same. [[raw/Games_design-june-19-2025.md]]
- **Power-meter slider must not always start at 100%.** Juan observed Banilla's slider starting at 50% on the left one spin, 50% on the right the next — never a cued 100% (which would let a player just hammer play). JVL's requirement: **randomly start the needle left or right, never at 100%** (or alternate the opposite of the prior event). [[raw/Games_design-june-19-2025.md]]
- **The "metronome" familiarity principle.** Get lots of video of the competitor's **speed / cadence / timing** because "that is the industry standard the players are exposed to" — JVL wants its skill mechanic's **metronome at the same rhythm as theirs** so it feels familiar; "we don't want to come across as unique, weird, or different… we want to be harmonious with the environment." [[raw/Games_design-june-19-2025.md]]

## Nebraska skill mechanics locked into Jira — power meter + circle pattern-match (June 23)

On the [[wiki/meetings/2025-06-23-nebraska-game-design-planning.md|June-23 Nebraska design + planning session]] ([[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/michael-gottlieb.md|Michael]], [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]], [[wiki/people/lena.md|Lena]], [[wiki/people/efim-podvoiski.md|Efim]]) the two skill layers from the June-4/5/12 design were turned into concrete, buildable specs written into Jira for the six Golden Husk games (market/schedule on [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md]]). (§12: transcript internally duplicates — facts taken once; garbled tail not mined.) [[raw/NE_game_design_and_planning-june-23-2025.md]]

- **Primary skill = the power/skill meter.** The **needle timer starts within ~1 second of the power meter popping up** (it animates immediately; touching the screen / pressing play **stops** it) — set to **30 seconds** (an operator had complained 15 s was too short: players celebrating a win let it lapse without interacting). On a win, the **win is shown first**, then the meter appears; if the player stops below 100% the meter **reduces** the payout. A **text overlay is always shown** (whatever the outcome), placed **above** the win meter (Banila/Vanilla puts it below): e.g. "$1 at 90% = $0.90", or "$1 at 100% = $1". If a spin triggers **free spins or the Golden Husk jackpot**, the power meter is presented **last** — after the free-spins/jackpot outcome and the post-racking of the big win. [[raw/NE_game_design_and_planning-june-23-2025.md]]
- **Round UP (ceiling) fractional cents.** Because discrete meter values (80/70/60%) don't always land on a whole cent, the reduced win is **always rounded up (ceiling)**, even a 0.1-cent fraction — "make ceiling, not round." Rationale (Val): the player already lost the expected win by not hitting 100%, so don't also shave the fraction. **RNG** determines the pattern randomness. [[raw/NE_game_design_and_planning-june-23-2025.md]]
- **Reject Banila's "last win" toggle.** Banila's win meter toggles between "win" and the player's **last win** amount; JVL will **not** copy it ("not typical Nevada behavior… leads to player dispute"). [[raw/NE_game_design_and_planning-june-23-2025.md]]
- **Secondary skill = circle pattern-match (Simon-style), on losses.** JVL uses **circle pattern-matching** (not Banila's "follow the banana"). The prompt text is **"touch here to pattern match"** — deliberately **low-contrast** (Michael: "light gray on white") and **lower-right justified** so it's hard to read ("comparatively difficult to read… the opposite of everything we value"); a darker background differentiates JVL's from Banila's white one. Behaviour: complete **25** successful pattern rounds → text "game complete" → return to base game with **104% of the original bet** added to the credit meter; a wrong circle → text "game over" for **2 seconds** → return to main game (deliberately "clunky, rude, and annoying"). A **rules screen** first shows the basic rules ("if the wrong selection is made, the game will end and no win will be awarded") with a **"touch here to continue"** button. [[raw/NE_game_design_and_planning-june-23-2025.md]]
- **Loading-screen intermissions.** After **4, 8, 12, 16, 20 and 24** successful pattern completions the screen changes to **blank blue with a "loading" bar for 15 seconds** then resumes — a deliberate "brain-dump" annoyance (the 15 s is a guess, adjustable; a large loading bar creates the illusion of progress to keep the player patient). [[raw/NE_game_design_and_planning-june-23-2025.md]]
- **Timer-expiry legality (open).** If the primary meter's timer reaches zero the player **100% loses**. Whether a timed-out win must then still offer the secondary skill (so "every outcome is winnable") is **awaiting operator confirmation** — Val/Michael lean against giving a second chance after the player had their skill opportunity, but it's pending. The **"aloha bet"** (auto-bet when the player lacks funds for one full bet) is kept as JVL's version, enable/disable via setup. [[raw/NE_game_design_and_planning-june-23-2025.md]]
- **No audio on the skill games.** Neither the primary meter nor the pattern-match game gets sound ("silent and miserable") — any hum/slide-whistle would just make the player linger on a screen JVL wants them to leave fast (get 100% and get out). [[raw/NE_game_design_and_planning-june-23-2025.md]]

## Mid-July 2025 — the "progressive-on-progressive overpay" hypothesis + the math-emulator test

The July-3/July-8 hold-problem thread sharpens into a concrete hypothesis on the [[wiki/meetings/2025-07-18-progressive-overpay-alignment.md|~July-18 alignment call]] ([[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]], [[wiki/people/efim-podvoiski.md|Efim]]), with the math emulator also raised on the [[wiki/meetings/2025-07-15-alignment-planning-catch-up.md|mid-July alignment-planning catch-up]].

- **Val's hypothesis: progressive is double-counted into the base RTP.** Val believes Efim reported a "base payout" of ~**92%** that already **folded the progressive in** — e.g. **88.5% base + 1.5% progressive = 90%, called "base pay"** — and then the **remote initial setup (RIS)** allocated **another ~1.5–2% to the progressive**, for ~91.5% total; so the progressive contribution is effectively counted twice and the machines **overpay**. ## Aug 7 2025 — the Fiery Ice negative-days problem and the Player Behavior Simulator

The [[wiki/meetings/2025-08-07-player-behavior-simulator.md|Aug-7 meeting]] ([[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/michael-gottlieb.md|Michael]], [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]], [[wiki/people/efim-podvoiski.md|Efim]], [[wiki/people/anton-leshkevich.md|Anton Leshkevich]]) turned a two-month-old performance puzzle into a new tool commission. Preceded/continued by a smaller Val+Efim+Anton working session ([[wiki/meetings/2025-08-08-player-simulator-engineering-discussion.md|≈Aug-8]]).

- **The problem (Juan):** the **Fiery Ice Link** game — first theme **Gayocho** (aimed at the Cuban player in Little Havana) — launched strong at three Florida casinos (**Seminole Classic** tribal; **Big Easy** commercial, ~50% tax rate; **Hialeah**, heavy Cuban play) but ~30 days in began posting **excessive negative-win days** — actually *losing* the casino money, not just low win. Over an 82-day data window it hit as high as **~30% negative days**. Big Easy coin-in was down ~50% month-over-month, more than the casino's floor average. [[raw/Player_behavior_simulator.md]]
- **The RTP finding (Efim):** across all machines for the whole period, **actual RTP ≈ 92.43%** vs **expected ≈ 91.72%** — less than a percentage point over, i.e. **not** the source of the losses. Major progressive events: **77 observed vs ~64 expected** (probability of ≥77 ≈ 7% — "quite normal"). The progressive resets to **$500**, increments at a **1.5%** contribution rate, and stays below the **$1,200 IRS lock-up** limit so casino staff don't flag repeat hits. Efim resisted slicing the data per-casino to hunt patterns ("sorcery"). [[raw/Player_behavior_simulator.md]]
- **Val's reframe — volatility, not RTP.** From 40 years operating games, Val's read is that the RTP is fine but the **volatility is too high** for the player to sustain, so players lose money/interest and leave. This crystallized when Efim earlier told him he was **not sure the Diamond-Link-derived game mechanic can be made less volatile while staying interesting** — which Val took as confirmation the mechanic itself may be wrong for JVL. Michael/Juan corroborated never having seen so many negative days on a popular machine; Efim countered that ~25-26% negative days is a **feature of the math** (his random-bet simulation reproduced it), and that utilization/brand-loyalty effects (popular games get played first in the off-season) also explain low volume. Recorded as the live tension, not resolved. [[raw/Player_behavior_simulator.md]]
- **The Player Behavior Simulator (commissioned).** Val specified a tool that emulates **player personas** — variables: **denomination, bet buttons, starting bankroll (~$100 average today), and time at the machine** — running many sessions and reporting how many end "happy" vs "frustrated." Explicitly **not** a math tester and **not** tied to a physical machine. Efim will build it (est. **~4 days**) in **Python**: the simulator carries **no math itself** — Efim first runs **~100 million games in his math simulator**, writes each outcome's frequency to a file, and the player simulator consumes that file to play sessions; parameters go in a file/batch run, results in a readable report. [[wiki/people/anton-leshkevich.md|Anton]] helps only if a **GUI** is wanted. [[raw/Player_behavior_simulator.md]] Val also insisted the tool be built "in the form of an experience," and used the meeting to declare JVL can build **"its own school"** of game design. [[raw/Player_behavior_simulator.md]]
- **Parallel math rework.** With Val's approval, Michael + Efim will separately build a **new, less-volatile math model for Fiery Ice** (same RTP, lower volatility) — a substantial rework, not a tweak; can start in parallel with the tool. [[raw/Player_behavior_simulator.md]]
- **Michael Gottlieb's design frame (durable principles):** the **$20 test** (a "successful session" = the player triggers at least one bonus on a $20 bankroll) has been eroded for years by ever-larger progressives that force higher assumed bankrolls, pushing the industry to a **"lottery-ticket philosophy"** (rarely win, chase a life-changer). **Hold-and-respin has largely replaced free-spins** as the dominant bonus mechanic (top-5 of the top-25 games all use it). RTP is effectively fixed; the accepted **base/bonus split is ~70/30** — move it a little and players don't feel it, move it a lot and it breaks. The design challenge is "how low can we take the limbo bar" on volatility before the bonus stops paying enough to feel worth it. [[raw/Player_behavior_simulator.md]]

Market/schedule consequences are on [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md]] (Feb-27); the durable design mechanics: [[raw/Game_Development-February-27-2026.md]] [[raw/Summary_Game_Development-February-27-2026.md]]

- **GC369 — the "dual box" coin-selector replaces the power meter (no toggle).** Inspired by market leader **Light and Wonder**'s Nebraska approach, the primary skill becomes a **two-box overlay**: text reads "to access win, choose the larger coin"; the left box holds a small dull coin, the right a large shiny coin with a gold tracer; pressing the correct box wins **100%**, the wrong box wins **0%**. It is a straight **50/50 choice** — faster than the power meter, which required physical dexterity to land on 100%. The old power meter is **removed entirely, with no on/off fallback**: the team rejected keeping a power-meter toggle as a regulatory safety net (see the debate on [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md]]). Michael updated the abstract to specify random presentation. (Same anti-pattern-learning rule as the June-2025 requirement that the power-meter needle never start at a cued 100%.) [[raw/Game_Development-February-27-2026.md]] [[raw/Summary_Game_Development-February-27-2026.md]]

Michael settled on calling it the **"dual box"** primary skill feature. This is a design-culture principle — the *naming* of a feature carries regulatory exposure. [[raw/Game_Development-February-27-2026.md]] [[raw/Summary_Game_Development-February-27-2026.md]]

[[raw/Game_Development-February-27-2026.md]] [[raw/Summary_Game_Development-February-27-2026.md]]

Applied **only** to these two new games; existing games keep their pay tables. ## Mar 4 2026 — the dual-box built, and hiding the secondary-skill trigger button

On the [[wiki/meetings/2026-03-04-game-development-schedule-review.md|Mar-4 game-development review]] ([[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]], [[wiki/people/michael-gottlieb.md|Michael]], [[wiki/people/lena.md|Lena]], [[wiki/people/juan-martinez.md|Juan]], [[wiki/people/efim-podvoiski.md|Efim]]) [[wiki/people/lena.md|Lena]]'s built implementation of the [[#feb-27-2026--the-nebraska-dual-box-primary-skill-feature-replaces-the-power-meter|Feb-27 dual-box]] primary skill and the secondary-skill trigger button were reviewed and approved. Market/schedule detail on [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md]] (Mar-4). [[raw/Game_Development_Schedule_Review-March-4-2026.md]]

- **The dual-box as built (velocity is the whole point).** On a **winning spin** the reels dim, the left border desaturates, and an **animated tracer runs around the perimeter of the right image**; the player picks a box — the **larger/shinier coin wins 100%**, the wrong box wins **0%** (all-or-none). The behavioral characteristic mirrors the old prize-win meter but is **~90% faster** (Juan's estimate — "takes 10% of what it used to be"), which Val called a big perceived player benefit. Michael's design ethos, restated: the **oversized touch boxes deliberately increase the touch area** so an imprecise, distracted or impaired player can "slap at the screen" and still hit the right one — no precise XY coordinate needed. Contrast with the old **power meter**, which required **timing** (watch the needle, land on green) and so caused hesitation, irritation and disappointment before the win. Juan noted the old power meter also offered **partial wins (70/50/30%)**, which the all-or-none 100% removes. Val's meta-point: JVL is now **developing its own design philosophy** for why it does things — "we borrowed/were inspired by someone else's implementation, but we're adopting it into our own inventory of philosophies," which he wants for every decision. [[raw/Game_Development_Schedule_Review-March-4-2026.md]]
- **Make the secondary-skill trigger button maximally unintrusive.** The **secondary skill game** (a pattern-match game offered on a **non-winning** outcome to recoup — nobody plays it because it takes too long) needs its **trigger button** present-but-hidden. The proposed change from the current clear implementation (readable "touch here to pattern match" text, contrasting border, black background, good-contrast font): make the button **transparent**, its text **desaturated, darker and smaller**, and the **border blended into the background** — plus the **grayed box itself shrunk**. The competitor that inspired it hides the text **dead-center** (where JVL's last-win meter sits) in a **font colour that matches the background** — "practically invisible." Michael deliberately did **not** go fully egregious (bad-eyesight players and unknown regulatory requirements), so it is "significantly less prominent but still findable." Val approved and told Juan he **may remove the border** entirely, leaving that call in Juan's court. [[raw/Game_Development_Schedule_Review-March-4-2026.md]]
