# FlexPay kiosk production and tooling

How JVL plans to **manufacture the FlexPay redemption kiosks** fast enough to win the Georgia market — the tooling-vs-throughput debate between [[wiki/people/val-levitan.md|Val]] and [[wiki/people/genadi-peari.md|Genadi]] on the Mar 27 2025 call. This is the production side of the FlexPay payment product (the software/integration side is on [[wiki/topics/flexpay-mobile-app-and-service-advocates.md]]; the payment processor is [[wiki/organizations/incom.md|Incom]]). [[raw/flex_pay_production-march-27-2025.md]]

## The problem: a short, sharp production burst

- JVL needs to produce a **large quantity in a short time**, then stop — once the market fills there is little further to build, and the other markets ([[wiki/projects/puerto-rico-expansion.md|Puerto Rico]], [[wiki/projects/nebraska-skill-market.md|Nebraska]]) are small. [[raw/flex_pay_production-march-27-2025.md]]
- **Market sizing (Val):** by **June 2026** every Georgia location must be filled with a redemption kiosk — one kiosk per location = **6,000 locations**. There are **two competitors**; an even split would be ~2,000 kiosks each, but Val believes JVL can take **~3,000 (≈50% of the market)** plus the recurring revenue, because the competitors build **from sheet metal** and don't pre-stock — they can place only ~100–200/month (~1,200–1,500 over six months). [[raw/flex_pay_production-march-27-2025.md]]
- The [[wiki/organizations/incom.md|Incom]] contract (materializing now) even lets JVL **operate** the kiosks itself for recurring revenue, but Val would rather **sell** them than operate them. [[raw/flex_pay_production-march-27-2025.md]]

## The tooling debate

- **Val's case ("insurance"):** invest in **tooling** rather than in stock, so that if he needs to raise quantity quickly it is easy — tooling is his hedge against demand. [[raw/flex_pay_production-march-27-2025.md]]
- **Genadi's pushback:** tooling won't solve the real constraint. The **plastics are already done**; there is very little metal (the metal bars are ~6 parts). With vs without tooling the difference is only ~**1.5 vs 3.5 weeks** and not a huge money difference; bar tooling is very expensive while the metal stands are cheap. The throughput limit is the **CNC machine** and the unavoidable **painting** step, plus **assembly time** — not shipping. Shipping a container or two is not the bottleneck; **fast assembly** is. [[raw/flex_pay_production-march-27-2025.md]]
- **Plastic option:** making some parts (even the cabinet walls) from **plastic** would avoid the painting step entirely. [[raw/flex_pay_production-march-27-2025.md]]
- **Devices:** each kiosk needs a bill validator (one or two), a card reader, and a dispenser — not very expensive; Genadi suggested possibly buying ready-made assemblies. [[raw/flex_pay_production-march-27-2025.md]]

## Volumes and the assembly bottleneck

- **~3,000 units in half a year ≈ 500/month.** One container holds roughly **500 sets** (maybe 400); for comparison ~**140 Flex machines** fit in a container. [[raw/flex_pay_production-march-27-2025.md]]
- A **metal cabinet must be screwed together**, which is slow; the priority is preparing a **fixture/jig** so assembly is fast. Units should not be shipped pre-assembled. [[raw/flex_pay_production-march-27-2025.md]]

## Val's "skinny unit" idea

- Late in the call Val floated an alternative: a **"skinny unit"** holding only a **dispenser** and a **fairly small screen**, with everything else removed. He wanted to think about it. [[raw/flex_pay_production-march-27-2025.md]]

## Open questions

- Tooling vs no tooling for the metal parts — unresolved; Val wants tooling as insurance, Genadi argues it barely moves the schedule. [[raw/flex_pay_production-march-27-2025.md]]
- Whether to move the cabinet (or parts of it) to **plastic** to skip painting, and whether the "skinny unit" is the right form factor. [[raw/flex_pay_production-march-27-2025.md]]
